Saga Overview & How to Run It

This is one house's ledger, told across five centuries of in-world time. The Exile Road is set in 337 AR — a full century before the empire's own final fall — the founding generation that carries the Valvano name into the fens, where the Tarling dux sheltering them is only beginning to style himself King of Viterra. The Reckoning Bridge picks up three centuries later, in 640 AR, when the Linns arrive at the Tarlington Gap and topple that same Tarling line. The Old Duke's Wars is Duke Braedyn Fenwood's deathbed confession in 840 AR, recounting two more centuries' worth of debts in seven vignettes. Within the Demon's Eye is the current saga proper: Shadowsteel's origin, the Sack of Lence retold from inside, and the present-day political intrigue of Veralyn's court.

Run all four as a single legacy campaign (heirlooms, oaths, and grudges from Book One resurface as late as Book Four), or run Book Four alone as a standalone veteran-tier campaign with the earlier books as GM background reading — see The Fenwood-Everblood Chronicles expansion for the connective genealogy and timeline.

Book One · 337 AR

The Exile Road

A Fenwood Legacy Adventure · Tier: Seasoned (41–90 XP) · 3–6 Players · 3–4 Sessions
The Valvano dynasty is dead. The Third Sack has ended our line — or so the heralds of the usurper proclaim. What remains is a cadet branch: a handful of cousins, servants, heirlooms, and a name that now draws pursuit like a wounded hart draws wolves.Lord Valerius Valvano

The empire of the Noon Bell has fallen. What flees eastward is not an army but a ledger — a column of names that must survive to be written in the next generation. This is the flight of the Valvano cadet branch, the Fenwoods-to-be, through burning Acasia to the dubious sanctuary of the Tarling fens.

Core Timers
  • Valvano Loyalty [6] — clinging to the old name and claims. Full: cannot abandon the name; bound to restore the dynasty or die trying.
  • Survival [8] — supplies, allies, shelter, will to continue. Empty: someone starves, an heirloom is lost, a PC gains Broken.
  • Tarling Favor [6] — goodwill of the Viterran house sought as refuge. Full: granted the pithy county of Fenwood.
  • Pursuit [6] — the usurper's agents on the trail. Full: a battle occurs; flee, surrender, or stand and be ended.

Act I: The Sack — by Lord Valerius Valvano

The PCs begin in the burning Valvano manor with minutes to gather what they can carry. Aqyl, the old scholar-witness, offers three heirlooms for the taking before the Pilgrim's Gate seals: the Laurel Seal (proof of lineage, +1 die to social rolls presenting it), the Bell Shard (makes allied rolls Dominant once per scene, but advances Pursuit when rung), and the Debt Ledger (ask the GM “what debt can we call in here?” once per session, at the cost of Obligation). A fourth, optional heirloom — the Sacred Coal from the Everflame chapel — marks its bearer Oath-Bound: unable to lie in direct sunlight without Harm, and heretical to the Temple of Light.

Key choices: whether to take in Livia, the Censor's daughter, possibly a Witness-cult spy or its last survivor; whether to accept an Oath-Brother, a Ykrul mercenary whose braid becomes a future favor from the Gray Ash clan; and whether a PC risks the flames to save the Debt Ledger from a collapsing archive. The City Collapse [6] clock governs how much of the escape goes clean versus catastrophic.

Act II: The Road — by Aqyl, son of Aqyl

The road east is a ledger of failures — Acasia frays, Silkstrand (then Ceylonne) welcomes coin, not refugees. At the Ykrul Ford, Khatun Sarnai of the Gray Ash clan recognizes the Laurel Seal from a hostage gift generations old, and offers passage for a price: acknowledge mixed-Ykrul kin (gain the follower the Unnamed Child), win a duel against her champion, or swear a Steppe Oath repayable in a future “fourth sack” campaign. Later, Linnic sea-raiders — ancestors of the Everbloods — offer an alliance against the dying Tarling dynasty in exchange for future land: accepting plants the Everblood Promise, the seed of everything Book Two builds on.

Act III: The Rejection — by a Retainer

The Vhasian duke's Mocking Feast tests the exiles' lineage claim through trials of Kon'reh, a duel, or reciting the Oath of the Noon Bell from memory — each failure advances Patience of the Gate [4]. A cousin's betrayal offer (selling the Valvano claim to the usurper) may be exposed or missed. The Tarling terms for sanctuary in the fens are humiliating by design: walk barefoot, surrender the Laurel Seal as tribute, or convert to the Temple of Light entirely — each term negotiable, at the cost of time and Patience.

Act IV: The Fens — by the Young Heir

Ash-Fenn, the cursed clearing granted as the new county, hums with something older than the empire. A Bog Witch offers to ward the dead ground for a price — a firstborn child (seeding a future curse track), a memory (a Bond or XP), or a promise collectible during the eventual “Fourth Sack.” At the spring moot, the cadet branch must choose its name: remain Valvano (loyalty 5+, name survives unbroken, no land), become Fenwood (favor 5+, gain the county, extinguish the coal), or compromise as Fenwood-Valvano (both timers at 3+, keep both, but the usurper will hear of it). Aqyl departs into the mist after the naming, leaving only the Debt Ledger and a braid of Ykrul hair.

A promise made under duress is still a promise. The bread and salt bind you as surely as chains, until you choose to break the guest-right. And if you break it, the road will remember. It always remembers.

Epilogue — The Braid and the Bell. Generations later, the Fenwood keep stands; the Tarlings are gone, deposed by the Everbloods with Fenwood swords. The Fenwoods are their trusted chancellor. The road does not forgive. It only reschedules.

Book Two · 640 AR

The Reckoning Bridge

A Fenwood Legacy Adventure · Tier: Seasoned–Veteran (41–150 XP) · 3–6 Players · 3–4 Sessions
The Linns came from the salt. They did not come as raiders, but as kings-in-waiting.Soren, Linnic Raid-Mother

The Linns arrive at the Tarlington Gap because the Fenwoods — those Valvanos reborn — sent them iron in the night, and a map of the low fords. The bridge itself is not Tarling, nor Viterran, but Aeler: a keystone clause carved into every span, a debt older than the empire that once borrowed it. This is a war of ledgers, keystones, and unpaid debts fought between three banners at once.

Core Timers
  • Linnic Momentum [8] — the invasion's progress. Full: the Linns reach Tarlington first and claim the crown by right of conquest.
  • Tarling Resistance [6] — the old dynasty's rally. Full: the invasion stalls; the Fenwoods are caught between hammer and anvil.
  • Fenwood Reckoning [6] — the countdown to Aldric's betrayal. Full: allegiance must be declared openly.
  • Keystone Debt [6] — the Aeler ledger's patience. Full: the bridge closes to Fenwood traffic until paid in blood or silver.
  • True Mason Favor [4] — the mendicant order's goodwill. Full: the bridge is reinforced and cannot be burned this season.
The True Masons
Mendicant masonry-mages who repair infrastructure and remember every keystone. “We do not build. We continue.”
The Fortune-Delvers
Rogue Aeler prospectors, unbound by Masonic oaths, always accurate and always costly.
Aeler Factors
Distant toll-collectors dealing in slate and wax seals; they speak of “load-bearing history.”

Act I: The Corsair's Map — narrated by Soren, Linnic Raid-Mother

PCs scout as Linnic raiders or Fenwood infiltrators. Orin of the True Masons offers to keep the bridge a neutral Safe Zone in exchange for a Keystone Offering and a sworn Bridge Oath; refusal weakens the wards and opens Bridge Fire [4]. A Fortune-Delver offers a shortcut through Aeler maintenance tunnels for a decade's toll-share — refuse, and she sells the same route to the Linns instead.

Act II: The Chancellor's Gambit — narrated by Aldric, Chancellor of the Fens

At the improved Fenwood keep, Chancellor Aldric has already sworn the Bridge Oath to Soren's ancestors in secret while performing loyalty at Tarling feasts. The Tarling War Council demands a levy; a True Mason's audit demands three years' unpaid tithe (twelve hundred bushels, a pledge of service, or refusal and an escalating Keystone Debt); a Linnic envoy presses for the Fenwoods to commit openly. Every choice here trades Tarling Suspicion [4] against Fenwood Reckoning.

Act III: The Bridge of Stone and Shadow — narrated by Mavron, Vhasian Chronicler

Three banners meet on the span itself. The ancient bridge-wardens — wraiths of the first True Masons, foremost among them Kaelen the Unspent, Toll-Keeper of the First Span — demand a toll to keep their protection: the Laurel Seal (if still held), a Repair Vow, or a Keystone Offering weighed against the bridge's own shadow. When Bridge Fire reaches 2, the span exists in two states at once — the Shadow Span, where a crossing costs a memory per hour but doubles your speed. At the critical moment, Aldric signals his hidden troops and the Fenwood levy turns on the Tarling rear guard: the PCs choose mercy or execution for the Tarling duke.

Act IV: The King's Capitulation — Aldric's final confession

The war ends not on the bridge but in a tent on the south bank: the King of Vhasia offers the Linnic captain (now Jarl Everblood) the crown of Viterra and a marriage pact. Depending on the party's choices across the campaign, House Fenwood emerges as Marquesses of the Tarlington Gap (bridge control, a permanent Standard Asset), Stewards of the Fens (an Everblood marriage alliance, the Everblood Promise as a String), or holders of a Keystone Charter from the grateful True Masons.

A ledger is a promise that outlives the hand that writes it. The Fenwoods have learned to pay. The Tarlings did not. The Aeler will wait. They have all the time in the world — or at least, until the bridge falls.
Book Three · 840 AR (flashbacks 792–838 AR)

The Old Duke's Wars

Duke Braedyn Fenwood's Deathbed Ledger · Frame Adventure · Requires The Exile Road, The Reckoning Bridge & the Kon'reh rules
The fire burns low. Outside, the Belworth churns with winter melt. Braedyn lies propped on pillows. A Canray slate rests on his chest. The pieces are not set.The Gray Wanderer

The old duke is dying — his left arm dead since the Sack of Lence, his breath shallow, but his eyes clear and his voice carrying sixty-eight years of ledger-keeping. The PCs are summoned to his solar not as servants but as witnesses. He tells seven stories, out of order, the way an old man's memory runs.

Frame Mechanics: The Seven Vignettes
Each vignette is a memory Braedyn bequeaths. After each, the players may ask one question; on a successful Wits + Insight (DV 3), they gain a String on the vignette's subject. When Braedyn dies at the end of the seventh, any unspent Strings become Obligations owed by his heirs.

Epilogue — The Road Reschedules. The fire is ash. Braedyn's chest rises once, twice, and stops. The Canray slate falls from his hand; the pieces scatter, all but one Blue lost to shadow. Outside, riders are already crossing the Belworth. The next ledger begins where this one ends — in Book Four.

Book Four · 832–841 AR

Within the Demon's Eye — The Prelude to Lence

Book One of the Within the Demon's Eye saga · Tier: Seasoned–Veteran · 3–6 Players · 9–12 Sessions across three acts
Under the lamp, blood and silk. The ink is smoke and altar-ash.

This is not one confession but three, stitched together against the wishes of everyone who gave them: Veralyn's, taken by force of friendship; Braedyn Fenwood's, given freely on his deathbed; and Ylena of Coldmorrow's, given for reasons that become clear only in Act III and should not be trusted further than the others. Two shadows fall across every page — Myrmis, who answers a prayer by taking as much as she gives, and Canray, the discipline the Fenwoods learned in exile of turning grief into a ledger entry.

Act I · 836 AR · Eryas Citadel and the Renard highlands

Shadowsteel — by Veralyn

Tier: Veteran (91–150 XP) · 3–4 sessions
They ask me what it felt like, killing my cousin on an altar to a goddess my mother prayed to in secret. I tell them it felt like nothing. That is the part they never believe.Veralyn “Demoneye” Everblood
Core Timers
  • The Shadow-Court's Reach [8] — assassinations and reprisals. Full: a member of the royal family is targeted directly.
  • Siege of Eryas [6] — the grinding cost of Wyllmot's assault. Full: the king dies mid-siege; the party inherits command.
  • The Altar's Hunger [6] — Myrmis's private ledger, GM-only. Full: the goddess collects (see Shadowsteel Awakens).
  • Renard Suspicion [4] — the court's growing certainty a Renard hand guided the Uprising. Full: Alaysha's secret is exposed before Veralyn is ready.

Wyllmot leads a fourth, fatal assault on Eryas — fixed history, but how he falls and who stands beside him is not. Seven weeks later, the party breaches the inner sanctum and Veralyn duels the Shadow-Court's champion, her distant cousin Gelfrey Renard, who does not draw a weapon: “You already carry hers. Come and collect what's owed.”

Shadowsteel Awakens
The instant the killing blow lands, the Altar's Hunger fills regardless of its mark. A blade is found beside the altar, unmarked, warm, and remembered by no one as having been placed there. Any PC who takes it up gains the Shadowsteel asset (Major, 8 XP): +1 die in defense of a claim believed just, but once per session the GM may spend SB 2 to make the blade “want” something. Refusing its want three times in a campaign ends the asset for good.
GM Only — Shadowsteel's Agenda
Shadowsteel does not want blood for its own sake — it wants reunion. It was Gelfrey Renard's, and before him it belonged to a line of Shadow-Court champions stretching back to the first debt Myrmis ever collected on Renard blood. Every “want” is a small rehearsal of that reunion: the blade wants its wielder to choose its account over their own, the way Gelfrey finally did. Honoring a want edges the wielder toward becoming the next Gelfrey; refusing one is met with patience, not anger — it has waited generations, and three refusals simply tell it this wielder is not the reunion it is owed. Never state this to players directly — play it consistently instead.
Act II · 832 AR (told as flashback) · The road to Lence & the village of Coldmorrow

The Sack of Lence — by Duke Braedyn Fenwood

Tier: Seasoned (41–90 XP) · 2–3 sessions
They ask me if I regret Lence. I tell them regret is a young man's luxury. Lence was the interest on that mercy. I paid it in someone else's city.Duke Braedyn Fenwood
Core Timers
  • The Sack [8] — fixed history; governs how much of the burning the party witnesses, not whether it happens.
  • Veralyn's Concealment [4] — how long the disguised princess stays hidden. Full: her father's men recognize her mid-battle.
  • Coldmorrow [6] — a secret debt clock. Advances with every mercy shown on the road; resets to 0 on arrival; its size becomes the size of the wound the village suffers.

Fifteen-year-old Veralyn, cropped hair and bound chest, squires in disguise for Seniro against her father's wishes. When the Sack fills, the burning of Lence runs as a montage chosen from the Ford of Ashes, the royal apartments, and the mirror-halls — Lyonel Vayle is wounded regardless of which the party attends. At Coldmorrow, Logan Thornhill — the child spared thirty years before, now a quiet cooper — fights “badly, and with everything he has” when cornered. Veralyn kills him with a mace; whether any PC tries to stop her, and what she says if they do, is not fixed. The village burns regardless, its size set by the party's own Coldmorrow clock — a debt for the mercies they showed elsewhere on the road, spent here.

Mercy shown on the road and cruelty shown at the destination are not opposites. They are the same coin, spent twice. The road remembers which face was up each time.
Act III · 840–841 AR · Veralyn's court, shortly after her coronation and Braedyn's death

The Long Game — by Ylena of Coldmorrow

Tier: Veteran (91–150 XP) · 4–5 sessions · rotating court intrigue, not a linear plot
Everyone at this court thinks they know what I want. They are all correct, and all incomplete. I want the throne to remember Coldmorrow the way I remember it — as a debt. I intend to collect.Ylena of Coldmorrow

Ylena — Logan Thornhill's daughter, saved from the Coldmorrow fire as an infant — has spent years inside Jaennis Fenwood's household as a trusted maid. She is not a villain in the simple sense: every move she makes is a Canray clause, not a blow, and a PC who beats her honestly at Kon'reh will, for the first time in the campaign, get the truth without asking. Signpost this early: a court rumor before the party ever plays her — “Jaennis's maid always gives ground at the ninth rim — lets you take it, smiles like she meant to” — and, if pressed further, “She's never lost a hand she cared about winning. Not once.” Neither rumor spells out the payoff; a player who puts it together and wins anyway should feel like they earned the reveal.

Core Timers
  • The Longblood Question [8] — Valdais's campaign to prove or disprove his own parentage. Full: he moves openly against the throne.
  • The Dauphin's Truth [6] — Elianor's hidden child in Ethor. Full: his identity goes public and every faction moves at once.
  • Kon'reh at Court [6] — the Fenwood discipline of favors, marriages, audits, and seals. Full: House Fenwood's position is unassailable for a generation — at a cost the party will regret trading.
  • Myrmis's Ledger [6] — GM-only, mirrors the Altar's Hunger. Full: Shadowsteel collects on its own account.

Run this act as a rotating set of court scenes: the Adria Audit (Wits+Lore, DV 4, uncovering irregularities in Valdais's tax rolls), the Ethor Nursery (Presence+Stealth or Sway, DV 4, Desperate, a glimpse of the Dauphin), the Widow's Silk (Jaennis Fenwood's trade of testimony against Ylena for a cleared name), and Shadowsteel's Want (SB 2 to force the blade's own agenda at court).

GM Advice — Four Clocks at Once
This act asks the table to track four timers simultaneously against a rotating, non-linear set of scenes — a lot to hold across four to five sessions. Put all four somewhere everyone can see: a physical court diagram with four dials, a shared digital whiteboard, even four dice towers on the table. Default to advancing at most one timer per scene (occasionally two, for a deliberate crux scene) rather than letting every scene ripple across all four — that keeps sessions legible instead of paralyzing the table with simultaneous countdowns.
Endings

The Ledger Closes (Kon'reh at Court fills first): House Fenwood's patient game wins outright — at a personal cost to Veralyn.

The Blade Collects (Myrmis's Ledger fills first): Shadowsteel chooses its own target, played as tragedy rather than twist.

The Sun Reforged (The Longblood Question and The Dauphin's Truth both fill): Vhasia fractures again, directly seeding a sequel campaign.

Every ending here is a version of the same question, asked in a different voice: which do you feed, the blade or the ledger? Neither answer is wrong. Both are expensive. The road does not forgive a debt. It only reschedules it.

Epilogue — Within the Demon's Eye. Veralyn keeps the blade close and the ledger closer. Somewhere in Ethor, a child who may or may not be a Longblood learns to read by candlelight. Somewhere in Adria, Valdais plays a very long game with a very short patience. And somewhere in the fens, an old duke's grave is visited once a year by a queen who still cannot say whether she regrets what he taught her. This is the first book of the Within the Demon's Eye saga. The threads left here are written to be picked up again.

Appendix — The Fenwood Legacy Tracker

This saga runs from a burning manor in Acasia to a court in Viterra roughly five centuries later. No table remembers everything, and no table should have to. Keep a single page — physical or digital, updated at the end of every session — across the whole run from The Exile Road through Within the Demon's Eye. It answers four questions whenever a later book reaches back for one.

Heirlooms
Which of the Laurel Seal, the Bell Shard, the Debt Ledger, and the Sacred Coal survived The Exile Road, and who holds each one now (or when, and to whom, it was lost, surrendered, or traded)?
Oaths & Debts
Every sworn oath and obligation taken on behalf of the line, to whom, and whether paid, forgiven, or still open — the Steppe Oath, the Everblood Promise, the Bridge Oath, Braedyn's Long Loan, the Unburned Oath, and any the table generated.
The Fenwood Ledger
A single cumulative lean — favors debt / balanced / favors mercy — updated at the close of each book. Colors how Ylena, Valdais, and Shadowsteel itself read the party's House by Act III of Within the Demon's Eye.
Who's Left
A running roster of major NPCs met across the saga, marked survived / lost / estranged and in which book. Aqyl, Sarnai, Aldric, Kaelen the Unspent, Logan Thornhill, and Ylena are the throughlines worth tracking deliberately.
