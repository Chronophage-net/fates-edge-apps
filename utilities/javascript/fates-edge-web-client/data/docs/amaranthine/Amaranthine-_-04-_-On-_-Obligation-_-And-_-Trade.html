On Obligation and Trade

The young think economics is about coin. The foolish think it is about resources. The reckless think it is about power. They are all wrong.

Coin is a convenience. Resources are a burden. Power is a promise you have not yet been asked to keep.

The world runs on obligation. Every transaction is a thread. Every trade is a knot. Every gift is a chain that can be pulled—now, or in a generation, or in a century when the one who gave it has been forgotten and the one who received it has become someone else entirely.

We of the Eight Doors understand this. We do not trade. We bind.

The Hollow Economy

In the lands beyond the settled places, the economy is not measured in silver or steel. It is measured in what is owed. The following are not metaphors. They are the currency of the world.

The Debt of Attention

The most valuable coin is the moment someone looks at you. To be seen is to be recorded. To be recorded is to be remembered. To be remembered is to be owed.

  • The Gaze of a Noble: When a lord looks at you, they have given you something. They have acknowledged your existence. You now owe them. You owe them a return glance, a report, a service, or simply the weight of knowing they saw you.

  • The Gaze of a Ghost: When the dead look at you, the debt is heavier. They have nothing else to give. Their attention is their entire existence. You owe them what they cannot have. You owe them a memory, a story, a name spoken aloud.

  • The Gaze of the Ninth: When the Ninth looks at you, you are no longer a person. You are a character. You are interesting. The debt is infinite. The only payment is a story that ends.

The Debt of Hospitality

To eat at someone’s table is to borrow their warmth. To sleep under their roof is to borrow their safety. To drink from their cup is to borrow their luck.

Hospitality is not free. It is a loan with no fixed term.

Across the Amaranthine, hospitality is measured in counts:

  • A guest who eats three meals must offer a service in return.

  • A guest who eats nine meals must offer a life-debt.

  • A guest who eats the ninth meal? The Hollow has already counted them.

The Debt of Gifts

A gift is not a gift. It is a test. To accept is to agree. To refuse is to declare war.

  • The Incomplete Gift: A gift that is missing something—a blade without a hilt, a cup without a bottom, a coin with one side blank—is a debt in motion. The receiver must complete it. The cost is their own.

  • The Shared Gift: A gift that is passed from one hand to another carries the debts of all who touched it. To accept a shared gift is to accept the obligations of everyone who held it before you.

  • The Forbidden Gift: A gift that cannot be refused—offered by a superior, a patron, or a ghost—is a trap. The debt is immediate. The terms are unknown.

The Debt of Promise

A promise is the most dangerous currency. It is pure obligation. It has no physical form. It cannot be seized or destroyed. It can only be kept—or multiplied.

The Economics of Promises:

  • A promise made in public is worth three times a promise made in private.

  • A promise made to a patron is worth five times a promise made to a peer.

  • A promise made to a child is worth ten times a promise made to an adult. The Hollow loves children. They remember everything.

  • A promise made to the dead is worth everything. The dead have nothing else to do but wait.

Regional Expressions of Obligation

Every culture develops its own customs around debt. These customs are not arbitrary; they reflect how that culture interacts with the Ninth and the Hollow. The following table presents a flexible template for designing regional obligation systems. A Game Master can adapt these entries to any setting.

Example regional obligation systems.
Region Type Primary Currency Debt Measure The Ninth’s Role
Agricultural lowlands Promise Tally-sticks The ninth stick is always held by the Hollow
Hill enclaves Hostage string Blood-tally The ninth knot is for the Hollow
Mountain spires Cup Step The ninth step leads into the Hollow
Coastal villages Bell Echo The ninth bell summons the Hollow

Adapting the Template

When designing a region’s economic customs, consider three questions:

  1. What is the physical token of a debt? (a stick, a string, a cup, a bell, a coin, a knot, a scar)

  2. How is a debt measured? (counts, weight, length, sound, memory)

  3. How does the Ninth manifest when the debt is ignored? (a door appears, a string tightens, a cup does not empty, a bell tolls without a ringer)

The answers to these questions will give you a unique economic flavour that is both mechanically useful and narratively rich.

The Real Economy

Do not let your players think they are trading in coin. The real trade is obligation.

  • When a character buys something: They are not exchanging coin. They are accepting a debt. The debt may be to the seller, to the community, or to the Hollow.

  • When a character accepts a gift: They are accepting an obligation. The gift will be called in. The Hollow will remember.

  • When a character offers a service: They are creating a debt. The debt may be repaid now, or later, or never. Never is not an option.

The Hollow’s Interest

The Hollow does not charge interest. It charges attention. Every debt that is not paid becomes more visible. The Hollow notices.

  • A small debt unpaid for a day: The Hollow sends a reminder—a knock, a whisper, a sudden chill.

  • A medium debt unpaid for a week: The Hollow sends a witness—a stranger who knows too much, a dream that does not fade.

  • A large debt unpaid for a month: The Hollow sends a collector—a figure who does not leave, a weight that does not lift.

Using Debt Timers

The Ninth and the Hollow work best when they are not random. They are cumulative. Use timers to track the pressure of unfulfilled obligations. The following table offers a general framework that can be adapted to any campaign.

Timer recommendations for tracking obligation.
Timer Size What It Tracks
Promise 4–6 Unkept oaths; ticks when a promise is broken or ignored. When full, the Hollow sends a reminder.
Debt 4–6 Unpaid obligations of any kind; ticks when a debt is acknowledged but not settled. When full, the Hollow sends a collector.
Attention 6–8 Escalation; ticks when characters take the ninth step, pour the ninth cup, or ignore warnings. When full, the Ninth manifests physically.

GM Guidance: The Balance of Obligation

The economics of obligation are not punishments. They are consequences. They should never feel arbitrary, and they should never feel unfair. When a character attracts the Ninth or the Hollow through economic entanglement, it should be because they made a choice.

The Golden Rules

  1. If a player could not have known what they were doing, do not invoke the Ninth or Hollow. Let it be a consequence of choice, not of ignorance.

  2. When the Ninth or Hollow appears, it should change the situation. A complication, not an ending. The players should still have agency. They should still be able to act. They should simply have to act around the debt.

  3. Debt is a story, not a number. Do not reduce it to a ledger. The weight of obligation is felt in whispers, in dreams, in the slow accumulation of dread.

Designing Regional Consequences

When a debt timer fills in a specific region, consider what form the Hollow’s attention might take. Use the region’s customs to guide you:

  • If the region uses tally-sticks, the Hollow might appear as a figure holding a stick with the character’s name.

  • If the region uses hostage strings, the Hollow might tighten an invisible cord around the character’s wrist.

  • If the region uses cups, the Hollow might present a cup that must be filled with a story or a memory.

  • If the region uses bells, the Hollow might ring a bell that cannot be unheard.

A Final Warning

The economy of obligation is not a system to be solved. It is a texture to be felt. The more you explain it, the less it works. Let the players discover it through experience, through the weight of their own choices.

Do not tell them the cost of a gift. Let them feel it when the gift is called in.

Do not tell them the terms of a promise. Let them learn them when the promise is broken.

The Hollow is patient. It will wait until the debt is interesting.

— Matron Alfiya, Keeper of the Eight Doors
