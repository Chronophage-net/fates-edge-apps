The Eastern Dolmis

“East of the Dolmis, the sea grows saltier and the land grows stranger. The halflings count their blessings in thread and silence; the gnomes measure their debts in stone and courtesy. The forest remembers empires that crumbled to dust, and the pirate-city feeds on the bones of a sunken age. Each shore is a different kind of hunger – but all of them remember the same ninth step.”
— The True-Mason

Aelaerem

Hearth & Hollow.

“The hollow counts your steps. The thread remembers your name. A proper harvest feast requires three things: the finest cider aged in the right barrel, a guest-loaf baked with intention, and the wisdom to know when to invite the quiet ones to sit by the fire. My geese know a stranger’s step from a mile off, and they’ve never been wrong. If they start honking at noon, you best check your larder – someone’s coming who wasn’t invited. And when the Hollow walks, the only safe place is a lit hearth with the ninth cup unpoured.”
— Apple-Matron Oria “Crimson Door”

What do people eat for breakfast? Oatcakes and honey – the oats are from the chalk downs, the honey from the bees that sleep in the barrow by the beech. The honey tastes of earth and old promises. The poor eat it with water; the rich eat it with cream and the knowledge that the Hollow might visit them instead.

What does a child learn before they can walk? To count in eights. Aelaerem children are taught the eight steps of the harvest dance, the eight knots of the guest-cord, the eight names of the ancestors who watch from the barrows. The ninth step is never taught. It is discovered – or it discovers you.

What is the first thing you notice about a stranger? Whether they carry bread and salt. A stranger who offers bread and salt before crossing a threshold is a guest. A stranger who does not is a threat – or a fool. The Hollow does not distinguish between the two.

What is the most common crime? Pouring the ninth cup. It is not a crime you can be arrested for. It is a crime the Hollow will punish. Those who pour the ninth cup find that the empty chair is no longer empty.

What does no one talk about but everyone knows? The Pale Shepherd is not a myth. He comes when lambs are born and when people go missing. He has no face. His hat is a shadow. He does not bargain. He only collects.

Where do people go when they die? Into the barrows. The dead are not buried – they are placed in the hollow hills with a cup of cider and a loaf of bread. The barrows are the Hollow’s larder. The dead are not dead. They are waiting.

How do you apologize for a serious wrong? You tie a red thread around your wrist and offer it to the wronged party. If the thread is accepted, you are forgiven. If it is cut, you are not. If it is tied around your throat, you have made a very serious mistake.

What is the most important festival? The Harvest Moot (autumn). The Apple-Matron pours the first cup for the eldest, the second for the guest, the eighth for the fire. The ninth cup is for the empty chair. The chair is never truly empty.

What is the secret that could destroy a powerful person? The Apple-Matron has never poured the ninth cup. The Hollow has been waiting for her to slip. She has not slipped. She is the only person in Aelaerem who has never made a mistake. The Hollow is patient. The Hollow is very, very patient.

In Amedell, the Aelaerem keep their bonds with bread, bells, and careful counting. Lanes run under ash and hawthorn; doors reddened with thread promise a seat and a story to any who step right. Their magic is housekeeping writ large — thresholds groomed, names jarred like jam, the Hollow fed with silence and the ninth cup left unpoured. When the Neighbors stir beneath the barrows, the folk answer with kettle songs and lantern law, and the fields behave because someone asked them to. Hospitality here is not kindness—it is a covenant. Every meal is a promise, every gift a thread, every candle an argument against the dark.

Amedell lies south of the Aelinnel homeland of Aevrossa, separated by the rolling downs and the slow waters of the River Adder. The two small-folk peoples share trade in red thread, copper, and seasonal hospitality, though the Aelaerem keep a wary distance from the fae-touched gnomish courts. It lies on the southeastern coast of the Dolmis, although the Aelaerem are rarely out to sea beyond coastal fishing.

What you notice first: The smell of baking bread and old apples. The way every threshold has a red thread tied at eye level. The silence of children when a stranger passes the village stone.

The one rule that kills newcomers: Never refuse an offered cup. The Hollow counts refusals as invitations. Pour the ninth cup for the empty chair, and you may feed it—or feed it.

Regional Mood: Warm Hearths, Cold Thresholds

Here, the land is not wilderness—it is a living weave of favors and omissions. The Aelaerem do not build walls; they build hedges, stiles, and the careful geometry of guest-right. A red door means bread, salt, and one safe night if you come honest. A cup-mark stone on the verge accepts butter as a prayer. The watch-geese know your step before you do, and the scarecrow turns to face the road when the Hollow is hungry. The Neighbors—the things that live under the barrows, in the uncounted ninth step, in the space between the candle and its shadow—are not enemies. They are neighbors. And neighbors, if treated politely, will stay on their side of the hedge.

The Hollow’s Attention (Campaign Timer)

The Hollow is not a monster. It is a forgetfulness that lives in thresholds, miscounts, and broken guest-right. Track its attention with a visible timer:

Hollow Attention [8] – Advances when:

  • A character refuses an offered cup (+1)

  • A promise is broken on a threshold (+1)

  • The ninth cup is poured (+2)

  • A guest-right is violated (GM spends 1 SB to tick +1)

  • A child points at an empty chair (the Hollow is already listening; +0, but the GM gains 1 SB)

At 4 segments: Lanterns flicker without wind. Dogs whine at nothing. The Hollow is watching. At 8 segments: The Hollow manifests. Roll on the Hollow Walks table. The party must offer a story, a memory, or a name to appease it.

Hollow Walks (d6)

When the Hollow Attention timer reaches 8, roll 1d6 for the manifestation:

  1. A pale figure stands at the edge of the circle. It asks for a name. Give a name (living or dead) and it leaves. Refuse, and it takes your shadow (–1 die on stealth for a week).

  2. The ninth cup pours itself. Whoever drinks from it forgets their greatest joy (lose 1 XP, refundable by a quest).

  3. A red thread appears tied around every PC’s wrist. It tightens when you lie. Cutting it costs 1 Corruption.

  4. The geese fall silent. The Hollow is inside the circle. It demands a story that ends with a death. If the story is true, it leaves. If false, it takes a memory.

  5. The Moot Oak bleeds sap. The Hollow speaks through it: “One of you carries a promise unpaid. Settle it now.” The one named must offer 1 Boon or 1 Supply. Refusal ticks the timer again immediately.

  6. The empty chair is occupied by a figure in gray. It does not speak. It eats. If you serve it food, it leaves. If you ignore it, it eats your luck (all rolls are Desperate for the next scene).

Guest-Right Violations (d6)

When a PC breaks guest-right (refuses a cup, draws steel under a red door, insults the host), roll 1d6:

d6 Consequence
1 The host demands a price of 1 Supply (the cost of a broken loaf).
2 The village stones turn inward. You are not welcome anywhere for a week (–1 Position on all social rolls).
3 A red thread ties itself around your wrist. It tightens when you speak to an Aelaerem. Cut it with a silver knife (cost 1 Boon) or suffer –1 die on all interactions.
4 The watch-geese chase you from the village. Gain a Marked by Geese condition (animals are hostile; +1 DV to all Beastcraft rolls).
5 The Hollow notices you. Tick Hollow Attention +2. The empty chair will be waiting at your next camp.
6 The Apple-Matron herself banishes you. Start a Gate of Thorns [4] timer — when full, you cannot enter any Aelaerem village until you pay a blood-price.

Aelaerem (Halflings) — “Hearth & Hollow”

Key Demographic: Aelaerem (Halfling) Captial: Amedell

Entanglements
To the North East:

Valewood – The downs rise toward the forest; the Green Gate sometimes opens onto halfling barrows. The Hollow’s attention is a shared dread.

To the North:

Aelinnel – The gnomes trade in copper and courtesy; the two small-folk peoples share the red thread custom but distrust the fae. The barrows and the fae courts have an unspoken treaty.

To the West:

The Dolmis Sea – Coastal fishing and rare trade with Zakov; the Hollow’s attention does not cross salt water easily, but the tide brings strange things ashore.

To the South West:

Theona (within the Dolmis) – The Green Host’s riders do not cross the water, but the Ninth Taboo echoes in the halfling tongue. The ninth cup is never poured on either shore.

(Lanes/Orchards/Mills/Downs/Barrows)

Willow ford with flat stones and a bowed pollard that remembers faces; cider-press barn with sweet reek, old songs, doors that stick after dusk; chalk sheep-downs with a turf maze cut like a wheel.

Spades — Places

  1. Willow Ford [RIVER] [OMEN]
    Flat stones, bowed pollard that remembers faces.
    The willow has a name. It will tell you if you sit beneath it and listen.
    Mechanical hook: To cross safely, you must whisper the willow’s name (Wits+Lore DV 3). On failure, the ford rises – lose 1 Supply (drowned cargo). On a critical failure, the willow asks for a story. Refuse and tick Hollow Attention +1.

  2. Cider-Press Barn [HARVEST] [MEMORY]
    Sweet reek, old songs, doors that stick after dusk.
    The press was built from a barrow oak. The oak still dreams.
    Mechanical hook: Sleeping here gives you dreams of the barrow. On a Spirit+Lore test (DV 4), you learn a secret of the dead (a Clue). On failure, the dream follows you – gain the Condition Barrow-Dreamt (–1 die on all Resolve tests for a day).

  3. Chalk Sheep-Downs [PASTURE] [RITUAL]
    Turf maze cut like a wheel; sheep know the pattern.
    The maze is a prayer. Walk it backward to ask a favor.
    Mechanical hook: Walking the maze backward (Body+Athletics DV 4) lets you ask one question of the land. The answer is a riddle. On a failure, you become lost – start a Lost in the Maze [4] timer.

  4. Millpond Under Alders [WATER] [OMEN]
    The wheel turns some nights without water.
    The water is not water. It is the memory of rain.
    Mechanical hook: Drinking from the pond restores 1 Fatigue but gives you a vision of a forgotten promise. The GM gains 1 SB (the promise will surface later). Refusing to drink ticks Hollow Attention +1 (the pond is offended).

  5. Bluebell Wood Path [FOREST] [WARD]
    Rabbit-gates and snares set by careful hands.
    The snares are for the Hollow. They catch only shadows—mostly.
    Mechanical hook: A character who trips a snare (failed Stealth) must test Spirit + Endurance (DV 4) or lose their shadow for one scene (–1 Position on stealth). On a success, the snare catches a shadow – the Hollow gains +1 SB.

  6. Hedge-Tunnel Lane [BOUNDARY] [TRICK]
    Between fields; nine stiles, locals count “eight-and-one.”
    The ninth stile is a gap in the hedge. Step through, and the lane behind you changes.
    Mechanical hook: Count the stiles aloud. If you reach nine, the lane loops. Tick Hollow Attention +1. If you stop at eight and step through the gap, gain +1 Position on navigation for the next leg.

  7. Cup-Mark Stone on the Verge [RITUAL] [OFFERING]
    Butter left in niches goes missing kindly.
    The butter vanishes before dawn. The Hollow leaves a pebble in exchange.
    Mechanical hook: Leave an offering (1 Supply) to clear 1 segment of Hollow Attention. If you take the pebble, you are marked – the GM may spend 1 SB to have the Hollow visit you in a dream.

  8. Barrow-by-the-Beech [DEATH] [SILENCE]
    Bees go quiet at noon.
    The bees are not sleeping. They are listening.
    Mechanical hook: A character who listens to the bees (Wits+Lore DV 3) hears the name of the next person to die in the village. On a failure, the bees swarm – mark 1 Fatigue. Speaking the name aloud ticks Hollow Attention +2.

  9. Market Green [COMMUNITY] [JUDGMENT]
    Maypole and stocks; carved village stones turned inward.
    The stones face the green. They watch the maypole dance—and the dancers.
    Mechanical hook: Any oath sworn on the green is witnessed by the stones. Breaking it ticks Hollow Attention +2 and the stones will not forget – the GM gains a String on the oath-breaker.

  10. Dovecote Hill [OMEN] [FEAR]
    The scarecrow faces the road, not the rows.
    The scarecrow has a face. It is the face of the last person who broke guest-right.
    Mechanical hook: If the scarecrow turns to face you, you have one hour to leave the village. Tick Hollow Attention +1 for each hour you stay. The scarecrow can be appeased with a story (Spirit+Performance DV 4).

  11. Mother’s Orchard [HARVEST] [PROMISE]
    Rows straighten if you don’t look—curve if you do.
    The orchard is a promise. Break it, and the apples will taste of ash.
    Mechanical hook: Picking an apple without permission ticks Hollow Attention +1. Eating an apple restores 1 Fatigue but gives you a Condition: Debtor to the Orchard (the next time you break a promise, the orchard will claim a memory).

  12. Moot Oak [LAW] [MEMORY]
    Lantern nails hammered deep; benches at the roots.
    The nails are from a hundred feuds. Each one holds a grudge.
    Mechanical hook: A character who sits on the benches may be judged by the oak. On a Presence+Sway test (DV 4), you may settle a feud. On failure, the oak adds a nail for you – start a Grudge of the Oak [4].

  13. Hollow Field [CURSE] [DANGER]
    A ring where no plow will hold a line, not for any coin.
    The field is a mouth. It has not fed in a century.
    Mechanical hook: Entering the field forces a Spirit + Endurance test (DV 4). On failure, you lose your appetite for a week (–1 die to all Endurance tests). The field can be fed a story or a memory to make it dormant for a year.

(Keepers/Elders/Mummers/Quiet Powers)

Hedge-witch midwife who ties red thread and unties feuds; miller and his watch-geese—better sentries than men.

Hearts — People & Factions

  1. Hedge-Witch Midwife [HEALING] [BOUNDARY]
    Ties red thread, unties feuds, and knows which births the Hollow watches.
    Her thread is from her own hair. She never cuts it—only knots it.
    What they carry: She once tied a knot around a stillborn child to keep the Hollow from claiming it. The knot is still there. If you help her cut it, she will teach you a thread-ward.
    What they ask: A lock of hair from a woman who has never lost a child. She will not say why.

  2. Miller [WATCHFUL] [LOYALTY]
    Watch-geese that remember faces; better sentries than men.
    The geese have names. One is “Stranger.” It has never been wrong.
    What they carry: The geese saved the village from a Hollow walk ten years ago. He has not slept since. If you watch the geese for a night (no roll), he will give you a Goose Feather (once, gain +1 die to detect ambush).
    What they ask: A name the geese do not know. He will carve it into a post.

  3. Orchard Reeve [AUTHORITY] [HARVEST]
    Tally-stick, cider-stained smile, and a list of who owes what harvest.
    His tally-stick is an apple branch. The notches grow back.
    What they carry: He lost a notch to a thief. If you find the thief, he will waive your harvest tithe for a year.
    What they ask: A bushel of apples (1 Supply) or a story about a harvest that failed.

  4. Beekeeper [SACRED] [SECRET]
    Keeps odd honey aside for “winter churches.”
    The honey is from barrow flowers. It tastes of earth and old promises.
    What they carry: The winter church once saved her bees from a blight. She has been paying in honey ever since. If you deliver a jar to the church, she will give you a Winter Honey (heals 1 Corruption, but the GM gains 1 SB).
    What they ask: A piece of wax. Melt it to seal a promise (the promise cannot be broken without the Hollow’s attention).

  5. Shepherd [COMMAND] [SPIRIT]
    Bone whistle that calls dogs—and other things.
    The whistle is from a wolf. The wolf still answers.
    What they carry: The wolf’s ghost follows him. He wants to lay it to rest. If you help, he will teach you the whistle.
    What they ask: A wolf’s tooth. He will not say where he got it.

  6. Lantern-Warden [WARD] [RITUAL]
    Trims lamps and knows which shadow is wrong.
    She lights the lamps in a pattern. The pattern is a prayer to keep the Hollow back.
    What they carry: One lamp went out last winter. The Hollow almost entered. She needs a new wick made from a hairless sheep. Find it, and she will bless your own lamp.
    What they ask: A night of silence (no speaking). If you break silence, the lamp gutters and ticks Hollow Attention +1.

  7. Mummers’ Captain [TRANSFORMATION] [LAW]
    Chest of masks, stricter rules than church.
    The masks are not carved. They are grown.
    What they carry: A mask was stolen last festival. The thief wore it and became someone else. Find the mask (or the thief), and he will grant you a Mummers’ License.
    What they ask: A story about a time you wore a false face. The mask will listen.

  8. Traveling Craft [TRADE] [OATH]
    Bright kettles, dull knife for cutting oaths.
    The knife was his father’s. His father used it to cut a promise with the Hollow.
    What they carry: The Hollow still wants the knife back. He will trade it for a story that makes the Hollow laugh. No one has succeeded.
    What they ask: A cut oath (void any minor promise). Using the knife ticks Hollow Attention +1 (the Hollow is watching).

  9. Bailiff of the Moot Oak [LAW] [MEMORY]
    Polite as a noose, carries a tally of grudges.
    His tally is bark. The writing grows deeper each year.
    What they carry: He was once a hedge-witch’s apprentice. She cursed him to carry the tally forever. If you lift the curse, he will give you a Grudge Waiver (once, cancel a feud).
    What they ask: A name from the tally. He will not say which one.

  10. Wold-Wardens [TRADITION] [BOUNDARY]
    Elders who swear by hedges, not crowns.
    They measure time in hedge-layers. A hedge that has grown for a century has a hundred opinions.
    What they carry: They lost a hedge-layer to a fire. The hedge is growing wild. If you help re-lay it (Craft test DV 5), they will grant you safe passage through any Aelaerem boundary for a year.
    What they ask: A cutting from the hedge. Plant it to claim a temporary threshold.

  11. Apple-Matron [AUTHORITY] [HOSPITALITY]
    Hostess of harvest feasts; power sits where she pours.
    She pours the first cup for the eldest, the second for the guest, the eighth for the fire. The ninth cup is for the empty chair.
    What they carry: She has never poured the ninth cup. The Hollow is waiting. If you offer to pour it for her (a sacrifice), she will grant you a Blessing of the Hearth (once, ignore a guest-right violation).
    What they ask: A cup of your own blood. She will not explain.

  12. Thresher-King [SEASONAL] [SACRIFICE]
    A title that moves but never leaves; harvest robes, seasonal authority.
    He is chosen by the last sheaf. The sheaf is always a little damp.
    What they carry: The sheaf is damp because it was cut from the Hollow Field. He is the field’s warden. If you help him harvest the field (a ritual), he will give you a Sheaf of Plenty (once, gain 1 Supply).
    What they ask: A sweat from your brow. The field is thirsty.

  13. The Pale Shepherd [DEATH] [THRESHOLD]
    Comes when lambs are born and when people go missing.
    He has no face. His hat is a shadow.
    What they carry: He is the Hollow’s messenger. He does not bargain. He only collects. If you meet him, you have one minute to offer a story, a memory, or a name.
    What they ask: Nothing. He is not a patron. He is a deadline.

(Folk Omens/Rites/Beasts/Night)

Unseasonal fog; the road walks you back to your own gate. Scarecrow turns; it watches the lane, not the field. Black sow through the orchard; hooves scuff every charm.

Clubs — Complications/Threats

  1. Unseasonal Fog [WEATHER] [TRAP]
    The road walks you back to your own gate.
    The gate is closed. The latch is red. Do not touch it.
    Mechanical hook: Navigation rolls are Desperate. On a Miss, you find yourself at the gate of a house that does not exist. The door opens. Inside, the Hollow waits. Offer a story or lose 1 Boon.

  2. Scarecrow Turns [OMEN] [FEAR]

Aelinnel

Stone, Bough, and Bright Things.

“Count the steps, weigh the words, and never trust a single telling. The law here flows like water over stone – it finds its own path, and woe to those who try to dam it with paper and pretense. Our kilns tell us more than your books ever could. When the smoke curls left, the tide’s with you. When it splits in three, best stay by your own hearth till it burns straight again. And never trust a court that speaks in only one voice – the silence is always the second witness.”
— Lady of Thorns

What do people eat for breakfast? Porridge with honey and a pinch of salt – the salt is from the tide-rift steps, the honey from the Moonwell basin. The salt tastes of memory; the honey tastes of forgetting. The balance is everything.

What does a child learn before they can walk? To count in prime numbers. Aelinnel children are taught the sequence: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19. The ninth number is not taught. It is discovered – and it is always a door.

What is the first thing you notice about a stranger? Whether they carry iron. Iron is not forbidden in Aelinnel; it is simply impolite. A stranger who carries iron is making a statement. The statement is heard. The hearing is remembered.

What is the most common crime? Breaking a geas. A geas is not a law; it is a word that has grown teeth. Those who break a geas find that the word follows them, and the word is always hungry.

What does no one talk about but everyone knows? The Green Gate is not a gate. It is a mouth. It opens when it is fed. It is always hungry. The Aelinnel feed it with exact change.

Where do people go when they die? Into the barrow galleries. The dead are not buried; they are placed in stone chimes that sing their names in the wind. The ninth note is always a sigh. The sigh is the Hollow’s answer.

How do you apologize for a serious wrong? You carve an oath-bead. The bead warms when the apology is accepted. If it cools, the apology is not. If it glows, the Hollow has heard you, and the Hollow is not pleased.

What is the most important festival? The Counting of the Stones (spring equinox). Every Aelinnel counts the stones of their threshold. Those who count correctly are blessed. Those who count incorrectly are visited. The ninth stone is always warm.

What is the secret that could destroy a powerful person? The Stone Prince’s seal is a lie. It was carved by a half-fae, half-mortal artisan who did not understand the law they were writing. The seal is still warm. It is still writing.

The Aelinnel do not build cities. They grow them – from granite and hawthorn, from tide-polished bone and the shadow of a laugh. Their halls thread through sea-cliffs and root-galleries, their roads are counted by antler-posts that move at night, and their doors open only when you have forgotten why you came. Here, promises have weight – not the weight of ink on parchment, but the weight of a stone dropped into a still pool. The ripples go on forever.

To the north, the Valewood breathes; to the south, the downs of Amedell where the Aelaerem tie red threads to keep the Hollow at bay. The Green Gate sometimes opens onto halfling barrows – a fact neither people speaks of aloud, because speaking aloud gives the gate permission to move.

No single crown rules the gnomish lands. Dozens of courts, seasonal pacts, and garden societies weave a shifting lattice of loyalty. Some Aelinnel keep the old fae treaties, speaking in two voices and never carrying naked iron; others barter in the Green Market with memories instead of coin, treating courtesy as a kind of lockpicking. Still others vanish into the deep woods, listening to stag-roads where the trees whisper gossip about the living. The gnomish heart is divided – and that division is the only reason the fae haven’t eaten them whole.

Craft and courtesy govern every traffic between mortal keeps and the courts under the hill. Gnomish stone-singers hum bridges into alignment, their melodies keeping the Green Gate at bay. Forester-wardens hammer copper nails into thresholds because iron, they say, startles the truth. In markets under living roofs, a sentence can be folded like a napkin, and a memory can be wrapped for travel – but never opened before the second sunrise. Paths sometimes shorten for those who keep good count, and lengthen for those who sneer at patterns. The fae are never rude – but they are never, ever safe.

  1. Tide-rift steps – Cut into black rock; limpets and old votive nails keep count for those who forget. The ninth step is underwater – and it still counts. [THRESHOLD]

  2. Moonwell basin – A granite bowl; coins turn green in a week, names in a season, and memories in a year. The water never reflects the same face twice. [MAGIC]

  3. Dolmen stair – Up a ridge; each capstone rings a different interval if you tap it true. The ninth stone is silent – it only rings when someone lies. [MUSIC]

  4. Charcoal coppice – Under witch-hazel; a neat ash-heap hides a door that opens on even knocks only. Knock an odd number, and the door will open – but not where you left it. [SECRET]

  5. Basalt organ cliffs – Sea-caves breathe like slow bellows; the wind prefers prime numbers. When it plays a ninth, the cliffs hum back. [NATURE]

  6. Stag Road – Game-trail marked by antler-posts; hooves know it best, maps accept it grudgingly. The posts move at night – not to confuse, but to keep the road honest. [TRAVEL]

  7. Quartz spring – Sand shines like ground stars; drinkers tell the same story with better structure. The ninth sip tastes of copper – and a promise you haven’t made yet. [HEALING]

  8. Elf-causey – Pale flags through a marsh; visible at dawn and dusk and whenever someone is counting aloud. Count to nine, and the flags turn to water. [FAE]

  9. Root gallery – Under an oak hill; lanterns hang from living bark – two lit, one listening. The listening lantern is for the Hollow. It never lights. [UNDERGROUND]

  10. Barrow gallery – Stone chimes; a cold draft from below plays scales no throat can sing. The ninth note is always a sigh. [DEATH]

  11. Thorn Court ring – Hawthorns trained into arches; petals fall like knife-points and settle into proofs. Step into the center, and the thorns will ask you a riddle. Answer wrong, and you become a hedge. [FAE] [DANGER]

  12. Hall of Aelinnel – A timber keep threaded between standing stones; floors level themselves for guests. The ninth stone in the floor is always warm – it remembers the last person who lied. [CIVILISATION]

  13. The Green Gate – In a sea-arch; at certain tides it opens to a forest not on any map, and expects exact change. It does not accept coin. It accepts explanations. [GATE]

Hearts — People & Factions (keepers, courts, craftsmen, hunters)

  1. Goat-herd of the stone edges – Measures danger in hoof-widths; never wrong twice. The third time, she doesn’t measure – she runs. [SURVIVAL]

  2. Charcoal-burner clan – Read draught and omen by smoke; their kilns hum in thirds. When the hum stops, someone has asked a question they shouldn’t have. [DIVINATION]

  3. Hedge-witch – A pocket of thorns and prices stated in unlesses. Her fees are always conditional: “I will heal your child, unless the moon is full, in which case I will only listen.” [MAGIC] [SOCIAL]

  4. Stone-singer (quarryman) – Hum finds a seam and a secret; walls relax when they pass. The ninth hum is for doors that do not exist – yet. [CRAFT]

  5. Forester-warden – Copper nails and a polite dislike of iron; paths obey their hammer. They never cut a branch – they ask permission, and sometimes the branch says no. [NATURE]

  6. Reed-net fisher – Knows when the selkies listen; mends nets to lullabies in 5s and 8s. The ninth verse is always for the drowned. [TRADE]

  7. Oath-carver – Sets promises in quartz so they can be seen; breaks none, bends many. A broken quartz-oath turns to sand – and the sand remembers. [LAW] [OATH]

  8. Way-keeper of the Stag Road – Never lost, often followed, sometimes found ahead. When she stops at a crossroads, she always turns her back to the ninth path. [GUIDE]

  9. Green-market broker – Trading truths for trinkets and back again; receipts in leaf-vein script. The truth she sells is always true – but never the truth you asked for. [COMMERCE]

  10. Green Knight – Antler helm, mirror-bright blade, courtesy old as frost; keeps a tally of saved insults. He never unsheathes his sword – he only shows it, and that is usually enough. [FAE] [DUEL]

  11. Lady of Thorns – Sovereign of a hawthorn court; smiles like a snare and forgives in exact measures. Her forgiveness is always exactly one thorn’s weight – no more, no less. [FAE] [LAW]

  12. Stone Prince of Aelinnel – Mortal crown with fae obligations properly indexed and current. He is the only gnome who has never lied – because he never speaks in declaratives. [AUTHORITY]

  13. Huntsman of the Moonlit Ride – His horn turns shortcuts into roads, and obligations into destinations. When he blows the ninth note, the hunt ends – but the hunted never comes back. [HUNT]

Clubs — Complications/Threats (glamour, geasa, weather, neighbors)

  1. Glamour fog – Time drifts, footfalls don’t match, conversations arrive neatly folded. Open a sentence, and you may find last Tuesday inside. [ILLUSION]

  2. Iron offense – Someone brought the wrong nails to the right grove; paths take it personally. The trees do not speak – they remember. [SOCIAL] [WEIGHT]

  3. Root-slide – Pulls a trail two ridges away while you blink; your footprints stay where they were. The footprints will wait for you – for nine days. After that, they walk home without you. [TRAVEL]

  4. Wrong tide at the sea-arch – Boats rise where arithmetic says no and physics excuses itself. The ninth wave is always the wrong one – it goes sideways. [WEATHER]

  5. Spoken geas – Catches on an unlucky word; the task names itself and refuses synonyms. You cannot say “fetch” when you meant “steal” – the geas knows the difference. [MAGIC] [OATH]

  6. Green Market price – Payment demanded in names and memories; change returned in riddles. The riddles are always about thresholds. The answers are always doors. [COMMERCE] [WEIGHT]

  7. Stone-wight stirs – In a barrow; echoes hate company and file complaints as drafts. The complaints are written in a language that only the dead speak – and they are very articulate. [DEATH]

  8. Lost day – The sun miscounts; your dawn arrives at supper and demands bread. The bread must be from a loaf you have not yet baked. [TIME]

  9. Thorn blight – Crawls across orchards; pruners bleed stories that grow where they drip. The stories are always about a door you left open. [CURSE]

  10. Stag horn sounds – Everyone owes the chase – especially you, especially now. The horn does not say who is hunting. It only says that the hunt has begun. [HUNT] [WEIGHT]

  11. Thorn Court tithe – Levied on the Hall; arrests wear flowers and quote etiquette. The charge is always insufficient courtesy. The penalty is always a lesson. [LAW] [SOCIAL]

  12. Muster of the Bough – Forest banners rise; travel becomes trespass by default. The banners were trees yesterday. They are still adjusting. [CONFLICT]

  13. Green Gate yawns – At the wrong hour; roads rewire across your path with excellent logic. The logic is always sound. The destination is always somewhere else. [GATE] [MYSTERY]

Diamonds — Treasures & Discoveries (charms, passes, host-rights, exceptions)

  1. Hazel token – Step through a hedge uncut, once; the scratch you did not get will remember you kindly. The token is a nut. Crack it open, and the hedge will part – but it will also ask a question. [PROTECTION]

  2. Salt license – Carry and use ward-salt where it’s discouraged; doors frown and open anyway. The license is a pinch of salt in a silk pouch. The silk was a promise. [UTILITY]

  3. Dolmen carving-right – Inscribe a name or claim on a speaking stone; arguments quiet while you carve. The stone will remember your chisel’s touch. It will also remember what you were thinking. [AUTHORITY]

  4. Guest-bough – From the Thorn Court; one night’s safe table among thorns; knives behave. The bough is a twig with three thorns. Break one thorn for each courtesy you ask. When all three are broken, the table becomes a door. [HOSPITALITY]

  5. Quarry allotment – Of clear quartz; subtle doors open to its bearer if asked in the right tense. The right tense is not past or future – it is conditional. [ACCESS]

  6. Green Market voucher – Buy a truth at face value (today only); tomorrow costs interest. The interest is always a memory – and the voucher chooses which one. [COMMERCE]

  7. Oath-bead – A spoken promise warms the bead when kept, chills when not, glows if misunderstood. A glowing bead is not a broken promise – it is a promise that has learned to speak for itself. [OATH]

  8. Tide-path key – Lawful crossing of a named sea-cave at neap; the cave will wait. The key is a piece of dried seaweed. It will dissolve after one use – but the cave will remember your face. [TRAVEL]

  9. Forest truce – Hunt, cut, or travel through a warded copse without offense; the birds file no reports. The truce is a single leaf. Carry it in your mouth, and the forest will not see you – but the Hollow will. [PERMISSION]

  10. Green Knight escort – From ridge to gate; watchers bow, paths part, insults lodge elsewhere. The Knight does not speak during the escort. The silence is the escort. [PROTECTION]

  11. Private audience – With the Lady of Thorns; a whisper exits as policy with petals attached. The petals are sharp. Do not handle them carelessly – they cut what they agree with. [AUTHORITY]

  12. Stone Prince’s seal – Levies defer, foresters guide, scribes stop arguing and fetch tea. The seal is a pebble. It only works when you are not looking at it. [AUTHORITY]

  13. Wild Hunt clemency – Ride under the horn for one night; no hound will take you, no obligation will sleep. The clemency is a silver bell. Ring it, and the Hunt will pass you by – but the Hollow will ride behind you until dawn. [PROTECTION]

Field Procedures (Use At the Table)

  • Two-Voice Talk: When parley stalls, the GM asks: “What was said? What was meant?” If players state both, cancel the first social SB this scene; if they refuse, the next bargain demands collateral (memory/name). The collateral is always returned – but never in the same condition.

  • Count or Be Counted: At any fae-touched crossing, a player who audibly counts in a fitting sequence (steps, heartbeats, breaths) shifts Position one step safer for that crossing. Boasting or mocking numbers adds a free “Geas Catches” SB. The geas is always about a door. The door is always behind you.

  • Copper Courtesy Check: Producing copper/brass tools in a ritual space removes the first Fae Offense tag this scene; drawing iron instead escalates the next Complication by +1 Cap. The escalation is always polite. The result is never.

Boss Archetypes in Aelinnel

Court & Courtesy (Fae Sovereigns)

  • Lady of Thorns (Etiquette Engine) – Punishes breaches with precise harms, rewards perfect courtesy with literal boons. Win by satisfying three courtesies in sequence (no iron, two-voice speech, return the waymark). The third courtesy is always a riddle. The riddle is always about a door.

  • Green Knight (Trial by Pattern) – Duel of paths and proofs, not blades. Win by presenting a higher-count route (Fibonacci, even-steps) or yielding a deserved apology at the right number. The right number is never nine. The right number is always eight – and a pause.

Threshold & Hunt (Environment that Judges)

  • Moonlit Ride (Wild Hunt) – Three phases: Sounding (horns), Coursing (paths shorten for the bold), Closing the Ring. Win by naming safe-clemency, joining the chase without taking a prey, or blowing the right counter-call (2–3–5). The counter-call must be a question. The question must be about the Hollow. The Hollow does not answer – but it listens.

  • Green Gate (Living Crossing) – Demands exact change in truths. Win by paying a memory that fits the posted proof; forcing passage triggers “Roads Rewired.” The posted proof is always incomplete. The missing part is the ninth word.

Numbers & Glamour (Logical Anomalies)

  • Glamour Fog (Layered Illusion) – Each layer dispelled by a correct count, courtesy, or copper rite. Win by completing the Three Proofs: count aloud, return a borrowed path token, trade a said/meant receipt. The third proof is always a promise. The promise is always to a shadow.

  • Lost Day (Time Miscount) – The sun miscounts hours. Win by reconciling two ledgers (what happened / what should have), then ringing a prime-number chime. The prime number is always 2, 3, 5, or 7 – never 9. 9 is not a number in Aelinnel. 9 is a suggestion.

Market & Oath (Contract Tricksters)

  • Green-Market Broker (Truth Arbitrage) – Buys your meanings, sells your words. Win by catching them in a one-voice statement or producing an Oath-Bead that chills (misunderstood clause). The misunderstood clause is always the one you left unsaid. The broker knew you would.

  • Oath-Carver Gone Wrong (Quartz Tyrant) – Binds towns with over-precise promises. Win by carving a Release Unless into communal quartz at cost of a public truth. The truth must be one you have never told. The quartz will remember it. The quartz will also share it – politely, and only with the Hollow.

Stone & Memory (Ancestral Hazards)

  • Stone-Wight Chorus (Echo Jurors) – Resent footsteps, adore proofs. Win by tapping the dolmen scale that matches the grievance and returning a taken token to the barrow. The token is always a memory. The memory is always someone else’s. Return it carefully – the dead are particular about provenance.

Among the Aelinnel, power flows through courtesy, oath, and the hidden geometry of the land. A Stone Prince’s authority rests on indexed fae treaties; a Green Knight’s on the trials he has witnessed. There is no single crown, only the shared understanding that a broken promise cuts deeper than a sword – and that a promise kept is a door that opens both ways.

For the GM: Power in Aelinnel society is measured in hazel tokens, oath-beads, and the precision of a courtesy. Patronage often takes the form of guest-boughs, tide-path keys, or the Green Knight’s escort – each tied to a specific court, grove, or obligation. To emphasize the cultural mechanics:

  • Use Two-Voice Courtesy to reward players who pay attention to the unspoken. The unspoken is where the fae live.

  • Enforce Copper over Iron – carrying iron is not forbidden, but it is a statement. The statement is always heard. The hearing is always remembered.

  • Let The Unsaid Clause be a source of future complications; the fae love what is not said. They will fill the silence with their own words – and their words have weight.

  • Track Obligation to the Green Gate as a persistent timer. Each crossing without proper payment ticks it toward a visit from the Huntsman. The Huntsman is always polite. The Huntsman is always hungry.

In Aelinnel society, the stone remembers every step – and the Green Gate forgets no promise. The ninth step is not a step. The ninth step is a door. The door is always open. You just have to remember how to knock.

Valewood

Empire Under Leaves.

“The forest remembers. Speak truth, step lightly, and never eat the fruit of a tree with a face. The forest here remembers empires that crumbled to dust, and its laws are older than your kingdoms. To walk these paths is to enter into covenant with powers that measure time in seasons, not years. The woods here got a mind of their own – they’ll lead you where you need to go if you ask right, but they’ll just as soon lose you if you’re carrying ill intent. Best know which is which before you wander off the path.”
— Hazel Queen Amalthea

What do people eat for breakfast? Berry porridge and mushroom tea – the berries are from the star-road groves, the mushrooms from the breathing city’s lower levels. The porridge tastes of memory; the tea tastes of what you have forgotten.

What does a child learn before they can walk? To name the trees. Valewood children learn the eight sacred groves: Hazel, Alder, Oak, Ash, Thorn, Yew, Willow, and the ninth – the grove that has no name. The ninth grove is where the empire’s heart still beats.

What is the first thing you notice about a stranger? Whether they are followed by a shadow. The forest gives shadows to those who walk lightly. It takes them from those who do not.

What is the most common crime? Name-theft. The forest remembers names, and it can be persuaded to forget them. Those whose names are stolen are not dead – they are simply no longer themselves.

What does no one talk about but everyone knows? The Genesis Vats still hum beneath the Amber Ziggurat. The Lethai-thora who sealed them did not die. They became something else. Something that still remembers the equations.

Where do people go when they die? Into the roots. The dead are not buried; they are woven into the forest’s memory. The trees speak their names in the wind. The names are always a warning.

How do you apologize for a serious wrong? You offer a name-bead. If the bead warms, you are forgiven. If it cools, you are not. If it shatters, the forest has judged you, and the judgment is final.

What is the most important festival? The Phasing of the Star-Roads (autumn equinox). The star-roads align with the empire’s fallen towers. Those who walk them at the right hour may see the empire as it was. Those who walk them at the wrong hour may never return.

What is the secret that could destroy a powerful person? The Hazel Queen is not a queen. She is a memory of a queen, preserved in amber and rooted in the forest’s heart. The memory is fading. The forest is forgetting her name.

The Valewood is older than kingdoms, older than the maps that skirt its borders. Beneath its canopy lie the bones of a Lethai empire whose name is forgotten, its towers swallowed by vine and root, its star-roads cracked and phasing between worlds. The air hums with half-remembered songs of magic, and the ruins bleed their mysteries into root and stream. To walk the Valewood is to risk being claimed by it: as guest, prey, heir—or trespasser.

But the empire did not fall to war or plague alone. In their final centuries, the Lethai-thora — the high elf archivists and sorcerers — grew desperate to match the Astroegro Empire’s arcane might. They turned to forbidden arts: flesh-shaping, soul-stitching, and the binding of patterns that should never have been woven. Deep beneath the Amber Ziggurat, in chambers sealed with runes that weep, they built the Genesis Vats. Here, elf and beast were combined, spirit and stone were fused, and the first beast-kin were born — not as servants, but as weapons. The vats still hum. The failures, the half-finished, the screaming ones, were loosed into the forest when the laboratories collapsed. They bred true. They became the monsters that now stalk the star-roads: the redcaps, the thorn-walkers, the things with too many eyes.

The empire fell, yes. But something else came through the cracks. A scent of honey and rot. A laugh from a clearing that should not exist. The Lethai-thora sealed the deepest laboratories and burned their records. The few survivors spoke of a “soft place” in the world’s fabric, a realm of endless twilight where the fae had always waited. They carved warnings into stone: Do not open the ninth gate. Do not sing the song that calls them. The gates were sealed. The songs were forgotten. But the forest remembers. And in certain clearings, when the moon is wrong, the air smells of jasmine and grave-soil.

What you notice first: The smell of wet moss and old stone. The way the light filters through leaves like stained glass. The silence that falls when you speak a name too loudly.

The one rule that kills newcomers: Never eat the fruit of a tree that has a face. The forest does not forget those who take, and the first bite is always a promise.

Regional Mood: Leaves, Oaths, and Echoes

Here, the past is not past — it phases in and out like moonlight on water. The Lethai-ar who remain are not conquerors but custodians, tending to laws written before the first human kingdom rose. The fae courts of Hazel and Alder play their endless games of courtesy and obligation, and the beast-kin speak in parliaments where every vote is a scent. Imperial shades march through walls that no longer exist, and the star-roads hum with geometries that twist the unwary into yesterday.

But the true horror lies beneath. The Lethai-thora’s experiments left scars that never healed. The beast-kin are not a single people — they are the descendants of those who survived the vats, their lineages mixed with wild creatures, their memories fragmented. Some still hear the whispers of the old laboratories, calling them to seek the sealed doors. Others have built a society of scent and scar, their laws a deliberate forgetting of what they once were. The monsters — the true monsters, the ones with no parliament — are the failed experiments: the redcaps, the thorn-walkers, the things that hunger for warmth. They are the empire’s unfinished business.

And somewhere, in a place where the trees grow in impossible spirals, the soft place still breathes. The fae courts are its children. They do not speak of what lies beyond the ninth gate. They only smile, and their smiles have too many teeth.

The Ninth Taboo Manifestation

In the Valewood, the ninth leaf to fall from any tree points to a hidden threshold. The wise catch it or pretend not to see. Those who follow the leaf’s direction find a place that was not there a moment ago, and something old waits inside. The threshold may lead to a sealed laboratory, a fae court, or a clearing where the air smells of jasmine and grave-soil. It is always warm.

The Lethai-al (Wood Elves)

Beneath the courtly titles and the star-road records, the true wood elves endure. They call themselves the Lethai-al—those who remain when the tower falls. They do not walk the high roads; they mend them from below, speaking to roots instead of stars. Their elders are oaks, their children are saplings, and their grievances outlast stone.

Where the Lethai-thora sought perfection and the courts seek dominion, the Lethai-al seek only reciprocity. They keep no scrolls and sign no contracts, yet nothing is given without expectation. A meal shared is a shield-pledge. A path shown is a future claimed. To them, the forest’s question is not spoken but lived: What was taken, and what will be returned? Outsiders rarely recognize the weight of these moments until the trees lean in.

The Lethai-al do not attend the parliaments of beast-kin or the audiences of Hazel. When they must speak with outsiders, they do so through intermediaries—moss-scribes, owl-sisters, or the rare gray pilgrim—so that the words are never truly theirs, and the obligation, if any, remains unrooted. They remember the sealed doors better than the archivists, for they were the ones who grew the thorns over them.

Quick use notes

In Zakov, power flows through the control of smuggling routes, the management of syndicate weights, and the ability to navigate the complex web of pirate politics. The Salt Prince maintains nominal authority, but real power lies with those who control the tides, the reefs, and the flow of information through the city’s taverns and black markets. The true authorities are those who can provide safe passage, protect cargoes, and mediate between competing syndicates.

The Kahfagians change everything. Above the syndicate politics, a quieter war is fought. Naval Intelligence wants to contain the Reaping and recover the artifacts. The Oligarchs want to control the artifacts and profit from the chaos. The Ordo Artificum serves both — and neither. Patron Alaric funds the artifact hunt. Commodore Voss commands the Adjutant agents. The Gray Lady’s cutter is a permanent fixture in the smuggler’s gate. Players who engage with the Kahfagian factions will find themselves caught between two imperial powers that do not trust each other and do not care about Zakov’s survival.

The Reaping changes everything else. Whoever controls the crystalline shard — or understands its nature — holds a weapon that could unmake the city or save it. The Salt Prince keeps the Reaping’s location secret, but the Syndicates whisper that he is no longer its master. The Reaping has begun to act on its own. It reaches through the tide, through the dreams of the drowned, through the taste of brine in a smuggler’s coin. Zakov is not ruled by the Prince. It is ruled by a hunger that has been patient for centuries. The Astroegro sealed it. The first Salt Prince bargained with it. Now the seal cracks, and the crystal grows. The Kahfagians know this. They are trying to stop it. They are also trying to exploit it.

For the GM: Patronage in Zakov revolves around smuggling rights, safe harbors, and the ability to provide protection from both legal and illegal authorities. Rewards often take the form of tokens, charters, and safe passage that can be leveraged into greater influence. The Kahfagian factions add a layer of imperial pressure: Naval Intelligence offers intelligence and support but demands compliance; the Oligarchs offer wealth but demand loyalty; the Ordo Artificum offers knowledge but demands sacrifice. To emphasize the city’s fluid power and the creeping horror of the Reaping:

  • Tie rewards to visible symbols (tokens, manifests, keys) that can be challenged, stolen, or voided — and that may carry a trace of crystal.

  • Let rival syndicates and Kahfagian factions issue conflicting protections, forcing players to choose whose favor matters more — and whose weights are safer.

  • Use the docks, taverns, and smuggling routes as arenas for social contests, where knowledge of custom and connections determines success — and where a character’s shadow may betray them.

  • Track Reaping’s Corrosion, Naval Intelligence Influence, and Oligarch Standing as campaign timers. Their advance is the city’s slow death. The players can delay it, redirect it, or even embrace it — but they cannot ignore it. The Reaping is patient. The Gray Lady is watching. Patron Alaric is counting.

In Zakov, your reputation is your wealth, and your wealth determines whether you rule the waves or feed the fishes. But beneath the waves, the Reaping feeds on reputation too. And it is very, very hungry. The Gray Lady is hungry too. She just doesn’t show it.
