Preface: Three Voices

This book is not a travel guide. It is a collection of thresholds: bridges that remember every toll, fog-bells that count your breaths, stones that warm when you speak a true name. Three travelers have walked these roads before you. They have agreed to lend their voices to the pages that follow. Listen to each; they will disagree, and that is the point.

Lady Isolde of the Scar, Fifth of Her Name

“The sun shattered at Lence. The pieces are still falling. Which shadow will catch you?”

I am Isolde, and I have worn a gorget to three tournaments, buried two brothers, and learned that chivalry is not a code but a weight that grinds the soul. You bear it or it breaks you; there is no middle path. My house has clung to the valley north of Rouen since before the Fenwoods set Lence aflame, yet each generation sees new banners rise and fall as foreign coin flows through our courts. In the high season we joust for hollow glory; in the low season we trade whispers and dagger‑kisses, for the Belworth is not merely a river but a wound that festers with the meddling of neighboring realms. I speak for a Vhasia torn asunder, where the crown and parlement gnaw at each other’s throats while outsiders pluck the choicest morsels. When I say a bridge will hold, I have counted each stone and noted the cracks where treason seeps. When I warn that a lord cannot be trusted, I have seen the seal cracked by a bribe stamped with a foreign sigil.

The Bravo of Silkstrand (no other name, no other need)

“Every bridge is a promise, each canal a secret well kept; but the truest secrets be those shared o’er a cup of wine, lest ye be beguiled. Hark! The tide doth whisper, and the night doth yield another crossing.”

I am nobody – or I was, once. The canals of Silkstrand do not care about your bloodline. They care about your stroke with an oar and your aim with a dagger. I started as a dock-rat, became a duelist, and now I am something else: a witness to the city that never sleeps. The Exchange closes at midnight, but the bargains never end. I have won purses and lost friends, and I still smile. Why? Because the sun rises over the laguna every morning, and there is always another bridge to cross. I speak for Silkstrand, for the Brass Coast, for the Sidhi freedmen who taught me that freedom is a story you tell yourself until it becomes true.

The True-Mason (Aeler, name forgotten by design)

“The mountain breathes. The stone remembers. I am the crack in both, and the hand that sets it right.”

I am a medicant Aeler, a dwarf of the deep halls, whose craft is not to hoard stone but to heal the works of others. When a foreign road sags or a bridge groans under neglect, I am sent—coin in purse, chisel in hand—to set true what the weak have let slip. Such repair is no mere charity; it is the quiet soft power of the Aeler, a testament that our mountain’s strength lies in the steadiness we lend to those who would otherwise falter. I know the load each lung can bear, the strain each beam can suffer. When a Tulkani chieftain swears an oath on his firstborn, that oath is a keystone I have already wedged into three different seals—his crack, my mark, and the mountain’s silent assent. When a Veiled Sister binds a demon, that terror is a fault line I have traced in the bedrock; her tremor is hers, the second tremor is the mountain’s warning. You see the surface as “full of problems”; I see the weight in the stone, the strain in the beam, and the chance to bind a fracture before it widens. Problems are not obstacles but the pressure that shapes the mountain—and the chance for us to extend our hand. I speak for the Aeler, for the Mistlands, for the deep vaults where the Hollow is not a myth but a flaw in the foundation. When I tell you a tunnel is sound, I have walked its stress lines and laid fresh stone where needed. When I tell you to flee, trust the stone’s memory, for it remembers both the weight we have borne and the weight we have mended.

The three of us have walked these roads. We have paid our tolls – in blood, in silver, in silence. Use the cards to find your own. When you are lost, ask: What does this place witness? And what does it ask in return?

Then listen. The road is patient. The Hollow is patient. We are not. But we have left the lamp lit for you.
