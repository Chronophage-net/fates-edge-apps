📖 How to Use This Document

This is a simulated, fully-annotated session of Fate's Edge — not a transcript of real play, but a realistic one, built to walk a new GM or player through how the rules actually feel at the table. Every mechanical beat in the fiction below is followed by a small annotation box that names the rule in play and points back to the guide that covers it.

Three annotation colors do three different jobs: GM Guide boxes explain a GM-facing procedure or judgment call, Player Guide boxes explain a player-facing procedure, and Rule boxes are quick reminders of the underlying mechanic. None of this is required reading before a real session — read the transcript for the story, and lean on the annotations only where you want the "why."

🎭 The Table: GM and Four Players

The party is investigating Castellan Voss's tower in Silkstrand, where a stolen Symbol — a minor relic tied to a terrestrial patron — is rumored to be locked in the steward's reliquary. This session picks up mid-campaign; everyone already knows their gear and their debts.

🛡️ Mateo — The Guardian

Body 4, Melee 3, Heavy Armor. Player: Priya
Priya is a tactical planner. She tracks her own Fatigue track and armor conversion table on a note card before session even starts, asks clarifying questions about range bands before she moves, and wants to know the DV before she commits to an approach. She is having fun optimizing — this is her fun.

🗡️ Lyra — The Rogue

Wits 3, Stealth 2, light blade. Player: Devon
Devon is a "rule of cool" improviser. He rarely asks what the DV is before describing something bold, spends Boons the moment he gets them, and treats a Miss as a plot twist to build on rather than a setback. He's the table's chaos engine — in a good way.

🎻 Sera — The Bard

Presence 3, Sway 2, first campaign. Player: Naomi
Naomi is new to tabletop RPGs and a little quiet at a table of louder personalities. She second-guesses her own ideas out loud ("is that even allowed?") and needs the GM to actively invite her into scenes rather than wait for her to jump in.

🐦‍⬛ Khor — The Summoner

Spirit 3, Presence 2, Pact-Whisperer. Player: Ollie
Ollie is an experienced player who wants the spotlight. He's not a bad table citizen, but he'll happily narrate three actions in a row if no one stops him, and he's the type to treat his Patron's Obligation as a resource to min-max rather than a story hook — until the GM makes the debt personal.

GM: Referred to below simply as GM. Running the table is Jess, a second-time GM who is deliberately using this scenario to practice spotlight balance across very different play styles — see the GM Takeaways section at the end for what she's doing on purpose.

Scene 1 — The Approach

Demonstrates: the 90-second play loop, Position & DV, dice reading, the Outcome Matrix, Boons
GM: The service alley behind Castellan Voss's tower is quiet — one lantern, one guard rotation, and a drainpipe running up to a shuttered window on the second floor. "What do you do?"
GM Guide — Core Loop, Step 1Setting the stage and asking "what do you do?" is the whole first step of the 90-Second Play Loop. The GM doesn't pre-decide the approach — she waits for the players to declare intent first.
Lyra (Devon): I'm already climbing the drainpipe before anyone finishes talking. Wits and Stealth, right? I want to be at that window before the guard turns back.
GM: Sure — declare it and I'll set the scene. Climbing an unguarded drainpipe in the dark, with a guard on rotation twenty feet away: this is Controlled Position, and I'll call it DV 3 — pressured, but nothing's actively opposing you yet.
Rule — Difficulty Value (DV)DV 3 is the explicit default in the Player's Guide: "pressured, mild opposition." The GM doesn't inflate it just because it's a heist — a DV should reflect real stakes, not vibes.
Lyra (Devon): Rolling Wits 3 + Stealth 2, five dice. 🎲 64183 Two Successes, one Story Beat.
Rule — Reading the DiceEach die showing 6 or higher is a Success; each 1 generates a Story Beat (SB) for the GM to spend. Two Successes against a DV of 3 is not enough to clear the bar outright.
GM: Two Successes against DV 3 — that's a Partial. You make it up the pipe and reach the window ledge, but you're not clean about it. And that 1 you rolled — I'm spending it now: as you get your fingers on the shutter, it groans, loud. The guard below pauses mid-step.
GM Guide — Outcome Matrix + Spending SB0 < Successes < DV is a Partial: progress, but the situation stays unresolved, and Lyra gains 1 Boon. The GM spends the 1 SB immediately rather than banking it — the guide's advice is "spend SB early: one strong complication is better than three weak ones."
Lyra (Devon): (grinning) Perfect, love it. I freeze on the ledge like a gargoyle. Mark 1 Boon.
Player Guide — BoonsPartial success grants 1 Boon (max 5 held, trimmed to 2 at scene end). Devon tracks this on his own sheet the moment it happens — no waiting for the GM to remind him, exactly what the Player Guide means by "you are the primary manager of your character's mechanical state."
GM: The guard listens for a three-count, shrugs, and keeps walking. You're in — but you used up your quiet option; nobody else is climbing that pipe without noise now. Mateo, Sera, Khor — you're still down in the alley. What's the plan?
Mateo (Priya): Before we commit — how far is the side door from our position, and is it locked or guarded?
GM: Near range, unguarded, definitely locked.
GM Guide — Answering Prep QuestionsPriya's habit of asking for concrete distances and states before committing is exactly the kind of table input the GM should just answer plainly — it costs nothing and lets a tactical player do what they enjoy.
Mateo (Priya): Okay. I'll stand watch on the alley mouth while someone deals with the door — Body 4, I can handle it if the guard comes back early.
Sera (Naomi): (quietly) I guess... I could try to pick the lock? I don't actually have Subterfuge though. Is that bad?
GM: It's not bad at all — you'd roll Wits + whatever's closest, probably at reduced dice, or you find another way in. What's Sera's read on that door — does she know locks, or is she working on instinct?
GM Guide — Inviting Quiet PlayersRather than let Naomi's hesitation pass by, the GM asks a leading question — the guide's advice is explicit: "Ask leading questions... gentle prompts help shy players find their moment." This isn't a rules mechanic, it's table facilitation, and it matters as much as any dice rule.
Sera (Naomi): Oh — okay. Um, instinct, I think? She grew up on the docks, she's picked a few crates open before. Not a real thief, but her hands know things.
GM: I like that a lot. Roll Wits + your general Subterfuge (0), so it's a small pool, but let's see what the docks taught her.
Sera (Naomi): Two dice, then. 🎲 25 ...zero Successes. That's bad, right?
GM: It's a Miss — but a Miss is never "nothing happens." Here's what I'm thinking: your pick snaps clean off in the lock. That's loud enough that the door across the alley creaks open — a second guard, off-duty, sticking his head out to see what the noise was. You gain 2 Boons. What do you do?
GM Guide — Fail ForwardA Miss always escalates rather than stalling the scene — here, a new NPC obstacle appears instead of the door just staying locked. Sera's Player Guide payout is explicit too: 2 Boons on a Miss, more than a Partial, because the story took a harder swing.
Sera (Naomi): Oh! Um — I stand up straight and just... talk to him? Like I'm supposed to be there. "Sorry, dropped my key, give me a second."
GM: I love that. That's an actual new roll — Presence + Sway, Controlled, DV 3, since he's suspicious but not alarmed yet.
Sera (Naomi): Five dice! 🎲 97432 Two Successes, no SB.
GM: Clean Success. He grunts, says something about the night watch getting lazy, and shuts the door. You're back to working the lock — and this time you've got 2 Boons banked. Want to spend one to improve Position before you try again?
Sera (Naomi): Yes! Actually yes, let's do that.
Player Guide — Spending Boons, "The Math of Fun"The GM offers the option rather than assuming Naomi knows it's available — a small but real teaching moment. The guide is blunt about this design intent: "Hoarding Boons for XP conversion leads to less engagement and lower fun scores... spend Boons freely, they are not XP tokens." Naomi taking the offer, out loud, for the first time this session, is exactly the payoff that advice is aiming for.

Scene 2 — Over the Wall

Demonstrates: Selling the Action, Position modifiers, Harm & Fatigue, armor conversion (player-calculated), the "roller-coaster" effect
GM: The lock gives. Inside, a narrow servant's stair — and at the top, one of Voss's house guard, alerted by all that noise at the door, coming down fast. Mateo, you're closest.
Mateo (Priya): I plant myself at the base of the stairs, shield up. He's got the height, so that's probably Controlled for me, or worse?
GM: Good instinct — Controlled, since the stairwell's narrow enough that his height advantage doesn't fully land.
Mateo (Priya): Before I roll — can I say something? Mateo doesn't fight for coin. His little brother is still indentured to a house like this one. Every guard he puts down is one step closer to getting him out. I set my feet at the bottom of those stairs like it's the only ground in the world worth holding.
GM: That's a stake, not just flavor — I'm improving your Position by one step. You're Dominant for this roll.
GM Guide — Selling the ActionThis is the character-driven description tier: a stated stake and a bond tied to the roll, not just adjectives. The guide's rule of thumb: "One heartfelt sentence counts more than a paragraph of adjectives" — and the payout is a full Position step, same as a tactical description would earn. Naomi watching this happen is part of why the GM chose to demonstrate it on Mateo's turn instead of quietly explaining it out of character.
Mateo (Priya): Body 4 + Melee 3, seven dice, Dominant so I re-roll a failure. 🎲 67352110 Re-rolling one of the blanks... 🎲 → 8 Successes: the 6, 7, the 10 counts as two, and the re-rolled 8. That's five, plus one SB.
Rule — Position & Critical 10sDominant lets you re-roll one failed die. A 10 always counts as two Successes and is never re-rolled by any effect, Position included — Priya already knew this and didn't try.
GM: Five Successes against DV 3 with a 10 in the mix — Clean Success, and I'm using the critical benefit. Your shield takes his first strike and turns his own momentum against him: he stumbles down the last two steps, off-balance, and lands hard in front of you. He's on the ground, Desperate for his own follow-up.
Mateo (Priya): I'll take that. What about the 1 I rolled?
GM: I'm banking it — one SB isn't worth a complication on a roll this clean. I'll spend it later.
GM Guide — Choosing Not to SpendThe GM isn't obligated to spend every SB the instant it's rolled — sometimes the better call is to save a single SB for a moment that needs it more.
GM: A second guard comes around the landing above and puts a crossbow bolt into you before you can react. Mateo — you take Harm 2.
GM Guide — Stating HarmThe GM states the raw Harm value and stops there. She does not calculate the armor conversion for Mateo — that's explicitly the player's job in Fate's Edge, freeing the GM to keep narrating instead of doing math for four characters.
Mateo (Priya): I'm in Heavy Armor. Let me check the table... Harm 2 in Heavy converts fully: Harm 0 remaining, Fatigue 1 gained.
Player Guide — Armor Conversion (Player-Calculated)Priya consults the Armor Conversion Table herself: Heavy Armor has Step 3, meaning Harm up to 3 converts fully to Fatigue. Harm 2 ≤ Step 3, so it all becomes Fatigue, at "Step − 1" efficiency (2 Fatigue per full step) — here that nets exactly 1 Fatigue, matching the table's Harm-2-row for Heavy. She reports the result back to the table rather than waiting for the GM to tell her.
Mateo (Priya): No Harm to apply, so Fatigue doesn't clear — I just add 1 Fatigue. That puts me at Fatigue 1 of 4. The bolt catches my pauldron and the shock runs down my arm, but the plate holds.
Rule — Apply OrderThe procedure is always Harm first (clears Fatigue), then add Fatigue. Here there's no Harm to apply, so nothing clears — Priya still narrates the moment rather than treating it as pure bookkeeping.
GM: Nice. The downed guard below you is scrambling for his sword — one more exchange before he's out of it.
Mateo (Priya): I finish him. Same roll, Controlled since he's on the ground and I'm not — Body 4 + Melee 3.
Mateo (Priya): 🎲 4365121 One Success, two SB. That's a Partial against DV 3.
GM: Partial — you drive him back but he's not down, and he gets one desperate cut in on his way past your guard. Take Harm 1. I'm also spending one of those SB: he shouts for the alarm bell as he breaks off.
GM Guide — SB Spend TiersOne SB for a shout that raises the alarm is a textbook 1-SB minor complication per the guide's spend table. The GM banks the second SB rather than stacking two complications on one Partial — restraint keeps a single roll from snowballing into an unfair pile-on.
Mateo (Priya): Harm 1 in Heavy — full conversion again, Fatigue 1 gained. I'm at Fatigue 2 of 4 now. Wait — this is where it gets interesting: I also earned a Boon from that Partial.
GM: Right, mark it. And the alarm bell means the whole tower's about to know you're here. Lyra, you're already inside — what do you see from that window?

Zooming out on Fatigue: if Mateo takes one more solid hit before he gets a chance to rest, his Fatigue track (equal to his Body, 4) will fill. When it does, the Player Guide's rule is automatic and immediate: increase Harm by 1 step and clear all Fatigue — the "roller-coaster effect," where a wound that should feel like the last straw instead clears his head. Priya already knows this is coming and isn't worried about it; she's using Fatigue as a resource to spend, not hoarding it out of fear.

Scene 3 — The Rite at the Threshold

Demonstrates: Obligation Capacity and overflow, summoning, the Leash, Boon Finesse, and redirecting a spotlight-heavy player
Khor (Ollie): While they're dealing with the alarm, I'm not waiting around. I call my patron's Rite to ward the stairwell door shut behind us, and then I'm summoning the wisp to scout the upper floor, and then I want to try reading the wards on the reliquary door myself before anyone else gets there —
GM: Let's take those one at a time — actually, let's take just the first one right now, because it's going to cost you something interesting. Invoking that Rite: what's your current Obligation?
GM Guide — Spotlight ManagementRather than let Ollie chain three declared actions with no pushback, the GM slows the pace down to one action, resolved fully, before the next. This isn't a punishment — it's the same "pass the spotlight" discipline the Core Loop calls for, applied gently so Ollie doesn't just get a full scene to himself while three other players wait.
Khor (Ollie): I'm at 3 segments already — one on my Patron, one from a Symbol I cracked last session, one more from a favor I haven't paid back yet. My capacity is Spirit 3 + Presence 2 = 5. This Rite is +1, so I'll be at 4. Still fine.
Player Guide — Obligation CapacityCapacity = Spirit + Presence. Ollie tracks his own Obligation segments across every Patron and Symbol he owes, exactly as the guide expects: "Declare thresholds... keep it visible." He's not hiding from the math — he's using it, which is part of why the GM is about to lean on it narratively instead of mechanically.
GM: Mark it. The ward goes up clean — the door won't budge from the other side. But here's the thing: this Rite belongs to the same Patron as the favor you haven't paid back. As the ward settles, you feel it — not a punishment, just... attention. She's watching how you spend what you owe her.
GM Guide — Obligation as Story EngineThe guide is explicit that Obligation is "not a punishment, it's a promise of future story," and that the GM's job is to frame Patron intrusions as story hooks. Jess isn't triggering a mechanical penalty here (Khor is still under capacity) — she's making the debt feel personal and present, which is the real payoff of the system for a player who might otherwise just treat it as a number.
Khor (Ollie): ...huh. Okay, that actually lands. Fine — the wisp next. I want eyes on the upper floor before we move.
GM: Summon it — one action, one Boon or Fatigue to Bind it. Its Leash is your Cap plus its own segments; let's say Leash 4 for a minor spirit like this.
Khor (Ollie): I'll pay the Boon rather than Fatigue, I want to stay sharp for whatever's up there. It slips out through the gap under the door — small, quick, curious little thing made of dust and lamplight.
GM: It comes back a moment later — there are two more guards up there, and one of them is already moving toward the stairs. As it slips back under the door to report, it brushes against the iron doorframe. Tick the Leash.
GM Guide — Ticking the LeashThe guide lists exactly this trigger: "tick the Leash whenever the spirit... crosses a [WARD]" — here, an iron threshold counts. The GM watches for these triggers actively; it's her job to notice, not the player's job to ask for it.
Khor (Ollie): That's Leash 1 of 4. I don't want it slipping loose yet — I'll spend a Boon to clear that tick. Boon Finesse, once per round, right?
GM: Right, that works — clear it. But you're down to your last Boon now.
Player Guide — Boon FinesseOnce per round, spend 1 Boon to clear 1 Leash tick before it fills. Ollie is treating his Boon economy as a real tradeoff rather than an infinite resource — spending his last one to keep the spirit calm rather than saving it "just in case," which is exactly the spend-don't-hoard instinct the Boon economy is designed to reward.

Scene 4 — The Steward's Study

Demonstrates: Setup and Assist (teamwork actions), Critical 10 benefits, and the Deck of Consequences for on-the-fly answers
GM: You reach the steward's study before the guards catch up — locked writing desk, and behind it, a portrait that's just slightly off-center on the wall. Someone wants you to think there's a safe behind it. Who's on the desk?
Lyra (Devon): Me, obviously. Wits + Subterfuge, and I want it open before the guards are on us.
Sera (Naomi): Can I help somehow? I don't have any lockpicking, but... I could watch the door and warn her the second I hear boots on the stairs, so she's not rushing blind?
GM: That's a great use of Setup — roll Wits + Insight, or honestly just Presence if you're listening for footsteps rather than reading anything. Success gives Lyra +1 Position on this roll.
Player Guide — Setup (Teamwork)Setup lets a non-specialist contribute meaningfully with their own roll rather than just describing help for free — the GM Guide's advice on player creativity is almost word for word here: "Let non-specialists contribute meaningfully... you can't pick the lock, but you can hold the torch steady." Naomi gets a real mechanical stake in the scene without needing a lockpicking skill of her own.
Sera (Naomi): Presence 3, two dice since I don't really have the skill. 🎲 84 One Success — that clears DV 2 for just listening at a door, right?
GM: Right, that's a clean pass. You catch the rhythm of the patrol below — you'll have real warning. Lyra, you're Dominant on this roll now.
Lyra (Devon): Six dice, Dominant so I re-roll a failure. 🎲 1074315 re-rolling one blank... 🎲 → 9 that's the 10 (two), the 7, the 9 — five Successes, one SB, DV was 3.
Rule — Critical 10sOne 10 in a successful roll grants a benefit from the critical table. A single 10 is a Strong Success: improve Position by one step on the next action this scene, or a minor advantage at GM discretion.
GM: Clean success with a critical on top. The desk clicks open — and because of that 10, I'll give you the minor advantage: tucked under the false bottom is a second item you weren't looking for. A sealed letter, Voss's own hand, addressed to someone outside the city.
Lyra (Devon): Oh, I'm reading that immediately. What does it say?
GM: Let's find out together. (draws from the Deck of Consequences) King of Hearts.
GM Guide — Deck of Consequences (Plot Generation)The GM has nothing prepped for "what's in the letter," so she reaches for the deck rather than stalling. Hearts mean the answer is about a relationship, loyalty, or emotion. A face card (King) signals major severity. Per the interpretation procedure: state the literal card ("King of Hearts = Betrayal"), then ask who is betraying whom.
GM: King of Hearts is Betrayal. So — who's betraying whom? I think Voss isn't loyal to the Cantor faction everyone assumes he serves. This letter is addressed to a rival house, offering to hand over the Symbol you're all here to steal, in exchange for asylum. He was planning to run before you ever showed up.
GM Guide — Linking the Draw to a TimerThe procedure's third step is linking the answer to an existing timer or stake. Jess ties this straight back to the session's core tension — the Symbol's location and ownership — rather than treating the draw as a disconnected fun fact.
Mateo (Priya): So the Symbol might not even be where we think it is.
GM: That's a very reasonable worry to have right now.

Scene 5 — The Reliquary

Demonstrates: Sidestep Strike, opportunity attacks, worsening Position, Fatigue overflow (the "roller-coaster" effect), and a Miss that changes the story instead of stopping it
GM: The reliquary door is open — someone got here first. Two of Voss's remaining guards are already inside, and they've heard you coming. This is a fight. Roll for what you're doing.
Mateo (Priya): I'm at Fatigue 2 already. If I take one more solid hit, I roll into Harm — I know that, and I'm fine with it. I move to Close range and attack the nearer guard.
GM: Controlled, DV 3, same as before.
Mateo (Priya): 🎲 3542415 ...zero Successes. Miss, and one SB for you.
GM: A clean Miss isn't "nothing happens" — his counter lands hard while your swing goes wide. Take Harm 3.
GM Guide — Fail Forward in CombatEven a whiffed attack roll produces a consequence rather than just "you miss, next turn" — here, that consequence is the enemy's counterattack landing.
Mateo (Priya): Heavy Armor, Harm 3 — that's exactly my Step, so full conversion: Harm 0 remaining, Fatigue 2 gained. I add that to my current Fatigue 2... that's Fatigue 4. My track is Body 4. That's overflow.
Player Guide — Fatigue OverflowOverflow triggers when Fatigue reaches or exceeds Body, not only when it exceeds it — Priya catches this correctly (Fatigue 4 = Body 4 counts). The rule: increase Harm by 1 step, then clear all Fatigue.
Mateo (Priya): So: Harm goes up by 1 — I've taken enough scattered hits tonight that this pushes me to Harm 1 overall — and my Fatigue clears completely, back to 0. Which is honestly great timing.
GM: Tell me what that looks like.
Mateo (Priya): The hit that should have dropped me instead snaps something loose — the ache in my shoulder just... goes quiet. Everything gets very clear. I'm not tired anymore. I'm just angry now.
Rule — The Roller-Coaster EffectThis is the exact dynamic the Player Guide names: "a character near collapse who takes a wound and suddenly finds a second wind." Mechanically Mateo is now at Harm 1 (−1 die on related actions) but Fatigue 0 — objectively worse off in the long run, but narratively primed for a big moment right now. Priya narrating the shift is what makes the swing land at the table, not just on the sheet.
Lyra (Devon): While they're focused on him, I want to close in on the second guard from Near range. Sidestep Strike into Close — I know that worsens my Position, but my knife wants Close range for the bonus.
Player Guide — Sidestep StrikeMoving one range band as part of a melee attack, without spending your Move action, always worsens Position by one step — Devon takes the tradeoff deliberately because the light blade's Close-range bonus (+2 dice) outweighs the cost for him.
GM: Controlled becomes Desperate, then. Roll it.
Lyra (Devon): Wits 3 + Stealth... no wait, this is Melee, I don't have that trained. Let me use Body instead as an ambush strike — Body 2 + Unarmed 1, three dice, plus the +2d Close bonus from the blade, five dice, Desperate so I re-roll a success if I get one.
Lyra (Devon): 🎲 67421 two Successes before the re-roll — Desperate re-rolls one success, so I have to re-roll the 6... 🎲 → 3 that drops to one Success, one SB.
Rule — Desperate Re-rolls a SuccessWhere Dominant lets you re-roll a failure, Desperate forces a re-roll of one success — it can genuinely make things worse, which is why the guide frames Desperate as "you act under duress," not just "harder."
GM: One Success against DV 3 — Partial. Your knife catches him but doesn't drop him, and because you left Near range to close in, he gets a free swing as you pass — that's an opportunity attack, Controlled Position for him, no action cost.
GM Guide — Opportunity AttacksThe GM correctly applies the rule: closing to Close via Sidestep Strike still triggers an opportunity attack unless the attacker also used Disengage, which Devon didn't. This is a real cost, not a gotcha — it's the same tradeoff the Player Guide warns about openly.
Lyra (Devon): Worth it. Mark my Boon from the Partial. Even if he hits me, that's a great scene either way.
Player Guide — "Fail Forward, Fun Either Way"This is Devon's whole approach to the game distilled into one line: he isn't trying to avoid bad outcomes, he's trying to generate interesting ones. The guide explicitly validates this instinct: "Don't fear Story Beats — they are fuel for drama, not punishment."
Khor (Ollie): My wisp's still out — I send it to distract the second guard, get his eyes off Lyra. Quick command, doesn't cost me my own action.
Player Guide — Quick CommandsSimple orders like "attack nearest, hold, distract" don't cost the summoner's action — only complex commands do. Ollie uses this correctly to help a teammate without giving up his own turn.
GM: It darts across his vision, and he flinches — but doing that out here, away from the warded stair, means it's exposed. Tick the Leash again. That's Leash 3 of 4 now, and you're out of Boons to clear it.
Khor (Ollie): I don't have a Boon, but I do have Fatigue to spare — can I substitute?
GM: No — Boon Finesse is specifically a Boon spend, that substitution rule is for other Boon-cost effects, not this one. You're at Leash 3. One more tick and it acts on its own.
GM Guide — Knowing the BoundariesThe GM catches an easy mistake before it becomes a house rule by accident: the Fatigue-for-Boon substitution applies broadly, but Boon Finesse for Leash-clearing is its own specific mechanic. A good GM knows where a general rule stops and a specific one takes over.
Mateo (Priya): I finish my guard — same roll as before, Controlled, and this time I'm not holding back.
Mateo (Priya): 🎲 769435 Three Successes, no SB. Clean Success.
GM: He goes down. That's one guard left, and he's rattled — Lyra's Partial already cut him, your wisp's still buzzing around his head, Khor. What's the last move?
Sera (Naomi): Can I try something? I've been listening at the door this whole scene — I step in and just... tell him it's over. Not a threat. Just true. "Voss already ran. There's nothing left here worth dying for."
GM: That's a full scene turn, not a combat action — and it's exactly the kind of thing that deserves a real roll. Presence + Sway, Controlled, DV 3. If you land it, he stands down instead of this becoming one more body on the floor.
GM Guide — Golden RuleRather than force a mechanically "correct" combat resolution, the GM leans on the guide's Golden Rule: "make the choice that serves the story." Ending the fight through Sera's words instead of one more attack roll is more interesting than grinding out the last guard's Harm track.
Sera (Naomi): Five dice. 🎲 610413 the 10 is two Successes, plus the 6 — three Successes, one SB. Against DV 3, that's Clean Success with a critical.
GM: He lowers his sword. Just like that, it's over. The critical benefit is yours to choose — improve Position, a minor advantage, or something else entirely?
Sera (Naomi): Can he... tell us where Voss actually went? Like, an actual advantage, not just combat stuff?
GM: Absolutely — that's exactly the kind of minor advantage a 10 is for. He tells you Voss left an hour ago, headed for the river gate, and he's not alone.
Player Guide — Critical 10 Benefits Aren't Just CombatNaomi asking whether a critical benefit can be narrative information rather than a mechanical bonus is exactly the right instinct — the guide's critical table explicitly allows "a minor advantage (GM's discretion)," and information is one of the most valuable advantages a scene can offer.

End of Session, Mid-Scene

The table calls it here — Voss is fleeing toward the river gate, the letter raises a bigger question than the heist answered, and everyone's mechanical state carries cleanly into next week. That cliffhanger is deliberate: the GM Guide's advice on scene pacing favors ending on a hook, not a resolution, whenever the group's energy is still high.

Rule — Boon CarryoverAt the end of each scene, Boons above 2 are lost. Lyra and Sera both banked extra Boons during the session and now trim down — a small, real cost for not spending them mid-scene, exactly the incentive structure the "Math of Fun" advice is built around.

🎲 Player Takeaways — Four Ways to Play

If you're a planner like Priya

The system rewards knowing your own numbers cold — your armor conversion, your Fatigue track, your Position ladder. Ask the GM for concrete facts before you commit; that's not slowing the table down, it's using the tools you're given. Just watch the clock: the 90-second loop assumes you'll act once you have the facts, not deliberate indefinitely.

If you're an improviser like Devon

You're already playing the game the way Story Beats and the Boon economy are built for — a Miss is a gift to the story, not a loss. Keep spending Boons; the game is explicit that hoarding them for XP is the weaker play, mechanically and dramatically. Just be aware that a bold physical move (like Sidestep Strike) has a real cost attached, not just a cool factor — read the fine print once, then enjoy paying it.

If you're new and quiet like Naomi

You don't need a trained skill to matter in a scene — Setup and Assist exist specifically so anyone can contribute with whatever they've got. If you're not sure an idea is "allowed," say it anyway; the worst case is a small dice pool, not a no. And when the GM asks you a leading question, that's not a test — it's an invitation.

If you're an experienced spotlight-seeker like Ollie

The system has plenty of room for ambition — Patrons, Symbols, and summoned spirits reward a player willing to juggle multiple debts and risks. But Obligation and the Leash aren't just resource management; they're the GM's built-in hooks into your character specifically. The more you let those debts become personal instead of purely tactical, the more the table gets to see your character's actual story, not just your build.

📋 Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet

Everything demonstrated above, in one place. Full detail lives in the Player's Guide (Core Mechanics) and GM Guide (Core Procedures, Managing Resources, Running Scenarios).

SystemRule
Dice PoolAttribute + Skill, roll d10s. 6+ = Success, 1 = Story Beat (SB).
Difficulty Value (DV)2 routine · 3 default (pressured) · 4 difficult · 5 extreme · 6+ mythic.
Outcome MatrixClean Success (S≥DV) · Success w/ SB (S≥DV, SB>0) · Partial (0<S<DV, +1 Boon) · Miss (S=0, +2 Boons, always escalates).
Critical 10sEach 10 = 2 Successes. On a successful roll, 1 ten = strong benefit; more tens = bigger benefit. Never re-rolled.
PositionDominant (re-roll a failure) · Controlled (no re-roll) · Desperate (re-roll a success). 1 Boon improves one step before rolling; 2 SB worsens one step after.
BoonsMax 5 held, trimmed to 2 at scene end. Spend to re-roll, activate an Asset, improve Position, or convert 2→1 XP (max 2 XP/session, downtime only).
Story Beats (SB)1 = minor complication · 2 = moderate setback · 3 = serious trouble · 4+ = major turn. Spend early rather than stacking.
Selling the ActionTactical description → improve Position one step. Character-driven stakes/bonds/fears → improve Position and bank 1 SB (or upgrade Effect).
FatigueTrack = Body. Each point worsens Position (or −1 die if already Desperate). Fills → +1 Harm, clear all Fatigue (overflow).
Harm & Armor ConversionGM states raw Harm; player converts via their armor's table, then applies Harm first (clears Fatigue) before adding Fatigue. Minimum 1 Fatigue per hit.
Sidestep StrikeMove one range band as part of a melee attack (no separate Move used); worsens Position by one step; may provoke an opportunity attack.
Opportunity AttacksLeaving an enemy's Close range without Disengaging provokes one free Controlled attack.
Setup / AssistSetup: a roll that grants an ally +1 Position or better Effect. Assist: spend 1 Boon for +1 die to an ally's roll (max +3 dice from all sources).
ObligationCapacity = Spirit + Presence. Exceeding it: +1 Fatigue per segment over. Double capacity: clear Fatigue, +1 Harm, trigger a Patron Intrusion.
Summoning & the LeashLeash = summoner's Cap + spirit's segments. Ticks on Harm, acting against nature, split focus, or crossing a ward. 1 Boon (Boon Finesse, once/round) clears 1 tick. Full Leash → spirit acts once on its own, then departs or turns hostile.
Deck of ConsequencesDraw for answers you haven't prepped. Suit = nature (Hearts: relationship, Spades: harm/obstacle, Clubs: cost/debt, Diamonds: secret/opportunity). Rank = severity. Link the answer to an existing stake or timer.

None of this is a rules dump to memorize before session one. The GM Guide's own advice to new GMs applies here too: you only really need the Core Loop, DV 3/Controlled as your defaults, the Outcome Matrix, and "spend SB early." Everything else in this cheat sheet is what you'll pick up naturally within your first two or three sessions, exactly the way this table did.
