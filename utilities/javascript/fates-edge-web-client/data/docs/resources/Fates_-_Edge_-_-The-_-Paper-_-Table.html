🎡 The Paper Table

Resource Printable No dice required Accessibility Author: Nicholas A. Gasper

Where dice are dangerous.
What this is.

Everything in this document prints on ordinary paper and replaces a set of dice. There is a spinner you can cut out, three methods that need no spinner at all, a lookup table that resolves a whole dice pool in two spins, and paper trackers for clocks, Boons, Story Beats, Harm and Fatigue. Print it in black and white; nothing here depends on colour.

Fate's Edge is played with ten-sided dice, and dice are not always available. Some tables have lost them, or travel light, or are playing on a bus. Some are playing where dice often aren't allowed. And some people simply read pips faster than they read small engraved numerals. The game does not actually require the objects — it requires a fair way to get a number from 1 to 10, and there are many.

1. The Wheel

Cut it out, or just lay the page flat and spin on it. Each sector is one tenth of the circle, so every number is equally likely — this is a d10, drawn instead of moulded.

Three ways to spin it

With a paperclip. Open one end, hold it at the centre with a pen or pencil tip through the loop, and flick. Read the sector the long end stops in.

With a pencil alone. Stand the pencil on its point at the centre dot, pinch the top, and spin it like a top. Read the sector it falls toward.

By dropping. Hold a pencil about a foot above the page, eyes on the centre, and drop it. Read where the point lands; drop again if it misses the circle. Slower, and the fairest of the three if your spinner is warped.

Is it actually fair? A spinner is fair if the sectors are equal and the spin is long enough to lose its starting position — two or three full turns is plenty. Sectors here are exactly 36°. The usual way a paper spinner goes wrong is a bent clip or a soft centre hole that drags; if one number starts coming up more than its share, re-cut the centre or switch to dropping.

2. If you have no spinner at all

All three of these produce a fair number from 1 to 10 using things you already have. None need a printed page.

The book.

Let a thick book fall open and read the last digit of the left-hand page number. 0 counts as 10. Any book over about two hundred pages works, and page numbers are uniform in their last digit regardless of where the spine likes to open — which is why this method is more robust than it looks.

Two digits, for the table in section 4: read the last digit of the left page, then the last digit of the right page.

The dropped pencil.

Number ten short lines across a page, or use the printed wheel. Drop a pencil from a hand's height and read the nearest number. Re-drop if it lands off the page.

The clock.

If you can see a second hand, glance away, then look: the seconds' last digit is your number, 0 counting as 10. Fair, instant, and needs nothing at all — but only once every few seconds, so it suits one-off rolls rather than a full pool.

What doesn't work.

Picking a number "at random" in your head, or stabbing a numbered grid with your eyes shut. People are reliably biased at both — middling numbers come up far too often and edges almost never. If it feels random, it probably isn't; use one of the three above.

3. Rolling a pool

A Fate's Edge roll is Attribute + Skill dice, so most rolls are four to seven spins. Spin that many times and tally as you go — a stroke for each success, a dot for each 1:

SpinCounts as
1No success, and 1 Story Beat to the GM
2–5Nothing
6–91 success
102 successes

Position works normally. Dominant: re-spin one failure (a 1–5), your choice. Desperate: re-spin one success (a 6–9); a 10 is never re-spun. A re-spun 1 keeps its Story Beat — mark the dot before you re-spin, and mark another if the new spin is also a 1.

4. The fast table — two spins for any pool

Spinning eight times gets old. This resolves an entire pool in two spins: the first is the tens digit, the second the units, giving a number from 01 to 00 (that is, 100). Read your pool's column and take the successes and Story Beats straight off it.

What this is, exactly. Each column is the true probability distribution of that pool, computed by enumerating every possible roll, then divided into a hundred slots. It is not an approximation of the game — it is the same maths the dice do, sampled in one step instead of eight. Slot allocation rounds each outcome to the nearest percent, so any single line is at most about one percentage point off, which is far below what anyone notices at a table.

Slot 00 is not a rounding bin. The very best and very worst results are rarer than one percent and would round away to nothing, so instead of quietly deleting them the table sends you to 00: spin the long way — one spin per die, per section 3. It comes up about one roll in a hundred and it is the only way the spectacular results stay reachable.

These columns are for Controlled Position. For Dominant or Desperate, spin the pool the long way and apply the re-spin; Position turns on a single die, and a table that has already added them up cannot tell you which one to change.

5. Paper trackers

Cut these out, or copy them by hand — they are only boxes. Fill a box with a stroke; erase or cross out to clear. Clocks come in four legal sizes and no others.

Clock [4] — The shortest legal clock.
Clock [6] — The workhorse.
Clock [8] — Long pressure.
Clock [10] — A campaign can end inside one.
Boons — max 5; trim to 2 at scene end.
Story Beats — the GM's pool.
Harm — 1, 2, 3. Three levels only.
Fatigue — track size equals Body.

6. If you have six-siders but not ten

A board-game box usually has d6s in it. The People's Die is a complete d6 conversion of the game — and if you have no d6s either, here is the wheel for it. Six sectors, same three spinning methods.

7. Printing notes

  • Print at 100% — "fit to page" will shrink the wheel and skew the sectors slightly.
  • Black and white is fine. Nothing in this document carries meaning in colour alone.
  • Card or a glued-on cereal box back makes a wheel that lasts; plain paper works for a session.
  • The wheel is about 3.5 inches across. Two fit side by side on a sheet if you want a spare.

A note for whoever is running this.

None of the above changes a single rule. The dice pool, the Outcome Matrix, Position, Boons and Story Beats are all exactly as written in the core book — a spinner is just a d10 that lies flat, and the table in section 4 is the same distribution the dice produce. A character built at a paper table is playable at any other table, and the reverse. That is the whole point: the rules were never the part that needed the equipment.
