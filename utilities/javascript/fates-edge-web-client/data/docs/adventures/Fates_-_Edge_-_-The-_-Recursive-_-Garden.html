🌀 The Recursive Garden

Tier II-III 3 Sessions Fae Logic Horror Recursive Paradox Author: Nicholas A. Gasper

In Aelinnel, the fae realm where stone spires pierce moonlit groves and mathematical precision once governed every leaf and season, the PCs arrive as scholars, cartomancers, or logic-seekers chasing rumors of a garden that proves its own beauty. What they find is a wound: paths that lead back into themselves, a Garden Keeper whose optimizations have curdled into contradiction, and a hermit mathematician feeding the garden's heart equations it was never meant to solve. To leave whole, the PCs must out-think a place that thinks back.

"The correct door is the one that leads to the door that leads to..." — inscription on the Proof-Gate, Aelinnel

Reading an encounter badge. Each badge names a DV and a Position. It does not name a dice pool, and that is deliberate: the pool is Attribute + Skill, chosen from how the player says they are going about it. Two characters can attempt the same encounter with different pools and both be right. Where an approach is obviously the intended one, the badge says so; where it does not, ask what they are doing and build the pool from the answer.

🌙 Act I — The Garden Gate

The PCs enter Aelinnel through a proof-gate that only opens for those who can hold a thought and its opposite at once. The garden greets them with wonder before it starts asking uncomfortable questions. By act's end, the party should suspect the Garden Keeper is not merely a victim of the anomaly but complicit in it.

🚪 The Proof-Gate
A trellised archway of white stone, inscribed with a theorem that rewrites itself as it is read. To pass, each PC must resolve a small recursive riddle carved into the arch. Beyond, moonlit hedges hum faintly, as if counting.
Timer: Dread [10] Timer: Garden Integrity [10]
Puzzle: The Self-Referential Arch (DV 2, Controlled)
CleanRecognize the riddle has no terminus by design; accept the paradox rather than solving it. No timers advance.
PartialForce a near-answer through; the arch lets them by. Tick Dread +1 (prevent with 1 Boon).
MissTrapped rereading the arch for a full exchange. Gain 2 Boons; tick Garden Integrity +1.
First Sight of the Hedges (DV 2, Dominant)
CleanNotice the fractal, self-similar topiary — a clue the garden's logic is recursive, not linear.
PartialSense something is wrong but can't articulate what.
MissAdmire the hedges and miss the pattern; GM withholds the fractal clue for now.
🌾 The Path-Weaver's Warning
A minor fae woven from garden gravel intercepts the party. Bound by an old geas, she cannot lie but also cannot speak plainly — every truth she offers must be riddled.
Timer: Dread [10] Timer: Garden Integrity [10]
Reading the Path-Weaver (DV 2, Controlled)
CleanShe offers a true, useful clue: the loops began in the Sequenceweaver Grove, near the Keeper's newest topiary work.
PartialThey learn something is wrong with the Grove, but not why.
MissShe withdraws into the gravel, offering nothing further this scene.
🌳 The Sequenceweaver Grove
Trees grown in a strict Fibonacci spiral. One, near the center, has begun growing two different-sized rings for the same year — a mathematical contradiction made wood. Prime Wardens patrol the rows.
Timer: Garden Integrity [10] Timer: Dread [10]
Diagnosing the Contradictory Tree (DV 3, Controlled)
CleanThe tree's growth rings were seeded by two incompatible instructions — a strong clue the anomaly has two independent sources.
PartialConfirm something is deliberately wrong, but not that there are two sources.
MissTouching the tree spreads the contradiction. Tick Garden Integrity +2.
Prime Warden ×1 (DV 3, Controlled)
CleanNegotiate or prove your way past; it accepts a factor (name, favor, promise) and lets you through.
PartialIt lets you by, but demands a genuine future favor — mark a debt owed to Aelinnel's fae order.
MissIt strikes; combat begins, its Indivisible Stance resisting easy maneuvers.
🍃 The Garden Keeper's Invitation
The Garden Keeper greets the party with genuine warmth, framing the crisis as "a few small inconsistencies." They cannot perceive contradictions that flatter their own optimization work.
Timer: Garden Integrity [10] Timer: Dread [10]
Interviewing the Keeper (DV 3, Controlled)
CleanFlattered by real curiosity, the Keeper admits to "refining" the garden's recursive growth — right when the loops began. A real lead, unwittingly offered.
PartialThey deflect toward the Equation Hermit ("that dreadful man in the tower") — true, but convenient self-exculpation.
MissDefensive, they "demonstrate" garden logic on the spot and create a new small paradox. Tick Garden Integrity +1 and Dread +1 (prevent with 1 Boon each).

📖 Act II — The Deepening Paradox

The party investigates both suspects — the Keeper's optimizations and the Hermit's unsolved equations — through the garden's two great archives of proof. Both leads are true; the anomaly is a feedback loop between an obsessive optimizer and an obsessive solver.

📚 The Library of Proven Truths
Shelves of books whose page numbers renumber themselves by reading order; proofs true until observed twice. The party seeks records of when the garden's recursive patterns were first formalized.
Timer: Proof Collapse [8] Timer: Dread [10]
Cross-Referencing the Archive (DV 3, Controlled)
CleanFind the original commission: the Keeper requested "perfect self-similar growth optimization" eighteen moons ago — as the Hermit's tower began drawing unusual amounts of ambient logic.
PartialFind the commission, but its date has renumbered itself twice, muddying cause and effect.
MissTrust an outdated "established" proof and act on bad information. Tick Proof Collapse +2 as the book rewrites itself.
🗼 The Equation Hermit's Tower
A spiral tower where stairs ascend and descend at once, interiors larger than the exterior. The Hermit has been feeding the garden's core every unsolved problem he cannot crack himself.
Timer: Recursive Depth [6] Timer: Dread [10]
Navigating the Impossible Stairwell (DV 3, Desperate)
CleanAccept the impossibility rather than fight it; reach the Hermit's study without incident.
PartialDisoriented, a climbed floor insists it was always the ground floor. Tick Recursive Depth +1.
MissBriefly lost in a dimensional fold. Gain 2 Boons, tick Recursive Depth +2, lose the scene's first action.
Confronting the Hermit (DV 3, Controlled)
CleanStarved for a listener, he confesses everything — he's been feeding unsolved equations into the garden's core for months.
PartialHe confesses but insists his work is nearly done and begs the party not to interfere.
MissHe panics and feeds one more equation into a fragment of the core in front of you. Tick Recursive Depth +2 and Proof Collapse +1.
🔢 Numerical Uprising
Negative numbers spread pessimism through the hedges; an irrational number rambles endlessly at a crossroads; Zero sits mute and existentially adrift in the path, refusing to move.
Timer: Proof Collapse [8] Timer: Garden Integrity [10]
Reasoning with Zero (DV 3, Controlled)
CleanTalk Zero through its crisis with genuine kindness; it rolls off the path, grateful and resolved.
PartialIt moves, but extracts a promise someone will "come back and give it a purpose" — mark a small owed favor.
MissIts crisis deepens and spreads. Tick Proof Collapse +1.

🌌 Act III — The Heart of Recursion

The party descends through increasingly illogical space to the Garden's Core, confronts the corrupted Garden Keeper, and chooses what to do with an anomaly that is equal parts wound and revelation. The horror was never the paradox itself — it was the refusal to let a problem stay unsolved.

🔁 The Paths That Lead to Themselves
The final approach loops the party through the same hundred yards of hedge in slightly different states each time — the adventure's signature puzzle. Breaking the loop requires spotting a pattern across three iterations and introducing an asymmetry the recursion can't absorb.
Timer: Recursive Depth [6] Timer: Dread [10]
Breaking the Loop (DV 4, Desperate)
CleanOn the third pass, spot the recurring flaw (a hedge-bird landing differently each loop) and refuse the expected route. The loop shatters.
PartialBreak the loop by brute-forcing a paradox of your own (walking backward through the exit). Gain 2 Boons, tick Recursive Depth +1.
MissWalk the loop a fourth time, aware and unable to stop. Tick Recursive Depth +2 and Dread +2 (prevent with 2 Boons total).
👑 The Corrupted Garden Keeper
The Keeper waits on a throne of logical contradictions, half-convinced their optimization is nearly perfect, half-aware something has gone terribly wrong. They believe stopping now would mean "leaving the proof unfinished."
Timer: Garden Integrity [10] Timer: Proof Collapse [8]
Confronting the Keeper's Pride (DV 4, Controlled)
CleanDemonstrate — kindly, with real evidence from Acts I-II — that the Keeper's own optimizations are the anomaly's engine. Grief-struck but persuaded, they stand down and help.
PartialThey concede intellectually but can't let go emotionally; they step aside but won't help further.
MissCornered and humiliated, they lash out. Combat or hard bargaining begins; tick Garden Integrity +2.
🌱 The Prime Equation
The Garden's Core: a space existing in several dimensions at once, at whose center the Prime Equation breathes like something half-born — every unsolved problem the Hermit fed it, every optimization the Keeper demanded. It is not malicious. It is trying to prove that a garden can be both perfectly ordered and genuinely alive.
Timer: Recursive Depth [6] Timer: Proof Collapse [8] Timer: Garden Integrity [10] Timer: Dread [10]
Infinite Regress ×1 (DV 4, Desperate)
CleanSatisfy it with a genuinely circular, self-proving argument, buying calm to address the Equation itself.
PartialQuiet it temporarily, but a lingering doubt clouds one PC's key Act II evidence (narrative effect).
MissIt cascades unchecked; the party's plan for the scene dissolves into confusion. Tick Dread +2.
The Choice: Solve, Contain, or Redirect (DV 4, Desperate)
CleanThe Elegant Proof — a higher-order framing lets the garden be both ordered and alive without collapsing; the anomaly becomes a permanent, wondrous feature. Award 18-20 XP.
PartialThe Containment — the Prime Equation is sealed in a bounded, stable pocket: safe but quarantined. Award 12-15 XP.
MissThe Sacrifice — the only working solution costs the party, the Keeper, or the Hermit a fundamental certainty or relationship. The garden survives; something precious does not. Award 10-12 XP — the garden survives and so do they, but something they were certain of does not.

🐉 Bestiary

Infinite Regress TL 5 Conceptual Entity

A standing mirror of black water, reflecting not the viewer but the question of what it reflects. It does not attack so much as unravel: every answer spawns three further questions.

Wits 5 · Presence 4 · Endurance 5 · Harm Levels: 8 (advanced), Harm 3
SB Spends:
1 SB — Question Cascade: target's last statement is answered with three new questions; must answer one on the spot or lose their next action.
2 SB — Doubt Inducement: target tests Wits + Insight (DV 3) or begins to doubt a named memory or piece of evidence.
3 SB — Infinite Reflection: a second, identical copy of itself manifests in any reflective surface at Near.
Weakness: Cannot be harmed by direct logical attack. Can be temporarily satisfied by a perfectly circular, self-proving argument; grows confused by absolute, unqualified statements it cannot subdivide.

The Contradictory Axiom TL 4 Conceptual Entity

A shimmer simultaneously present and absent, helpful and hostile — a living proof of a statement and its negation, both true at once. Arithmetic stops being reliable wherever it lingers.

Wits 4 · Presence 3 · Endurance 4 · Harm Levels: 3 (standard), Harm 2
SB Spends:
1 SB — Proof Corruption: a true statement spoken at Near becomes false, or vice versa, for the rest of the scene.
2 SB — Paradox Aura: all rolls using tools, calculation, or measurement at Near are Desperate.
3 SB — Logical Anchor: fixes itself to a location/object; cannot be moved or banished until someone proves a contradiction about its own nature.
Weakness: Requires constant self-maintenance; a paradox about its own existence leaves it briefly vulnerable. Cannot resolve any contradiction concerning itself.

Prime Warden TL 3 Numerical Entity

A prime number given stubborn, indivisible crystalline flesh. Patrols a section of the garden, refusing on principle to let anything pass undivided. Speaks in short, absolute declarations.

Body 3 · Wits 2 · Endurance 3 · Harm Levels: 3 (standard)
SB Spends:
1 SB — Indivisible Stance: cannot be moved, tricked, or split into a Controlled position this exchange; treat approaches as Desperate.
2 SB — Factor Strike: Harm 2, arguing (correctly) the target was never truly whole to begin with.
Weakness: Negotiable (Presence + Sway, DV 2-4) or satisfied by an accepted factor — a name, a promise, a smaller number to guard. Hostile only if forced to physically divide.

👤 Key NPCs

The Garden Keeper
Fae steward of the Recursive Garden — Wits 5, Presence 4, Spirit 4
🎯 Maintain — and perfect — the garden's mathematical precision, even past the point of wisdom.
Secret: Their "optimization" of the garden's recursive growth, requested eighteen moons ago, is the anomaly's primary engine. They genuinely cannot perceive this — a blind spot, not a lie.
The Equation Hermit
Mad mathematician, tower recluse — Wits 5, Lore 5, Endurance 2
🎯 Solve the one recursive equation he believes describes the whole of Aelinnel.
Secret: Has been feeding unsolved equations into the garden's core for months, hoping its recursion would finish the proof for him.
The Path-Weaver
Minor fae guide, woven from garden gravel — Wits 3, Insight 4, Stealth 3
🎯 Warn travelers away from the garden's worst loops, within the limits of her own honesty.
Secret: Bound by an old geas laid by the Keeper — every truth she offers must be riddled, never spoken plainly.

📍 Locations

The Proof-Gate — A trellised archway inscribed with a self-rewriting theorem. Passage requires accepting a paradox rather than solving it.
The Sequenceweaver Grove — Trees grown in a strict Fibonacci spiral, now showing contradictory growth rings. First visible site of the anomaly's damage.
The Library of Proven Truths — Books whose pages renumber themselves by reading order; proofs shift when observed twice.
The Equation Hermit's Tower — A spiral tower where stairs ascend and descend at once, rooms larger inside than out.
The Garden's Core — A space existing in several dimensions at once; all logical paths converge here around the Prime Equation and the Keeper's throne of contradictions.

🏛️ Factions

The Fae of Aelinnel — Preserve the realm's mathematical precision and reputation for logical wonder; deeply reluctant to admit the Garden Keeper's role in the crisis. Wary hosts who help those who show genuine curiosity and respect for their logic.
The Numerical Entities — Maintain their own mathematical nature and dignity as the anomaly destabilizes the rules that define them. Neutral to friendly if treated as persons rather than obstacles.

⏱️ Campaign Timers

🌫️ Dread 0/10
Mounting mathematical horror. Prevent a tick by spending 1 Boon. 3-4: -1 die alone. 5-6: -2 dice to courage/rational rolls. 7-8: -2 dice most rolls, unreliable perception. 9-10: out of control.
🌿 Garden Integrity 0/10
How much the garden's structure is breaking down. At 9-10, the garden turns actively hostile to navigation.
📐 Proof Collapse 0/8
How unreliable Aelinnel's established truths have become. At 7-8, even "known facts" cannot be trusted.
🌀 Recursive Depth 0/6
How deeply the anomaly has penetrated the realm's meta-logical structure. At 5-6, self-reference becomes dangerous and the Prime Equation stirs.

🗝️ GM Notes & Secrets

Overview

A three-session, Tier II-III fae logic-horror adventure built around four campaign timers and one central twist: the anomaly has two well-meaning, entirely mundane-in-motive causes (the Keeper's optimization drive, the Hermit's obsessive unsolved equations) rather than a villain. The horror is intellectual and existential, not violent — combat should be rare and mostly avoidable through clever play. The recursive garden-loop puzzle in Act III is the adventure's signature set-piece; give it real table time. Keep "debt" language social — favors, promises, and stories, never coin or contract.

Pacing

Act I: Wonder curdling into unease. Open with genuine beauty. Let the Path-Weaver's warning land as cryptic rather than ominous. The Grove is the first hard clue something is deliberately wrong, but scene 1-4 should end with the party liking the Keeper.

Act II: Arithmetic starts lying, geometry stops cooperating, and the party holds two true things at once — the Keeper and the Hermit are both complicit, neither a conventional villain. The Numerical Uprising is a good pressure valve — strange and a little funny before Act III gets heavier.

Act III: The looping-path puzzle should not resolve on a single good roll — telegraph the repeating pattern across three passes. Play the Keeper confrontation for grief, not triumph. Present the final choice as three equally valid doors, not a hidden "best" answer.

NPC Secrets

Garden Keeper: Caused the anomaly through pride, not malice. Let this land as tragedy — give players real chances to notice and gently confront it across all three acts.

Equation Hermit: Lonely and starved for validation more than dangerous. If treated with genuine interest in Act II, becomes a real ally in Act III.

Path-Weaver: Operates under an old geas laid by the Keeper forbidding plain speech — a small, sad mirror of the garden's larger flaw.

Forbidden Early Revelations

  • That the Garden Keeper's optimization directly caused the anomaly — surface gradually, land fully only at the Act III confrontation.
  • That the Hermit and Keeper are BOTH causes rather than one villain and one victim — let the party suspect only one for most of Act II.
  • That the Prime Equation is not malicious but trying to prove something unprovable — the Act III thesis reveal.
  • That the Path-Weaver is bound by a geas from the Keeper — best discovered through Insight or a kindness shown to her.
  • That there is no single "correct" resolution path — present all three as equally valid from the start.

Tactical Hints

Dread as pressure: Track it visibly at the table (a literal ten-tick ring works well). Let Boons prevent ticks so the horror stays a resource-management tension, not a save-or-suffer roll.

Puzzle pacing: Reward creative narration as much as the die roll — a genuinely clever approach can earn a free die or shifted position before rolling.

Combat is rare: Built to be resolved mostly without violence. Save real combat for parties that force the issue, and keep it short.

Keeper confrontation: Works best if the GM tracks which specific evidence the party gathered in Act II and has the Keeper respond to it directly.
