🔥 The Forge of Souls

Tier III-IV 4-6 Sessions Tactical Warfare Attrition & Sacrifice Underground Campaign Author: Nicholas A. Gasper

Six weeks ago the deep works beneath Khaz-Vurim began to glow again. The Hollow Anvil — the furnace the hold sealed and stopped naming four centuries ago, in the season the dwarves still call the Quiet Burning — is lit, and it is not making tools. It is making soldiers. Every corpse that goes into the ash-draft comes back out walking, welded into ranks, and the ranks are moving up the Spine Underway toward the Halls at the pace of a slow tide. House Khaz-Vurim cannot field an army; it can barely field a watch. What it can do is hand the party a scratch force, a map of a collapsing tunnel system, and the keys to a war it has already privately decided it will lose.

The Forge of Souls is a war fought in corridors. It is the module for tables that want fronts, supply, ground given and taken, and command decisions with names attached — run entirely through the ordinary Fate's Edge roll and timer, with the mass-combat and miniatures tools from GM Guide Appendix B doing the tactical work. There is no separate wargame here. There is a dice pool, a Position, a set of timers, and a furnace at the bottom of the map that gets stronger every time somebody dies — including your side.

"You will not save the Underway. You will decide how much of it burns, and who is standing in it when it does. That is the whole of your commission. Do not embarrass us." — Vault-Queen Thora Stonebeard
Tone guardrail — grimdark, not grim-stupid. Khaz-Vurim's people are competent, disciplined and doomed, and they know it. The horror is not gore; it is the arithmetic. Every dwarf who falls in the Underway stands up again in the enemy's line within the hour, so the hold's own dead are the forge's recruitment. Play the sergeants as professionals, the losses as bookkeeping that hurts, and the forge as something that does not hate anyone and has never once stopped working. Nobody in this module is evil. That is the point.
Content note. This module deals in attrition, mass death, the desecration of the recently dead, and command decisions that spend lives. Raise it in Session Zero. Appendix E of the GM Guide has the tools; the X-card and Lines & Veils both apply cleanly here, and the module survives cutting the corpse-reanimation imagery entirely — make the Muster stone-and-slag automata instead and every mechanic below still works.

Reading an encounter badge. Each badge names a DV and a Position. It does not name a dice pool, and that is deliberate: the pool is Attribute + Skill, chosen from how the player says they are going about it. Two characters can attempt the same encounter with different pools and both be right. Where an approach is obviously the intended one, the badge says so; where it does not, ask what they are doing and build the pool from the answer.

⚔️ Running the War

Everything below is core Fate's Edge plus GM Guide Appendix B. Read this section once before Act I and you will not need to look anything else up.

Forces are Followers. Each body of troops in this module is a Follower with a Capability, a Supply timer and a Morale timer, exactly as Appendix B: Detailed Warfare describes. A force assists a character's roll at min(Cap, the relevant Skill), and no character takes more than +3 dice from all sources on any roll. That single line is the whole of the "how many soldiers do I have" question — resolve it and move on to what the players are actually doing.
Command rolls move objectives. A player directing a force rather than swinging a sword rolls Presence + Sway (order, exhortation, threat) or Wits + Insight (reading the ground, timing, spotting the lane) against the objective's DV, and the result fills the objective timer: Clean Success or Success with a Story Beat fills 2 segments, Partial fills 1, a Miss fills none and hands the table 2 Boons. Story Beats from command rolls are spent on the fog of war — a runner killed before the order lands, a stair collapsing behind the flank, a sergeant who heard something other than what was said.
The Ash Tithe. This is the module's one bespoke rule and the engine of the whole campaign. Whenever a friendly force's Morale timer fills, or a named ally dies, or the party leaves a fight without recovering their dead, fill 1 segment of Muster Strength. The forge takes what falls. Losses do not merely subtract from your side; they add to theirs.
Denying the tithe — the Rite of Ash. A scene spent burning your own dead properly denies the tithe for that engagement. Spirit + Lore or Body + Endurance, DV 3, Controlled. Clean Success or Success with a Story Beat: no tithe, and the force's Morale timer empties 1 segment. Partial: no tithe, but the pyre costs 1 segment of that force's Supply. Miss: the tithe is taken anyway and every participant gains 1 Fatigue. Players will work out fast that they can trade supplies and daylight for the enemy's growth curve. Let them. That trade is the game.
Miniatures are optional and supported. If your table wants hexes, Appendix B: Miniatures Mode is written for exactly this module. Base DV starts at 3 and rises 1 per complicating keyword to a maximum of 5; Disengage is DV 4; a tougher enemy does not raise the DV, it worsens your Position. Every location in Act II names its hex keywords. If your table does not want hexes, ignore them — the keywords read perfectly well as prose ("the gallery is Difficult" simply means the footing is bad and the Position is worse).
What the party can actually do on any given turn of the war. Offer these five as a standing menu and let the fiction pick the roll. Fight — hold or take ground, advancing The Line Holds. Command — a command roll on an objective timer. Salvage — Wits + Craft, DV 3, to fill a force's Supply. Burn — the Rite of Ash, denying the tithe. Scout — Wits + Insight or Wits + Stealth, DV 3, which reveals the next front's keywords and grants Dominant Position on the first roll made there. There is never enough time to do all five. That scarcity is the tactical layer; the dice are just how you find out.

🔔 Act I — The Muster

Khaz-Vurim admits, out loud and in front of witnesses, that it cannot hold its own floor. The party is given a commission, a scratch force, and a door that is already warm to the touch.

🏛️ The Vault-Queen's Commission
Thora Stonebeard gives the party command in a hall deliberately emptied of courtiers, because what she is about to say cannot be said in front of the other holds. The Ninth Gate has fallen. The watch that held it has not come back and has been seen since — upright, armoured, and marching the wrong way.
Timer: Forge Heat [10], starts at 3 Take the Commission (DV 3, Controlled) Read What She Is Not Saying (DV 4, Controlled)
Negotiating the Commission (DV 4, Controlled)
Clean: Full command authority in writing, plus Warden-Sergeant Halda Grimmel seconded to the party as a Follower. Nobody in the Underway will question an order.
Success with a Story Beat: The same, but the writ names a witness from a rival house who will report everything the party does.
Partial (+1 Boon): Command is granted in practice but not on paper. Every order given to a unit not already loyal is one Position step worse.
Miss (+2 Boons): Thora keeps the authority and lends the party only her confidence. They will be fighting a war they have to argue their way through.
🪓 The Scratch Force
What is left: two under-strength watch companies, a guild sapper crew with more blasting powder than sense, and forty-odd hold militia who were stonemasons a week ago. They are formed up in the Muster Yard and they are looking at the party to find out whether this is a defence or a funeral.
Timer: Supply, Underway Command [6], starts at 4 Speak to the Muster (DV 4, Controlled) Inspect and Reorganise (DV 3, Controlled) Requisition from the Guild (DV 4, Controlled)
Speak to the Muster (DV 4, Controlled)
Clean: Underway Command's Morale timer starts empty and the party may choose one unit to raise to Cap 4.
Success with a Story Beat: As Clean, but one specific dwarf believes a promise the party did not quite make. Note the name. It will matter in Act IV.
Partial (+1 Boon): Morale starts at 1. They will follow, but they have counted the odds themselves.
Miss (+2 Boons): Morale starts at 2 and the sapper crew's foreman openly asks whether anyone has actually been given a plan.
🚪 The Ninth Gate
Four hours down the Spine Underway, the Ninth Gate stands open and the air past it is thirty degrees warmer. The gate-warden is still at her post. She has been welded into it — stone-and-iron grown up through her armour, her hands fused to the winch she was turning when the draft found her. She is awake. She is apologising.
Timer: The Line Holds [8] Fight the Warden-Sergeant (DV 4, Desperate) Talk Her Down (DV 4, Controlled) Sever the Gate Mechanism (DV 3, Controlled) Hex keywords: Difficult, ZoC, Elevation
Warden-Sergeant Bruni Ashgrave (TL 6)
Clean: She is released — killed, or unwelded and carried out dying, the party's choice. Fill 2 segments of The Line Holds. Her body is recovered, so no Ash Tithe.
Success with a Story Beat: The same, and she tells the party what she saw come up the shaft before she names it and stops. Spend the Story Beat on the gate falling shut behind them.
Partial (+1 Boon): The party gets past her. She is still in the gate, still apologising, and she is now behind them.
Miss (+2 Boons): The gate holds against the party instead of for them. Fill 1 segment of Forge Heat and take the long way — the Cinder Stair, which is worse.
The lesson of the Ninth Gate. This encounter exists to teach the table one thing before the war starts: the enemy is people you knew, and killing them is how the enemy grows. If the party leaves Bruni's body where it lies, take the Ash Tithe in front of them and say what it means. They will never leave a body again, and that decision — carrying your dead uphill while the clock runs — is the module.

⛰️ Act II — The War in the Underway

Three fronts, one force, and not enough of it. The party cannot hold all three. This act is the campaign, and it ends when The Line Holds [8] fills — or when Forge Heat [10] does, and the module goes to its failure state instead.

How Act II runs. Present all three fronts at once and let the party divide their force. Each front the party commits to in person plays as a scene with the encounters below; each front they delegate resolves on a single command roll at the listed DV, one Position step worse, with no player present to spend Boons. An undefended front advances Forge Heat by 1 each time the party turns their attention elsewhere. There is no correct allocation. There is only the allocation the party can live with, and the one they have to explain afterwards.

🌉 Front One — The Foundry Span

A single stone walkway crosses the old casting floor, four hundred feet of it, with the crucible pits still live underneath. Whoever holds the Span controls the only route the Muster can bring its heavy ranks up. It is also the only route the party can bring anything back down. Holding it is correct. Holding it is also how you lose people.
Timer: The Span Holds [6] Hold the Span (DV 4, Desperate) Drop the Span (DV 4, Controlled) Rig the Pits to Vent (DV 3, Controlled) Hex keywords: Difficult, ZoC, Elevation, no flanking lane
Hold the Span against the Cindersworn (DV 4, Desperate)
Clean: The rank breaks on the narrows. Fill 2 of The Line Holds. The dead are on your side of the gap and can be recovered — no tithe.
Success with a Story Beat: As Clean, but the Story Beat buys a Reinforcement: a second rank is already climbing, and the party learns the Muster does not need to rest.
Partial (+1 Boon): The Span holds, but the line is pushed back over the gap. Fill 1 of The Line Holds and 1 of Underway Command's Morale. Bodies are left on the far side — Ash Tithe unless the party goes back for them.
Miss (+2 Boons): The Span is lost. Fill 2 of Forge Heat, take the Ash Tithe, and the Muster now has a road.
Drop the Span (DV 4, Controlled) — the sappers' answer
Clean: Four hundred feet of walkway goes into the crucible. The Muster's heavy ranks are cut off for the rest of the module — no Slagborn Breaker ever reaches the upper Underway. Fill 3 of The Line Holds.
Success with a Story Beat: As Clean, but the sapper crew is still on it when it goes. The party chooses whether to be honest about why.
Partial (+1 Boon): The Span drops half-fallen — impassable to ranks, crossable one at a time by anything patient. Fill 2 of The Line Holds.
Miss (+2 Boons): The charges go early. The Span stands, the sappers do not, and the party has spent its blasting powder. Fill 1 of Forge Heat and take the Ash Tithe.
The Span is the module's best trap. Dropping it is tactically correct and strategically catastrophic: it also cuts the party's own line of retreat and every route by which Khaz-Vurim could ever reinforce them. Do not warn them. Let Halda Grimmel warn them, once, flatly, and then do what she is told.

🕯️ Front Two — The Cinder Stair

Nine hundred steps in a shaft the width of three dwarves abreast, spiralling down past a dozen sealed side-galleries. The Muster does not use it — the Muster does not need to hurry. The Stair is where the party's scouts go, where the wounded come up, and where the ash-draft comes through, which is why the sealed galleries are no longer sealed.
Timer: Ash-Draft [4] Clear a Gallery (DV 3, Controlled) Fight in the Shaft (DV 4, Desperate) Reseal the Draft (DV 4, Controlled) Hex keywords: Difficult, Elevation, ZoC on every landing
Reseal the Draft (DV 4, Controlled)
Clean: Ash-Draft empties and stops advancing for the rest of Act II. The upper Underway stops producing new Ash-Thralls.
Success with a Story Beat: As Clean, but the seal takes the Stair with it — the wounded now come up the Span or not at all.
Partial (+1 Boon): The draft is slowed. Ash-Draft advances on every second trigger instead of every one.
Miss (+2 Boons): The gallery the party opened to reach the vent was not empty. Fill Ash-Draft to full and roll an encounter from it immediately.
Ash-Draft [4] — when it fills: the hold's own recent dead, carried up the Stair for burial over the last six weeks and interred in the upper galleries, get up. Six Ash-Thralls in the party's rear, wearing Khaz-Vurim colours, with names that people in the party's own force can read off the armour. Empty the timer and let it start again. If the party has been running the Rite of Ash properly, they can say so, and it costs them nothing but the memory of the argument they had about whether it was worth the powder.

⚒️ Front Three — The Rememberers' Gallery

The old master-craft workshops, where the Molten Rememberers work: not soldiers, not thralls, but the forge's smiths — bound artisans who were once the hold's best, still making things, still good at it. They are producing the Muster's armour. They will stop if asked correctly. They cannot stop on their own, and they know it, and they have been trying to be asked for four hundred years.
Timer: The Work Continues [6], starts at 4 Parley with a Rememberer (DV 4, Controlled) Learn the Binding (DV 4, Controlled) Break the Gallery (DV 5, Desperate) Commission a Work (DV 4, Controlled) Hex keywords: Difficult, Altar [WARD] at each anvil
Parley with a Rememberer (DV 4, Controlled)
Clean: The Work Continues empties. The Muster stops receiving new armour — every Cindersworn encountered from here has Armor 1 instead of 2. The Rememberers ask, once, to be freed, and remember who said no.
Success with a Story Beat: As Clean, and one Rememberer follows the party down. It is not permitted to leave the gallery. It comes anyway, and the forge feels it go.
Partial (+1 Boon): The Work slows — the timer advances only when the party is elsewhere.
Miss (+2 Boons): The Rememberers hear the request as a commission and fulfil it with the material available, which is the party's dead. Fill 2 of Forge Heat.
Commission a Work (DV 4, Controlled) — using the Crafting rules
The Rememberers will make one thing for the party, properly, to the standard of the hold at its height. Resolve it as a Work under SRD §6.9 Crafting: a [4] Timer, Attribute + Craft, DV set by ambition, and the Rememberers' materials and skill set the Position to Dominant. A Wonder is possible here and only here — a [6] Timer, Arcana, and the XP and Obligation costs at the Talent tier as written. The Obligation is owed to the gallery, and the gallery is owned by the forge.

🔥 Act III — The Hollow Anvil

The Line Holds is full. The Muster is broken in the field, which changes nothing, because the Muster was never the problem. The party goes down to the furnace and finds out why it lit.

🕳️ The Descent to the Deep Works
Below the last worked stone the tunnels stop being dwarven and start being something the dwarves cut into. The heat is structural now — the rock itself is at working temperature, and the party's remaining force cannot follow them past this point without cooking in their own armour. Whoever goes down goes down alone.
Endure the Descent (DV 4, Desperate) Read the Deep Works (DV 4, Controlled) Fatigue: 1 per character, unmitigated
📜 The Quiet Burning, Read Correctly
The forge-records are still legible because nothing down here has ever been damp. Four hundred years ago Khaz-Vurim did not seal the Hollow Anvil to stop it. They sealed it to keep it fed slowly — a maintained banked fire, drawing one life a decade, quietly, from those the hold could spare. The seal was a rationing device. It has held perfectly for four centuries and it failed six weeks ago because the hold stopped paying, because the hold could no longer afford to.
Piece Together the Records (DV 4, Controlled) Confirm the Tithe Ledger (DV 5, Controlled) Confront Halda With It (DV 4, Desperate)
Who knew. Thora knew. Halda Grimmel knew — her post as Warden of the Ninth Gate was the tithe's delivery duty, and she has walked people down that stair herself. Nobody lied to the party; nobody was asked. The revelation should land as a professional obligation quietly discharged for four hundred years by people who considered it the least bad option and were, arguably, correct. Do not let any NPC apologise for it. Let one of them explain the arithmetic and mean it.
🔨 The Hollow Anvil
A furnace the size of a cathedral nave, built around an anvil with nothing on it, and a draft that goes down further than the light does. It is not a monster and it will not fight. It is a machine, running correctly, at the load it was given. It has no opinion about any of this.
TL 10 — Harm Levels: None (puzzle) Understand the Draw (DV 5, Controlled) Find the Cistern Sluice (DV 4, Controlled) Touch the Anvil (DV 5, Desperate)
The Anvil cannot be beaten in a fight, and the module does not pretend otherwise. It has no Harm Levels. Attacks land, matter, and change nothing — the metal takes the damage and the draft takes the attacker's Fatigue. Anyone who insists on fighting it gains 1 Fatigue per exchange and learns, unambiguously, that this is not the shape of the problem. Give them that information cleanly and early rather than punishing them for the assumption.

🕯️ Act IV — The Last Ash

Three endings, one failure state. None of them is clean, and the module is explicit with the players that none of them is clean. Put all three on the table before the last roll — this is a decision, not a puzzle with a hidden right answer.

💧 Ending One — Quench
Open the deep cistern into the fire-chamber. The Hollow Anvil dies. So does forty miles of the Spine Underway, in steam and collapse, and Khaz-Vurim is cut off from the deep holds for at least a generation.
Open the Sluice (DV 5, Desperate) Time the Withdrawal (DV 4, Controlled)
Clean: The forge is out and the party is out. The Underway is gone. Khaz-Vurim survives as a hold that will spend the next fifty years explaining why it is poorer.
Success with a Story Beat: As Clean, but the Story Beat is who did not make the stair. Name someone from the Muster Yard scene.
Partial (+1 Boon): The forge is out. The party is on the wrong side of the collapse and walks out the long way, through the deep, over weeks — a campaign's worth of Travel Framework if you want it.
Miss (+2 Boons): The cistern goes into a fire-chamber that is still at full draw. The steam front takes the upper Underway too. The party lives; the Muster Yard does not.
🕯️ Ending Two — Bank the Fire
Restore the seal. Restore the ration. The Anvil goes back to one life a decade and Khaz-Vurim keeps its road, its trade, and its bargain — and the party is now the party that chose to reinstate it, in writing, with their names on the ledger.
Restore the Seal (DV 5, Controlled) Sign the Ledger (DV 4, Desperate) Obligation: +2 to each participating character, permanent
Clean: The seal holds. The Underway reopens within the year. Every character who signed carries 2 Obligation that does not clear, and the Rememberers' Gallery is theirs to commission from — at the gallery's price.
Success with a Story Beat: As Clean, and the first name on the new roll is chosen in front of them.
Partial (+1 Boon): The seal holds imperfectly. Forge Heat does not reset; it starts the next campaign at 4 and advances once per season.
Miss (+2 Boons): The seal is set wrong. It will hold for eleven years. The party will not be told which eleven.
💀 Ending Three — Feed It the Muster
The forge does not care what it is given, only how much. Drive the entire Ash Muster back down the draft — every thrall, every legionary, every Breaker, every dwarf of Khaz-Vurim it has ever taken — and the Anvil will be sated for a century on what it made itself.
Drive the Muster Down (DV 5, Desperate) Command the Rearguard (DV 5, Desperate) Rite of Ash, at scale (DV 4, Controlled)
Clean: A hundred years bought in an afternoon. The forge is banked without a living tithe and the hold owes the party everything it will never say out loud.
Success with a Story Beat: As Clean, but the Muster included people the party's own force is related to, and the drive was witnessed.
Partial (+1 Boon): Most of it goes down. The remainder scatters into the deep galleries and becomes somebody's problem in thirty years.
Miss (+2 Boons): The drive stalls at the lip. The party is between the Muster and the draft, and the only mass left to give the forge is standing in the chamber.
This is the ending the module is built for, and it must not be free. It requires the Span intact (so it cannot be taken by a party that dropped it in Act II), Underway Command with Morale below full, and at least one Rememberer willing to open the draft. A party that plays Act II purely defensively cannot reach it. That is the reward for tactical patience, and it is the only reward this module hands out for free.
☠️ Failure State — The Muster Reaches the Halls
If Forge Heat [10] fills before Act III, the war ends where wars end. The Ash Muster comes up the Spine Underway into Khaz-Vurim Halls and the module becomes a defence of the Vault Gate: a single extended scene, DV 5 throughout, Desperate throughout, with every timer the party built in Act II spending itself to buy rounds.
Hold the Vault Gate (DV 5, Desperate) Evacuate the Halls (DV 4, Controlled)
This is a losing fight and should be played as one. The question is not whether Khaz-Vurim falls but how many go out through the high passes before it does, and whether the party is among them or on the gate. Both answers are playable. Neither is cowardice. Say so at the table.

📍 Locations

Khaz-Vurim Halls — The hold above. Perfect stonework, thinning population, a Muster Yard that has not been used in three generations and is now full.
The Ninth Gate — The last sealed door on the descending Underway. Warm to the touch on the wrong side.
The Foundry Span — Four hundred feet of walkway over live crucible pits. The only road for heavy ranks, in either direction.
The Cinder Stair — Nine hundred steps, three abreast, past a dozen burial galleries that were sealed for good reason.
The Rememberers' Gallery — Master workshops still in production. Warded anvils, excellent light, and the finest craft in the deep world.
The Deep Works — Below the last dwarven cut. The rock is at working temperature and the geometry stops being anyone's.
The Hollow Anvil — A furnace the size of a cathedral nave, an anvil with nothing on it, and a draft that goes down past the light.
The Deep Cistern — Four centuries of meltwater held above the fire-chamber by a sluice gate somebody built deliberately, and then wrote nothing down about.

🏛️ Factions & Forces

Forces are Followers with Supply and Morale timers, per GM Guide Appendix B: Detailed Warfare. A force assists at min(Cap, relevant Skill), and no roll takes more than +3 dice from all sources.

Underway Command — the party's scratch force
Cap 3 Supply [6], starts at 4 Morale [6]
Two under-strength watch companies, forty hold militia, one guild sapper crew. Disciplined, undertrained, and entirely aware of the odds. When Morale fills, the force routs and the Ash Tithe is taken.
Grimmel's Watch — regulars
Cap 4 Supply [6] Morale [8]
The professionals. Slow to break, expensive to replace, and there will be no replacements.
Kesta's Crew — guild sappers
Cap 3 Supply [4] Morale [4]
Blasting powder, bracing timber, and the only unit in the module that can change the map. Fragile. Supply is literal here: when it empties, no more demolitions.
The Ash Muster — the forge's army
TL 7 Muster Strength [6] No Morale timer
It does not rout, resupply, rest, or negotiate, and it grows every time anyone on any side dies within reach of the draft. It has no Morale timer because it has no morale. Each time Muster Strength fills, empty it and add one rank: first Cindersworn, then Slagborn Breakers, then a second Breaker line.
House Khaz-Vurim — Controlled, sliding to Desperate. Negotiation DV 4/5. Will not commit further troops and will not say why.
The Rememberers' Gallery — Dominant in its own workshops, bound everywhere else. Parley DV 4. The only faction in the module that can be freed rather than defeated.
Edgewalkers Guild — Controlled, mercenary but honest. Cooperation DV 3/4. Keeps a ledger of favours in standing, never in coin.

💀 Bestiary

A furnace. It is not awake, not malevolent, and not addressable. It draws what it is given at the rate it was built to draw, and it has been given the whole Underway. It has no Harm Levels because there is nothing in it to kill.

🗒️ GM Notes

The arithmetic is the horror. Never describe the Muster as evil, hungry, or hateful. Describe it as on schedule. The forge is a correctly functioning piece of dwarven engineering being asked to do more than it was rationed for, and every atrocity in the module is downstream of an accounting problem somebody solved reasonably four hundred years ago.
No PC dies by GM fiat. The original draft of this adventure asked the GM to choose a player character to sacrifice. That is not how this game works and it is not in here. Every ending that costs a life costs it through a roll the player made or a choice the player stated, in public, at the table.
Act I: Spend real time in the Muster Yard. Name four dwarves. Let the players talk to them. Every one of those names is a lever the module will pull later, and none of them work if the party met the force as a number.
Act II: Run the three fronts as a standing pressure, not a sequence. Ask "where are you" at the top of every session and mean it. The correct feeling is that the party is always one place short, and that this is nobody's fault.
Act III: Let the reveal be quiet. Halda does not confess; she answers a question. Thora does not justify; she assumes the party already worked it out. Nobody in Khaz-Vurim thinks the tithe was a secret. They think it was a duty, discharged discreetly, and they are right that it kept the hold alive.
Act IV: Put all three endings on the table explicitly, with their costs, before anyone rolls. This is not a puzzle to solve; it is a decision to own. Then let them argue about it for as long as they want, because that argument is the climax and the dice are only the epilogue.
Scaling. Written for Tier III–IV. For Tier II, drop every TL by 2, cut the Slagborn Breaker entirely, and shorten Forge Heat to [8]. For Tier V, add a second Breaker line at Muster Strength full, raise the Hollow Anvil's Draw to 2 Fatigue, and start Forge Heat at 5.
Companion module. The Dwarven Debt shares this hold, this Vault-Queen, and the Spine Underway. Running it first gives the party a reason to care that Khaz-Vurim survives — and Ending One, which severs the Underway for a generation, lands very differently on a table that has already walked it.
