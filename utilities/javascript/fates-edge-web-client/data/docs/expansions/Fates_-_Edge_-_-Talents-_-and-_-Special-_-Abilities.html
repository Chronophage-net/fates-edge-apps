Talents and Special Abilities

Core Rules Expansion Character Advancement XP Catalog Author: Nicholas A. Gasper

Talents are the building blocks of character specialization — learned techniques, supernatural gifts, and cultural inheritances purchased with Experience Points (XP). This document is a complete reference catalog organized into four XP tiers, from small once-per-scene tricks to legendary, story-defining powers, grouped further by archetype (Healer, Rogue, Monk, Runic Warrior, Free Caster, and more). Use it alongside character creation and advancement: pick an archetype's early talents first, then follow the tier guidance and synergy examples in the closing section to plan a build across a campaign. Every entry lists its cost, activation type, full mechanical effect, and any prerequisites.

Understanding Talents

Talents are purchased with Experience Points and provide special capabilities that go beyond simple skill bonuses. They often have specific activation conditions, may grant narrative permissions (letting you attempt things others cannot), and can define your character's unique identity at the table.

Talent Costs by Tier

TierCostScope
Minor2–3 XPSmall situational bonus, once per scene.
Major4–6 XPStrong upgrade, permanent effect in a niche.
Prestige7–10 XPCampaign-defining, breaks fundamental limits.
Epic11+ XPLegendary ability, shapes the story.

Activation Types

  • Passive: always on; no action required.
  • Active: requires an action or scene focus to use.
  • Reactive: triggers automatically on a condition.

Limits and Economy

Unless a talent says otherwise:

  • Per scene uses refresh at scene end.
  • Per session uses refresh after downtime.
  • Some talents allow you to spend Boons to push effects further.
Only One Active Talent?

The rule “only one talent can be active at a time” applies only to activated abilities that require a decision or action. Passive talents (e.g., +1 die to perception) are always active and stack. Active talents that require an action or reaction cannot be used simultaneously — you choose which one to activate when the trigger occurs. Talents that are “once per scene” reset at the end of the scene.

Minor Talents (2–3 XP)

Minor talents are small, situational bonuses — typically once-per-scene tricks or narrow passive edges. They are the cheapest way to give a character a distinct signature move early in a campaign.

Mounted Combat Traditions

Among the horse-clans of the Ykrul and the steppe riders of the Kuvani, archery from horseback is not a specialization — it is a birthright. Characters from these cultures may take Mounted Archery at a reduced cost (1 XP instead of 2) and Horse Archer's Art at 4 XP instead of 5. Game Masters may extend this cultural discount to other mounted traditions in their setting (e.g., Linn skirmishers, Vilikari border riders).

Talent Synergies and Building Strategies

The talents above are designed to combine. This section gathers proven synergy chains, tier-by-tier purchase guidance, and worked character-build examples to help you (and your GM) plan a talent progression across a campaign.

Synergy Examples

  • Spellcraft + Elemental Focus + Elemental Mastery: reliable freeform casting with reduced backlash and epic control.
  • Backstab + Shadow Dance + Deathblow: a stealth assassination chain.
  • Ritual Mastery + Efficient Invocation + Crack Specialist: fast, safe, low-cost rituals.
  • Defensive Survival + Elusive Dodge + Miststep Reflex: high survivability in melee.
  • Iron Fist Technique + Flowing Spirit Technique + Untouchable Form: unstoppable monk durability.
  • Iron Fist Technique + Perfect Timing Technique + Inevitable Strike: a monk counterattack master.
  • Effortless Charge + Relentless: charge over difficult terrain once per scene without a Fatigue cost.
  • Stubborn Pursuer + Tactical Movement: stay glued to a disengaging foe while preserving your own movement options.
  • Free Caster Progression: Controlled Burn → Spell Echo → Resonance Anchor → Weave Harmonist reduces SB generation and turns risk into personal resource.
  • Rogue Progression: Light Fingers → Always Another Exit → Ghost Heist for the ultimate phantom thief.
  • Rogue Cantor Progression: Crowd Veil + Shadow Song for the infiltrator who sings guards to sleep.
  • Mounted Combat Progression: Mounted Archery → Horse Archer's Art for the steppe rider who rains arrows from a galloping horse.
  • Social Face Progression: Smooth Talker + Trusted Reputation + Spymaster for the character who knows everyone and can call in favors across the city.
  • Follower/Asset Progression: Efficient Recruiter + Loyalty Bond + Delegated Upkeep for the commander who builds a loyal network that runs itself.
  • Cantor Progression: Projecting Voice → Captivating Performance → Master Cantor for the legendary performer who inspires armies.
  • Healer Progression: Field Dressing → Triage for the combat medic who keeps allies fighting.
  • Ranger Progression: Keen Tracker → Camouflage for the scout who moves unseen and never loses the trail.
  • Artificer Progression: Master Craft → Master Artificer for the crafter who builds anything, faster and cheaper.
  • Witch Progression: Craft of the Hedge → additional Hedge Gifts → a Hedge Gift of the Patron for the threshold worker who masters the quiet work of names.
  • Runic Warrior Progression: Flesh Codex → Witnessed Scar → The Unbroken Vow for the warrior whose body is a covenant and whose scars are scripture.
  • Beastfolk Progression: Keen Scent of the Valewood → Run With the Pack for the wild hunter who moves with the pack.
  • Dragonfolk Progression: Hoard-Sense → Draconic Breath for the dragon-kin who hoards secrets and breathes fire.
  • Other (Liminal) Progression: Step Sideways → Unsettling Truth for the threshold-walker who speaks in omens.

When to Take Talents (By Tier)

Early Game (0–40 XP): take 1–2 access talents (Spellcraft, Codex, Familiar) and 2–3 minor talents that support your core concept.

  • Free Casters: prioritize Controlled Burn and Weave Resilience.
  • Rogues: take Light Fingers and Silk Palms.
  • Mounted Archers: take Mounted Archery.
  • Social Characters: take Smooth Talker and Ear to the Ground.
  • Healers: take Field Dressing.
  • Rangers: take Keen Tracker.
  • Crafters: take Master Craft.
  • Performers: take Projecting Voice.
  • Witches: take Craft of the Hedge.
  • Runic Warriors: take Flesh Codex and Oath-Bound.
  • Beastfolk: take Keen Scent of the Valewood.
  • Dragonfolk: take Hoard-Sense.
  • Others (Liminal): take Step Sideways.

Mid Game (41–90 XP): add major talents (Patron's Symbol, Backstab, The Velvet Glove, Iron Fist Technique, Horse Archer's Art, Trusted Reputation, Efficient Recruiter, Triage, Camouflage, Master Artificer, Captivating Performance, Poisoner, Heavy Armor Training, Light Cavalry, Desert Rider, Scarred Resilience, Witnessed Scar, and similar) plus 2–3 synergistic power talents.

  • Free Casters: take Elemental Attunement and Backlash Siphon.
  • Rogues: take Always Another Exit and The Forger's Eye.
  • Monks: take Iron Fist Technique and Flowing Spirit Technique.
  • Runic Warriors: take Engraved Defense and Shared Burden.
  • Commanders: take Delegated Upkeep.
  • Dragonfolk: take Draconic Breath (if Tier II+).

Late Game (91+ XP): acquire 1–2 prestige or epic talents that define your legacy.

  • Free Casters: Spell Echo, Resonance Anchor, Weave Harmonist.
  • Rogues: Shadow Dance, Deathblow, Ghost Heist.
  • Social Characters: Spymaster.
  • Performers: Master Cantor.
  • Runic Warriors: The Unbroken Vow, Avatar of the Codex, Runebound Resilience.
  • Monks: Untouchable Form, Fist of the Mountain.
  • Beastfolk: Run With the Pack.
  • Others: Unsettling Truth.

Worked Build Examples

Each example traces one path through the tiers above, showing how a concept accretes talents from Tier I to Tier III+.

The Shadow Assassin

A rogue who takes Backstab at Tier I, Shadow Dance at Tier II, and Deathblow at Tier III can execute a devastating assassination chain: attack from stealth with Backstab (+1 Harm, ignore armor); after a kill, use Shadow Dance to re-enter stealth; then later strike from Dominant Position with Deathblow. This combination requires careful positioning and Boon management, but is highly effective.

The Silent Infiltrator

A rogue who takes The Unlocked Window at Tier I, then Always Another Exit at Tier II, can bypass security and escape any corner. Add Shadow Song for the ability to lull guards and unlock spirit-locks with song.

The Stable Free Caster

A Free Caster who takes Controlled Burn at Tier I, Weave Resilience at Tier I–II, Spell Echo at Tier II, Resonance Anchor at Tier III, and finally Weave Harmonist at Tier IV can reduce the swinginess of free casting dramatically. The first 1 of each scene becomes player currency rather than GM fuel; backlash is softened; and once per session, the caster can completely ignore SB from a critical spell.

The Unarmed Master

A monk who takes Disciplined Body and Open Hand Technique at Tier I, then Iron Fist Technique and Flowing Spirit Technique at Tier II, and finally Untouchable Form at Tier III becomes a durable, mobile unarmed fighter who converts harm to fatigue, shrugs off fear, and cannot be grappled.

The Horse Archer

A Ykrul rider who takes Mounted Archery at Tier I and Horse Archer's Art at Tier II can fire accurately from a galloping horse and use the Skirmish action to move twice as far while shooting.

The Face of the Party

A social character who takes Smooth Talker and Negotiation Stance at Tier I, then Trusted Reputation and Information Broker at Tier II, and finally Spymaster at Tier III can navigate any court, gather intelligence from thin air, and call in favors across the city.

The Commander

A leader who takes Efficient Recruiter at Tier I, Loyalty Bond and Delegated Upkeep at Tier II, and builds a network of Cap 3+ followers can run a faction from the shadows.

The Legendary Performer

A performer who takes Projecting Voice at Tier I, Captivating Performance and Voice of Reason at Tier II, and finally Master Cantor at Tier III can calm a riot, inspire an army, or negotiate peace between warring factions.

The Combat Medic

A healer who takes Field Dressing at Tier I and Triage at Tier II can keep allies alive through the deadliest battles.

The Master Artificer

A crafter who takes Master Craft at Tier I, then Master Artificer and Asset Crafter at Tier II can build a workshop, forge magical items, and construct Assets at half the cost and time.

The Hedge Witch

A witch who takes Craft of the Hedge at Tier I, selects Steady Hand and Salt Line as Hedge Gifts, then takes additional Hedge Gifts at Tier II can work subtle threshold magic and ward against spirits.

The Runic Warrior

A Runic Warrior who takes Flesh Codex at Tier I, Oath-Bound and Scarred Resilience at Tier I–II, Witnessed Scar at Tier II, and finally The Unbroken Vow or Runebound Resilience at Tier III+ becomes a warrior whose body is a living covenant.

The Beast of the Wild

A Beastfolk character who takes Keen Scent of the Valewood at Tier I and Run With the Pack at Tier II becomes a fearsome hunter who reads the wild and moves with their pack.

The Dragon's Heir

A Dragonfolk character who takes Hoard-Sense at Tier I and Draconic Breath at Tier II+ becomes a hoarder of secrets and a force of elemental destruction.

The Threshold Walker

An Other (Liminal) character who takes Step Sideways at Tier I and Unsettling Truth at Tier II moves through thresholds and speaks in omens, their very presence unsettling the world around them.

Custom Talents

Work with your GM to create custom talents:

  • Base them on story events.
  • Use the cost tiers above (2–3 / 4–6 / 7–10 / 11+ XP).
  • Define clear prerequisites, activation, and limits.

Talent Respecification

With GM approval, you may retrain talents during downtime (one downtime period). Provide a narrative justification. Typically limited to once per major story arc.

Talent Selection Quick Reference

Minor (2–3 XP): small bonuses, once/scene tricks.

Major (4–6 XP): strong permanent upgrades, core path abilities.

Prestige (7–10 XP): campaign-shaping, breaks limits.

Epic (11+ XP): legendary, story-defining power.

Remember: passive talents stack; active talents require an action or reaction.
