Preface: On Rising from the Gutter

I was seven when I picked my first pocket — a merchant from Kahfagia, fat with spice money and arrogance. He never felt my fingers slip his purse. I watched him laugh with his friends while I bought bread for a week.

They never caught me. Not because I was clever — I wasn't, not yet. Because I was nothing. A gutter rat, a ghost in the crowd, a face that blends into wet cobblestones. That is the first lesson: power is not in the blade or the coin. It is in being forgotten.

Twenty years later, I own three warehouses, two gambling dens, and a brothel that also launders secrets. The Watch Captain owes me his promotion. The Dye Syndicate pays me not to undercut their prices. And the Archivolt notaries keep a drawer full of my forged seals — they do not know which ones are real.

I am Madam Serafine. They call me the Velvet Touch, because I never leave bruises.

This book is not for heroes. It is for those who understand that the world runs on whispers, debts, and the careful omission of the truth. I learned from the streets. I learned from Ikasha, who sleeps in shadow, and from Livaea, who dresses power in silk and smiles. I learned that a song can open a lock faster than any pick, and that a forged document is worth a thousand swords.

"The night I stole my first counting-house, I had nothing but a cold pick and a prayer. Livaea answered. She did not ask for my soul — not yet. She asked for a single night of my memory, a night I still cannot recall. I have been paying interest ever since. Every lock I pick, every secret I sell, every mark I ruin — a fraction of the principal. She is patient. She knows I will never be able to pay it all. That is the cruelty of her kindness. That is the velvet touch of her chain."

You want to be a thief? Good. But first, forget everything you think you know. The mark is not the victim. The mark is the one who believes they are safe. And the coin you take is never the one that costs you. It is the one you leave behind — the debt that collects.

— Madam Serafine, Silkstrand, under the Three-Queens Bridge

Part I — The Philosophy of the Quiet Hand

🌑 On Being Nothing

The first time you break into a counting-house, you will be afraid. Good — fear keeps your fingers light and your ears open. The moment you feel safe, you are already caught.

A thief is not a warrior. We do not fight. We slip — through a window left unlatched, through a crowd that parts without knowing, through a contract signed in too much haste. The world is full of gaps: the ninth step un-counted, the witness who looked away, the seal pressed a moment too soon.

The Three Principles of the Velvet Hand

The Coin That Is Not There

Never take what will be missed before dawn. A silver coin from a rich man's purse? He'll curse his servants and forget by noon. A ledger page that proves his fraud? He'll burn the city to find it. Take what they do not know they have until it is too late.

The Witness Who Looks Away

Every crime is witnessed. The trick is not to be invisible — it is to be unremarkable. A servant carrying linens. A clerk with a stack of writs. A beggar sleeping in the archway. The guards see you. They just do not notice.

The Debt That Pays Twice

When you steal, you create a debt — not yours, theirs. They owe you for the fear you planted, the reputation you damaged, the secret you now hold. A true thief does not take gold. Gold spends. A secret spends forever.

Part II — The Forger's Art

📜 The Weight of Paper

In Silkstrand, the right seal is worth more than a ship. A stamped manifest can move contraband past the harbor master. A notary's ribbon can turn stolen goods into legitimate auction lots. A letter of credit can buy a warehouse with no coin changing hands. Forgery is not crime. It is reality editing.

The Forging Mini-Game

To forge a document, you need three things:

  1. Knowledge of what the genuine document looks like — its paper, ink, seal, and language.
  2. A Sample — at least one authentic example to copy, or a witness who can describe it in detail.
  3. A Workshop — a quiet place with the right tools: quills, inks, sealing wax, a press, and a steady hand.

The Four Steps of Forgery

Step 1: Research Wits + Insight, DV 2-4

Learn the document's specifics. On a hit, gain +1 die to the Craft roll. On a miss, you miss a crucial detail — the forgery will be detectable.

Step 2: Materials Wits + Deception, DV 3

Source matching paper, ink, and seal. On a hit, the materials are adequate. On a partial, they're close but flawed (the forgery holds for one scene, then degrades). On a miss, you substitute inferior materials — the forgery starts with a Suspicious tag.

Step 3: The Craft Wits + Craft, DV 3 simple / 4 standard / 5 complex

Roll to create the forged document. On a hit, you produce a Forged Diamond. On a partial, produce a Fragile Diamond — it works once, then crumbles (detected). On a miss, the forgery is worthless; you may salvage materials for another attempt, but the GM gains 1 SB.

Step 4: Presentation Presence + Deception vs. examiner's DV

When you present the forgery, roll against the target's Insight or relevant skill. On a hit, the document is accepted. On a partial, they're suspicious — offer a concession (bribe, favor, or they keep the document "for verification"). On a miss, they detect the forgery; your Exposure ticks +1 and the document is seized.

Forged Diamond Types

  • Cargo Manifest — Legal immunity for smuggling. Lasts one voyage.
  • Notarial Seal — Validates a contract or deed. Lasts one arbitration.
  • Letter of Credit — Withdraw funds from a named merchant house. One use.
  • Writ of Passage — Skip tolls and inspections on a named road or river. One journey.
  • Identity Papers — Assume a false identity for one scene.
  • Pardon — Commutes a minor crime. Requires a public reading.

Failure and Consequences

When a forgery is detected, the GM may choose one:

  • Exposure — Your reputation among officials drops. Tick Exposure [4] for the city or faction.
  • Debt — The examiner does not report you — instead, they demand a favor.
  • Bounty — A warrant is quietly issued. A rival thief may claim it.
  • Counterfeit Trail — Your forged seal is used by someone else later, framing you.

🧺 Fence and Laundry

Once you have stolen goods, you need to sell them. The art of laundering is not hiding — it is recontextualizing.

Market Tiers

Different fences operate at different levels of legitimacy and scrutiny, affecting the difficulty of moving goods:

  • Street Market (DV 2) — Stolen goods sold openly; quick but risky (pawn shops, street vendors).
  • Guild Market (DV 3) — Hot cargo requiring some paperwork; moderate risk (guild merchants, legitimate fronts).
  • Court Market (DV 4) — Tracked items needing forged documents; lower risk but higher cost (nobles, officials).
  • Arcane Market (DV 5) — Warded or psionically significant items; highest risk and reward (rare, supernatural goods).

Partial results when fencing: price spike (Bank -1), Crew Heat +1, a quality flaw (-1 Effect on next use), or a seller's temporary String. On a complete miss: a sting spring (Sting [6]+2), counterfeit gear, or an owed favor to a rival fence.

Laundering Timers

To launder a stolen item or forged Diamond, start a Laundry Timer [4] (small items) or [6] (major items). Each downtime scene, work one tick by:

  • Bribing a fence (spend 1 Favor or 1 Boon)
  • Moving goods through a neutral market (risk Exposure +1)
  • Using a genuine paper trail (requires a Forged Diamond)

When the timer fills, the item is clean — sellable at full value or usable without suspicion. If you fail to complete the timer within three sessions, the item becomes Hot. Selling it then risks Exposure +2 and a visit from the original owner's agents.

Part III — The Cantor's Cadence

🎵 The Voice That Opens Doors

Singing is not for taverns alone. A well-timed tune can calm a crowd, distract a guard, or deliver a secret message in plain hearing. The Cantor's path is the thief's second skin — especially when the singer cares more for the lock than the lyric.

The Rogue Cantor: Song as Setup

Most Cantors perform for applause. A rogue Cantor performs for opportunity. While the audience watches the singer, their pockets are already empty.

Performance as Distraction

When you sing or play in a public space, roll Presence + Performance against a DV set by crowd size (2 for small, 4 for large). On a hit, you create an Engrossed Audience tag — for the duration of your song, any ally attempting Stealth or Subterfuge in the same area gains +1 die. On a partial, the crowd is attentive but some watch the exits — Stealth rolls are unchanged, but allies gain +1 Position to social rolls.

The Encore Gambit

Once per session, after a successful performance, declare an Encore. The crowd demands more. This extends your distraction for an additional scene, but the GM gains 1 SB — the guards are curious now.

Corrupted Cantors: The Cult of the Velvet Note

Not all Cantors walk the road alone. Some find patrons in the shadows — beings who grant power in exchange for a different kind of song.

Ikasha's Whisper (Shadow Cantor)

Ikasha speaks in the space between notes. Followers learn to sing in frequencies that do not carry, to hum a lullaby that makes guards sleep, to whistle a tune that unlocks spirit-locks.

Corruption: Your voice develops a second timbre — a whisper that follows your sung words. Strangers shiver when you speak. Allies sometimes hear you when you are not there.

Cradle Song (Low)
Lull a single target (Resist DV 3). Costs 1 Fatigue.
Lockpick's Refrain (Standard)
Unlock one mundane or warded lock by humming its true pitch. Costs 1 Obligation.

Livaea's Velvet Hook (Social Cantor)

Livaea is the patron of courtiers, concubines, escorts, and companions — all who trade in desire. She teaches that every song is a seduction, every glance a contract, every touch a negotiation. Her followers do not charm; they bind. A promise whispered in her name is a thread that tightens with every breath.

Corruption: Your eyes take on a reflective quality. In dim light, others see their own desires mirrored there. Your skin always feels faintly warm to the touch. Those who sleep near you dream of lost lovers.

Golden Tongue (Low)
Gain +2 dice to Sway for one social exchange. Costs 1 Fatigue; afterward, the target feels a mild attraction or obligation and may seek you out later.
The Velvet Invitation (Low)
Gain automatic entry to any private social function (masquerade, salon, pleasure house). The invitation appears in your pocket, signed by a name you can borrow for one night. Costs 1 Obligation.
The Unrefusable Offer (Standard)
Sing or whisper a bargain while touching the target (a hand on the arm, a brush of fingers). The target must accept the offer or suffer -2 dice to all rolls until they do. The offer must be something they could conceivably agree to (no suicide, no self-mutilation). Costs 2 Obligation; afterward the target remembers the moment as strangely intimate.
The Crimson Masquerade (High)
Assume the appearance of any person you have touched skin-to-skin, for one scene — your voice matches theirs perfectly. Costs 2 Fatigue and marks a Debt Timer [4] with Livaea.
Societal Impact

Livaea's cult is woven into every pleasure house, every high-class escort service, every discreet companionship agency in the Amaranthine. Her temples are not buildings; they are the back rooms of brothels, the upper floors of massage houses, the private salons of courtesans who buy and sell secrets along with flesh. A Livaean courtesan is as dangerous as any assassin — her weapons are a smile, a tear, a carefully timed yawn.

The Velvet Court has a standing agreement with the Livaean network: information flows both ways, and a thief who insults a Livaean courtesan finds every door closed and every guard alerted. Madam Serafine herself is said to be the Velvet Court's living bond to Livaea — a pact sealed in blood and silk, renewed each year with a secret she has never told anyone.

Malachai's False Note (Desperate Cantor)

Malachai answers when the thief has nothing left to lose. Her Cantors sing of easy scores, of curses as blessings, of the coin that spends itself. The price is always a piece of the singer's soul. She is the patron of gamblers, addicts, and those who have sold themselves one too many times.

Corruption: Your voice cracks on certain notes. Those who hear you feel a brief, inexplicable hunger. Your shadow seems thinner, hungrier. You find yourself counting coins you never had.

The Lucky Pick (Low)
Reroll a failed Stealth or Deception roll. Costs 1 Fatigue and 1 Corruption tick.
The Debt Note (Standard)
Sing a curse on a rival — they suffer -1 die on their next heist. Costs 2 Fatigue and marks a Debt Timer [4] that Malachai will collect. The collection is always painful and often ironic.
The Final Score (High)
Once per campaign, automatically succeed at any single heist roll — but immediately mark 2 Corruption and gain a permanent Marked by Malachai condition. The coin you take will never be enough.

Part IV — The Velvet Court (Expanded)

🕸️ The Court That Never Sleeps

The Velvet Court is not a building. It is a network — of whispers, of debts, of favors owed and collected. It has no single leader, though many claim the title. It has no charter, no treasury, no walls. It is the space between the legitimate and the condemned, and its members are the ones who know how to walk that line.

"I am Madam Serafine, and I hold the largest share of the Court's influence. But I did not build it alone. No one builds a shadow. You simply learn to stand where it falls."

The Hierarchy of Shadows

The Velvet Court is not a guild. There are no titles, no uniforms, no initiation rituals. Yet every thief in Silkstrand knows who to ask, who to fear, and who to pay. The Court's structure is a web of Creditors and Debtors. Reputation is currency. Favors are interest.

The Creditors (The Velvet Seats)

Six individuals hold the majority of the Court's influence, each controlling a different resource:

  1. Madam Serafine — Information, Forgery, Laundering
  2. Old Kes — Fence, Smuggling, Markets
  3. The Dockmaster — Movement, Ships, Hidden Warehouses
  4. Sister Agatha — Cover, Sanctuary, False Identities
  5. The Archivist — Legal Precedent, Forged Writs, Court Delays
  6. The Gray Eminence — Unknown; rumored to be a Veiled Sister or a Covenant judge

Each Creditor has a Favor Timer [6]. When you do a significant service for them, tick the timer. When it fills, they owe you a major favor (a heist's worth of help). When you owe them a debt, they may call it in at any time.

The Debtors (Everyone Else)

Every thief, fence, smuggler, and corrupt clerk in Silkstrand owes someone. The Court tracks debts through Strings, which can be:

  • Intelligence — a secret, a map, a guard schedule
  • Safe Passage — a route, a blind eye, a forgotten warrant
  • Muscle — a favor from a bruiser, a crew for a job
  • Coin — silver, goods, or a Forged Diamond

Strings are traded, sold, and called in. The Court's ledger is oral — written ink can be burned. A remembered debt is forever.

Faction Reputation: The Velvet Dial

Track the party's standing with the Velvet Court using a Velvet Dial from -3 (Enemy of the Court) to +3 (Creditor-in-Waiting). Adjust the dial when:

  • Complete a job for a Creditor (+1)
  • Pay off a major debt (+1)
  • Betray a fellow Court member (-1)
  • Get caught by the Watch and name names (-2)
  • Kill a Creditor (reset to -3, but gain a permanent enemy)
-3 to -2: Hunted

Guards are tipped off; fences refuse you; no safe house in Silkstrand.

-1 to +1: Neutral

Standard access to minor fences, but Creditors watch you warily.

+2 to +3: Trusted

Request a Favor from any Creditor once per arc; you may attend the Velvet Moot.

The Velvet Moot

Once per season, the Creditors meet at a secret location. At Velvet Dial +2 or higher, you may be invited. At the Moot, you may:

  • Bid on a Job — gain exclusive access to a high-value heist
  • Settle a Debt — pay off a major obligation with a rare item or secret
  • Propose a New Creditor — challenge a current Creditor for their seat (risky)
  • Learn Rumors — gain 1d3 Strings of intelligence about future events

🗄️ The Court's Assets (What the GM Can Offer)

When players work for the Velvet Court, they can earn access to:

  • The Blue Ledger — a record of every bribeable official in Silkstrand
  • The Maze of Whispers — a network of hidden passages under the city
  • The Ghost Crew — a team of urchin spies, all Cap 1, good for reconnaissance
  • The Scribe's Seal — a notary who will stamp anything for a price
  • The Unstable Bridge — a safe house that can be collapsed to cover an escape

Velvet Heat Dial

The Court tracks two timers that measure active attention from authorities and rivals:

  • Watch Interest [6] (Crew Heat) — fills when jobs go loud or evidence is left.
  • Syndicate Pressure [6] (City Heat) — fills when rivals or the Exchange take notice.
Watch Interest [6]
  • 0-2: Background hum.
  • 3: Patrol Sweep [4] begins.
  • 4-5: Random stops; Notoriety +1 on flashy moves.
  • 6: Sting [6] + Exposure +1.
Syndicate Pressure [6]
  • 0-2: Routine.
  • 3: Curfew murmurs, stop and search; Patrol Sweep [4] starts in hot districts.
  • 6: Crackdown [4] (DV +1 on criminal actions).

Informant Web Mechanics

The party can cultivate 3-5 informants (dock porter, lamplighter, clerk, etc.). Each has Reliability [4] (ticks down on 1s). Pay their Price (a favor, a coin, a secret) for a Clue or DV -1 on one roll. Two informants on the same topic: +1 Effect, and only the lowest Reliability ticks down.

Running a Web
  • Access — what information the informant can provide.
  • Reliability [4] — tracks trustworthiness; decreases when the informant is stressed or compromised (a roll of 1 when used).
  • Price — cost to gain information (favor, coin, secret).
  • Flags — special traits (e.g., "Connected to Watch," "Lives in Dye District").
Using Informants

Pay the Price to gain a Clue (+1 die on an investigation roll), a Small String (a minor favor or piece of information), or DV -1 on one roll within the informant's sphere of influence.

Informant Complications

When an informant's Reliability reaches 0, they flip allegiance or vanish; start a Burn [4] timer to recover or replace them. The GM may spend SB to create immediate problems.

Cross-Check Advantage & Masking Source

Spend 2 informants on the same topic for +1 Effect on related rolls (only the lowest Reliability decreases on a roll of 1). Spend 1 Bank to prevent a Reliability -1 once when using informants.

⏱️ Court-Approved Heists (The Heist Timer)

When the players accept a job from the Court, the GM sets up a Heist Timer [6] (simple) to [10] (complex) to track overall progress toward the objective. Additionally, the GM may establish Threat Timers to track escalating danger during the heist.

The Velvet Heist Planner

Before a job, each player describes one preparation (casing, bribing, stashing gear). Each strong success on a Setup roll grants +1 Position or -1 DV later. Each player starts with 2 Kit Tokens (lockpicks, dark lantern, false papers) that can be spent during the heist for:

  • +1 die on a roll
  • Improve Position by one step
  • Tick the Heist Timer forward by one segment
  • Pause a Threat Timer (prevent it from advancing for one tick)

After use, the token is consumed.

The Heist Timer

The Heist Timer advances when players complete a phase of the heist (casing, infiltration, acquisition, escape), acquire a key asset (a map, a key, a guard schedule), or eliminate a major obstacle (bribe a captain, disable an alarm).

It ticks backward when a guard raises an alarm, a rival thief interferes, or the party is forced to leave evidence.

When the timer fills, the heist is complete — the players gain the loot plus 1 Favor with the Creditor who hired them. The GM may also award a Court Favor (a one-use String tradable for help, information, or a safe house). If the timer empties (reaches 0), the heist fails — the party must escape, may lose a member, and gains a Debt owed to the Creditor, to be called in later.

Threat Timers

The GM sets up Threat Timers to track specific dangers:

  • Alarm [4] — Security response time and alert level.
  • Security [4] — Methodical search patterns and guard rotations.
  • Inside Trouble [4] — Unexpected problems from within the target location.

When a Threat Timer fills, the corresponding complication occurs (e.g., Alarm filling means guards are fully alerted and responding).

🌒 Ikasha — She Who Sleeps in Shadow

Lore

Ikasha is the hush between footfalls, the patience of dark water, the black-feathered watcher at every threshold. She does not command. She sleeps. But in her dreams, she whispers possibilities. Thieves who follow Ikasha learn to move unseen, to speak unremembered, to become shadows that slip between what is and what could be.

Advantages for Rogues

  • The Unobserved — When you are Hidden, Social SB cannot be used to frame you (unless you act first).

Drawbacks

  • Light Sensitivity — In bright conditions (direct sun, magical illumination), your Stealth rolls suffer -1 die.

🌹 Livaea — The Crimson Courtier

Lore

Livaea is the whisper behind the curtain, the smile that sells secrets, the velvet hand that closes a cage. She rules through allure, performance, and the hunger for connection. Her domain encompasses not just courtiers, but escorts, concubines, companions, and all who trade in intimacy as currency.

Thieves who follow Livaea do not steal coins — they steal trust. A stolen kiss, a forged letter, a lover's secret sold to the highest bidder. Livaea's temples are the pleasure houses of Silkstrand, the massage parlors of Zakov, the discreet companionship agencies of the Brass Coast. Her priests are courtesans who know that a whispered secret is worth more than a night's fee, and that a carefully cultivated attachment can move armies.

Advantages for Rogues

  • Courtly Graces — +1 die to Sway and Performance in any refined setting.
  • The Velvet Glove — Once per session, declare a minor NPC is your secret admirer or blackmail victim. They will help you once.
  • Unreadable Mask — Spend 1 Boon to ignore one Social SB spend against you (the scandal simply fails to stick).
  • Favoured Companion — A standing introduction to any pleasure house, escort agency, or discreet social club in the Amaranthine. The staff won't betray you unless the price is overwhelming.

Drawbacks

  • The Jealous Court — Using Livaea's power publicly marks a Rival Timer [4]. When it fills, a former paramour or jilted client becomes a recurring enemy.
  • Addicted to Adoration — Go more than a session without a notable social triumph, and you suffer -1 die to all social rolls until you perform a grand gesture (a public seduction, a scandalous revelation, a purchased favor).
  • The Velvet Price — Livaea expects her followers to keep the oldest profession thriving. Refusing to trade in intimacy (even as a broker, not a direct participant) may cause her to withdraw her favor.

🃏 Lucky Jack — The Lord of Thieves (Luck & The Heist)

Lore

Lucky Jack was once a mortal — a thief so skilled that even the gods took notice. He stole a coin from the Hollow's own pocket, and the Hollow laughed. "You are clever," it said. "But cleverness is a debt. One day, I will collect." Jack has been collecting luck ever since.

He is the Unspent Coin, the Magpie King, the patron of urchins, beggars, and thieves who plan their work and work their plan. His followers are not murderers — they are professionals. They know the greatest heist is the one that leaves no trace, no victims, and no debts. Yet every stroke of luck is a loan, and Jack's interest rates are legendary. When he comes to collect, you will wish you had never met him.

Among the peoples of Fate's Edge, Lucky Jack is known by many names. In Silkstrand, he is the Cut Purse's Blessing, whispered by pickpockets who know a moment's distraction is worth more than a year's labor. In Vhasia, he is the Gorget-Breaker, patron of disgraced knights turned thieves. In Zakov, he is the Tide-That-Eats-The-Shore, a slow, patient consumption that reshapes coastlines. In the Brass Coast, he is the Freedman's Luck, invoked by escaped slaves who steal to survive. Wherever a lock is picked, a purse is cut, or a plan comes together at the last possible second, Lucky Jack is already there — tipping his cap and counting the interest.

"Luck is not a gift. It is a loan. Pay it forward, or pay it dearly. The coin that never spends is the debt that never dies."

Advantages for Rogues

  • The Lucky Coin — Once per session, reroll one die that came up 1; you must keep the new result. If the reroll is also a 1, the GM gains 2 SB instead of 1.
  • The Clever Hand — On a Deception, Craft, or Sleight of Hand roll, spend 1 Boon for +2 dice. If you fail, the GM gains 1 SB.
  • The Quick Exit — When caught or cornered, reroll one failed escape roll. If you succeed, the GM gains 1 SB — your escape was too close.
  • The Magpie's Eye — Once per scene, ask the GM one yes/no question about a target's weakness, habit, or hidden valuables. The GM must answer truthfully.

Drawbacks

  • The Unpayable Debt — Every use of Jack's luck marks a Debt Timer [4]. When it fills, Jack demands a service: a heist, a confession, or a coin given to a beggar who does not deserve it.
  • Compulsive Gambler — You must take a risk when one is presented; -1 die to refuse a bet or a tempting opportunity.
  • The Hollow's Coin — If you ever accumulate three unfulfilled Debts, the Hollow takes notice. You gain the Marked by Jack condition: -1 die to all luck-based rolls until you settle with him.

Rites for the Rogue Cantor

Gain +2 dice to all rolls for the remainder of the session. Jack will demand repayment before the next session — complete a heist of at least 6 segments, or lose all accumulated Boons. Costs 2 Obligation.

Demand one service from a creature who has accepted your help in the past. They must comply or gain a permanent Condition (Marked by Jack) imposing -1 die on all luck-based rolls until they comply. Costs 3 Obligation.
The Cult of the Unlucky Coin

Lucky Jack's cults gather where thieves naturally congregate: taverns, rooftops, the back rooms of pawn shops, and the spaces beneath bridges where urchins sleep. The Unlucky Coin is not a secret society — it is a fraternity of professionals. In one ritual, each member places a single coin in a central pot, then tells a story of a heist that went wrong. The best story wins the pot; the loser buys drinks for the circle. The coins are melted down and reforged into a single Unlucky Coin, passed to the season's most deserving thief. Their creed is stamped on every coin: "Luck is not a gift. It is a loan. Pay it forward, or pay it dearly." Outsiders may listen, but must speak one truth about a crime they've committed — or leave before the stories begin.

🐎 Aveh — The Rider Behind the Storm (For Fugitives)

Lore

Aveh is the faceless rider at the horizon's edge. She offers freedom at the cost of belonging. Thieves who need to vanish — from a bad debt, a murder charge, a betrayed partner — pray to Aveh.

Advantages

Adventure Seed: The Unspent Coin

Madam Serafine has lost something she cannot afford to lose: a single unmarked coin she has carried since her first heist. The coin is the interest on her pact with Livaea — as long as she holds it, the debt is manageable. Without it, Livaea can call in the principal at any time.

The coin was stolen by a rival thief who does not know its true value — a follower of Lucky Jack who believes it's just a lucky charm, and is using it to win at cards in the Dye District.

Serafine cannot retrieve it herself — that would be a sign of weakness. She hires the party to find the thief, recover the coin, and return it to her without anyone knowing she sent them. The party must decide: return the coin and earn Serafine's eternal gratitude, or keep it and gain a powerful bargaining chip against the Velvet Court's most dangerous Creditor.

"The coin is not silver. It is not gold. It is a piece of my soul, minted the night I made my deal. If you keep it, you will have power over me. If you return it, you will have my loyalty. Choose carefully. Both are debts that can never be fully paid."

💋 The Silk House — A Livaean Brothel Asset

The Velvet Altar

In the back rooms of Silkstrand's finest pleasure houses, behind curtains dyed with madder and saffron, small shrines to Livaea stand in alcoves — a candle, a mirror, a single silk ribbon tied in a lover's knot. Livaea does not demand celibacy or shame. She blesses the transaction: the honest exchange of comfort for coin, of secrecy for safety, of power for pleasure.

A brothel dedicated to Livaea is not merely a business. It is a threshold — a place where masks are laid aside, where the powerful become vulnerable, where a whisper in the dark can ruin a dynasty. The courtesans are priestesses. The Madame is her high priestess. Every client who crosses the threshold leaves behind a piece of themselves — a secret, a preference, a name that should not have been spoken.

The Velvet Court has long maintained an understanding with Livaea's faithful. Information flows both ways. A thief who insults a Livaean courtesan finds every door closed. A patron who pays generously may find a lock unpicked by morning.

Running a Velvet Racket

When the party controls a territory (e.g., the Dye District docks, the Three-Queens Bridge approach), the GM notes this as a Turf Node. Each node has a Racket (Fencing Operation, Smuggling Cut, Protection Circle, or Information Stall). Holding turf requires a Hold roll (Wits+Insight vs DV 2-4) each session.

Example Turf Node: Dye District Docks — Racket: Fencing Operation

Hit: Bank +1 or local goodwill (reduce DV by 1 on one social roll made in this territory).
Miss: War [8] timer starts with 1 segment filled (rivals begin contesting control).

The Silk House as an Asset (Major Asset, 12 XP)

A Silk House is a Major Asset (12 XP) that provides off-screen leverage through blackmail, rumor, and social access. It cannot be purchased quickly; establishment requires one month of in-game time to recruit staff, secure premises, and build a clientele (or take over an existing establishment).

  • Cost: 12 XP
  • Establishment: 1 month (or as story permits)
  • Upkeep: Pay 12 XP per downtime, or devote a full scene to managing it (settling disputes, bribing the Watch, maintaining cover).
  • Capacity (Optional): Counts as one Asset against your Spirit limit.

Free Use (Once per Session, Off-Screen)

Choose one benefit at the start of a scene. The effect is narrative, not rolled.

  • Blackmail File — Compromising information on a named minor NPC. Once, improve Position by one step on a social action against them, or demand a minor favor. The file is consumed after use; a downtime scene is needed to gather new material (part of upkeep).
  • Rumor Mill — Before a city scene, state a plausible rumor about a person, place, or upcoming event. The GM must incorporate it as true (though possibly incomplete or misleading).
  • Soft Influence — Skip one social gatekeeping roll (gaining an audience, obtaining an invitation) by stating a Silk House client made the introduction.
  • Safe Harbour — A fugitive or witness can hide in the Silk House for one scene without risk of discovery (unless searchers explicitly search brothels).

Scene Surge (Spend 1 Boon, On-Screen)

  • The Velvet Curtain — A courtesan distracts a guard or mark at a critical moment; the next Stealth or Deception roll gains +1 die.
  • A Whisper in the Right Ear — An influential client intervenes on your behalf; reduce DV by 1 on a single Petition or Broker roll.
  • The Back Stair — Escape through a hidden passage known only to the house; convert a pursuit consequence into a temporary complication.
  • A Taste of Honey — A courtesan loosens a target's tongue; the next Sway roll against that target treats Position as Dominant.

You may spend 2 XP to trigger the Free Use benefit an additional time in a session.

The Silk House is a living organization with its own will, debts, and demands. The Madame (a named NPC, Tier II or III) acts as a terrestrial patron. Characters who draw deeper on the House's resources may swear an oath to Livaea (or to the Madame) and incur Obligation.
