Tactical Play

How to use this book

This is an optional grid overlay for the core combat procedure. Read The Grid, Movement, and Zones of Control first. The Miniatures Options section adds footprints and facing; it follows the Position and Effect rules above where the older Miniatures Guide used different bonuses or movement values. The grid does not add hit points or replace core resolution.

Rules compatibility

The core rules comprise the SRD plus the extensions in the Player’s Guide and GM Guide. This expansion applies only its stated additions and exceptions. Keep the core loop: declare intent and approach, set Position and DV, roll Attribute + Skill, count successes and Story Beats, resolve the outcome, and apply consequences.

This module adds optional grid-based procedures for movement, reach, cover, Zones of Control, flanking, and facing. It uses the core resolution loop unchanged: the grid helps establish fictional position and effect but does not replace a roll, create hit points, or add a separate damage track.

What this module does

  • Adds a grid (square or hex, 5 ft cells) to measure distance, line of sight, and reach.

  • Introduces movement rates, terrain costs, zones of control (ZoC), and flanking – all converted directly into the existing Position ladder.

  • All actions resolved with standard Attribute + Skill pools, Story Beats (SB), Boons, and Position/Effect outcomes.

  • No HP, no new damage tracks. Harm and Fatigue work exactly as in the core rules.

  • Completely optional – use for one encounter, a whole campaign, or never.

Core Principles

  1. Narrative first. The grid is a storytelling tool. Every move must be described in fiction.

  2. No mechanics changes. Dice pools, Story Beats (SB), Boons, Position and Effect work as written.

  3. Predictable tactics. Cover, flanking, reach and facing give players concrete ways to gain Position before rolling.

The Grid

Scale

  • 1 square (or hex) = 5 ft. Square or hex – pick before session.

  • Most characters occupy one cell. Large creatures (e.g. horse) occupy 2×2.

Range Bands (grid version)

Close Adjacent cells (melee)
Near Up to 6 cells (≈30 ft)
Far 7–12 cells (≈35–60 ft)
Absent Beyond 12 cells or off-screen

Core range bands still apply; the grid just gives precise counts.

Movement

Movement Rate

  • Move action: Body + 3 cells. (Example: Body 3 →\rightarrow 6 cells)

  • Dash: Use your action to double movement. After dashing, your next defense is at Desperate.

  • Difficult terrain: 2 movement points per cell (mud, rubble, shallow water).

  • Elevation: Moving up costs +1 movement point per level; moving down is free.

Zones of Control (ZoC)

  • ZoC = all orthogonal (or all six) adjacent cells.

  • Entering enemy ZoC ends your movement and places you in melee (Controlled with that enemy).

  • Leaving ZoC requires a Disengage test: DV 4 (plus any modifiers). Failure leaves you Desperate for your next action.

  • Stacked ZoC: +1 to Disengage DV per extra enemy.

  • Pushing Through ZoC: To move through an enemy’s Zone of Control without ending movement, make a Skill test (Athletics or Acrobatics) vs DV 4+(N−1)4 + (N-1), where NN is the number of enemies exerting ZoC in the target path. On success, you may continue your movement through the ZoC. On failure, movement ends as normal (entering ZoC places you in melee). Regardless of outcome, pushing through incurs one cost:

    • Gain 1 Fatigue,

    • or GM gains 1 Story Beats (SB),

    • or your next action suffers Risky (if currently Controlled) or Desperate (if currently Risky).

    (Higher skill ranks improve success chance—veterans thread through chaos where novices falter.)

Cover & Position

Partial cover (low wall) Defender gains Controlled against ranged attacks only.
Full cover (solid wall) Defender gains Controlled against all attacks; attacker gains Dominant when defending against that target.

Cover can be destroyed (GM spends Story Beats (SB)).

Flanking & Rear Attacks

  • Flanking: Two allies on opposite sides of a target (orthogonal or hex). Next attacker gains Dominant for that attack.

  • Rear attack: Attacker directly behind target. Gain Dominant and upgrade Standard to Great for that strike.

Weapon Tags (Grid interactions)

Tag Grid Effect
[Reach] Can attack up to 2 cells away (Near). Enemies without Reach suffer –1 Position when trying to close.
[Fast] After a successful melee hit, shift 1 cell (no ZoC trigger).
[Set] Plant weapon as an action. Any enemy that charges into that cell (≥4\ge4 cells straight) triggers an automatic attack at +1 Effect.
[Brutal] On a hit, push target 1 cell orthogonally. Target’s next defensive Position drops one step.

Action Economy (Tactical)

Each turn:

  1. One Action – attack, cast, use skill, activate talent, etc.

  2. One Move – up to movement rate (can be taken before, after, or split around the action).

Exceptions: Dashing uses the Action, no separate attack that turn (unless a talent gives a free attack).

Position Sources – Quick Lookup

Rear attack (attacker) Dominant + Great
Dashing (defender next) Desperate

All other Position assignments follow the core rules (terrain, surprise, etc.).

Line of Sight & Concealment

  • Line of sight blocked by solid obstacles (walls, large creatures, dense forest). GM may call for Notice/Stealth if partially concealed.

  • Concealment (fog, smoke, darkness) gives the defender Controlled against ranged attacks. Melee unaffected unless attacker must enter the concealed area.

Example Turn

  1. Harper (Body 4, Melee 5) stands 5 cells from a guard behind a low wall (partial cover).

  2. Harper dashes 12 cells, ending adjacent (enters guard’s ZoC). Guard now Controlled, Harper Dominant for his attack.

  3. Valeros is already on the opposite side – flanking active.

  4. Harper attacks at Dominant (flanking bonus). Rolls Body + Melee = 9 dice.

  5. 6 successes vs DV 3 →\rightarrow clean hit. Harper uses [Brutal] to push the guard 1 cell away, out of cover.

  6. Guard’s next defense is at Desperate (push effect).

  7. Valeros attacks the now coverless, Desperate guard with [Fast] dagger, shifts 1 cell after the hit.

Dice, Boons, and Outcome matrix untouched; only Position derived from the grid.

GM Guidance

  1. Draw the grid early – dry-erase board or VTT.

  2. Read Position first: “What is the attacker’s Position? What can the defender do to change it?”

  3. Spend Story Beats (SB) on dramatic consequences (cover collapse, reinforcements, etc.).

  4. Use Boons to upgrade Position – a powerful tactical choice.

Miniatures Options

These options add miniature footprints, facing, and a few table aids to the Tactical Combat rules. Use the movement, Zone of Control, flanking, and Position/Effect rules in the preceding sections. Where the earlier Miniatures Guide differs, this combined reference uses the Tactical Combat rules as the shared baseline.

Base Sizes

  • Small: one cell.

  • Medium: two cells by one cell.

  • Large: two cells by two cells.

  • Huge: three cells by three cells.

Large and Huge creatures project their Zone of Control from each edge cell of their base. A miniature’s footprint changes the cells it occupies; it does not change its movement rate.

Facing

Choose a facing after a Move. On a square grid, the front arc extends three cells ahead, the side arcs cover the two flanks, and the rear arc extends three cells behind. On a hex grid, the front arc extends three hexes, the side arcs cover the two lateral directions, and the rear arc extends one hex behind. Use these arcs to determine whether the existing flank or rear Position/Effect benefit applies; facing does not add a separate die bonus.

Optional Actions

Shove.

On a successful action, move the target one cell if the fiction and its size permit. A target entering an enemy Zone of Control stops there; a shove does not bypass a Zone of Control.

Brace.

Until your next turn, gain Controlled Position when resisting a shove. This does not extend your Zone of Control.

Take Cover.

Use a Move to reach suitable terrain. The GM sets Position against ranged attacks using the cover rules above.

Optional Boon Maneuvers

  • Spend one Boon to disengage automatically.

  • Spend two Boons for a Heroic Rush: move up to your normal movement rate through enemy Zones of Control, then end Engaged with one enemy in reach.

These are optional tactical spends. They do not alter the core Boon economy or grant extra actions.

Magic on the Grid

Apply a spell’s existing range, cost, and outcome first; these notes describe how its effect occupies the grid.

  • [WARD] effects can block passage when the spell says they do; resolve a crossing with the stated test and cost.

  • [CONJURE] effects occupy their stated cells and may block line of sight or movement if the spell says so.

  • [TRANSPORT] effects require a legal destination under the spell’s range and Zone of Control rules.

  • [PASSAGE] effects identify a route or opening; apply only the Position or Effect the spell explicitly grants.

  • [AREA] effects use the spell’s stated radius and target rules; check friendly and enemy cells before resolving.
