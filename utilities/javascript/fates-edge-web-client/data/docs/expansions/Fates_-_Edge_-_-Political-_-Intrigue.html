Political Intrigue

Political Intrigue Campaign Framework
for Fate’s Edge — Version 0.3 (Expanded)

Rules compatibility

The core rules comprise the SRD plus the extensions in the Player’s Guide and GM Guide. This expansion applies only its stated additions and exceptions. Keep the core loop: declare intent and approach, set Position and DV, roll Attribute + Skill, count successes and Story Beats, resolve the outcome, and apply consequences.

Adds faction, court and conspiracy procedures with their influence timers. Anything it introduces is an addition to the core loop, not a replacement for it: a named track or timer here is its own measure and never stands in for Fatigue, Harm or the Outcome Matrix, and no option waives the roll, the Story Beats it generates, or the stated cost unless it says so explicitly.

Design Goals

  • Why: Enable long-form play focused on courts, city-states, guilds, and synods where choices echo across seasons.

  • Mechanical Need: Formalize social conflict and faction play using existing engines (Position, DV, SB, timers).

  • Narrative Richness: Turn every roll into a living thread—alliances, scandals, mandates, and crises.

  • Player Appeal: Provide levers (Favor, Leverage, Reputation, Strings) without heavy bookkeeping.

Principle: This module adds procedures, not new math. Use core rules for rolls; this tells you what to prep, track, and tick.

Quickstart (2 minutes)

  1. Pick a Seat of Power (city, court, guildhall, synod).

  2. Create 3–5 Faction Sheets. Give each faction an Influence timer [6], an Exposure timer [6], and a Stability timer [6–8].

  3. Place Campaign Dials: Mandate and Crisis at 2 (0–6 each) unless fiction says otherwise.

  4. For each session, declare a Situation Timer [4–8] for the immediate political question.

  5. Play scenes. Any 1s generate SB; the GM uses Social SB options.

  6. Between sessions, run the Intrigue Cycle to advance timers off-screen and seed the next scandal.

Political Intrigue – The War of Laws and Lances

The throne is empty. The cantons sharpen their quills. The condottieri sharpen their swords. And the Everflame burns both.

— — Dusana of the Raven Road, on the Belworth crisis

This expansion provides procedures for long-form political conflict in the fractured realms of Vhasia, Viterra, and the contested marches. Here, a duel of advocates can decide what a chevauchée cannot; a printed broadsheet can ruin a bloodline faster than a cannon; and a loan from a coin-house may buy a crown—or sell it.

The Three Pressures

Three forces grind against one another in every court, every hedge, every mercenary camp:

The Chivalric Remnant

– Knights who still speak of honor, yet fight for ransom. Jousts are theater; the tilt is a negotiation. The old codes buckle under the weight of gunpowder and gold.

The Hedge-Law Ascendancy

– Magistrates, advocates, and clerks who believe that a well-written charter is worth a company of lances. Parlements multiply; precedents are printed; the very soil is argued over by the rod.

The Condotta Engine

– Mercenary captains who sell steel to the highest bidder, and the coin-houses that bankroll them. Their loyalty is a season long; their ledger is written in blood and interest.

None of these forces can win alone. A knight needs a lawyer to seal the treaty. A magistrate needs a captain to enforce the writ. A condottiere needs a coin-house to pay the muster. Their friction is your lever.

What This Expansion Adds

  • Faction Sheets with Influence, Stability, and Exposure timers.

  • Intrigue Actions (Petition, Bribe, Blackmail, Expose, Shield) and a Social SB menu.

  • The Parlement Mini-Game – a three-beat legal duel for courts and councils.

  • The Round of Five Virtues – a joust scoring system that turns combat into moral theater.

  • Campaign Dials (Mandate and Crisis) that track your party’s public legitimacy and the opposition’s growing strength.

  • The Intrigue Cycle – off-screen faction moves between sessions.

A Mantle for the Table

When the game turns from dungeons to duchies, repeat this truth:

“The lance is judged, but the ledger is weighed. Raise your banner, but keep your seal wet. Winter is coming—and the law does not freeze.”

The following pages give you the tools to play that winter.

Faction Play: Components & Templates

Faction Sheet (Template)

[FACTION NAME]
Tier: (street / guild / city / crown / synod)
Aim: (what they want this season)
Stance toward PCs: (Allied / Wary / Hostile)
Levers (3): (money, law, clergy, blades, secrets, ships, votes, rites… )
Leader & Lieutenants: (names + one sentence intent)
Reputation Tags (2): (Merciful, Ruthless, Old-Blood, Reformers, Ascetic… )
Timers:
- Influence [6]: how aligned they are with PC aims; fills to grant access/patronage
- Stability [6--8]: how coherent the faction remains; fills  $→$  schism/coup/purge
- Exposure [6]: public risk & scandal; fills  $→$  scandal event triggers
Holdings/Assets: (2--5 named assets with a sentence of function)
Strings: (1--3 hooks tying them to locales, laws, rites, or other factions)
When to tick (with examples)

  • Influence: Tick when PCs advance or protect the faction’s stated Aim or humiliate an active rival of that Aim. (e.g., secure a river charter the Harbor Guild wants = +1; discredit the Tide-Chart Office opposing them = +1.) Routine gifts without movement on the Aim do not tick.

  • Stability: Tick when internal strains surface beyond normal bickering: succession wobbles, ideological schism, budget shortfalls, a key lieutenant defects, or magical overreach taxes rites/logistics. (e.g., failed purge = +1; whispered heresy in the Ember-Temple = +1.)

  • Exposure: Tick on visible missteps: public concessions/humiliations, failed high-profile actions (especially with Social SB spends), leaks of leverage, or collateral harm to bystanders. (e.g., bribery discovered = +2; foiled blackmail exposed = +1.)

Strings — What They Do

A String is a concrete tie: “Controls river tolls,” “Holds three Synod votes,” “Custodian of the Lantern Rites,” “Patron of the Playhouse.”

  • Acting with/through a relevant String: improve Position +1 or treat DV as –1 (once per scene).

  • Acting against a target’s String: Position –1 or GM banks +1 SB.

  • Cutting or seizing a String is a 4–6 segment timer; on fill, move the String to the victor or strike it.

Relationship Map (Strings & Stakes)

Sketch the web. For every arrow between two factions, write a stake (e.g., “river tolls,” “chapterhouse votes,” “forbidden codex”). Mark two arrows volatile; these will pop first under pressure.

Volatility Triggers

  • When either linked faction’s Exposure fills, immediately trigger the volatile stake as an on-screen complication or new Situation timer.

  • When Crisis rises by 1, the GM may trigger one volatile arrow anywhere on the map.

  • A targeted Social SB spend may flip a volatile stake early (GM choice; announce loudly).

Map-to-Action Hooks

  • Acting along an arrow where you have positive Strings on either node: +1 Position or DV –1.

  • Acting against a node with negative entanglement (Hostile stance or disgraced tag): –1 Position or GM banks +1 SB.

  • These are caps; do not chain multiple arrows for stacking.

Campaign Dials

Mandate (0–6)

Table’s public legitimacy and door-opening power.

Crisis (0–6)

Pressure from rivals, costs, and backlash. Set both to 2 at start unless fiction says otherwise.

Mechanical Hooks

Mandate

  • 0–1: Cold reception. Public venues start Desperate unless you bring Favors/Strings.

  • 2–3: Recognized. Once per social scene, treat Position as one step higher when invoking a relevant Favor or String.

  • 4–5: Credible power. Once per session, cancel one Social SB spend targeting you in public venues; public institutions set DV –1 versus your petitions.

  • 6: Standard-bearers. Start public scenes Dominant unless fiction strongly opposes; your endorsements create a 4-timer Bandwagon on targets.

Crisis

  • 0–1: Calm waters.

  • 2–3: Tension. At the start of each session, GM banks +1 SB for political scenes.

  • 4–5: Boiling. First failed social action each session ticks party Exposure +1.

  • 6: Breaking point. Start political scenes with a Counter-Timer [4] in play.

Scene Procedures for Social Conflict

Social Conflict at a Glance

  1. Intent → Position/DV → Roll → Outcome → Ripple.

  2. Set Dominant/Controlled/Desperate and DV from fiction. Any 1s generate SB in social scenes.

Intrigue Actions (examples)

Each uses the table’s attribute/skill combination. Suggested positions/DVs and on-hit effects below.

  • Petition (Presence+Sway): Present a case to a decision-maker. Dominant with writs/favors; Desperate when hostile. DV 2–4. Hit: fill Situation; strong hit: tick ally Influence.

  • Bribe (Wits+Deception/Lore): Move resources to open doors. Dominant; worsens if watched/holy ground. DV 2–3. Hit: create a temporary tag (Greased Palms); miss: tick Exposure.

  • Blackmail (Wits+Insight): Apply leverage. Dominant → Desperate if unverified. DV 3–5. Hit: concession now or place a 4-timer Compromise usable later for Position Dominant.

  • Broker (Presence+Diplomacy): Reconcile two parties. Dominant on neutral ground; Dominant with ritual hospitality; Desperate if blades present. DV 3–4. Hit: reduce both parties’ Exposure by 1; fill Influence for the favored side.

  • Expose (Presence+Performance): Publicly reveal truth/forgery. Dominant with corroboration; Desperate with hearsay. DV 3–5. Hit: add Disgraced tag; fill 2–3 segments on target Exposure.

  • Shield (Wits+Insight/Lore): Pre-empt rival plays via law/protocol/counter-messaging. Dominant; Dominant with counsel/rites. DV 3. Hit: reduce incoming Social SB by 1 this scene; mark ally Stability −1 (cohere under pressure).

  • Host Feast/Vigil (Presence+Performance/Rite): Gather elites; set tone. Dominant if traditions observed; Desperate if taboo. DV 2–4. Hit: create Audience tags; if triumph, Mandate +1.

  • Infiltrate (Body/Wits+Stealth/Deception): Plant evidence, steal ledgers, ghost a meeting. Desperate when warded/sanctified. DV 3–5. Hit: gain Leverage (1) or fill Situation; on partials, start a Security or Rumor counter-timer.

Social SB Options

When 1s appear, GM spends SB to:

  • Trigger faux pas; spread rumor; demand concessions; rival interjects; gallery turns; patronage flips.

  • Currency hits: lose 1 Favor with an implicated institution; a used Leverage becomes public (tick either party’s Exposure +1); shift an Audience/Institution tag against the PCs.

Resolution

A scene resolves when the Situation Timer fills or fiction dictates. Then ripple:

  • Advance/reduce involved Faction Timers (Influence/Stability/Exposure).

  • Adjust Mandate/Crisis (usually ±1 each based on victory and visible costs).

  • Record lasting Audience/Institution Tags.

Situation vs. Faction Timers — Default Ripple

  • Success (PC-favoring): Allied Influence +1 (or +2 if it advances their Aim); if win was clean, allied Exposure –1; hostile Exposure +1.

  • Failure (PC-hurting): Opposing Influence +1 (or +2 if on-Aim); allied Exposure +1. If failure was internal, also tick that faction’s Stability +1.

  • Compromise: Each side Influence +1 or both Exposure +1, guided by fiction.

The Intrigue Cycle (Between Sessions)

Use once per session (or per in-fiction week/month on jumps).

Step A — Update World State

For each active faction, advance Stability or Exposure by 1 if they suffered public pressure or internal strain. Tick unresolved thread timers by 1 or start a 4–6 timer.

Step B — Draw/Prompt for Events

Method: Draw blindly from a standard deck or deliberately pick suits matching themes. Use Suit for type (Hearts=court/crowd; Spades=violence; Clubs=resources/strikes; Diamonds=environment/omens) and Rank for severity (low=+1, mid=+2, face/ace=+3 segments).
Apply: Place each card on a faction/timer and immediately tick by severity; write one sentence of fallout that must surface next session.
Examples: Hearts 7 → public oath/romance, tick Exposure +2. Clubs 10 → trade shock/strike, start a 6-timer Shortages.

Step C — Off-Screen Actions

Each PC directs one off-screen move using a follower/asset/network. Resolve with one roll at Significant Time scale; on 1s, bank SB and/or tick Exposure.

Step D — Recompute Dials

If wins were visible and valued, Mandate +1. If costs, scandals, or riots mounted, Crisis +1. Both may move.

Step E — Prep Next Situation

Name the next immediate political question. Put a 4–8 timer on it. Grab 2–3 complications tied to factions and prior tags.

Currencies: Favor, Leverage, Exposure, Reputation

Favor (Table Currency)

Earn a Favor when you deliver visible benefit or save a leader’s face. Spend a Favor to:

  • Improve Position by one step against that person/institution.

  • Cancel one Social SB spend aimed at you in their venue.

  • Access a restricted office/archive/sanctum once.

Favors are narrow; specify scope (“Harbormaster’s Office,” not “All of Kahfagia”). Three unspent favors from the same seat convert to Standing (Tag) with them.

Leverage (Portable)

  • One Leverage (1) forces a concession (1 segment on a Situation Timer ) or holds Position at Dominant when it would drop to Desperate.

  • Decay: Only public uses count. After two public uses of the same leverage, convert it to Exposure +1 on you or the target (your choice). Private uses do not decay it, but three private uses make it stale; refresh with new proof/witness/circumstance before it functions again.

Exposure (You & Them)

Track Exposure [6] for the party (or per face) and on major factions. When a track fills, trigger a Scandal Event: public censure, hostile audit, inquisitorial summons, riot, duel demand, etc. Clear 2 on a successful Shield scene or by accepting a humiliating concession on-screen.

Reputation Tiers

Check at arc end (or every 2–3 sessions). If two are true, advance Reputation Tier:

  • Convert 3 favors from one institution into Standing (Tag) or hold Standing with two institutions.

  • End with Mandate ≥ 4 or resolve a city-scale Situation Timer [8] in your favor.

  • Your deeds create/resolve a regional law/rite or seat/remove a leader.

Gatekeeping Effects: Higher tiers grant default access to higher-tier factions/venues; DVs for petitions to lower-tier offices drop by 1; timers scale up (use 6–8 segments at higher tiers; 4–6 at low tier). When your tier outstrips a scene’s stakes, the GM may simply hand you the room unless opposing Strings/Leverage are present.

Followers & Assets in Politics

On-Scene

Assign a follower to Assist or to take an independent scene action with its own risk. Harm to followers often lands as Stability or Exposure ticks on their home faction.

Off-Screen Orders (Intrigue Cycle)

Each PC may direct one follower/asset on a Significant-Time operation (surveil, seed rumors, counter-bribe, audit a ledger, escort witnesses). This can occur mid-adventure as a cutaway or between sessions. Resolve with one roll using the follower’s capability as positioning; on a hit, apply 1–2 segments to the intended timer; on 1s, bank SB and mark Exposure +1.

Upkeep & Entanglements

Track names, not ledgers. When the GM spends 2+ SB in a political arc, an ally may face the consequence instead (compromised informant, bribed clerk, stolen seal). Convert entanglements into new threads and timers rather than binary losses.

Spades: violence, duels, arrests, street pressure.
Clubs: resources, trade shocks, supply, strikes.
Diamonds: environment, festivals, omens, disasters.

Worked Examples

  • Hearts 7: A scion’s oath of love becomes public; tick Exposure +2 on their house; start a 4-timer Secret Betrothal.

  • Spades King: A duelist challenges the Tide-Marshall; start a 6-timer Public Blood; if it fills, Mandate –1 for the city guard.

  • Clubs 6: Dockworkers strike; tick Harbor Guild Stability +1; create tag Pickets at Dawn.

  • Diamonds 9: Omens sour during a procession; tick Exposure +2 on the Ember-Temple unless rites appease it.

Long-Term Campaign Management

Running a multi-arc intrigue campaign requires pacing and player investment. Use these additional tools.

Seasonal Arcs

Each arc (4–6 sessions) should focus on a single Seasonal Aim for the party (e.g., “Seat an allied candidate”, “Expose a treasonous conspiracy”, “Negotiate a trade treaty”). At the end of the arc, the party may:

  • Permanently increase Mandate by 1 (if the aim succeeded and was visible).

  • Gain a Legacy String (a permanent tie usable once per arc).

  • Convert a major rival faction’s Influence timer into a Debt on them.

The Calendar as a Lever

Three calendars run in this setting and a court uses all three (see The Three Reckonings). For intrigue purposes, only a handful of days matter, and they matter enormously.

Tally, and the tenday.

Manorial and chancery courts sit on Tally, the seventh day of every tenday. A writ served on Road or Hearth waits until Tally; a writ served on Tally is heard that morning. Three days’ difference in when a paper is handed over decides whether a rival has time to answer it. Let players notice this once and they will never stop using it.

Still.

Nothing may be distrained on Still, the tenth day. A debtor who reaches his own gate by Still cannot be taken there until Yoking. This is the oldest and best-known exemption in the Amaranthine world, and every bailiff, every fugitive and every village in the valley knows it exactly.

Goldmeet.

Accounts are read publicly and oaths of service begin and end. If a household is going to be publicly humiliated, it will be on Goldmeet, and everyone concerned has known the date all year.

The Borrowed Day.

One year in four, added after Winter’s Crest. Nothing sworn on it binds; nothing witnessed on it is evidence; nothing lent on it is owed. Treat it as a scene in which every social action resolves normally and then does not count — which makes it the only safe day in the calendar to meet an enemy, confess, or hand something over. Players will work this out. Let them. The trap is that the other side worked it out first.

The Ash Day.

The Civil Count’s equivalent, and its opposite: a leap day the Censor’s Hall does not number, so nothing registered on it was ever registered. A marriage, a manumission, a transfer of title or a confession dated to the Ash Day is void — not disputed, not voidable. It has been used deliberately at least four times the record admits, and a forged Ash Day dating is the cleanest way in the setting to destroy a document without touching it.

One is holy and one is a filing convenience, and a campaign that uses both will find its players arguing about the difference, which is the correct outcome.

Faction Rivalry Timers

In addition to Influence/Stability/Exposure, you may track a Rivalry Timer [6] between two factions. Each time one faction publicly humiliates the other, tick the timer. When full, a decisive confrontation occurs (duel, vote, raid, trial). The PCs can influence the outcome by spending Favors or Leverage.

Managing Downtime

Between arcs, run a Downtime Phase where players narrate their characters’ activities (3–5 scenes). Each activity:

  • Restores a resource (Favor, Leverage, or clears Exposure).

  • Advances a personal project (gaining a new String or improving an Asset).

  • May trigger a Personal Complication (GM banks 2 SB for the next arc).

Delegation While Away – Maintaining the Household in Absentia

When the party is absent for a full season (spring, summer, autumn, or winter) on an adventure, diplomatic mission, or military campaign, the household does not simply pause. All timers would normally tick –1 at season end, but the party may avoid a death spiral by delegating authority to a trusted follower.

Core Principle

A Cap 4 or Cap 5 follower (see Player’s Guide, §12.4) who has been delegated a minimum number of maintained assets (see Player’s Guide, §12.14.1.1) may act as Steward. They prevent one or two household timers from automatically ticking and may attempt to improve those timers using their own capabilities.

Asset Requirements

The follower must have at least the following number of assets delegated to them and in Maintained condition:

A Cap 4 follower with two assets is still limited to one timer – the extra asset does not grant a second. Only Cap 5 followers can manage two timers simultaneously (e.g., Loyalty and Security).

Procedure

Before departure, the party:

  1. Names a qualified follower as Steward.

  2. Designates which household timer(s) the Steward will manage (up to the limit above).

  3. The Steward remains at the keep for the entire season.

At the end of the season:

  1. All non-delegated timers tick –1 (wear of time).

  2. For each delegated timer, the Steward attempts to improve it using the normal Household Action rules (see §[MISSING REF: sec:household-actions]). The Steward rolls a number of d10s equal to their Cap. They do not add attributes or skills unless they have a relevant talent (e.g., Efficient Steward). The GM sets Difficulty Value (DV) and Position based on the Steward’s competence, the season’s fiction, and the state of the holding.

  3. Apply the standard outcome matrix:

    • Clean Success: Timer advances by 1.

    • Success with SB: Timer advances by 1, GM gains 1 Story Beat (complication).

    • Partial: Timer unchanged, but the Steward learns a secret (gain a String) or the GM gains 1 SB.

    • Miss: Timer ticks backward by 1 (or a minor crisis occurs).

  4. If a delegated timer reaches 0 despite the Steward’s efforts, a Crisis Event (see §[MISSING REF: sec:crisis-events]) occurs. The crisis must be resolved by the party upon their return; the Steward cannot resolve it alone.

Limitations and Strategic Choices

  • Only one follower may act as Steward per season. (If multiple qualified followers remain, they may assist, but only the designated Steward rolls.)

  • Cap 3 or lower followers cannot delegate – they lack the authority or infrastructure to manage a noble household alone.

  • If two or more player characters remain at the keep, they may each take a household action normally; delegation is only needed when all player characters are absent.

  • A Steward who is also the player’s heir or successor (see Player’s Guide, Legacy Engine) is an excellent narrative choice – delegation becomes part of their training.

Optional Rule – Delegation with Risk

For tables that enjoy deeper strategic planning, players may give the Steward specific orders before departure. The player rolls Presence + Sway (or a relevant skill) against a DV set by the complexity of the order (usually 3–4). On a success, the Steward gains +1 die on their season-end roll for each delegated timer. On a failure, the Steward is confused or resentful, and each delegated timer ticks –1 regardless of the Steward’s roll (in addition to any other outcome).

This optional rule adds a layer of pre-planning but also extra dice rolls; use it only if your table enjoys such strategic depth.

Integration with Existing Rules

  • This rule assumes the four standard household timers (Loyalty, Prosperity, Security, Hospitality). If additional timers are used (e.g., Armor Closes for Vhasia, Legal Exposure for Viterra), the party may delegate one additional timer per extra Cap 5 follower (maximum two delegated timers per season, requiring two separate Stewards).

  • See Player’s Guide §12.4 for follower Cap costs (Cap 4 costs 16 XP; Cap 5 costs 25 XP). The asset requirements ensure that delegation is a reward for investing in both the follower and their infrastructure.

Court & Keep – Managing a Noble Household

A keep is not stone. It is a ledger of loyalties, a harvest of favors, a winter of patience.

— — Duchess Gwen of the Thornwood, on her sixtieth year of rule

This chapter provides procedures for players who hold a noble title, command a fortress, or seek to build a political dynasty. It is designed for campaigns that span seasons or years, where influence, heirs, and household management matter as much as swords and spells.

The Noble Household – Core Components

A noble household consists of four interconnected tracks. Each is a timer (size 6-8) that the party must maintain. Neglect one, and the others may suffer.

Loyalty [6

] – The devotion of your household staff, lesser nobles, and common tenants. High loyalty means servants warn you of plots; low loyalty means whispers in dark corners.

Prosperity [6

] – Harvests, trade, coin reserves, and infrastructure. High prosperity funds improvements and bribes; low prosperity leads to debt, famine, and desertion.

Security [6

] – Walls, patrols, wards, and spy networks. High security deters raids and assassins; low security invites them.

Hospitality [8

] – Your reputation as a host, the splendour of your feasts, the loyalty of visiting knights and envoys. High hospitality attracts allies; low hospitality offends potential patrons.

Seasonal Upkeep

A season ends on a quarter day of the Old Count — Greenmeet, the Long Day, Goldmeet, the Long Night — and these are the only four days in the year on which no court sits, no interest runs and no work is done, which is precisely why a household’s year is settled on them. At the end of each season each timer ticks automatically by 1 (the wear of time). The party may take actions to reduce or reverse these ticks (see below). If any timer reaches 0, a Crisis Event occurs – see the Crisis table.

Running the Household – Actions Per Season

During each season, the party may assign themselves or trusted followers to one or more of the following actions. Each action requires a roll (or, if using a follower, a use of their capability). A character can only perform one action per season unless they have a relevant Asset or Talent (e.g., Efficient Steward).

Improve Loyalty (Presence + Sway)

Roll to raise the loyalty of a specific group (servants, garrison, tenant farmers). DV 3 (peaceful region) to 5 (discontent after a harsh winter).

  • Success: Loyalty timer advances by 1.

  • Success with SB: Advances by 1, but GM gains 1 SB (rival spreads a rumor).

  • Partial: No change, but you learn a secret (String).

  • Miss: Loyalty ticks backward by 1 (a trusted servant resigns).

Improve Prosperity (Wits + Craft, Commerce, or Lore)

Invest in trade, farming, or infrastructure. DV 3 (normal year) to 5 (drought or siege).

  • Success: Prosperity advances by 1. You may also gain a minor Asset (e.g., new mill, trade route).

  • Partial: Prosperity advances by 1, but you incur a Debt Timer [4] (loan from a merchant or patron).

  • Miss: Prosperity ticks backward (blight, embezzlement).

Improve Security (Wits + Insight, Stealth, or Arcana)

Patrols, espionage, or warding. DV 3 (peace) to 5 (border war).

  • Success: Security advances by 1. Reveal one hidden threat (an assassin, a spy, a cursed object).

  • Partial: Security advances by 1, but the GM gains 1 SB (the enemy learns your methods).

  • Miss: Security ticks backward; a minor breach occurs (stolen letter, dead sentry).

Improve Hospitality (Presence + Performance or Sway)

Host a feast, a hunt, a tourney, or a diplomatic reception. DV 3 (modest) to 5 (lavish, with many guests).

  • Success: Hospitality advances by 1. Gain a temporary String with one guest faction.

  • Partial: Hospitality advances by 1, but the feast incurs a minor scandal (drunken brawl, insulted herald). Tick Exposure +1.

  • Miss: Hospitality ticks backward; the feast is a disaster (poisoned wine, open duel). Crisis may trigger.

Reputation Across Seasons – The Renown Dial

In addition to the four timers, the party has a Renown Dial ranging from –3 (infamous) to +3 (exalted). Renown affects all social rolls with nobles, traders, and foreign courts.

  • -3 to -1: Disgraced. You are received Desperate in any formal setting. Trade goods cost double. Followers may desert.

  • 0: Neutral. Standard Position. No modifier.

  • +1 to +3: Respected. You start social scenes Controlled or Dominant. You may demand audience with higher nobles. Merchants offer credit (one free Debt Timer of 4 segments per season).

Changing Renown

Renown shifts by ±1 at the end of each season based on:

  • Major victory in war or law: +1

  • Hosting a legendary feast (Hospitality filled to 8): +1

  • Public scandal or failed oath: –1

  • Losing a keep or being defeated in a duel: –1

  • Marrying into a powerful house: +1

The GM may also award or deduct Renown based on story events.

Marriage Alliances – Skill Challenge

Marriage is the sharpest tool of diplomacy. To arrange a marriage between your house and another, run a skill challenge. The challenge requires 3 successes before 2 failures. Each success represents a key hurdle overcome; each failure introduces a complication that may raise the price.

Hurdles (examples)

  • Proposal (Presence + Sway): Formally ask the other house for the match. DV 3 if they are eager; DV 5 if they are reluctant.

  • Dowry or Bride-Price (Wits + Commerce or Lore): Negotiate the exchange of lands, gold, or favors. DV 4.

  • Religious Approval (Spirit + Lore): Secure a priest’s blessing, or navigate differing faiths. DV 3-4.

  • Convince the Betrothed (Presence + Sway): The prince or princess may have their own opinions. DV 4.

  • Feast (Presence + Performance): Announce the betrothal before witnesses. DV 3.

Outcomes

  • 3 Successes: The marriage is agreed. Gain a permanent Alliance String with the other house. Increase Renown by +1. The couple produces an heir (a new NPC).

  • 2 Successes, 2 Failures (partial): The marriage goes forward, but at a cost. Increase Renown by +0. Gain a String, but also a Debt Timer [6] (the other house expects a future favor).

  • Failure (2 failures before 3 successes): The marriage fails. Renown –1. The other house may become hostile (new Rival Timer [8]).

Instruments (for a high-stakes match)

At crown and ducal tier a marriage is not a promise, it is a document, and the document is where the real play is. Three instruments out of Belworth practice, all of which have been used and all of which have gone wrong:

  • The Right of Acknowledgment. A king or duke may acknowledge a child of his body, before witnesses, in eleven fixed words. The child takes standing, though not precedence. The Right cannot be revoked and can only be disputed — which means every acknowledgment plants a lawsuit that will ripen in the next generation. In play: a Legacy String for the acknowledged party and a permanent volatile arrow between the two houses.

  • Recital. Any existing right may be recited into a marriage settlement, which closes it: a thing recited cannot afterwards be reopened without reopening the whole settlement, and no house will reopen a settlement it is living on. This is the single most powerful defensive move available in a negotiation and costs one hurdle’s worth of concession. In play: spend a success to make one String permanent and un-attackable. Rivals must attack the marriage itself or nothing.

  • Renunciation. A party renounces a claim — for himself, or for his issue, or both, and the difference between those three is worth a duchy. A mother who renounces on behalf of her son has disinherited him and may have saved his life, and both readings will be argued at her funeral. In play: remove a claim permanently; the renouncing house gains Stability +1 now and a Rivalry Timer that opens the moment the renouncing party dies.

GM note. Let the players read the draft. Put one clause in it they did not ask for. Make it favourable. The best intrigue in this setting is not a knife; it is a sentence somebody agreed to and did not read closely, and the whole of The Widow’s Hand is one long worked example of it.

Example: The Betrothal of Lord Reynard and Lady Celandine

The party acts as Lord Reynard’s advocates. They roll:

  1. Proposal (Presence+Sway, DV 4): Success – 1 success.

  2. Dowry (Wits+Commerce, DV 5): Partial – no success, but no failure. GM gains 1 SB.

  3. Convince the Betrothed (Presence+Sway, DV 4): Failure – 1 failure.

  4. Religious Approval (Spirit+Lore, DV 3): Success – 2 successes, 1 failure.

  5. Feast (Presence+Performance, DV 3): Success – 3 successes, 1 failure. Partial success. The marriage is agreed, but the dowry negotiations were strained; the party incurs a Debt Timer [6] (a future political favor).

Crisis Events (When a Timer Reaches 0)

When any household timer reaches 0, roll or choose a Crisis Event from the table below. The crisis is immediate and requires a scene to resolve. After resolution, the timer resets to 2 (or 3 for Hospitality), and the party may begin rebuilding.

ClockCrisis Event
LoyaltyServants betray a secret to a rival; a faction of tenants threatens to leave. To restore, the party must perform a public act of generosity or punish a known abuser.
ProsperityFamine or bankruptcy. Food stores empty; creditors demand immediate payment. The party must find a new source of grain (quest) or sell an Asset.
SecurityA raid, an assassination attempt, or a magical breach. The party must defend the keep (combat skill challenge) or track down the infiltrator.
HospitalityA guest is insulted, injured, or murdered under your roof. Your reputation crumbles. The party must offer weregild, host a second feast, or duel the offended party.

Optional Rule – Heirs and Succession

If the party wishes to establish a dynasty, track the primary heir as an NPC. At the end of each year (four seasons), roll a die:

  • 1-2: The heir falls ill or suffers an accident (timer [4] to recover).

  • 3-5: No event.

  • 6: The heir comes of age. The player may transfer their character to the heir (retaining some skills and Strings) or keep the heir as a powerful follower (Cap 4-5).

The heir inherits the household’s current timers (adjusted for the new generation) and gains one bonus Talent based on their upbringing (e.g., Disciplined Body for martial education, Courtly Graces for diplomatic).

GM Guidance

The Court & Keep framework is designed for campaigns that value long-term consequences. Use it when players acquire a fortress, a title, or a permanent base. Emphasize that each season brings new opportunities and threats. Let the players choose which actions to prioritise – they cannot fix everything at once.

A simple mantra for the table: “Stone and title are debts. Pay them on time, or the winter will collect.”

Blood on the Belworth

A Political Intrigue adventure for Fate’s Edge using the v0.3 framework

Kindling 840 into Mending 841 AR. The Belworth valley, from Tarlington south.

Duke Braedyn Fenwood is dead at six-and-sixty, in his own bed, with an unfinished game on his chest. He had been the most consequential man in two kingdoms for forty years and he chose the worst possible fortnight to stop.

The Queen is four hundred miles north. Veralyn “Demoneye” Everblood — Duchess of Aberiel, crowned at Valora in the summer — rode for the northern earls on the nineteenth of Nonis with the whole of her levy and nothing behind her, and everyone in this valley who can count knows what that means: the river is open. Eleven days later Valdais Longblood, Duke of Adria, crossed out of Vhasia under the cry Vhasia for Vhasians — revenge for Lence, and no two people agree on what he actually wants.

What he needs is not in dispute. He needs crossings. There are four between Tarlington and Aberiel: three ferries and a half-built Aeler bridge nobody has been able to finish for nine years.

Into that, at dusk, a ferry comes ashore with its lanterns guttering and three stabbed men aboard, and a Fenwood quartermaster’s armband nailed to the mast. The river turns to rumor overnight. In the dead duke’s solar the Canray board has been moved — the Blue is isolated — and his last written line is on the table beside it, in a hand that had got very bad: Count exits before enemies. The road reschedules.

Timeline Note (GM Eyes Only)

Where this sits. The Fourth Sack of Ecktoria (c. 620 AR) fell seventy years after the Third Sack and the Valvano exile; The Exile Road and The Nameless belong to that era. Braedyn Fenwood lives two centuries later, after the Tarling kings have fallen, the Fenwoods have risen to ducal rank, and the Everbloods have taken the crown. The debt foreshadowed in The Exile Road was paid long ago. This adventure is about the consequences: the Black Scrip indemnities, the ghost of the Thornhill child, and the Aeler keystone rights.

The immediate past, and the part that matters at the table:

  • 832 — The sack of Lence. Braedyn Fenwood kills the Blue and forces Vhasia to re-forge. A fifteen-year-old Veralyn is present, disguised, and is not supposed to have been.

  • 838 — Brightstride Everblood dies of a fever at three-and-twenty, four months married to Jaennis Fenwood, Braedyn’s granddaughter. Nobody has ever established who, if anybody, did it. Jaennis is the dowager queen and is nineteen.

  • 840, summer — Veralyn is crowned at Valora. The northern and eastern earls, who do not want a queen, begin to correspond with Adria.

  • 840, Nonis — Veralyn goes north with nothing behind her, on purpose. Rekedim Many-Scars goes with her. So does most of the realm’s strength.

  • 840, late Nonis — Valdais crosses.

  • 840, Kindling — Braedyn dies. The adventure opens here.

The useful fact. Every figure in this valley with the standing to settle a quarrel is somewhere else, dead, or nineteen. That is the adventure. The PCs are not helping the great; they are standing in for them, on a river that four thousand men need to cross, with a fortnight before anyone with authority can arrive.

Prior novellas. The Widow’s Hand, A Roll of the Fallen and Count Your Exits cover 785–840 from three sides and are the source for everything above. A GM who has read them will know what Valdais wants. A GM who has not should run him as a man whose spending can be read and whose mouth cannot, which is how everyone in the fiction experiences him.

Using This Adventure

This module is built on the Political Intrigue Campaign Framework (v0.3). Run each major question with a Situation Timer (4–8) and at least one Counter-Timer (4–6). Use Strings to tune Position/DV and as prizes/failure states. Apply Mandate/Crisis dials and the Situation → Faction Ripple rules after each scene.

Intended length: 2–4 sessions. Tone: border-court drama, false flags, rites of hospitality, hedge-law wrangling. Prerequisite: The adventure assumes the events of The Old Duke’s Wars (vignettes of Braedyn’s life) have recently occurred. The party may have played those vignettes, or the GM can narrate the key beats.

Seats, Locales & Assets

  • Tarlington (Viterra): Ducal seat; marble halls, Hedge-Law Moot Court, lantern beacons along the river stairs. Old Braedyn’s solar now holds his Canray board and a sealed letter.

  • Belworth River Corridor: Ferry towns, reedmarshes, customs towers, lantern-law posts, ruined Tarling fortlets.

  • Lence (the Crownlands): Half-rebuilt after the sack of 832. Restorationist salons; a palace-in-exile flavour in a place that is not, technically, in exile. The sliver of Lence crownland lies directly across the water from Tarlington, which is why the two towns hate each other at conversational volume.

  • The Red Span: A half-finished Aeler bridge across the Belworth; nine years of budgets and graft. The True Masons hold a dormant keystone clause that could swing control of it in an afternoon, and have been asked twice this month what it would cost to invoke — once by a Fenwood steward and once by somebody who did not give a name. It is the only crossing between Tarlington and Aberiel that a column could use in winter.

  • Lantern-Rites House of Oria: Neutral ground by custom; oaths, feasts, and river burials are sanctified here. Sister Oria was a confidante of old Braedyn.

Inciting Incident – “Blood on the Ferry”

At dusk, the ferry Belworth Grace drifts to shore with its lanterns guttering. A Vhasian courier and two clerks lie stabbed; a Fenwood quartermaster’s armband is nailed to the mast. Survivors whisper conflicting tales. The river turns to rumor overnight.

In Braedyn’s solar, his Canray board has been moved – the Blue piece is now isolated. Those who see it read a warning: the king is vulnerable.

It is Hammer of the first tenday of Kindling. The moot court sits on Tally — the day after tomorrow — and then not again for ten days, which gives every party in the valley exactly two days to decide whether it wants this heard in public.

Immediate Situation Timer : [6]: Contain the Spiral – keep the border calm until Tally.
Counter-Clock: [4]: Hotheads March – Seniro’s men assemble to cross at dawn.
Counter-Clock: [4]: Justice Now! – Restorationists demand arrests at the Lantern-Rites House.

If the PCs do nothing: Crisis +1, Fenwood Exposure +1, Regency Influence +1, and the volatile stake Black Scrip triggers (audit squads arrive).

Act I – Holding the River

Goal: Prevent escalation; establish an inquest under Lantern-Rites.

Core Scenes (pick 2–3)

Petition at Lantern-Rites

Claim neutral inquest rights before Sister Oria. Position: Dominant with offerings (a candle, a tithe, a memory of Braedyn); Desperate if arriving with blades. DV: 3–4 On hit: Situation +1–2; Lantern-Rites Influence +1. On miss: Exposure +1 (you profane rites).

Shield the Captains

Keep Seniro’s hotheads from marching. Use Hedge-Law precedents or Sister Oria’s writ. Position: Dominant with Hedge-Law dicta; Dominant if you secure Oria’s seal. DV: 3 On hit: Counter-Timer Hotheads March –1; Seniro grudgingly respects you.

Broker a Truce

Arrange a dusk meeting between Regency envoys and Fenwood stewards at Lantern-Rites. Position: Dominant if hosting on sacred ground; Desperate if in the open. DV: 3–4 On hit: Both sides“ Exposure –1; start a concession timer Shared Inquest [4].

Infiltrate the Red Span

Sneak onto the unfinished bridge to see who planted the quartermaster’s armband. Position: Desperate (saboteurs may still be present). DV: 4–5 On hit: Gain Leverage (1) – ”Seniro’s quartermaster took bribes from Ghiro“ – or fill Contain the Spiral by 1.

If the party spared the Thornhill child in the vignettes, his ghost appears during infiltration, whispering warnings. This grants +1 die to the roll but adds a Haunted condition (Dread +1).

End of Act I: If Contain the Spiral fills, Mandate +1. If any counter-timer fills first, Crisis +1 and trigger a volatile arrow (GM’s choice: likely Fenwood ↔︎ Contractors or Fenwood ↔︎ Regency).

Act II – Ledger and Blood

Goal: Identify the hand behind the ferry murders.

Clue Threads (any 3 lead to the culprit)

  1. Tithe Ledger – Missing customs stamps the night of the murder (points to Syndicate colluding with Contractors).

  2. Printing House in the Reeds – Broadsheets were printed hours after the killings (Restorationists prepped the narrative).

  3. Black Scrip Clerk – The dead courier carried documents for a renegotiation of the indemnity (someone wanted that negotiation to fail).

  4. Quartermaster’s Armband Fibers – Match a Red Span uniform batch, not Fenwood’s armory (Contractors frame-job).

Likely Culprits (choose one or let players expose one)

  • Ghiro’s Contractors – Staged a false flag to prolong funding and tie Seniro closer.

  • Restorationists – Struck to collapse any indemnity deal, forcing a hard break.

  • Syndicate Hardliners – Provoked crisis to win permanent night-route exemptions.

Tip: leave two culprits plausible; the third is a red herring that still moves timers.

Core Scenes (pick 3–4)

Expose Budget Graft at the Moot Court

End of Act II: If culprit identified on-screen and public mood calms, reduce Crisis –1 (min 0). On messy wins, tick Fenwood Exposure +1 (your methods smell of law-twisting).

Act III – Judgment on the Water

Goal: Secure a settlement that prevents war, punishes the guilty, and sets the river’s law.

Final Situation Timer : [8]: Bind the Belworth Accord
Counter: [6]: March at Dawn (if Seniro’s influence is high) or Honor the Fallen (if Restorationists are ascendant)

Settlement Options (pick one primary + one concession)

  • Hedge-Law Trial at the River – Culprit faction yields its leader to a public rite at Lantern-Rites. Effect: Mandate +1; culprit Exposure fill → scandal.

  • Indemnity Rescript – Black Scrip terms renegotiated; Lence gets relief; Fenwood pays in river tolls (not gold). Effect: Regency Influence +1; Syndicate gains Standing (Tag) Night-Routes Deputized.

  • Red Span Purge – Contractors ousted; work crews handed to Syndicate and Lantern-Rites oversight. Effect: Fenwood Stability –1 (short-term), Mandate +1 (public cleansed).

  • The Crossings Compact – The four crossings between Tarlington and Aberiel are placed under Lantern-Rites custody for the duration of the war, with Syndicate pilots and Fenwood tolls, and no armed body of more than forty may pass without Oria’s seal. Effect: Adria’s Agents –2. Fenwood Influence +1, Fenwood Stability –1 (the marshal is furious). This is the option Braedyn would have taken and the party may find him saying so, in his own hand, after they have chosen it.

  • Marshal’s Oath – Seniro swears to Constano before Sister Oria to obey lantern-law in all crossings. Effect: House Stability +1; if broken later, automatic Exposure fill.

Resolution Scenes (choose 2)

  • Broker the Accord (Presence + Sway, DV: 4–5) – Fill Bind the Belworth Accord by 2 on success.

  • Expose last-minute sabotage (Wits + Insight, DV: 3–4) – Prevent a counter-timer advance.

  • Shield the rite from blades (Body + Melee or Stealth, DV: 3) – Protect the ceremony.

  • Petition the Queen’s chancellor to ratify (Presence + Sway, DV: 4) – Gain Mandate +1.

Success

If the PCs fill Bind the Belworth Accord before the counter-timer triggers:

  • Mandate +1

  • Crisis –1

  • Apply Settlement effects.

  • Advance allied faction Influence +1 (+2 if the settlement directly satisfied their Aim).

  • Braedyn’s legacy: The old duke’s Canray board has a compartment, and in it the keystone rights to the Red Span, bought outright from the True Masons in 823 and never registered — because a right you have not registered is a right nobody can see you spending. Gain Asset: Keystone Charter. The purchase was settled, in the Aeler manner, over a board; the memorandum is in K’thra notation and the party will need somebody who reads it.

Failure

If a counter-timer fills first:

  • A crossing turns bloody. Crisis +2 (maybe +3).

  • Tick House Fenwood Stability +1 (schism risk) and Exposure +1 (butcher tales).

  • Rival faction Influence +1.

  • The Gray Wanderer appears in the aftermath, shaking his head: “He counted the exits wrong.” He is being generous. Nobody counted them at all, which is the failure this valley was always going to have while the only person who counts is in the north.

The Crown’s People (present, and each a problem)

  • Jaennis Fenwood, dowager queen, nineteen. Braedyn’s granddaughter; four months a wife and two years a widow. She is small, was ill so often as a child that her household stopped reporting it upward, reads enormously, and has a face that makes people carry things for her. She is also the only person in the building who has read every roll in it. She outranks Constano and has no authority whatever, and both of them are managing that with great courtesy. She wants the crossings held and will not say for whom. Lever: she can open any muniment room on the river. Cost: she never asks for a thing she has not already decided she is owed.

  • Elyas Everblood, eight-and-twenty, the most beloved man in the valley and its most valuable hostage. Youngest son of Oswyn, Duke of Istovar, whose armies have sat across the Belworth from the Fens for thirty years and are sitting there now. He has known what he is since he was nine and decided, at nine, that the answer was to be impossible to dislike. It has worked for nineteen years. Lever: he will carry anything to anyone and be welcome. Cost: every use of him is a reminder of what he is, and he lets it show exactly twice in his life.

  • Tylan Vayle, four-and-thirty, second son of a house that was nothing a generation ago and is now a county. Enormous, better with a blade than the master-at-arms ever admitted, and the most prepared man anyone has met — he carried a spare bowstring at eleven and has never in his life walked into a room he had not first worked out the shape of. Lever: he already knows where the keys are. Cost: he is standing on ice and knows it, and a man on ice does not gamble for you.

  • Rekedim Many-Scars — absent. The Queen’s bodyguard in the roll, her teacher in fact, and the one man in Viterra who could have told this valley what Valdais is doing. He is four hundred miles north. His nine boxes of papers are at Valora and are not. His absence is the single largest reason the party is required.

Rewards, Standing & Fallout

On a public, clean win: convert 3 favors from Lantern-Rites or the Syndicate into Standing (Tag). Grant Audience Tag: Steady River. Increase Mandate +1 (now at least 3). Braedyn’s slate fragment – Minor Asset granting +1 die to any roll involving strategy or timing, once per session.

On messy wins: rival Exposure +1 but allied Stability –1. The settlement holds, but someone whispers: ”The Fenwoods won by cheating.“

On failure: the volatile Black Scrip stake triggers – audits, seizures, or arrests. The river stays closed for a season. Set up the next arc.

Running Notes

  • Stay Visible: Every spend changes the map: a tag, a timer, a dial.

  • Escalate Honestly: When Crisis climbs, show it: protests, inquisitors, budgets, bans.

  • Reward Face-Saving: In intrigue, dignity can be worth more than coin.

  • Keep It Human: Even in a world of rites and spirits, politics is people.

  • Use Strings as positional levers and as victory spoils.

  • Braedyn’s Canray board can be used as a prop. The GM may reveal that the positions of pieces on his board mirror the political landscape – the last unfinished game of a dying duke.

Integration with The Old Duke’s Wars

If the players have completed the vignette adventure The Old Duke’s Wars, incorporate the following echoes:

  • If they spared the Thornhill child, the ghost haunts the Red Span, granting a one-time +1 die but adding a Haunted condition.

  • If they exposed the Renard conspiracy, Veralyn’s court is more cooperative — Mandate starts at 3 instead of 2. If they did not, the Queen’s mother’s name is still worth something in the Aelerian foothills, and somebody on the wrong side of this river knows it.

  • If they executed the Thornhill child, the Restorationists are fanatical – Justice Now! counter-timer starts at 2/4 instead of 0.

  • If they kept Baldvyn’s letter unopened, the letter contains a secret Aeler clause – the party gains the Keystone Charter asset immediately.

The GM may also allow players to use Braedyn’s Canray proverbs as a minor resource: spend 1 Boon to ask for a ”Kon“reh insight” – the GM gives a cryptic but actionable clue.

This file presents procedures and a complete adventure. Defer to the Fate’s Edge core SRD for roll math and basic adjudication.
