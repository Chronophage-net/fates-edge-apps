Campfire Mode

One table, any playstyle — a guide for mixed-ability groups

The Golden Rule

Everyone at this table uses the exact same dice roll. Pick your Attribute + Skill. Roll that many d10s. Count 6–9 as 1 success, 10 as 2 successes, and every 1 as a Story Beat for the GM. Compare your successes to the Difficulty Value (DV) the GM set.

That's it. The dice always speak the same language, whether the character sheet behind them is one line or ten.

Every word on this page is a real word from the full rules. Campfire Mode does not use a simpler vocabulary — it uses fewer of the same things. Nothing here has to be unlearned later.

The Base Character (for everyone)

Fits on an index card. Distribute 1, 2, 2, 3 among the four attributes.

1. Pick Your Stats

Body
Being strong, tough, and athletic.
Wits
Being quick, sneaky, and perceptive.
Spirit
Being brave, focused, and magical.
Presence
Being charming, scary, and commanding.

2. Pick Your Skills (6 points, max 2 per skill)

  • Melee — sword, axe, spear, or fists
  • Athletics — climb, run, swim, sneak, keep going
  • Sway — persuade, lie, perform, command a room
  • Insight — spot clues, read people, know things
  • Arcana — weave spells, read runes, work rites
  • Craft — pick locks, build traps, fix gear, mend a body

The full game has twelve skills. These six are those twelve gathered into groups, so each one you learn here is a real skill with a real name — you never have to trade in a word. When you want finer control, the group splits:

HereSplits into
MeleeMelee · Ranged
AthleticsAthletics · Stealth · Endurance
SwaySway · Deception · Performance
InsightInsight · Lore
ArcanaArcana (rites use Spirit + Lore in the full game)
CraftCraft

So a bow is Melee here and Ranged there; sneaking is Athletics here and Stealth there. Same character, finer grain.

Health Track (Stamina + Wounds)

  • Fatigue — you get tired. Track = your Body. Mark it when you push hard. Each mark drops your Spot by one step (see below): tired people are in worse trouble. A night's sleep clears all of it.
  • Harm — you get hurt. Harm 1 = −1 die on anything the wound touches. Harm 2 = −1 die on nearly everything until you are patched up. Harm 3 = down and out of the fight.

The Resolution Loop

  1. Step 1: The GM sets the scene. “What do you do?”
  2. Step 2: Pick your Attribute + Skill.
  3. Step 3: The GM says the Difficulty Value (DV) and your Spot.
    • DV 2 — Easy (kick open a rickety door)
    • DV 3 — Normal (fight a guard)
    • DV 4 — Hard (climb a cliff in the rain)
    • DV 5 — Extreme (convince a king to surrender)

    There is no DV 6. If something is harder than Extreme, it isn't a bigger number — it's several rolls, a worse Spot, or just “no”.

  4. Step 4: Roll the dice!
  5. Step 5: Look at the result (see table below).
Your Spot (the full game calls this Position)

Before you roll, the GM says what kind of spot you're in. It doesn't change your dice — it changes what the dice are allowed to do to you.

  • Dominant — a good spot. Re-roll one die that failed.
  • Controlled — a normal spot. Roll as they land.
  • Desperate — a bad spot. Re-roll one die that succeeded. (Never a 10 — a 10 is yours to keep.)

The ladder stops at both ends. Nothing is better than Dominant or worse than Desperate, and extra advantages never turn into extra dice. Re-rolling a 1 does not take back the Story Beat it already gave the GM — and if it comes up 1 again, that's another one.

Roll ResultWhat HappensYou Get…
Hit (Successes ≥ DV)You do it! No problem.Nothing extra.
Success with SB (Successes ≥ DV, and you rolled at least one 1)You do it! …but something goes wrong.Nothing extra for you.
Partial (0 < Successes < DV)You sort of do it, but it costs you.You get 1 Boon (luck coin)

Story Beats are counted separately, every time. Each 1 on the table gives the GM one Story Beat — on a hit, on a miss, on anything. That is the only place they come from, and three 1s means three of them. The outcome row tells you what you get; the 1s tell you what the GM gets.

The Two Treasuries

Player's Boons (luck coins)
  • Get them on Partial (1) or Miss (2).
  • Hold up to 5. At the end of each scene, drop back to 2 — luck does not keep.
  • Spend 1 to: re-roll a die you already rolled, improve your Spot by one step before you roll, or switch on a special item or pet.
  • Note that a Boon buys you a better Spot, never extra dice. Your dice are your Attribute + Skill and nothing else — that is what keeps everyone's rolls readable at one table.
  • (Veterans: convert 2 Boons → 1 XP at end of session!)
GM's Story Beats (trouble tokens)
  • Get one for every 1 the players roll. Always. They bank up.
  • Spend 1 — “A guard turns around.” (a nudge, or tick a timer)
  • Spend 2 — “The alarm goes up.” (you lose your Spot, or a small enemy arrives)
  • Spend 3 — “Reinforcements. Real ones.” (something serious breaks)
  • Spend 4+ — “The floor collapses!” (the scene turns over)

Expansion Packs (add complexity later)

Players who want to keep it simple just use the Base Character above. Players who want more crunch can pick one of these packs.

Pack 1: The Talented (simple feats)
  • Pick 3 cool things your character is great at. (e.g. “Duelist: +1 die when fighting one enemy”, “Thief: pick any normal lock without rolling”)
  • Mechanic: When you use a Stunt, you just say “I use my X” and roll with advantage (e.g. +1 die, or ignore a negative modifier).
Pack 2: The Free Caster (easy magic)
  • You know 2 simple spells (e.g. Fire Bolt, Healing Touch).
  • Roll Wits + Arcana. DV is usually 3 for a cool trick, 4 for a big blast.
  • Backlash: If you roll a 1, the GM does something annoying (your robe catches fire, you get pushed back). Improvise it!
Pack 3: The Runekeeper (obligation)
  • You made a deal with a patron. You have 3 special moves (Rites).
  • To use a Rite: roll Spirit + Arcana (DV 3). (The full game rolls rites on Spirit + Lore.)
  • Obligation: Mark a box (track = Spirit + Presence). If it fills, take 1 Fatigue. Clear it by doing a small favor for your patron.
Pack 4: The Summoner (monster buddy)
  • You have a cool monster buddy (spirit). Summon it: spend 1 Boon (or take 1 Fatigue).
  • The buddy has 3 Health (Leash). It can do one simple thing per round (attack, scout, carry).
  • If it gets hit, it loses 1 Health. If it loses all Health, it vanishes and you take 1 Fatigue.
  • Veteran upgrade: track a full “Leash” track — but for kids, it's just “pet with 3 HP.”
Pack 5: The Prodigy (great at one thing)
  • Instead of 1, 2, 2, 3, spread your attributes 1, 2, 2, 4. You are better at one thing than anyone your age has any business being.
  • That 4 does not go up. It is already as good as it gets for a long while. Everything else about you improves normally; the gift waits.
  • People notice. The GM starts every scene with 1 extra Story Beat to spend on you being noticed — someone who wants to test you, hire you, beat you, or warn you.
  • Pick this at the start or not at all. You cannot decide later that you were always gifted.

GM Tips for Mixed-Experience Tables

1. The Spotlight Shift
Rotate the spotlight. Let the simple Fighter roll a huge Body + Melee. Then let the complex Summoner roll Spirit + Arcana. They both roll the same dice, but the narrative looks different.

2. Color the Consequences
When the simple player rolls a 1, the problem is physical (“Your sword gets stuck”). When the complex player rolls a 1, the problem is mystical (“Your patron glares at you”).

3. The “Fiction First” Rule
If a player doesn't understand their Rite, revert to the core rule. “What do you want to happen?” → “I want to make the enemy fall asleep.” → “Cool. Roll Spirit + Arcana. DV 3. If you hit, they sleep. If you roll a 1, they get angry.”

4. Don't Track Resources for Everyone
The kid tracks Boons + Fatigue. The veteran tracks Boons, Fatigue, Leash, and Obligation. That's their job, not yours.

5. End of Session
Give everyone the same XP (e.g. 6 XP). The kid buys a new Stunt; the veteran buys a new Rite. They progress together.
