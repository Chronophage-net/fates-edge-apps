The Bound

Saikou Ira's Compendium of the Veil — Chapter 6

Demons and the Infernal

I use the term demon with reluctance. The entities that the Church of the Everflame calls demons are not a unified species. They are a category of convenience—spirits that refuse to abide by the covenants of mortal society, that cannot be negotiated with in the language of debt and obligation.

A ghost, a ghoul, a lycanthrope – they are endings I can understand. A debt unpaid, a hunger unmet, a rage that forgot its own face. They can be pitied, sometimes even saved. But the things that come through the holes? The ones that were never alive, that owe nothing and take everything?

When I walk into a room where one of these dwells, I smell burnt clove and cold iron. My silver mirror sweats. The Witness goes quiet – as if it, too, is afraid of being seen.

The Demon’s Table of Ranks

The Church classifies by behavior – a practical crutch. I classify by rank, for every demon holds a station in the abyss, whether it remembers it or not. These are the orders I have witnessed at the threshold.

Rank I: The Whisperer (Imp)

What they leave behind: A contract written in invisible ink, a page of accounts with one entry more than you remember signing. They offer power, knowledge, revenge – but always through a bargain witnessed by no one. A contract sealed without a third party is a hole shaped like your own hand. Counter: A Runekeeper of Mykkiel can enforce the letter, but the price is always that the witness becomes the next one who owes.
Rank II: The Ravager (Fury)

What they leave behind: A scorch mark on the wall in the shape of a door, a bell that rings once for every corpse. Summoned by violence or mass death, they do not speak or bargain. They erase. Fire and silver wound them, but the only true banishment is overwhelming witness – three or more souls who saw it arrive and refuse to look away. Counter: The moment the last witness looks down, the Ravager returns.
Rank III: The Possessor (Marquis)

What they leave behind: Nothing at first. Then, slowly, the host’s favorite food tastes like ash; their mother’s face blurs; they wake with their hands around a lover’s throat. Counter: An exorcism witnessed by a representative of the host’s faith – not the Church’s, not mine. The demon must be argued out, not forced.
Rank IV: The Parasite (Duke)

What they leave behind: An emotion that no longer belongs to you. Fear that grows teeth. Grief that walks into the next room and closes the door. Ambition that starves the body. Counter: Starvation – rigorous emotional discipline, sometimes requiring the host to live in a sensory deprivation cell for a moon. I have seen a man forget how to be afraid. He was not saved. He was hollowed.

Above these four ranks lie the Princes of the Abyss, entities of such scale that their names alone would crack the stone of this page. I have never faced one. I pray I never will.

On Harm Levels: none of the four ranks above have any. A possessing demon is not brought down by Harm — the host’s body has its own Harm Levels, but the demon riding it is only removed by naming, witness, and the Sentinel’s rite. Treat every demon in this chapter as a puzzle wearing a person’s shape, not a monster to be worn down in a fight.

Investigation Notes

Demon summoning is forbidden in every nation that has a functioning legal system, and for good reason. Every summoning is a hole in the world.

Demon hunters require three things: silver, fire, and witness. I carry a censer that burns a compound of saltpeter, silver filings, and ironwood resin. The smoke reveals demonic possession: the host’s shadow lags; the demon’s shadow moves ahead.

The Weight of a Name

Every demon has a true name. Not the word it answers to in the abyss – that would kill the exorcist to speak. The true name is the first lie it told to enter the world.

The Whisperer’s name is the clause it hid. The Ravager’s name is the violence it used as a key. The Possessor’s name is the moment the host said “yes” to something they should have refused. The Parasite’s name is the first hunger it learned to wear as a virtue.

To learn the name, I do not summon the demon. I audit the hole. I place a silver mirror at the threshold where it entered, light a censer of saltpeter, silver filings, and ironwood resin, and I ask the absence:

“What was the first thing you borrowed that was not yours?”

The smoke reveals the answer – not in words, but in a memory of the host’s that is not the host’s. I have seen a woman weep a child’s tears for a child she never bore. I have seen a man scream in a voice that was his, but cracked like a bell that had been rung too many times.

The name is always a wound. And once I know it, I owe the demon nothing – but the host will owe me a story they will never tell.

The Sentinel’s Exorcism (A Procedure in Three Mirrors)

This is the rite that never closes. Not because it fails – because it works, and the cost is always visible.

You will need:

  • A silver mirror for the host (polished in moonlight, never in sun).
  • A silver mirror for the demon (kept wrapped in black cloth until the moment).
  • A silver mirror for the exorcist (my own – it has a crack across the left edge. I do not remember when the crack appeared).
  • A circle of salt, iron filings, and powdered bone (the bone must be from a person who died unafraid).
  • A witness – someone who loves the host, someone who fears the demon, and someone who is neutral. (Three is the minimum. I have used seven. The more witnesses, the slower the demon’s lies.)

Step 1: Draw the Circle

[ACTION: 10 MINUTES]

Walk the circle three times, counter-timerwise, sprinkling the salt-iron-bone mix. Each pass, speak the demon’s true name once. The circle will tighten – the air inside will feel cold; the host’s breathing will become shallow.

If the circle hisses: The demon is trying to speak through the host. Do not answer. Answering gives it a thread to pull.

Step 2: Place the Mirrors

[ACTION: 1 ACTION]
  • Host mirror (facing the host).
  • Demon mirror (facing the host’s eyes – the demon’s reflection will appear there, always half a second behind).
  • Exorcist mirror (facing the demon mirror).

The three reflections must form a triangle. I have seen the mirrors crack in sequence when the demon resists. A cracked mirror does not fail – it bites.

[TAG: WITNESS] – The neutral witness must stand at the triangle’s apex. They do not speak. They do not blink. Their presence is the anchor of the seen.

Step 3: The Three Calls

[ROLL: SPIRIT + LORE vs. DV = DEMON’S RANK (4–6)]

First call:

“I name you [TRUE NAME]. You are seen. You are witnessed.”
The host convulses. The demon mirror shows a face that is not the host’s. On a miss: The demon speaks through the host and gains one thread (it can force the host to take one action on its turn).

Second call:

“I name you [TRUE NAME]. You are bound. You are contained.”
The demon mirror sweats. Condensation drips down the silver. On a partial: The demon thrashes – mark 1 FATIGUE on the host. On a miss: The demon breaks eye contact; the exorcist mirror cracks, and the demon gains control for one exchange.

Third call:

“I name you [TRUE NAME]. You are unmade.”
The demon mirror shatters. The shards fly inward, toward the demon’s reflection. The demon screams – not with the host’s voice, but with the sound of a door slamming in a house with no walls.

On a success: The demon is forced into the central mirror (the exorcist’s). The host collapses, unharmed. On a critical success: The demon is shattered so thoroughly that the Witness whispers the name of the person who summoned it. On a failure: The demon possesses the exorcist mirror. I have seen this happen once. The exorcist’s reflection smiled and walked out of the glass. It took three days to trap it again.

Step 4: Seal the Shards

[ACTION: 1 HOUR]

Each shard of the exorcist mirror must be wrapped in black cloth and disposed of in separate bodies of water – a river, a well, a cistern, the sea. The Sentinel seals each shard as it sinks.

“You are distributed. You are forgotten. You are no longer one.”

If any two shards are brought together, the demon can reform. I have spent years tracking down shards from exorcisms I performed decades ago. The debt never fully closes.

Case Study: The Censor’s Daughter (Ecktoria, 859 AR)

I did not want this case. The girl was fourteen. Her father was a Censor – a man who weighed grain and judged contracts, who had never believed in anything except what was owed.

The demon was a Possessor (Marquis). It had been with her for three years, since the night her mother died. The Inquisition had already tried to burn her twice. Both times, the fire would not light.

When I arrived, the girl was sitting in a stone cell, her hands chained to the wall. She did not look at me. The demon looked through her eyes.

I have exorcised demons from seventeen children. I remember sixteen of their faces.

“You are the one who breaks mirrors,” it said.
“I am the one who listens to the hole,” I replied.

I learned its true name not through the girl, but through her father’s own hidden accounts of what he owed. The demon’s name was the weight of a debt the Censor had taken on to pay for his wife’s medicine—passed from hand to hand until no one living could say who was owed, or how much. It had grown into a debt that could never be paid. The demon was that debt, given will.

The exorcism took six hours. Three mirrors. Seven witnesses – because the girl’s father, the Censor, refused to be one of them.

The demon fought hardest at the second call. It used the girl’s voice to recite the names of every person her father had bankrupted. The witnesses wept. I did not look away.

When the third call shattered the mirror, the girl screamed – not in pain, but in relief. Her father confessed to the loansharking. The demon’s shards were scattered into the Dolmis, the cistern of a Mykkielan chapel, a well in a village that no longer exists, and a bottle of ink I keep on my desk.

The girl survived. She works as an archivist now. She wears gloves, because her fingerprints sometimes change.

Her father died in prison, taken for what he owed. It was never paid. It never is.

The Exorcist’s Debt

Every exorcism costs me something. Not fatigue – I can sleep. Not obligation – I owe the Sentinel nothing.

It costs me witness.

After I seal a demon, I lose a memory – not a skill, not a name, but a moment of my life. The demon’s shard takes it as toll. I do not know what I have forgotten until I try to remember.

I know I have a daughter. I cannot recall her face. I know I was a marine. I cannot recall my ship’s name. I know I have been doing this for forty years. I cannot recall why I started.

The Witness does not take these memories. The demon does. The hole that the demon crawled through is also the hole where the past goes. However, demon never takes the memory of an exorcism. They want you to remember. They want you to lie awake at three in the morning with the sound of your own chanting, the weight of the shattering mirror, the face of the host as the demon left their eyes.

That memory is a hook. They can pull it whenever they wish.

One day I will forget my own name. That will be the last exorcism.

But not today.
