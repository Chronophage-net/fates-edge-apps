Saikou Ira – Field Equipment

“I carry no codex. I carry witnesses.”

  • Silver Mirror (cracked on the left edge): Used for the Mirror Array and exorcisms. The crack appeared during an exorcism in Ecktoria. Saikou does not remember which one.
  • Censer (brass, inscribed with the Sigil of the Witness): Burns a compound of saltpeter, silver filings, and ironwood resin. The smoke reveals demonic possession and lingering Echoes.
  • Silver Coin (worn smooth): For the Silver Test (placed on the tongue). It tastes of copper when a vampire, lycanthrope, or demon is near.
  • Bell (bronze, no clapper): Rung to attract the attention of lesser spirits or to mark the boundary of a containment circle. The clapper was lost decades ago; the bell still tolls for Saikou alone.
  • Small Leather Pouch (salt and iron filings): Used to draw containment circles and ward thresholds. The salt is from the Mistlands; the iron was filed from a set of manacles that once held a possessed child.
  • Journal (waterproofed with eel-fat): Contains case notes, autopsy sketches, and the true names of three Class C demons. The pages are numbered in eights; the ninth page of each entry is left blank.
  • Thorn-Pin (silver): Used to prick a possessed subject’s finger. A drop of blood on the pin reveals the demon’s true name as a faint whisper. The pin was a gift from a Daughter of the Cord – a debt he has not yet paid.
  • Knife (curved, no reflection): For cutting cords, opening sealed letters, and, when necessary, cutting short an exorcism gone wrong. The blade does not show in mirrors; Saikou never uses it on the living.
  • Moonsteel Cutlass (curved, no reflection): A sword that culls. A sword that whispers.
I once heard of a Trow who faced three mirrors at a crossroads at dusk. He was forced to trade his own name. Now he wanders, nameless.The Gray Wanderer

Conclusion: On the Necessity of Witness

I have written this compendium not to frighten, but to prepare. The creatures I have described are not myths. They are neighbors, patients, clients, and—sometimes—the faces we see in the mirror when we are alone at midnight.

The Witness has taught me that truth is not a weapon. It is a tool. The truth reveals the weak point, the bargain, the memory that binds the spirit to its hunger.

I have killed. I have saved. I have wept over bodies I could not help and celebrated over curses I could not break.

The work continues.

If you are reading this, you are now a witness. The creatures I have described are real. They are hungry. They are waiting.

But so are we.

The ninth cup is not poured. The ninth bell is not rung. The threshold is held—by you, by me, by the quiet ones who tend the lamps and count the breaths and remember the names that others have forgotten.

Do not look away.

Saikou Ira
Port Shed, Dhaharan Coast
