The Unquiet Dead

Saikou Ira's Compendium of the Veil — Chapter 2

The Spirit Taxonomy

The dead do not remain for a single reason, nor do they manifest in a single form. Over years of investigation—and, I confess, more than a few exorcisms conducted in haste—I have developed a working taxonomy of the unquiet. This is not an academic exercise. The method of laying a spirit to rest depends entirely upon why it remains and how it manifests.

Class I: The Echo

The Echo is not truly a spirit. It is a recording—a psychic imprint left by a traumatic death, repeated endlessly until the conditions that created it are addressed. Echoes do not speak, do not learn, and cannot be negotiated with. They are the scream that plays again at midnight, the footsteps that cross the same corridor at the same hour, the face that appears in the window of a house that burned a century ago.

Investigation Notes: Echoes require witness to fade. A single observer who speaks aloud the truth of the event—who names the dead and describes their end—will often break the loop. If the death was unjust, the Echo may persist until justice is done. In such cases, I have found that a public acknowledgment of the wrong, witnessed by a representative of the community, suffices where private ritual fails.

The Witness’s Technique: When I encounter an Echo, I place a small silvered mirror facing the site of the imprint. The Echo will play once into the glass. Afterward, the mirror may be shattered—each shard holds a fragment of the memory, which can be used in later investigations or ritually cleansed.

Case Study: The Lamplighter of the Third Stack (Aelerian Ranges, 847). A miner’s death in a gas explosion repeated every night at the same hour. The Echo was not the man’s death, but his final attempt to ring the warning bell. We placed a recording mirror at the gallery entrance, then had the mine’s foreman read the names of all who had died in that disaster into the glass. The Echo ceased. The foreman slept peacefully for the first time in thirty years.

Class II: The Anchor

An Anchor is a spirit bound to a specific object, location, or person by unresolved emotion—grief, rage, love, or duty. Unlike the Echo, an Anchor possesses will and memory. It can speak, bargain, and choose to manifest or withhold itself. It is, in essence, a person who has forgotten how to leave.

Variants by Cultural Tradition:

  • The Hearth-Bound (Vilikari, Aelaerem): Spirits of grandparents or parents who refuse to depart until they have ensured the flourishing of their descendants. These Anchors are generally benevolent, though they may become irritable if their advice is ignored repeatedly.
  • The Duty-Bound (Aeler, Kahfagia): Soldiers, watchmen, and sailors who died at their post and continue to perform their duty. I once met a lighthouse keeper who had been dead for seven years. The lamp never went out. He did not speak, but his logbook was written in a hand that was not quite attached to a wrist.
  • The Wronged (Universally encountered): Victims of murder, betrayal, or accident who refuse to pass until their death is acknowledged and, where possible, avenged. These are the most dangerous Anchors, as their grief curdles into something that resembles hunger.

Investigation Notes: An Anchor cannot be exorcised by force without risk of creating a Poltergeist (Class IV). The proper method is negotiation. Determine what the spirit requires to complete its business. Offer witness—a third party who will attest to the resolution. In cases where the spirit’s demand is impossible (the murderer died decades ago, the lover has since remarried), a ritual of substitution may suffice.

The Witness’s Technique: I conduct Anchor interrogations in a room with three mirrors—one facing the spirit, one facing myself, and one placed between us to catch the reflection of the truth. The Anchor cannot lie in the space between two reflections. It may refuse to speak, but it cannot deceive.

Case Study: The Widow of the Red Tower (Vhasia, 852). A noblewoman’s ghost haunted her husband’s estate, refusing to pass until he confessed to poisoning her. I placed three mirrors in the great hall and asked the spirit to show me her death. The reflection did not lie. The husband confessed before dawn. The widow vanished with a smile that I still see in my dreams.

Class III: The Procession

A Procession is not a single spirit, but a route—a path that the dead walk, sometimes for centuries, between two points of significance. Processions are most common in regions where death was sudden and widespread: battlefields, plague pits, shipwreck coasts.

Characteristics:

  • Processions occur on a regular schedule (nightly, monthly, on the anniversary of the disaster).
  • They follow a fixed route that predates the current roads.
  • The spirits within a Procession do not interact with observers. They are marching, not haunting.
  • Interrupting a Procession—by standing in its path, attempting to speak to its members, or building over its route—provokes a violent response from the entire column.

Investigation Notes: Processions are not malevolent, but they are inflexible. The proper response is to observe, document, and—if the route must be altered—negotiate with the destination, not the dead. If a Procession marches to a specific grave or shrine, moving that terminus will often reroute the spirits.

The Traveler’s Intervention: When I need to cross where a Procession walks, I invoke The Traveler. A single pinch of road dust from a crossroads where three murders occurred, blown toward the column, causes the spirits to step aside for exactly the time needed to pass. I have used this technique four times. On three occasions, it worked. On the fourth, the Procession followed me for a week. I do not recommend relying on this method unless you are prepared to run.

Case Study: The Phantom Caravan of the Saffron Pass (Dhahara, 855). A merchant’s new road cut directly across the path of a weekly Procession. The column attacked every merchant who attempted the crossing. I consulted with a Fhara caravaneer, who revealed that the Procession was marching from an old well to a date grove. We moved a single stone—a marker that had served as the terminus—to the side of the new road. The Procession followed the stone. The road was safe by nightfall.

Class IV: The Poltergeist

The Poltergeist is an Anchor that has been corrupted by rage, starvation, or the failure of negotiation. It is no longer a person—it is a wound that has learned to strike back. Poltergeists manifest as telekinetic force, localized environmental disturbances, and, in advanced cases, violent possession of the living.

Stages of Poltergeist Development:

Stage One (The Knock):
Unexplained sounds, objects shifting slightly between observations, drafts in sealed rooms.
Stage Two (The Grasp):
Objects thrown with force, doors slamming, furniture overturned. Living creatures experience nightmares, nausea, and a persistent sense of being watched.
Stage Three (The Hunger):
The Poltergeist begins to feed on fear and pain. It will target specific individuals, escalating from psychological torment to physical harm. Bruises, scratches, and broken bones become common.
Stage Four (The Mask):
The Poltergeist possesses a living host, using their body to commit acts of violence. Exorcism at this stage is dangerous and often fatal to the host.

Exorcism with The Sentinel: The Sentinel’s power is that of the witnessed boundary. I perform the following ritual:

  1. Identify the Anchor’s original location of death or burial. This is the root.
  2. Surround the root with a circle of salt and iron filings, blessed by a representative of the local faith. (The Sentinel does not care which faith; only that the witness is genuine.)
  3. Place a silvered mirror at each cardinal point within the circle, facing outward.
  4. Speak the name of the dead three times—once for acknowledgment, once for judgment, once for release.
  5. Shatter the mirrors simultaneously. The Sentinel seals the break.

This ritual does not destroy the Poltergeist. It returns it to the state of an Anchor, at which point standard negotiation may resume.

Case Study: The Plague House of Port Shed (Dhaharan Coast, 858). A family of Sidhi dockworkers was being terrorized by a Poltergeist in Stage Three. The Anchor was a young woman who had died of a fever three months prior—not violently, but alone, her family having quarantined her. I performed The Sentinel’s containment. At the moment of release, I placed a small hand mirror in the center of the circle, reflecting her own face. The Poltergeist collapsed into a weeping Anchor. She passed into the care of a local medium. She has not returned.

Class V: The Unquiet Dead

Definition – An Unquiet Dead is an Anchor that has progressed beyond violent telekinesis (Class IV) to sentience and presence, but remains bound to a personal object, memory, or unfinished business. It is a wound of grief or longing that has manifestations as subtle, sensory disturbances to outright sentience and the ability to physically attack. The entity is neither truly dead nor fully alive; it is a sentient echo that can perceive the living, but only speaks when the conditions of its attachment are approached.

Typical Anchors –

Jewelry
(lockets, rings, brooches) that once held a loved one’s heart or a promise.
Clothing
(a baby-blanket, a priest’s cassock) steeped in personal memory.
Written items
(a diary, a farewell letter) that contain a final, unfulfilled declaration.

The anchor is the focus of the spirit; when the anchor is moved, disturbed, or left unaddressed for generations the Unquiet Dead awakens.

Signs of Presence (all may be observed without overt danger) –

  • A localised drop in temperature of 2–5 °C, felt only in the immediate proximity of the anchor.
  • A faint, intermittent scent of the person’s favorite perfume, spice, or hearth smoke.
  • Soft weeping or sighing that seems to emanate from the direction of the anchor, but without any visible source.
  • Echoes of a voice saying a name or a question, heard only once per hour and never repeated verbatim.
  • Small objects (a single coin, a thread) that appear on the floor next to the anchor at night.

Stages of Manifestation –

Stage One – Whisper
The anchor vibrates faintly; the first sensory cues (scent, temperature) appear.
Stage Two – Lament
A keening or weeping becomes audible, often at the same hour each night; the anchor may gently shift or float a few centimeters.
Stage Three – Recall
The spirit attempts to communicate directly, repeating a phrase or an unfinished promise. The anchor may move more noticeably (e.g., a locket bobbing in water).
Stage Four – Release
The entity’s purpose is fulfilled; the anchor becomes inert, and the disturbances cease. If the purpose is not achieved, the Unquiet Dead may degenerate into a Poltergeist (Class IV).

Ritual of Release – The Lamentic Binding (performed by a Sentinel, exorcist, or any practitioner who can bear witness) –

  1. Identify the root – the place of death or the site where the promise was broken. It need not be a burial site; a hearth, a battlefield, or the location of a last farewell can serve.
  2. Surround the root with a circle of salt (to ground lingering energy) and a single line of iron filings (to bind emotional charge). The materials need only be pure; no particular faith is required, only the sincere presence of a living witness.
  3. Place the anchor at the center of the circle, facing outward. If the anchor is a piece of jewelry, lay it on a small dish of water; if it is a cloth, drape it over a stone.
  4. Recite the Three-fold Lament:
    1. Acknowledgment – “I see you, spirit of [Name], bound to this [object].”
    2. Judgment – “Your longing is known, but the world has moved on.”
    3. Release – “May your word be carried on the wind, and may you find peace.”
    Speak each line three times, allowing a pause of a heartbeat between repetitions.
  5. As the final Release is spoken, break the dish (or if the anchor is a locket, open it) and let its contents fall into the surrounding salt. The act severs the emotional tether; the spirit fades with a final sigh.

The ritual does not destroy the spirit; it merely returns the Anchor to a state of inert remembrance and allows the Unquiet Dead to pass on. Should the Anchor be destroyed before the ritual, the spirit may become a violent Poltergeist (Class IV) and must be handled accordingly.

Case Study: The Floating Locket of the Black River (Thepyrgos)

The Black River ran dark and slow through Thepyrgos, its waters stripped of moonlight by a thin veil of mist. Mid-river, where the water gathered into a shallow pool behind an ancient stone pier, a single silver locket bobbed on the surface as if cradled by the current itself. The metal was dulled by age, veins of verdigris catching the faint torchlight that flickered from the nearby tavern’s shutters.

When the wind fell silent, a soft, mournful weeping rose from the pool, as though a woman knelt in grief and let her tears dissolve into the water. The sound was neither a sob nor a splash, but a low, resonant sigh that seemed to echo from beneath the riverbed. A faint, pale shape—barely more than a ripple—briefly formed above the locket, a translucent visage of a woman in a gray, thread-bare dress. Her eyes were closed; her shoulders trembled with the weight of an unspoken promise.

The woman’s spirit lingered only a heartbeat. A single, whispered word slipped through the mist: Margot.“ Then the locket rose a fraction of an inch, hovered directly over the water’s surface, and, with a gentle pop, dropped into the dark depths. The weeping ceased instantly, and the river resumed its ordinary, mute flow.

I arrived at the pier moments later, my boots crunching on the riverbank’s pebbles. I found only the still water and the faint scent of lavender—a noblewoman’s perfume—lingering where the locket had vanished. No trace of the spirit remained; the anchor had been reclaimed by the river, completing the promise she had made to return her child’s locket to the place it had been forged.

I recorded the event as ”Case V-01: Unquiet Dead – The Floating Locket.“ I marked the river’s pool as a sanctified site; should anyone later disturb the water at night, the ritual of the Lamentic Binding may be invoked pre-emptively, though the spirit had already found its release.

Thus the Unquiet Dead, bound to a single object of love, concluded its business without violence, leaving only the soft echo of a woman’s grief and a locket’s final descent into the Black River. However, I heard the wailing of another spirit nearby, north, near the crossing of the river.
