Lycanthropes

The werewolf is a creature of paradox: a curse that feels like freedom, a transformation that erases the self, a hunger that is also a family. I have tracked lycanthropes across three continents. I have killed two. I have saved four.

Origins and Cultural Variations

Lycanthropy is a spiritual infection—a fragment of Malachai’s chained essence passed from host to host through violence, but only if the victim is in a state of profound emotional vulnerability.

Linn Sea-Wolf:
Transforms into a massive seal or walrus. Solitary, melancholic, preys on ships.
Ykrul Sky-Hound:
Hawk-headed hunter that transforms during the day. Hunts oath-breakers, not flesh.
Valewood Root-Walker:
Transforms into a tree—rooted, immobile, but all-seeing. Guardians, not predators.
Aeler Stone-Hide:
Massive, slow, nearly invulnerable. Transforms only underground. A mining hazard.

Investigation Notes

  • Silver through the heart or brain kills them.
  • Aconite (wolfsbane) poultice applied within the hour can prevent transformation.
  • The full moon is not a universal trigger; transformation responds to emotional cycles.
  • The Well of Ichor can purge lycanthropy, but the journey to Zahk’Kozahn is itself a trial.

The Witness’s Observation: Lycanthropes who remember their human lives clearly are less dangerous than those who do not. The memory of love acts as a leash on the beast.

Case Study: The Hunters of the Scarlet Wood (Crimson Valley, 862). A pack of feral lycanthropes with no memory. I placed a mirror at the cave entrance. As the first wolf emerged, it saw its own reflection—and for a moment, I saw recognition. That wolf was a young silk-weaver who had gone missing two years prior. I spoke her name. She stopped. We negotiated a quarantine. She now feeds on deer and speaks to her daughter through a silvered glass.

Vampires

Vampires are the eldest children of Malachai, the first to drink from the Chained Angel’s wound.

The Thirteen First Vampires

I have met three of the Thirteen. I will describe them briefly, as a warning.

Baron Quentin Asselin:
The first to drink. Disillusioned, melancholic, almost gentle. Converts guests or drives them mad. Never accept his wine.
Sister Maris:
Runs a children’s hospice. The children never leave. The Everflame has tried to destroy her hospice four times. Each time, the children defended her.
The Sparrow:
Kills only the wicked, or so he claims. Cannot look at mirrors. Has saved my life twice. I suspect he is merely bored.

Lesser Bloodlines

  • Linn Draugr: Breathe underwater, trapped by sea-salt circles.
  • Aeler Stone-Sleepers: Drink breath, awakened by bronze bells at dawn.
  • Ykrul Bone-Eaters: Turn to dust when killed, but rise if their name is spoken. Erase the name.
  • Vhasian Candle-Wights: Extinguish flames through windows. A lit candle at the threshold bars them.
  • Lethai-ar Silk-Drinkers: Feed on desire. Vulnerable to vows witnessed by a third party.

The Sentinel’s Ward: When I must enter a vampire’s lair, I invoke The Sentinel to bless the threshold of my mind. A silver mirror worn over the heart, inscribed with the sigil of the Sealed Gate, prevents the vampire’s hypnotic gaze from finding purchase. I have walked into the dens of three vampires wearing that mirror. Only once did I have to run.

Case Study: The Court Painter of Mbaro (Oshiira, 856). A Silk-Drinker feeding on nobles’ ambition. I confronted him in the Widow’s weigh-house. He did not flee. He wept and confessed. The Widow commuted his death sentence to indefinite confinement in a silver-lined cell. He paints the Widow’s portrait each year. She has not aged in seven years. I do not know if this is related.
