World Travel & Gateways

“Every Diamond you earn is a tooth knocked out of someone else’s jaw. Every Debt you incur? I already bought it from your victim for half what you’ll pay – and I’m collecting interest in your marrow before you finish reading this sentence. The road is not just a ledger. It’s a butcher’s block.” — The Edgewalker (as recorded by Dusana)

“Dusana wrote that down because he says it well, not because it is true. I have crossed forty of these gateways. Not one of them was a throat. Several were somebody’s whole life’s work, kept open in bad weather by people whose names he did not ask for.” — Mira of the Wandering Circle, in the margin of Dusana’s copy

Global Travel Procedures

Core Travel Procedure

For each leg of a journey, draw 3–4 cards using the decks for your destination and controlling authority.

  • ♠ from the destination deck: sets the scene (place).

  • ♡ from the destination deck: introduces the local actor or faction.

  • ♣ from the Wilds (general hazards) or destination (if strongly policed): brings pressure.

  • ♢ from the authority that gates the route: papers, escorts, rights, or exceptions.

Set a travel timer by the highest rank:

  • 2–5 ⇒ 4 segments

  • 6–10 ⇒ 6 segments

  • J/Q/K ⇒ 8 segments

  • A ⇒ 10 segments

On success, advance to the next leg; on failure, mark delay, debt, or diversion and resolve a consequence in the fiction.

Mode Rules

Sea Legs

(Amaranthine, Dolmis, Aberderrin, Gulf of Oshiira, Inland Sea). When crossing international waters, the controlling authority is either the origin port’s navy or a neutral convoy league.

Mountain Passes & Underways

(Aeler, Himadri, Taharkan highlands). Any A may convert a surface route to an under-route. ♢ from Aeler always codifies outcomes — no roll — for sealed doors, breath time, and escorted segments.

Desert Legs

(Sahel, Red Desert, Fhara drylands). Water is the primary resource. A dry well hazard can end a journey.

River Legs

(Belworth, Ayokhan rivers, Oshiiran canals). Bridges, booms, and law in Vhasia/Viterra; reed-mazes and bell-lines in Mistlands; canal mothers and weigh-houses in Oshiira.

Steppe Legs

(Violet Steppe, Kuvani grazing lands). Remount stations and grazing compacts. The Ykrul and Kuvani both use story tolls.

Shadow Corridors

(Valewood, Ways Between, Thin Shore). Add a regional taboo: never count to nine, never speak the ninth name. A 9 in the seed triggers the Hollow’s attention.

Universal Travel Hazards (d18)

When drawing a Club from the Wilds deck or from a region’s own hazard table, you may roll on this expanded table for additional flavor. These hazards integrate elements from the Eastern Realms, Dhahara, and the Way of Silk expansions.

d18 Hazard
1 Bell-Line Failure (Mistlands) – A ward-bell cracks. Wraiths cross the levee. Test Spirit + Endurance (DV 4) or gain Marked by Mist (next spirit encounter starts Desperate).
2 Star-Road Phasing (Valewood) – An ancient Lethai road flickers. Navigation DV +2; on failure, emerge in a different hex (GM chooses).
3 Spirit-Dust Storm (Ashaan) – Red sand whispers. All actions Desperate for the scene. On any 1, a bound spirit breaks free (Cap 2).
4 Ninth Night (Tulkani Circle) – The Hollow walks. A Oath-Keeper demands a story, memory, or name. Refusal starts a Hollow Attention timer [4].
5 Well-Spirit Offended (Fhara) – A dry well flows bitter water. Spirit demands apology (dates or a true story). Failure poisons the party’s water supply.
6 Tariff Shift (Silkstrand/Brass Coast) – Customs official declares new duties. All trade goods cost +1 Measure until next port. Bribe (Presence+Sway, DV 4) avoids the hike.
7 Dry Well – The oasis keeper is dead. Lose 1 Supply. (Original entry)
8 Ghost Caravan – Spectral camels pass at midnight. One of your beasts becomes a spirit for an hour.
9 Debt Collector – Someone recognizes a Tulkani vow-cord you carry. Pay in story or a favor to be named later.
10 Sandstorm – Navigation DV +1; mark Fatigue.
11 Customs Inspection – Roll Papers or start a 4-timer Audit.
12 Oasis Court – A dispute over water rights. Mediate or be delayed.
13 Elephant Stampede – Test Stealth or take Harm 1.
14 Mirage – False well; lose 1 Supply and mark Fatigue.
15 Ashfall – Distant volcano; all actions Desperate.
16 Singing Dunes – Sand hums. A spirit offers a riddle; solve for a Clue.
17 The Ninth Hour – Sunset lasts twice as long. GM gains 1 SB to spend in the next scene.
18 Widow’s Omen – Distant thunder; the party dreams of ledgers and death.

Edgewalker’s Notes on Leverage

Every Diamond you earn in these generators isn’t a reward—it’s a weapon. Some open doors. Some silence witnesses. Some are debts I’ve already bought from your victims.

  • Passage Tokens: Pass-stones, harbour charters, toll waivers. Use them early—they tend to “expire” when the issuer has an unfortunate accident.

  • Seals & Stamps: Official marks that prove you belong. A forged seal works once. A real one works until someone audits it.

  • Story Debts: Tulkani vow-cords, Lethai-ar witness cords, Ukwe leaves. Break them, and the Hollow visits.

  • Spirit Objects: Storm-charms, tide-beads, nkisi shards. They attract attention. Attention means bids.

Never use a Diamond if you can trade it for three smaller ones. Always keep a ninth—a secret, a story, a knot tied in a victim’s hair—for when the toll exceeds your conscience. (It always does.)

Second Opinions

The section above is the Edgewalker’s, and it is the most useful page in this book if what you want is to win. The other three voices from the preface were shown it. Their replies are printed here because a GM who runs Diamonds only his way will produce a campaign in which every reward is a weapon and every NPC is a mark, and that campaign gets thin by the third session.

Mira of the Wandering Circle:

A Diamond is not a weapon and it is not a reward. It is somebody handing you a thing, which means it comes with the reason they handed it over. Ask what the giver wanted you to do with it. Half the time they will tell you, and half the time what they tell you is a lie you can use. Nothing on that list of his says who is out one pass-stone now that you have it.

Ume Nineterrace, Rota-Keeper:

Most Diamonds in these tables are somebody’s chore made portable. A pass-stone is a week somebody walked. A witness cord is a night somebody sat up. Treat them as consumables and you are burning other people’s labour, which is fine, and is also exactly why the third village will not open the gate for you. Spend one and ask, at the table, out loud, who now has to do that work twice.

Naru of the Fourth Terrace:

His advice to always keep a ninth is good tactics and a bad life. A party that never spends its last leverage is a party that has decided in advance it will need it, and will arrange the fiction until it does. Once a campaign, spend the ninth on something that gains you nothing. Watch what happens to the table.
