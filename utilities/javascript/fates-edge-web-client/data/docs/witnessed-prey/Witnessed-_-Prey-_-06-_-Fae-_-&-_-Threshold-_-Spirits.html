Fae & Threshold Spirits

The Witnessed Prey, a Hunter's Guide — Chapter 6

“The fae do not lie. They simply keep a different reckoning. What you call a trick, they call a fair price.” —Aqyl, son of Aqyl

The fae are not monsters. They are neighbors. They live in the hedge, the hollow, the space between the ninth step and the tenth. They borrow milk. They leave small gifts. They remember every promise you forget.

Trow—Shadow Bargainers

Enigmatic fey who keep to thresholds and almost-there spaces. Most hint at an unnamed patron—the Gray Benefactor, the Under-Guest—but never speak that allegiance twice beneath the same roof.

  • TL 6. Incorporeal (when they choose), Intelligent, Bound by Courtesy.
  • Harm: 4. Fatigue: Spirit.
  • Traits: Shadow-Step—move between witnessed thresholds in Near as a move action. Night-Guest Writ—once per leg, declare dusk-right between two duskmarks; hostile checks to bar passage suffer DV +1 unless they bring a counter-witness.
  • Clock: Gray Courtesy [4]—ticks each time players skip lamp, bread, or salt. On fill, hospitality voids; all bargains turn literal.
  • Resolution: Bread-and-salt under a lifted lamp binds the Trow to guest-right for one night and one truthful answer. Payment must be in honest memory.
  • Harm Levels: 8 (advanced).

Neighbors—The Hollow’s Kin

The Neighbors are a category of attention—the forest’s awareness of intruders, manifesting as silence, footsteps that aren’t there, a voice that speaks your language but does not belong to any throat.

  • TL 3. Incorporeal, Attentive, Patient.
  • Harm: Cannot be harmed. Fatigue: None.
  • Traits: Silence—in the presence of a Neighbor, all sound is muffled. The Watching—a character who fails a Endurance test (DV 4) in a Neighbor’s presence gains the Condition Marked—the Neighbor knows their location for the rest of the scene.
  • Clock: Neighbor’s Patience [6]—ticks each time a taboo is broken (ninth cup, ninth step, unoffered red thread). When full, the Neighbor manifests as an Oath-Keeper.
  • Resolution: Speak a truth about yourself that you have never told anyone.
  • Harm Levels: None. A Neighbor cannot be harmed — it is a puzzle of attention and taboo, not a fight.

Green Court Fae

The courts of the Hazel Queen and the Alder King are not places; they are obligations.

Hazel Queen’s Emissary

A fae courtier wearing leaves and law. Their skin is the color of hazel bark; their eyes are acorns.

  • TL 6. Corporeal, Courteous, Bound by Law.
  • Harm: 4. Fatigue: Spirit.
  • Traits: Leaf and Law—the Emissary cannot lie, but they may omit. Courteous Snare—any gift accepted from the Emissary creates a Reckoning Timer [4].
  • Resolution: Present a hazel token—a nut cracked in the presence of a witness.
  • Harm Levels: 8 (advanced).

Lady of Thorns

Sovereign of a hawthorn court. Smiles like a snare, forgives in exact measures. She has never lied. She has never needed to.

  • TL 9. Corporeal, Courteous, Absolute.
  • Harm: 8 (what she deals, if it ever comes to that). Fatigue: Spirit.
  • Traits: Thorn Sentence—once per scene, the Lady may declare a character “bound by courtesy.” The character cannot take hostile actions for one exchange unless they speak a truth that wounds them.
  • Resolution: Cannot be fought. Cannot be bargained with on unequal terms. Offer a gift of equal value to what you seek.
  • Harm Levels: None. The Lady of Thorns is an Avatar-tier power — she is a puzzle of courtesy and equal exchange, not a fight to be won by attrition.

The Hollow’s Oath-Keeper

The Oath-Keeper is not a spirit of a person. It is the Hollow’s attention given shape. It appears when a character ignores three omens.

  • TL 8. Incorporeal, Inexorable, Patient.
  • Harm: Cannot be harmed. Fatigue: None.
  • Traits: Demand—the Oath-Keeper asks a single question: “What do you owe?” The character must answer truthfully. Mounting Toll—each time the Oath-Keeper appears, what is owed grows.
  • Clock: Hollow’s Patience [8]—ticks each time a debt is ignored. When full, the Oath-Keeper claims the name of the one who owes.
  • Resolution: Pay the original debt—in coin, in kind, or in a story worth as much as what was first taken.
  • Harm Levels: None. The Oath-Keeper cannot be harmed — it is a puzzle of debt and truth-telling, not a fight.
