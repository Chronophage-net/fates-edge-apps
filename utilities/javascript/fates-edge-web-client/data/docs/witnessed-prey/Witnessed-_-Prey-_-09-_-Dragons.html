Dragons

The Witnessed Prey, a Hunter's Guide — Chapter 9

Dragons are not beasts. Drakes are not dragons. The first are epochs with teeth—thinking sovereignties whose slumbers shape kingdoms. The second are animals: clever, dangerous, and sometimes supernatural, but animals nonetheless. Between them stand the dragon-kin, the mortal creatures who have traded breath, blood, or oath for a share of draconic fire.

This chapter is not a complete bestiary. It is a field guide to the three kinds of scaled things that matter most in a Fate’s Edge campaign: the wise, the wild, and the sworn.

Dragons — The Thinking Calamity

A dragon is a force of narrative gravity. When one wakes, borders redraw themselves. When one dies, the weather remembers. They do not hunt for food alone; they hunt for tribute, for stories, for debts that nations have forgotten.

Use the following template when an elder dragon must be statted. Treat the dragon as a setting choice first and a combatant second.

Elder Dragon Template

Tier: IV–V Harm: 6–8 Armor: 3–4

Harm Levels: An elder dragon has no Hit Points and no single pool to grind down. Run it multi-phasic: 8 Harm Levels per phase (Waking, Wrath, Last Coil, or whatever three beats the lair demands), each phase changing the lair itself when it ends.

Tags: Ward, Flame, Flier, Tyrant, Ancient

Dragon Moves (choose two or three)

  • Reposition the World. Stairs invert, Near becomes Far, archways swallow pursuit.
  • Demand the Tithe. The lair asks a price—blood, vow, name, or memory.
  • Answer with Weather. The dragon emotes through storm, flood, quake, or frost for one beat.
  • Arouse the Hoard. Treasures animate to defend the dragon’s history.
  • Close the Eye. Vision narrows; ranged actions become Desperate until the characters change position.

Waking Violation: Name the one act that wakes the dragon immediately. Common violations include broken pacts, stolen heart-pieces, fire-rites in frozen lands, or a king who forgets fear.

Drakes — The Animal Fire

Drakes are the living barometer of frontier fate. They are not puzzles; they are omens. Clever play, bribes, and geasa matter more than raw harm. A drake does not scheme like a dragon; it reacts like a beast—territorial, hungry, and superstitious.

Running a Drake

When the party meets a drake, ask three questions:

  1. What territory does it claim?
  2. What has disturbed it?
  3. What does it want that is not meat?

A drake can often be bought off with food, a shiny object, a draconic geis, or a show of submission. Killing one may draw the attention of a true dragon.

Dragon-Kin — The Mortal Flame

Dragon-kin are mortals who carry the wyrm’s shadow. Some gain power through pacts; others are shaped by worship, service, or bloodline curse.

Runekeepers

A Runekeeper bargains with an ancient wyrm for magical power. The wyrm becomes a mystic patron. The price is always more than gold.

Pact Price (Obligation):

  • Protect a treasure, name, land, or secret.
  • Enforce a vow sworn by others.
  • Extend the dragon’s influence through fear, tribute, or renown.
  • Once per season, deliver a worthy offering.

Refusing a demand marks +2 Obligation. Betraying a law awakens the wyrm.

Sample Rites:

  • Spark of the First Flame. Ignite a melee weapon for one scene.
  • Scaled Skin. Gain Armor 1 against mundane attacks.
  • Dragon’s Gaze. Force a creature to obey a short, simple command.
  • Crown of Scales. Gain Armor 2 and immunity to fire for one scene.
  • Molten Breath. Exhale a cone of flame for area harm.

Dragon Cults

A dragon cult is built from three pillars:

  1. Myth Engine. What story powers the cult?
  2. Scarce Rite. What can only initiates do?
  3. Territorial Claim. Where does its influence hold?

Cults move slowly. Track three timers:

  • Infiltration [6]. Converts militia, merchants, clergy, children.
  • Manifestation [8]. Tries to physically call, awaken, or anchor a dragon.
  • Rupture [10]. Success changes local law, season, or geography.

Rites (sample):

  • Ember-Send. Burn a sealed scrap; smoke carries a whispered message.
  • Scaled Veil. Initiates appear as favored kin, inspiring dread.
  • Hoard-Calling. Metal leaps to form a nest-mound.
  • Scale-Dawn. A spectral draconic shape blots the sun; panic spreads.

Terrestrial Allegiances

Mortal patrons use dragons the way dragons use kingdoms. Build a terrestrial allegiance quickly:

  1. Who? A guildmaster, commodore, magistrate, archivist, or duchess.
  2. What do they want? Bind the dragon, monopolize a tithe, recover a stolen oath, or weaponize dragon-weather.
  3. Boon. Choose one: Seal of Passage, Leveraged Favor, Licensed Rite, Logistics Surge, Heraldry of Safe-Conduct.
  4. Obligation. Pay a tithe, carry their mark, share first pick, or do not slay the dragon without their assent.
  5. Escalation Track. Notice → Audit → Sanction → Seizure.

Knightly Orders and Geasa

Knightly orders are political weathervanes as much as warriors. A geis is the sharp edge of honor.

Swearing a Geis:

  1. Propose concrete terms with a trigger and a limit.
  2. Choose a seal: blood, name, relic, or witness.
  3. Mark Obligation segments per magnitude.
  4. Gain a situational tag or bonus die in the vowed context.
  5. Breaking the geis triggers Backlash and ticks all segments at once.

Lairs and Hoards at a Glance

A dragon’s domain is alive. A dragon’s hoard is a biography. Use this quick build procedure when the party stumbles into scaled territory.

Quick Lair Build

  1. Choose a domain: volcanic caldera, storm fortress, glacier cathedral, sunken metropolis, ruined city crown, etc.
  2. Pick one lair heart: Hoard Chamber, Throne of Element, Court of Oaths, Egg Crypt, Observatory of Scars, Ossuary Forge.
  3. Choose two approaches/hazards: knife-ledge path, ashfall, mirror-pool maze, siren vents, glass rain, avalanche memory.
  4. Pick one or two denizens: Drake-Knights, Hoard-Wyrds, Sky Serpents, Herald Gargoyles, Ember Imps.
  5. State the tithe and the violation.
  6. Choose two lair moves.

Quick Hoard Build

  1. Choose one item from each stratum: Coin, Art, Name/Oath, Cursed Complication.
  2. Assign one or two tags as properties.
  3. State a Hoard Law: who may touch what, and what happens when it is broken.

Spending hoard treasure does not buy grain. It buys exceptions: a favor, a seal of transit, a year of silence, a cleared name, or a writ of passage. Each time treasure is spent this way, tick Dragon’s Attention [6]. At six ticks, the wyrm knows where its wealth went.

Adventure Seeds

  • The Sleeping Crown. A dragon older than the capital lies beneath the city’s foundation. The wards fail one by one. The dragon does not want vengeance; it wants a throne rebuilt.
  • Ash Above the Orchard. A village prospers because a young scarlet drake nests in the orchard caves. Then an elder dragon comes to reclaim its “child.”
  • A Tax of Wings. Every spring, black-scaled tithe-collectors darken the sky. The tyrant-dragon is gone, but its brood still comes. The nobility offered the pact willingly, long ago.
  • The Knight With No Shadow. A famous commander never casts a shadow because his shadow is a dragon bound in human form. The dragon does not want freedom; it wants the knight’s body.
