The Unquiet Dead

The Witnessed Prey, a Hunter's Guide — Chapter 2

“The dead do not forget. They do not forgive. But they will negotiate—if you remember to count to eight before you speak.” —Saikou Ira

The dead are not a single kind of hunger. This section catalogs the unquiet: from the faint echo of a scream repeated at midnight to the poltergeist that has learned to count your breaths. Some cannot understand that they have died. Others defend a home that no longer stands, repeat a terror they cannot escape, or deliberately torment the living. Discover what keeps this spirit here; an apology cannot comfort an echo that has no mind to hear it.

The Spirit Taxonomy (After Saikou Ira)

Ira’s taxonomy is not a scientific classification; it is a pragmatic record of how the dead manifest and how they may be quieted.

Class I: The Echo

Not truly a spirit—a recording. A psychic imprint left by a traumatic death, repeated endlessly. Echoes do not speak, do not learn, and cannot be negotiated with.

  • TL 2. Incorporeal, Repetitive, Trapped.
  • Harm: Cannot be harmed directly. Fatigue: None.
  • Harm Levels: None. An Echo is resolved by witness, not violence.
  • Traits: Incorporeal—cannot be touched, cannot touch. Repetitive—performs the same sequence of actions every cycle, with no variation. Trapped—cannot leave the site of its imprint.
  • Clock: Echo’s Loop [4]—when filled, the Echo expands its range.
  • Resolution: Witness a truth about the death (DV 3) to banish permanently. A single observer who speaks aloud the truth of the event—who names the dead and describes their end—will often break the loop.

Class II: The Anchor

A spirit bound to a specific object, location, or person by unresolved emotion—grief, rage, love, duty. Unlike the Echo, an Anchor possesses will and memory.

  • TL 3–4. Incorporeal, Intelligent, Bound.
  • Harm: 3. Fatigue: Spirit.
  • Harm Levels: 3 (standard) at TL 3–4; 8 (advanced) at TL 5.
  • Traits: Manifestation (DV 3, Fatigue 1)—the Anchor becomes visible and may speak. Poltergeist Shove (DV 4, Harm 1)—telekinetic force.
  • Clock: Anchor’s Patience [6]—when filled, the Anchor becomes a Poltergeist (Class IV).
  • Resolution: Fulfill the Anchor’s unfinished business, or perform a witnessed substitution ritual (DV 4).

Class IV: The Poltergeist

An Anchor that has been corrupted by rage, starvation, or the failure of negotiation. It is no longer a person—it is a wound that has learned to strike back.

  • TL 5–5. Incorporeal, Raging, Hungry.
  • Harm: 4. Fatigue: Spirit.
  • Harm Levels: 8 (advanced).
  • Traits: Telekinetic Throw (DV 3, Harm 2), Possession Attempt (DV 4, Spirit+Resist or Controlled). Aura of Fear—all actions Desperate within Near range.
  • Clock: Poltergeist Escalation [8]—when filled, the spirit reaches Stage Four (The Mask): possession of a living host.
  • Resolution: Containment ritual (The Sentinel’s mirror rite) followed by negotiation with the underlying Anchor.

Ghoul (Starved Anchor)

An Anchor that is denied resolution for too long may begin to consume the life force of the living to sustain its own existence. These ghouls retain their intelligence and memory.

  • TL 3–4. Corporeal, Hungry, Intelligent.
  • Harm: 4. Fatigue: Body.
  • Harm Levels: 3 (standard) at TL 3–4; 8 (advanced) at TL 5.
  • Traits: Claw (DV 2, Harm 1 + Fatigue 1), Bite (DV 3, Harm 2 + poison), Regeneration—ignore first 2 Harm each scene.
  • Clock: Hunger [6]—when filled, the ghoul attacks indiscriminately.
  • Resolution: Silver weapon to the heart, or fire. Negotiation possible if the ghoul is fed.

Spectral Procession (Class III)

A Procession is not a single spirit, but a route—a path that the dead walk, sometimes for centuries, between two points of significance. Do not stand in their way.

  • TL 6 per spirit (treat as a swarm). Incorporeal, Processional, Unresponsive.
  • Harm: Cannot be fought; each spirit has Harm 2, but they move in a column of 1d20.
  • Harm Levels: None. A Procession is resolved by redirecting its route, not by violence.
  • Traits: Marching—the spirits follow a fixed route that predates current roads. Unresponsive—they do not speak or react unless interrupted.
  • Clock: Procession’s Gait [8]—ticks each time the route is blocked or diverted. When full, the column becomes a Poltergeist swarm.
  • Resolution: Observe, document, and—if the route must be altered—negotiate with the destination, not the dead.
