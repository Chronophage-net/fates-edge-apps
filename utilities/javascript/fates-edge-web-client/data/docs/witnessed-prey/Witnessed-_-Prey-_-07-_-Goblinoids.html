Varkh

“A name learned from a frightened sailor is worth recording. It is not necessarily the name of the people he saw.”

—Lerris Fenwood, notes on travelers’ accounts

The Varkh are a family of mortal peoples whose principal homelands lie in the distant west, across the Great Sea and outside the known world. They have children, languages, farms and rival powers. They are not born from broken promises or dreamed into existence by the Hunger Beneath. The few accounts that reach the east disagree about their western countries; a northern captive’s testimony cannot fill that gap.

GM comparison: Varkh covers the familiar goblin, hobgoblin and bugbear spectrum: small, quick folk; tall, broad-jawed folk; and large, furred folk with a remarkable capacity for moving quietly. These are related peoples, not stages of growth, ranks in a universal army, or three temperaments. The familiar fantasy labels are rules comparisons. Use Varkh, a local people’s name, or a character’s own name in the world.

There are no ordinary Varkh populations scattered through the known world’s roads, cities or underways. The Grímhúð of the far north are an isolated population of the tall branch, separated from the main western populations. Their oath-clans, jaw tattoos, filed teeth and dealings with the Winter Hall are local history and custom. They do not define all Varkh, or even all communities of the tall branch. The smaller and furred branches are not additional inhabitants of the Howling Ice’s northern settlements.

The Linn call these northerners “dog-faced.” It is a slur, sustained by songs and carvings that turn a projecting jaw into a canine muzzle. It is neither an anatomical description nor a Varkh self-name. A Linn speaker may use it; a GM’s neutral description should use a personal or clan name. The Grímhúð have their own disagreements, households and reasons for fighting. Their isolation does not make them a surviving sample of a single unchanged western culture.

The profiles below describe possible adversaries in a western expedition. The warrior profile can also support an appropriate Grímhúð encounter; fit its equipment and training to the particular person. A skirmisher, soldier or stalker is an occupation, not the purpose of a whole people. The supernatural variants and Hunger material are separate options, not an account of Varkh origins.

Varkh Skirmishers

Description: A small, quick Varkh scout with sharp teeth, patched clothes and equipment chosen to travel lightly. The short blade and climbing line have been maintained; the bent fork may simply be for supper. This profile suits a scavenger crew, a border scout or a raider in the distant western lands.

One scout is a modest combat threat. A dozen who know the ravine can pin down a much stronger company. Give them somewhere to withdraw and something to protect or carry away. Their small stature does not make them expendable to one another.

Varkh Skirmisher NPC Template

TL: 2–3 | Corporeal, Quick, Opportunistic

  • Resilience: 3 (standard)

  • Dice: Stealth 4d (Wits 2 + Stealth 2), Melee 3d (Body 2 + Melee 1), Subterfuge 4d (Wits 2 + Deception 2), Notice 3d (Wits 2 + Insight 1)

  • Harm: 2 (knife or sharpened tool)

  • Armor: Step 0 — None (scavenged cloth, no conversion)

Traits:

  • Scout’s Cunning — The GM may spend 1 SB so a Varkh skirmisher that only Partially fails to stay hidden or bluffs its way through slips free anyway, vanishing into cover or getting the benefit of the doubt.

  • Nimble Escape — When the Varkh skirmisher would take Harm, the GM may spend 1 SB to have it Disengage and move one range band instead, avoiding the hit without provoking Opportunity Attacks.

  • Scavenger’s Eye — Varkh skirmishers can spot hidden or overlooked objects with a Wits + Insight test (DV 2). Success reveals a useful item, a hidden door, or a clue in the scene.

Etiquette: This crew values quick thinking. Solving a practical problem or spotting a trap grants +1 Position in negotiation; overwhelming force grants +1 Position in intimidation. Decide what the speaker actually wants before treating either approach as persuasive.

Complications: A scout may risk a retreat to recover a companion, conceal a rival’s mistake or demand the return of a stolen tool. The crew’s loyalties need not follow the party’s estimate of who is most useful.

Resolution: For a hungry, lightly equipped scouting party, the following approaches may secure cooperation:

  • Bribe: Spend 1 Boon to offer food, shiny objects, or a promise of safety. The Varkh skirmisher leaves or provides a minor service.

  • Threaten: Succeed on a Presence + Sway test (DV 3). On a Clean Success, the Varkh skirmisher flees. On a Partial, it flees but takes something on the way out.

  • Trick: Succeed on a Wits + Deception test (DV 3). On success, the Varkh skirmisher is outsmarted and leaves embarrassed. On a Miss, the GM gains 2 SB and the Varkh skirmisher becomes hostile.

Varkh Skirmisher Variants

Optional western variants follow. The deepway population has adapted to its habitat; the ember, tide and star profiles describe magical alterations. None establishes a colony in the known world, and none belongs to the Grímhúð by default.

Deepway Varkh (Western Deepways)

Description: Pale, blind, and skittering. Deepway Varkh have adapted to the darkness of the deep ways. They navigate by sound and vibration, and they are fast.

Stats: TL 2. Corporeal, Blind, Fast.
Harm: 2. Fatigue: Body 1.

Traits:

  • Echolocation — Immune to visual stealth. Can detect any creature moving within Near range automatically.

  • Skittering Charge — Once per scene, the Varkh skirmisher may move two range bands and attack in the same turn without Fatigue.

  • Light Blindness — In bright light, the Varkh skirmisher suffers -1 die to all actions.

Ember-Touched Varkh (Optional Fire-Touched Variant)

Description: Blackened skin, smoldering eyes. These western Varkh have been altered by a magical fire. Establish the local disaster or bargain that caused it; it is not an inherited trait of their people.

Stats: TL 2. Corporeal, Smoldering, Unstable.
Harm: 2. Fatigue: Body 1.

Traits:

  • Smoldering Touch — On a successful melee attack, the target marks 1 Fatigue (heat).

  • Ember Burst — When the Varkh skirmisher is killed, it explodes in a burst of ash and heat. All creatures in Close range must test Body + Athletics (DV 3) or mark 1 Fatigue.

  • Water Vulnerability — A splash of water deals Harm 2 to the Varkh skirmisher and forces a Spirit + Endurance test (DV 3) or it flees.

Tide-Touched Varkh (Optional Western Shore Variant)

Description: Webbed fingers, seaweed hair, salt-crusted skin. Tide-touched Varkh patrol the tide-lines, scavenging what the sea brings in.

Stats: TL 2. Corporeal, Amphibious, Opportunistic.
Harm: 2. Fatigue: Body 1.

Traits:

  • Amphibious — Can breathe water and move at normal speed while swimming.

  • Tide-Cunning — While in or near water, the Varkh gains +1 die to Stealth and Athletics.

  • Drowning Grasp — Once per scene, the Varkh skirmisher may attempt to drag a target underwater (Body + Athletics vs DV 3). On success, the target marks 1 Fatigue and is pulled one range band toward the water.

Star-Touched Varkh (Optional Western High-Pass Variant)

Description: Dust-gray, with eyes that reflect constellations. Star-touched Varkh live in the high passes, where the air is thin and the stars are close.

Stats: TL 2. Corporeal, Star-Touched, Unstable.
Harm: 2. Fatigue: Body 1.

Traits:

  • Star-Struck — Once per scene, the Varkh skirmisher may reroll a failed Notice or Lore roll. The GM gains 1 SB.

  • Starlight Shimmer — In starlight, the Varkh skirmisher has Dominant Position on Stealth rolls.

  • Star-Fall — When the Varkh skirmisher is killed, it leaves behind a small shard of crystal that glows faintly in the dark. The shard can be used as a minor light source or sold for 1 Supply.

Varkh Warriors

Description: A tall, broad-jawed Varkh soldier in well-kept armor, carrying a sword or polearm. This profile represents training and a practiced company. It does not make every member of the tall branch a soldier.

Some western powers field disciplined legions. The isolated Grímhúð have their own harpoon crews, sled guards and household fighters; do not give them a distant legion’s ranks, uniform or beliefs merely because their ancestry is related. Replace plate with locally appropriate protection when the encounter calls for it, using the chosen Armor Step.

Varkh Warrior NPC Template

TL: 3–5 | Corporeal, Disciplined, Tactical

  • Resilience: 3 (standard); an especially large or veteran band’s leader may use 8 (advanced)

  • Dice: Melee 6d (Body 3 + Melee 3), Command 5d (Presence 3 + Sway 2), Athletics 5d (Body 3 + Athletics 2), Endurance 5d (Body 3 + Endurance 2)

  • Harm: 4 (sword or polearm)

  • Armor: Step 2 — Medium (scavenged plate)

Traits:

  • Veteran Discipline — Once per scene, the GM may spend 1 SB to have the Varkh warrior shrug off a stated Fatigue penalty for one exchange, fighting on as if unhindered.

  • Tactical Awareness — Varkh warriors cannot be surprised while their band is intact. They gain +1 Position on the first action of any combat.

  • Oath-Broken Armor — Varkh warriors are bound by a silent oath to their band. If their band is broken (half or more incapacitated), they suffer -1 die to all actions until they retreat or reform.

Etiquette: A veteran wants to know whether an offer can be carried out. State the service, the route and who will answer if it fails. Orders from a stranger are not automatically persuasive; demonstrated competence may be.

Complications: The company may have sworn to defend a place its employer has abandoned, or its captain may want a truce the younger fighters resent. Settle what holds this particular band together. It can break, surrender or change allegiance for reasons its members understand.

Resolution: For an armed company holding a road, use these possible approaches:

  • Earn Respect: Succeed on a Combat or Command test (DV 4) in front of the Varkh warrior. On success, negotiate terms at Controlled Position.

  • Pay in Service: Offer to perform a task or service in exchange for passage or aid. The GM sets a Progress Timer (4—6 segments) representing the task.

  • Oath of Blood: Spend 1 Boon to bind a temporary oath. The Varkh warrior will not attack you or your allies for one scene.

Varkh Stalkers

Description: A large, furred Varkh hunter whose quiet movement is startling at that size. Broad hands keep the weapon close to the body; pauses are long enough for the forest to settle again. The profile suits a practiced western stalker, not every member of the furred branch.

Choose a quarry and a reason: food, a dangerous animal, a fugitive, an enemy scout. The hunter may have a home to return to and companions waiting elsewhere. Silence while stalking says little about conversation beside that hunter’s own fire.

Varkh Stalker NPC Template

TL: 5 | Corporeal, Silent, Patient

  • Resilience: 8 (advanced)

  • Dice: Melee 8d (Body 4 + Melee 4), Stealth 7d (Wits 4 + Stealth 3), Athletics 7d (Body 4 + Athletics 3), Endurance 7d (Body 4 + Endurance 3)

  • Harm: 5 (claws or heavy weapon)

  • Armor: Step 1 — Light (thick fur)

Traits:

  • Hunter’s Patience — The GM may spend 1 SB so a Varkh stalker that only Partially fails to stay hidden remains undetected anyway.

  • Surprise Strike — If the Varkh stalker attacks from Hidden, it deals +2 Harm on the first hit.

  • Unnatural Stillness — The Varkh stalker can hold a position, unmoving, for hours. It cannot be detected by any mundane sense while holding still.

Etiquette: A hunter who has spent days approaching a quarry will dislike a loud interruption. Signal that you have seen them; offer information or wait for an answer. This profile’s patience is learned fieldcraft, not an inability to negotiate.

Complications: The party may be crossing a hunt, protecting the quarry or mistaken for a rival crew. Several stalkers working together can block escape routes while one makes contact. They have reasons to withdraw as well as reasons to pursue.

Resolution: When this stalker is watching or pursuing the party, the following can buy time or redirect the hunt:

  • Offer a Secret: Spend 1 Boon to offer useful knowledge of a trail, quarry, or rival. The Varkh stalker’s attention shifts, granting the party a window to escape or act. The GM gains 1 SB as the Varkh stalker files the information.

  • Be Interesting: Succeed on a Performance, Lore, or Deception test (DV 4) to capture the Varkh stalker’s curiosity. On a Clean Success, the Varkh stalker watches but does not attack for one scene. On a Partial, it watches while moving into a better position.

  • Provide a Greater Hunt: Point the Varkh stalker toward a more worthy target (a rival, a monster, a dangerous individual). Succeed on a Sway or Deception test (DV 4). On success, the Varkh stalker leaves you alone to pursue the new quarry.

Wargs — Hunger’s Howl

The wargs in this section are supernatural predators associated with the Hunger Beneath. They are not a fourth Varkh branch, and their presence does not imply a nearby Varkh settlement.

Description: Lean, wolf-like predators with matted fur and eyes that gleam like wet stone. Wargs move in loose packs along boundary edges, drawn to places where balance has frayed. Their howls carry no malice—only the sharp insistence of unresolved tension. They do not hunt for sport, but to remind the world of what it owes itself.

Wargs are the Hunger’s reminders. They appear where a community has learned to look away: a broken promise, an abandoned child, a name no one will speak. At first they watch. Their persistence makes ordinary life impossible; the same eyes appear beyond the schoolyard, the funeral fire, the wedding lamps. The pack waits for someone to acknowledge what everyone knows.

The Hunger envies belonging as fiercely as possession. Wargs watch people welcome one another home, share food, and grieve together. They linger longest where someone has been shut out. This does not make them rescuers. Their attention can turn a frightened settlement against the very person whose exclusion drew them there.

Warg NPC Template

TL: 3–6 | Corporeal, Persistent, Attuned

  • Resilience: 3 (standard); an alpha or elder warg may use 8 (advanced)

  • Dice: Melee 6d (Body 3 + Melee 3), Athletics 6d (Body 3 + Athletics 3), Notice 6d (Wits 3 + Insight 3), Lore 6d (Wits 3 + Lore 3)

  • Harm: 3 (bite or claw)

  • Armor: Step 1 — Light (thick hide)

Traits:

  • Hunger’s Echo — The GM may spend 1 SB so a warg that only Partially fails to track or place someone still finds them anyway, the imbalance too loud to lose.

  • Pack’s Patience — When the pack would take Harm, the GM may spend 1 SB to have it ignore 1 Fatigue from the hit.

  • Howl of Unpaid Balance — Once per scene, a warg pack’s howl forces all who hear it to test Spirit + Endurance (DV 3) or gain 1 Fatigue as the weight of neglected duty presses upon them. The GM gains 1 SB for each character who fails.

Etiquette: Wargs respect quiet endurance and unflinching presence. A character who stands firm without aggression gains +1 Position when encountered. They ignore those who flee or overreact—only the steady draw their attention.

Complications: Warg packs dissolve if their perceived imbalance is corrected—but reform swiftly if new tension arises. They bear no grudges, only acute awareness of what remains unsettled.

Resolution: Most wargs withdraw when offered patient observation or a shared moment of silence:

  • Match Stillness: Succeed on a Spirit + Endurance test (DV 3). On a Clean Success, the wargs withdraw or linger as silent guardians. On a Partial, they withdraw but the GM gains 1 SB.

  • Share Silence: Spend 1 Boon to offer a moment of stillness. The wargs observe but do not attack for one scene.

  • Acknowledge Imbalance: Succeed on a Lore or Insight test (DV 4) to name the imbalance the wargs track. On success, they withdraw. On failure, the GM gains 1 SB and the wargs press closer.

Varkh Bargains — People with Something to Offer

Begin with the people present and what they need. A patrol may want provisions, a guide may want safe passage home, and a hunter may want information. Payment is not proof of an ancestral bargain with a supernatural power. Use the options below where they fit the encounter, rather than requiring every Varkh to desire the same things.

Payment Options

Varkh Payment Options
Payment Type Effect Cost
Food A short-provisioned crew may accept a shared meal for a modest service. Spend 1 Supply or offer a meal
Valuables Offer something the recipient can use or trade; ask what it is worth here. Spend 1 Boon or offer a valuable trinket
Names A captain may accept the name of someone who will vouch for you or answer for your promise. Offer a name (grants GM 1 String: The Name Known)
Silence A hunter may trade passage for confidential information about a route or quarry. Offer a secret (grants GM 1 SB as the secret enters circulation)

Bargain Resolution

  1. Offer Payment: Declare what you offer (food, valuables, a guarantor, information).

  2. Set Position: The GM sets Position based on the Varkh’s mood and the value of the offer (typically Controlled to Desperate).

  3. Roll: Sway (Presence + Sway) for negotiation, or appropriate skill (Insight, Deception, Sway).

  4. Outcome:

    • Clean Success: The bargain is accepted. The Varkh provides service or leaves peacefully.

    • Partial: The bargain is accepted, but the GM gains 1 SB (a hidden condition or future complication).

    • Miss: The Varkh refuses. It may become hostile or demand a higher price. The player gains 2 Boons.

When Payment Fails

If a specific promise has been made and broken, establish who knows and what they can do about it. The GM may:

  • Spend 2 SB to bring that existing obligation into a crucial scene.

  • Have the injured party confront the company at Desperate Position where pursuit is plausible.

An unsuccessful bargain does not conjure pursuers or create a new debt by itself.

Varkh Encounters — Framing and Resolution

Encounter Setup

  1. Determine the Varkh’s Goal: What do they want? (food, passage, payment, information, a name).

  2. Set Initial Position: Based on the party’s recent actions and the Varkh’s mood.

    • Dominant: The party has leverage (intimidated a Varkh skirmisher, earned respect).

    • Controlled: Neutral encounter (the Varkh are watching, waiting).

    • Desperate: The party is cornered, outnumbered, or has failed a bargain.

Encounter Resolution

  • Social Resolution: Use the Bargain mechanics above. Sway or Deception, as the approach requires.

  • Combat Resolution: Use standard Fate’s Edge combat. Varkh skirmishers (TL 2), Varkh warriors (TL 3), Varkh stalkers (TL 5).

  • Clever Resolution: A well-placed trick or clever idea may bypass the encounter entirely. The GM may set a DV 3—4 test and allow the party to spend Boons to improve Position.

GM Quick Cues

  • Skirmishers use cover and escape routes. Give the crew a reason to risk contact.

  • Warriors use their training and companions. Decide who can order a retreat or accept terms.

  • Stalkers study the ground and their quarry. Patience can be interrupted by another need.

  • Wargs belong to the separate supernatural material below. Their behavior is not Varkh psychology.

The Hunger’s Hunting Shapes (Optional)

The Hunger Beneath is a Dark Arch-Fae whose envy can take a hunting shape. This optional encounter device uses the combat profiles above for its dream-creatures. They are manifestations, not Varkh: give them an impossible feature, such as a shadow facing the wrong way or a voice heard before the mouth moves. They do not found settlements, reproduce as mortal people or reveal a hidden truth about Varkh ancestry.

Use these shapes only where the Hunger’s presence has already been established. A broken promise alone is not permission to insert them on an ordinary known-world road. The Grímhúð’s Winter Hall oath is a separate local history; it does not make them Hunger-spawn.

Spawn Mechanics

Within an established Hunger-haunting, the GM may:

  • Spend 1 SB to manifest one small hunting shape using the skirmisher profile from a broken promise or forgotten debt.

  • Spend 2 SB to manifest an armored hunting shape using the warrior profile from a broken oath or an unbalanced transaction.

  • Spend 3 SB to manifest a lurking hunting shape using the stalker profile from a forgotten grudge or a long-suppressed truth.

  • Spend 4 SB to manifest a warg pack from a neglected duty or a frayed boundary.

Each manifestation:

  • Occurs at the most inconvenient moment (GM’s choice).

  • The party may resolve the spawn by addressing the underlying imbalance.

GM Notes: Running Varkh Encounters

  1. Place the encounter. The main populations are across the Great Sea in the distant west. The isolated northern Grímhúð are the exception used in The Howling Ice, not evidence of Varkh colonies throughout the known world.

  2. Name the company. Establish the household, crew or polity involved. Use its own name when known. “Dog-faced” belongs to Linn hostility, never to a neutral creature label.

  3. Choose a want. A toll, a safe return, a stolen boat, a missing relative. Let members disagree over means and risk. A trait on a combat profile is not a rule for every person’s conduct.

  4. Offer a situation. A stranded sled, disputed anchorage or stalking party can lead to aid, trade, pursuit or battle. Let physical skill and bold action matter alongside negotiation.

  5. Separate the supernatural. The Hunger’s shapes and wargs require their own fictional cause. Encountering one proves nothing about a mortal people’s loyalties or origin.

A Note on Accounts

The northern testimony is the fullest we have, which makes it the easiest to misuse. A woman can tell us what happened at her hearth. She cannot be made to answer for a country across an ocean merely because a catalog puts both under one heading. I have kept the names of the speakers wherever the collectors supplied them.

—Lerris Fenwood
