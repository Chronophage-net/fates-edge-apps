Mortal Adversaries

The Witnessed Prey, a Hunter's Guide — Chapter 1

“I have seen a man forget his own name because he refused to count the ninth breath. The witness does not forgive; it only remembers.” —Lerris Fenwood, marginal note

The dead are not the only hungry things. Mortals take to the roads for bread, conviction, revenge, or the chance to become someone else. A mercenary may stand by a hated employer because the recruits trust her; a faction agent may burn a village to protect a secret he sincerely believes would start a war. This section catalogs the living: mercenaries, cultists, faction agents, and desperate locals. Give each something they want and something they will not willingly sacrifice. Their loyalties need not survive the encounter.

On Harm Levels: mortals do not have Hit Points either. Rank-and-file threats (Threat Level 1–2: rabble, recruits, novices) have 3 Harm Levels. Named professionals and faction operatives (Threat Level 3: assassins, captains, inquisitors, judges) are tougher and craftier than a single mortal has any right to be, and carry 8 Harm Levels as advanced adversaries.

Generic Templates

“They are not evil. They are hungry. There is a difference, though the difference will kill you all the same.” —The Gray Wanderer

The following templates can be scaled using Threat Level (see (see reference)). Use them for filler encounters, random patrols, or the quiet before the storm.

Bandit Rabble (TL 1)

Desperate locals with rusty blades. They flee if half their number falls. Their hunger is for bread, not blood.

  • Body 2, Wits 1, Spirit 1, Presence 1.
  • Harm: 2. Fatigue: Body 2.
  • Harm Levels: 3 (standard).
  • Key Skills: Melee 1, Athletics 1.
  • Trait: Rabble—On a Miss when they attack, the GM gains 1 SB (they trip, argue, or hesitate).
  • Clock: Courage [4]—Ticks each time one of them is wounded. When full, they rout.

Cult Novice (TL 1)

True believer, poorly trained. Will gladly die for the cause. Their eyes have the shine of a candle that has been lit from both ends.

  • Body 1, Wits 1, Spirit 2, Presence 1.
  • Harm: 2. Fatigue: Body 1.
  • Harm Levels: 3 (standard).
  • Key Skills: Lore 1 (cult-specific), Sway 1.
  • Trait: Faith’s Crumble—If a player successfully discredits the cult’s doctrine (Presence + Lore vs DV 4), the novice surrenders or flees.
  • Clock: Devotion [4]—Ticks each time the novice is wounded. When full, they trigger a suicide attack (Harm 2 to everyone in Close).

Assassin (TL 3)

Professional killer. Prefers poison, stealth, and a clean exit. You will not see them coming.

  • Body 3, Wits 3, Spirit 2, Presence 2.
  • Harm: 4. Fatigue: Body 3.
  • Harm Levels: 8 (advanced).
  • Key Skills: Stealth 3, Melee 2, Deception 2.
  • Traits: First Strike—On the first round of combat, the assassin has Dominant Position. Poisoned Blade—On a Critical Hit, the target gains the Condition Poisoned (–1 die to physical actions until treated).
  • Clock: Exit Strategy [4]—Ticks each round. When full, the assassin disengages and vanishes.

Faction Operatives

“The law here flows like water over stone—it finds its own path, and woe to those who try to dam it with paper.” —Stone Prince of Aelinnel

These adversaries are tied to specific factions. Use them when the party has made enemies of the powerful—or when the powerful have taken an interest.

Chain-Lantern Inquisitor (Thepyrgos)

Witch-hunter of the Thepyrgos Synod. Iron mesh hood, brass lantern. The lantern is not for light; it is for testimony.

  • Body 3, Wits 3, Spirit 3, Presence 3.
  • Harm: 4. Fatigue: Body 3.
  • Harm Levels: 8 (advanced).
  • Key Skills: Lore 3, Melee 2, Insight 2.
  • Traits: Lantern Test—Once per scene, force a PC to test Spirit + Endurance (DV 4) or reveal a surface thought. Proscription—If the Inquisitor knows a PC’s true name, they gain +1 die to all social rolls against that PC.
  • Clock: Arrest Warrant [6]—Ticks each time the party obstructs or lies. When full, the Inquisitor returns with 1d4 guards and a sealed writ of seizure.

Ykrul Road-Judge

Tumen scribe with seals from three empires. Settles disputes on the steppe. They carry a bell that never rings; if you hear it, run.

  • Body 3, Wits 4, Spirit 3, Presence 2.
  • Harm: 4. Fatigue: Body 3.
  • Harm Levels: 8 (advanced).
  • Key Skills: Lore (Kon’reh) 3, Sway 2.
  • Traits: Kon’reh Binding—Once per scene, the Road-Judge may draw a board in the dirt. The party must win a contested Wits + Lore roll (DV 4) or accept a Geas (minor task, no direct harm).   • Clock: Foedus Recall [4]—Ticks each time the party breaks steppe law. When full, the Judge invokes an ancient treaty; the party is declared oath-breakers and cannot trade at Midh Ahkaz for one season.

Black Banner Mercenary Captain (TL 3)

Veteran commander of a free company. Scarred, pragmatic, and always watching the horizon. Loyalty is a coin that fluctuates with the market.

  • Body 4, Wits 3, Spirit 3, Presence 3.
  • Harm: 6. Fatigue: Body 4.
  • Harm Levels: 8 (advanced).
  • Key Skills: Melee 3, Sway 3.
  • Traits: Condotta Flip—Once per scene, the Captain may switch sides. Roll a die: on 1–3, the Captain and 1d4 mercenaries betray the party; on 4–6, they demand double pay. Payday—At the start of each scene involving the Captain, the GM may tick the Arrears timer.
  • Clock: Arrears [6]—Ticks each time the party fails a negotiation or loses a fight. When full, the Captain abandons the contract.

Rival Adventuring Parties

“The road changes the walker. It changed them. It will change you.” —The Gray Wanderer

Not every adversary is a monster. Some are mirrors. Use the following generator to create a rival party in under 5 minutes.

2The Brass Legion (gears, discipline, precision)Muscle (berserker, knight, duelist)
3The Thorns of Winter (ice, silence, cruelty)Mage/Weird (cantor, free caster, runekeeper)
4The Gilded Chain (wealth, obligation, cages)Scout/Thief (infiltrator, poisoner, sniper)
5The Salt Pact (oaths in brine, pirates)Face/Healer (negotiator, spy, medic)
6The Unfaced (masks, secrets, identities)Specialist (engineer, summoner, trapper)

Patron (d6—roll once per party):

  1. Ikasha (Shadow)—secrets, thresholds, omens
  2. Malachai (Curse)—corrupted bargains, venomous gifts
  3. The Traveler (Roads)—journeys, shortcuts, debts
  4. The Ninth (Knowledge)—forbidden truths, fractured minds
  5. Khemesh (Depths)—unstoppable pressure, drowning
  6. Palinode (Performance)—rapture, encores, hungry fame

Goal (d6):

  1. Retrieve the same artifact
  2. Capture/kill a mutual contact
  3. Secure a patron’s favor
  4. Eliminate the PCs for a rival
  5. Claim a contested site
  6. Prevent a prophecy

Resource (d6):

  1. Hidden safehouse—can hide from pursuers once
  2. Bribed official—may delay a legal timer once
  3. Spy network—gains +1 die to gather info
  4. War chest—can hire temporary muscle
  5. Patron’s omen—once per arc, avoid a Miss
  6. Stolen map—shortcut to any location once

Clock: Rival Agenda [6]—Ticks when the party ignores them, when the GM spends 2+ SB on their off-screen activity, or when a session ends without contact. When full, the rivals achieve a major milestone.
