Optional campaign tools

The Deck of Consequences and Crown Spread are presented as optional tools in the existing rules. Read their purposes before deciding whether they suit your table. The archetype discussion then helps a group think about different styles of participation. These tools do not replace the core resolution loop.

Deck of Consequences (Optional)

The Deck of Consequences is a narrative tool for injecting drama, setbacks, and twists. It replaces or supplements GM fiat with randomized complications that remain thematically consistent. Its use is entirely optional.

Deck Structure

Use a standard 52-card deck (jokers optional). Suits map to complication themes:

SuitThemeExamples
HeartsSocial / EmotionalBetrayal, scandal, broken trust, awkward moment, ally under strain.
SpadesPhysical / HarmBruise, injury, collapse, gear damage, ambush.
ClubsMaterial / CostResource loss, gear failure, debt, ruined supplies, theft.
DiamondsMagical / SpiritualOmen, curse, Patron attention, reality bend, haunting.

Card Ranks & Severity

  • Ace Scene-altering twist; compels immediate response.
  • King, Queen, Jack Major complication with lasting effects.
  • 10–8 Moderate complication that reshapes the current scene.
  • 7–5 Minor complication; a nuisance that creates tension.
  • 4–2 Subtle complication or foreshadowing omen.

Jokers (Optional)

  • Red Joker: Catastrophic event (environmental collapse, Patron intervention).
  • Black Joker: Dark boon (immediate help, but with lasting cost or debt).

When to Draw

The GM may draw from the deck when:

  • A roll shows multiple 1s (e.g., three or more).
  • The GM spends 2+ Story Beats at once.
  • A major timer fills (scene or campaign timer).
  • During travel to generate an unexpected encounter.

Aim for 1–2 draws per session; more if the tone skews chaotic.

Procedure

  1. Note the number of SB spent (or generated).
  2. Draw a number of cards equal to the SB spent (minimum 1, maximum 3).
  3. Synthesize a single narrative complication from the combination of suits and the highest rank.
  4. Do not reshuffle until the deck runs out or the session ends — this creates a natural "fate budget."

Synthesizing Complications

Combine the suits and the highest rank into one fiction-first twist. Examples:

  • Hearts + Spades: Emotional betrayal that leads to physical harm ("Your ally flees, and the portcullis drops between you.").
  • Clubs + Diamonds: Resource drain with magical backlash ("The ritual consumes your healing herbs and leaves a scar on the threshold.").
  • All four suits: A crowd riots, loots your supplies, and a spirit feeds on the chaos.

Shortcut Table for Fast Interpretation

SuitLow (2–5)High (J–A)
HeartsAwkward social momentBetrayal, scandal, broken trust
SpadesBruise, small stumbleSerious injury, structural collapse
ClubsMinor loss (rations, coins)Major asset destroyed, debt called
DiamondsOmen, eerie noisePatron demand, reality bends

Integration with Fiction

  • Translate the raw card result into a fiction-first complication. Do not apply mechanical penalties alone.
  • The complication should build on what is already happening in the scene, not restart the story.
  • Respect player agency — allow clever mitigation, but ensure the consequence lands.

GM Quick Cues

  • Use the deck to surprise yourself as well as the players.
  • If a card does not fit the current fiction, draw again or adjust the rank up or down by one.
  • For campaign-scale foreshadowing, draw a card at the end of a session and note its theme; introduce the complication in a later session.

Crown Spread (Optional)

The Crown Spread is a campaign-framing tool that uses a spread of cards to establish the long arc of a story. It provides seeds for GMs and players to weave motifs, omens, and foreshadowed events. Its use is entirely optional.

Setup

  • In Session Zero (or before the campaign begins), lay out 5–7 cards in a semicircle (the "Crown"). Use either the Deck of Consequences or a standard card deck.
  • Each card anchors a motif, omen, or looming event.
  • Record the spread openly on a Campaign Sheet or digital log.

Interpreting the Spread

PositionMeaning
Position 1 (Root)The underlying tension or theme of the campaign.
Position 2 (Crest)A key faction or patron influence that will rise.
Position 3 (Crown)The climax image or major confrontation the arc builds toward.
Position 4 (Left Hand)A bond, ally, or relationship that anchors play.
Position 5 (Right Hand)A rival, betrayer, or challenger who pressures the party.
Positions 6 & 7 (Optional)Expansions for setting-wide twists (environmental, mystical, or political).

Using the Spread in Play

  • Each drawn card becomes a Foreshadow Timer [4] attached to its motif. Advance the timer when events lean toward that omen (e.g., when the GM spends SB on related complications, when a major scene involves that faction, or when a travel leg passes through an area tied to the motif).
  • When a Foreshadow Timer fills, the motif manifests concretely in play — a faction rises, a betrayer reveals themselves, a curse activates.
  • Tie Spread cards to Campaign Timers for pacing.

Campaign Timer

The Campaign Timer tracks rising stakes across the entire arc.

  • Default size: 8 segments.
  • Advance the Campaign Timer when: multiple SB overflows in a session (e.g., three or more 1s on a single roll), when travel legs resolve with major cost (e.g., a character reaches Harm 2), or when Spread omens manifest concretely.
  • When the Campaign Timer fills, the Crown confrontation arrives. Play through its fallout as the campaign climax.

Ending & Legacy

  • After the Crown confrontation resolves, hold an epilogue session.
  • Resolve any remaining Foreshadow Timers as epilogue vignettes.
  • Players may mark Legacy Bonds — new anchors for future campaigns or descendants.

GM Quick Cues

  • The Spread is not a railroad — it foreshadows, not dictates.
  • Reinterpret cards liberally as play evolves; symbols matter more than literal events.
  • Remind players of their omens between arcs to build tension and payoff.

Player Archetypes at the Table

Fate's Edge gameplay flexes to different player archetypes. These roles are not strict classes but rather approaches to play that help a group balance spotlight and tone.

The Solo

Focus: Mastery of self, independence, one-on-one drama.

Strengths: Quick spotlight scenes, duelists, scouts, specialists.

Risks: Can drift into isolation or hog solo arcs. Needs explicit ties to group goals.

GM Tools: Use Bonds, rival duels, and one-on-one omens to keep engagement tethered to the group.

The Mixed

Focus: Hybrid adaptability — both support and lead.

Strengths: Bridges gaps in group dynamics; excels in team tactics.

Risks: May feel overshadowed by extreme specialists.

GM Tools: Spotlight them when versatility matters: flexible magic, leadership, negotiation, or sudden pivots.

The Mastermind

Focus: Schemes, plans, and command over the long arc.

Strengths: Drives strategic play, coordinates others, excels in intrigue.

Risks: May over-plan or dominate spotlight with metagame thinking.

GM Tools: Challenge them with shifting information, Patron demands, and rivals who anticipate their moves.

Balancing Archetypes

  • A healthy table mixes all three archetypes, ensuring solo tension, group cohesion, and strategic play coexist.
  • Encourage players to flex between archetypes scene by scene; they are fluid, not fixed.
  • Spotlight balance: in a 3-hour session, each archetype should anchor at least one major scene.

GM Quick Cues

  • Use archetypes as a lens for framing scenes: duels for Solos, shifting tactics for Mixed, grand reveals for Masterminds.
  • When spotlight imbalances arise, rotate complications or bonds to another archetype's strength.
  • Archetypes inform how Patrons and factions court PCs: Solos as champions, Mixed as envoys, Masterminds as plotters.

Scene Example: The Bridge at Dusk

This example shows the core resolution loop in practice across multiple player actions.

Setup

The party—Lyra (rogue), Thane (fighter), and Sera (caster)—is on a narrow stone bridge overlooking a ravine. Cultists are chanting on the far side, completing a ritual that will summon a demon. The GM has already established a Ritual Completion [6] timer with 3 segments filled.

GM: "You're on the bridge. The cultists are 40 feet away, their chant rising. You have 3 segments left on the Ritual Completion timer. What do you do?"

Player Action 1: Lyra Scouts Ahead

Lyra: "I creep forward, keeping to the shadow of the bridge's railing, trying to see how many cultists there are and what they're doing."
GM: "Controlled, DV 3. On a Miss, you dislodge a stone and they notice you."

Lyra rolls (Wits 4 + Stealth 2 = 6 dice): 9, 7, 4, 3, 2, 1.

  • Successes: 2 (9, 7) vs DV 3 → Partial
  • SB: The 1 gives the GM 1 Story Beat.
  • Boons: Lyra gains 1 Boon.
GM: "You get a good look. There are six cultists—four chanting around a stone altar, two watching the bridge. You count the segments: the ritual is at 4 of 6. They're close. But your foot scrapes a loose stone. The watch cultists are looking your way."
Lyra: "I freeze."
GM: "They don't see you. But they're alert now. What does everyone else do?"

Player Action 2: Thane Charges

Thane: "I charge the watch cultists while they're distracted. I want to take them out before they raise the alarm."
GM: "Charge is a Full Turn Action. Move two bands and attack. Costs 1 Fatigue. Your next defense is Desperate. The bridge is narrow—open ground, so no penalty."
GM: "Controlled, DV 3. On a Miss, you stumble and the cultists get a free shot."

Thane rolls (Body 3 + Melee 3 = 6 dice): 10, 8, 6, 5, 4, 2.

  • Successes: 4 (10 counts as 2, 8, 6) vs DV 3 → Clean Success
  • SB: None.
  • Boons: None.
GM: "You barrel into the first cultist—he goes down hard (Harm 2 from your medium weapon). The second cultist stumbles back. The bridge shakes. I'll tick Collapse [4] +1—you're at 1 of 4. The ritual's chant falters, but doesn't stop."
Thane: "I'm in the middle of them now. Desperate defense?"
GM: "Yes. Your next defense is Desperate."

Player Action 3: Sera Casts a Spell

Sera: "I cast a [FIRE][AREA] burst to scatter the remaining cultists and disrupt the ritual. I want to hit the altar."
GM: "That's two tags—DV 4 (1 + 2 tags). It's a two-action spell (Weave then Cast). You're not in melee, so Controlled Position. On a Miss, backlash."
Sera: "I weave." (Action 1)
GM: "The fire gathers in your palm. The cultists see you. The chant quickens. Roll to cast."

Sera rolls (Wits 4 + Arcana 2 = 6 dice): 1, 1, 8, 7, 6, 3.

  • Successes: 3 (8, 7, 6) vs DV 4 → Partial
  • SB: Two 1s → GM gains 2 Story Beats.
  • Boons: Sera gains 1 Boon.
GM: "The fire bursts outward. Two cultists are thrown back (Harm 1 each). The altar cracks—the ritual chant stutters. But the backlash: fire licks back at you. Mark 1 Fatigue, and the bridge timbers catch fire. I'll tick Collapse [4] +1—you're at 2 of 4. Also, I'll spend 1 SB: the lead cultist survives and screams, 'The Ninth will rise!'"

Spending Story Beats

The GM now has 3 SB total (1 from Lyra, 2 from Sera). The GM spends 2 SB:

GM: "I spend 2 SB. The lead cultist's scream echoes—and the bridge groans. You feel the stone shift. Tick Collapse [4] +1 (now 3 of 4). And a new timer: Reinforcements [6] starts ticking—something is coming from the far side of the ravine."

Resolution

The party has 1 segment left on the Ritual Completion timer, 1 segment before the bridge collapses, and a new threat incoming. The scene escalates.

GM: "The bridge groans. The fire is spreading. The lead cultist is reaching for a bell. What do you do?"

What This Example Teaches

  • Position and DV are set based on fiction—not arbitrary numbers.
  • Partials give Boons and progress, but with costs.
  • 1s give SB even on successful rolls.
  • SB are spent to escalate tension, not punish.
  • Timers are visible and ticked openly.
  • Fail forward: Even Partials and Misses change the situation—the story never stalls.
