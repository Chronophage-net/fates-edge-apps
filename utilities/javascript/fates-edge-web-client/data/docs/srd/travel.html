Travel Framework

Journeys are more than distance; they are sequences of pressure, discovery, and consequence. The travel framework turns long roads into a series of dramatic legs without micromanaging miles.

Legs and Travel Timers

  • Break a long journey into legs, each representing a significant change in terrain, danger, or political region.
  • Each leg uses a Travel Timer (default size 4 segments).
  • The timer advances through progress actions, encounters, or scene resolution.
  • When the timer fills, the party arrives at the destination (or the next region). Unresolved complications carry forward.

Roles on the Road

Assign one role per character each leg. Roles may rotate each leg.

RoleKey TestBenefit on Success
GuideWits + LoreTick the Travel Timer +1.
ScoutWits + StealthParty cannot be surprised on the first encounter of the leg.
QuartermasterWits + CraftPrevent one Supply drop or recover 1 spent Supply.
WatchPresence + InsightParty gains +1 Position on the first defense roll of any ambush.

Each role action requires a roll (Attribute + appropriate skill, DV set by the GM). Failure may tick the Travel Timer backwards or introduce a complication.

Encounters and Events

Every 1–2 segments filled, the GM introduces an encounter or hazard. At minimum, each leg should include one meaningful encounter.

TypeExamplesTypical Resolution
DiscoveryRuins, strange omens, hidden sitesInsight or exploration roll.
HazardTerrain challenge, illness, extreme weatherSurvival or Endurance test; may use a hazard timer.
EncounterBandits, emissaries, beasts, rival travelersCombat or social resolution.
ComplicationBond tested, supplies strained, pursuit gains groundRoleplay or skill challenge.

Encounters use the standard resolution loop (Position, DV, Outcome). On a failed travel roll, the GM may spend SB to introduce an encounter immediately.

Supply and Depletion

Track shared party supplies with a Supply Timer [4].

  • Tick the timer when the Quartermaster fails a roll, when the GM spends SB on scarcity, or when the party is forced to march without resupply.
  • When the timer fills, the party runs out of a critical resource (food, water, fuel). Apply a Condition (Hungry, Thirsty, etc.) or mark 1 Fatigue per character.
  • Resupply at settlements, through successful foraging, or by spending a Cache token (see Asset rules).

Optional: Regional Travel Decks

If using the optional Regional Decks, draw a card from the destination's deck when the Travel Timer ticks. Suits suggest the nature of the encounter:

SuitTheme
SpadesPlace – a landmark, ruin, or terrain feature.
HeartsActor – a traveler, patrol, or local guide.
ClubsPressure – weather, injury, resource loss.
DiamondsLeverage – a shortcut, a cache, or a friendly face.

The highest rank may set the Travel Timer size (e.g., 4 for 2–5, 6 for 6–10, 8 for Jack–King, 10 for Ace).

Weather and Terrain Modifiers

The GM may apply modifiers based on conditions:

ConditionEffect
Difficult TerrainMovement costs double; Charge/Skirmish require open ground
Extreme Weather (rain, snow, heat)First travel roll each leg is Desperate.
Hazardous Weather (storm, blizzard, sandstorm)Travel Timer ticks +1 at leg start; navigation DV +1.
Favorable WeatherFirst travel roll each leg gains +1 Position.

Foraging and Subsistence

A character may forage during a leg instead of taking a role action. Roll Wits + Lore (DV by terrain, typical 3–4).

  • Clean Success: Gain 1 Supply.
  • Partial: Gain 1 Supply but mark 1 Fatigue (time or exposure).
  • Miss: No Supply; GM gains 1 SB (scarce resources, dangerous plant, predator sign).

GM Quick Cues

  • Travel should advance story stakes, not pause them. Use legs to foreshadow threats, deepen bonds, or reveal Patron omens.
  • Let players showcase talents in their chosen roles.
  • Mix discovery and hardship to keep legs tense but rewarding.
  • A failed travel roll should never result in "nothing happens." Use SB to introduce an encounter or tick a hazard timer.
