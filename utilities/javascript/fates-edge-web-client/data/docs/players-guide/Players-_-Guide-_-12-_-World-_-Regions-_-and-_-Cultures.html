World Regions and Cultures

Chapter 12 · World Regions and Cultures

Using This Compendium

You only need to read the region your character is from or where the campaign starts. The GM will tell you when other regions become relevant.

Each entry includes:

  • Fantasy subgenre – the kind of stories you can expect.

  • Attitude toward magic – how locals react to spellcasters.

  • Typical dress / customs – to help describe your character.

  • Player hook – a story seed to tie your character to this place.

  • Key stakeholders – major NPCs and factions.

  • Mini-generator – quick prompts for GMs and players (d6 or d8 table).

For GM-only secrets (hidden agendas, faction timers), see the GM Guide.

Acasia — “The Broken Province”

Genre: Low fantasy / mercenary realism / border feud.

Attitude toward magic:

Hedge-witches respected; visible sorcery can start a levy. The Curse is blamed on ancient magic.

Typical dress / customs:

Wool and leather, scarves to hide clan markings. Feasts often end in oath-swearing; never sign a contract on a bridge.

Player hook:

You owe a blood-debt to a free company captain – or you are that captain.

Key stakeholders:

Margravine of the Broken March, The Lame King, Blackwood Matriarchs, Free Company Captains.

Cultural bonus:

Border-wise. Once per session when navigating a contested border or disputed territory, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about local patrols, safe passages, or hidden trails.

Acasia Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. A bridge toll has tripled overnight; the toll-keeper is a deserter from your old company.

  2. A hedge-witch offers to break your blood-debt – for the memory of your first kill.

  3. The Lame King travels with a mute jester who writes prophecies on fallen leaves.

  4. Bandits stole a Blackwood Matriarch’s curse-box. Recover it before it opens.

  5. A free company captain wants to hire you – but their last three hires died of “accidents”.

  6. The Margravine’s seal is missing. Whoever finds it can rewrite border law.

Aelaerem (Halflings) — “Hearth & Hollow”

Genre: Folk horror / pastoral quiet with dark undercurrents.

Attitude toward magic:

Hearth-magic (red thread, cup-marks) is everyday. Open magic may attract the Neighbors.

Typical dress / customs:

Bright wool, red doors, nine cups never poured. Hospitality is sacred – and a trap.

Player hook:

The Pale Shepherd came for your grandmother. You still hear her footsteps.

Key stakeholders:

Apple-Matron, The Pale Shepherd, Mummers’ Captain, Lantern-Warden.

Cultural bonus:

Lucky break. Once per scene, you may improve your Position by one step (e.g., Desperate \(\rightarrow\) Controlled, Controlled \(\rightarrow\) Dominant) as a free action before rolling. This represents a flash of well-timed luck or an unexpected opening.

Aelaerem Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. You poured the ninth cup by accident. The Hollow Bride left a silver coin under your pillow.

  2. A red thread appears around your wrist each morning. Who ties it – and what do they want?

  3. The Apple-Matron’s orchard produced blood-red fruit. Eating one lets you speak with crows.

  4. A mummers’ troupe wears masks of your dead relatives. They invite you to join.

  5. The Lantern-Warden is deaf. She hires you to “hear” which bell-line is failing.

  6. The Pale Shepherd walks beside you for one night. You may ask one question – but you must answer one truth in return.

Aeler (Dwarves) — “Crowns & Under-Vaults”

Genre: Industrial / political / survival horror (underground).

Attitude toward magic:

Rituals are a craft, guild-licensed. Unsanctioned magic damages air and stone.

Typical dress / customs:

Heavy aprons, breath-masks, bell-tokens. Count your breaths in eights; never waste water.

Player hook:

You broke a seal in the deep ways and something followed you up.

Key stakeholders:

Vault-Queen, High King Beneath the Peaks, True Masons, Edgewalkers.

Cultural bonus:

Stone-sense. You have an innate feel for worked stone and underground spaces. Once per session, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about structural weaknesses, hidden passages, or ventilation in an underground or stonework environment.

Aeler Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. The ventilation system is ticking – you have one hour before the lower galleries suffocate.

  2. A vein-serpent wakes. It will trade a lost treasure for your last breath.

  3. The Vault-Queen’s ledger shows a keystone that was never carved. You must invent it.

  4. Edgewalkers report singing stones. The song is in a dialect that has been dead for three centuries.

  5. A rival clan sabotaged your breath-counters. Find their spy before the audit.

  6. The High King Beneath the Peaks demands a new oath – carved into your own collarbone.

Aelinnel (Gnomes) — “Stone, Bough, and Bright Things”

Genre: Fae / mathematical / courtesy horror.

Attitude toward magic:

Fae rites, geasa, and bargain-magic. Iron is taboo; copper is polite.

Typical dress / customs:

Bright coats, copper tools, counting beads. Never trust a single account.

Player hook:

You spoke a word you shouldn’t have, and now the Green Gate demands payment.

Key stakeholders:

Stone Prince of Aelinnel, Lady of Thorns, Green Knight, Oath-Carver.

Cultural bonus:

Knack for the right thing. Once per scene, you may declare that you have a small, mundane item on your person that is useful in the current situation (e.g., a coil of rope, a lockpick, a candle, a chalk stick). This costs no Boon and requires no roll. The item must be plausible for your character to carry.

Aelinnel Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. The Green Gate appears. It asks: “What is the number of a promise that was never spoken?”

  2. A Thorn Courtier offers you a cup of tea. If you drink, you forget your own name.

  3. The Oath-Carver has gone mad, carving the same impossible equation into every door.

  4. Copper coins scream when touched. They are trying to recite a forgotten treaty.

  5. The Stone Prince’s throne is a living geas. Sitting on it makes you honor one ancient oath.

  6. A tide-path opens at midnight. It leads to a library where every book is blank – but whispering.

Black Banners — “Condotta & Crowns”

Genre: Military / mercenary / low fantasy warfare.

Attitude toward magic:

Battle-magic is useful; unreliable mages don’t get paid. Condotta captains hire cantors for morale.

Typical dress / customs:

Tabards over rusted mail, scarves to hide old loyalties. Payday is sacred; betrayal is business.

Player hook:

Your company was sold out from under you. Now you hunt the broker who took the coin.

Key stakeholders:

Banner-Captain, The Black Colonel, Clan-Mother (Ykrul), Veteran Sergeant.

Cultural bonus:

Camp-wise. Once per session when dealing with mercenaries, soldiers, or camp followers, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about their loyalties, pay situation, or recent movements.

Black Banners Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. Payday is late. The troops are muttering. The quartermaster has disappeared.

  2. The Black Colonel’s banner is found torn – but no one saw it happen.

  3. A rival condotta offers double pay to switch sides – but your current captain holds your hostage.

  4. The Veteran Sergeant knows a secret passage into the enemy camp. He also knows it’s cursed.

  5. A cantor’s song heals the wounded but slowly erases their memories of home.

  6. The Clan-Mother sends a war-host. She demands a blood-price paid in songs, not gold.

Ecktoria — “Marble & Fire”

Genre: Political intrigue / legal drama / fallen empire.

Attitude toward magic:

Licensed thaumaturges file casting notices. Unlicensed magic is a crime, often punished by the Everflame inquisitors.

Typical dress / customs:

Togas, seal rings, procession masks. Law is a weapon; never refuse a toast in a coin-house.

Player hook:

You forged a document that could topple a senator – or save a heretic.

Key stakeholders:

Grand Magistrate, High Priest of the Everflame, Duchess-Regent, Censor’s Clerk.

Cultural bonus:

Legal ear. Once per session when navigating bureaucracy, courts, or official proceedings, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about which official is corrupt, what a document really means, or who has the real authority.

Ecktoria Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. The Grand Magistrate’s son has been cursed. The only cure is a confession that would ruin a family.

  2. A Censor’s clerk offers you a “redacted” page from the imperial archives. It shows your name.

  3. The Everflame’s flame gutters for one second. In that second, you see a ghost wearing your face.

  4. A legal duel is declared. Your champion must fight – but the opponent’s blade is made of frozen lies.

  5. The Duchess-Regent hosts a masked ball. Each mask is a binding contract.

  6. A heretic claims the Everflame is not divine – it’s a prison for a forgotten angel. The inquisitors are already on their way.

Linn — “Skerries & Storm-Oaths”

Genre: Viking / maritime / oath-bound raiding.

Attitude toward magic:

Weather magic is sacred; false rites are crimes against the ship. Skalds use songs for law and memory.

Typical dress / customs:

Wool, leather, whale-bone runes. Oaths sworn on the tide cannot be broken without drowning.

Player hook:

Your father’s sword was lost in a raid. The sea-queen says the drowned still carry it.

Key stakeholders:

Sea-Queen, High Jarl, Volva of the Mist, Skald.

Cultural bonus:

Tide-reader. You have an innate sense of the sea’s rhythms. Once per session when on or near salt water, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about tides, currents, weather, or submerged hazards.

Linn Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. A drowned longship rises from the fjord. The crew is still aboard – and still owes a debt.

  2. The Volva of the Mist demands you cut your shadow loose. It will serve as a spy for one year.

  3. A skald sings a verse that names you as the heir to a curse you never knew existed.

  4. The Sea-Queen’s crown is made of a single whale’s tooth. It whispers the names of traitors.

  5. A storm-oath is broken. The wind will not return until the oath is sworn again in blood.

  6. The High Jarl’s funeral pyre burns for nine days. On the ninth night, he sits up and asks for his sword.

Mistlands — “Bells, Salt, and Breath”

Genre: Folk horror / gothic / supernatural dread.

Attitude toward magic:

Bell-lines and salt are the only trusted magic. Outsider spellcasters are viewed with suspicion.

Typical dress / customs:

Gray wool, fog-veils, bell-charms. Never breathe deeply after the ninth bell.

Player hook:

You were born during a bell-line failure. The fog knows your name – it has visited twice.

Key stakeholders:

Legate of the Mists, Bell-Warden, Salt-Monk, Mist-Seer.

Cultural bonus:

Fog-wise. Once per session when in fog, mist, or heavy precipitation, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about directions, hidden dangers, or the presence of spirits or wraiths in the immediate area.

Mistlands Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. A bell-line goes silent. The fog pours through the gap, carrying a voice that asks for a story.

  2. The Salt-Monk’s salt pans have turned black. The curse will spread unless you find a new vein.

  3. A Mist-Seer offers to read your future in the fog. The price is one memory you never share.

  4. The Legate of the Mists is an Aeler exile. He will trade safe passage for a secret about your past.

  5. A corpse on the bell-line has no face – but it wears your grandmother’s wedding ring.

  6. The fog parts for one minute, revealing a city that should not exist. Its bell is ringing the ninth time.

Silkstrand — “City of Bridges & Dyewater”

Genre: Urban / mercantile / noir.

Attitude toward magic:

Curse-magic feared; trade magic (dye-wards, bridge-blessings) accepted. The Matron employs a weft-reader.

Typical dress / customs:

Dyed silks, debtor’s stains, bridge tokens. Every bridge is a treaty; every handshake a contract.

Player hook:

You’re the only one who knows the secret of the red dye. The guild wants the recipe – or your hands.

Key stakeholders:

The Matron, Guildmistress of Dyers, Bridge Bailiff, Night-Boat Smuggler (“Ravel”).

Cultural bonus:

Bridge-runner. Once per session when navigating an urban environment (city streets, docks, markets), you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about shortcuts, hidden routes, or the location of a specific shop, tavern, or safe house.

Silkstrand Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. A bolt of cloth bleeds when cut. The blood shows a murder that hasn’t happened yet.

  2. The Bridge Bailiff auctions a debt. You can buy it – but the debtor is a ghost.

  3. The Night-Boat Smuggler offers you a package. Do not open it. Do not ask what it is.

  4. The Guildmistress of Dyers demands a new color – dyed from the ashes of a broken oath.

  5. The Matron’s weft-reader sees a thread connecting you to the Drowned Man. She refuses to cut it.

  6. A dye-vat overflows, turning a canal to ink. Words form on the surface – a list of names. Yours is last.

Theona — “Three Greens, No Ninth”

Genre: Celtic / folk horror / taboo-driven.

Attitude toward magic:

Old rites (well-blessings, bride-peace) are essential. Breaking a taboo invites the Green Host.

Typical dress / customs:

Peat-stained cloaks, hawthorn pins, counting in eights. The ninth cup is never poured.

Player hook:

You were at a bride-peace feast when the ninth name was spoken. The Green Host rode – but you survived.

Key stakeholders:

Matron of Wells, Three-Isles King, Lady Beneath, Taboo-Witness.

Cultural bonus:

Green-sight. Once per session when in natural settings (forests, moors, hills), you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about the presence of fey, spirits, or the Green Host, or about a local taboo’s consequences.

Theona Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. A well runs dry. The Matron of Wells says the water was stolen – by your own reflection.

  2. The Three-Isles King marries a second time. The bride is a woman you killed (or failed to save).

  3. The Taboo-Witness catches you pouring the ninth cup. She offers a deal: your right hand, or a memory.

  4. The Green Host rides tonight. You can join them – but you will forget how to return.

  5. The Lady Beneath sends a dream: a coin lies at the bottom of the marsh. Take it, and she will remember your name.

  6. A bride-peace is broken. The groom’s family demands a blood-price; the bride’s family offers you instead.

Thepyrgos — “City of a Thousand Stairs”

Genre: Byzantine / academic / arcane mystery.

Attitude toward magic:

The Synod licenses magic; unorthodox casting is not illegal – merely unfashionable. Witch-hunters conscript talent.

Typical dress / customs:

Academic robes, bell-badges, stair-passes. Every stair climbed is a word in a conversation.

Player hook:

You tested as “gifted” on the candle-smoke test. The university wants you; the Chain-Lanterns want you in chains.

Key stakeholders:

The Archon, Lighthouse-Patriarch, Nomophylax, Palkar Captain.

Cultural bonus (Hyro heritage):

Echo-memory. Your ancestors survived the Astroegro collapse, and fragments of that resilience linger. Once per session when trying to recall a piece of forgotten lore, a hidden door, or a buried secret, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about the past or about hidden things. This represents ancestral whispers, not scholarly knowledge.

Thepyrgos Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. The Lighthouse-Patriarch has not spoken in three years. His shadow gives lectures on forbidden texts.

  2. A stair token is stolen. Without it, you cannot reach the upper archives – and a demon is loose there.

  3. The Nomophylax declares your magical talent “unlicensed”. You have one day to pass the Synod exam.

  4. A Palkar Captain offers to erase your criminal record – in exchange for a ritual performed in the cisterns.

  5. The Archon dies. His last word is your name. The election for his successor begins at midnight.

  6. A student has been erased from every record. Only you remember them. The Chain-Lanterns want to know why.

Ubral — “The Stone Between Spears”

Genre: Highland clan / reiver / honor culture.

Attitude toward magic:

Clan shamans are respected; outsider mages are watched. Stone-speakers (dwarf-trained) are tolerated.

Typical dress / customs:

Tweed, iron brooches, guest-tokens. Never refuse a cup from a hearth-aunt – hospitality is a contract.

Player hook:

You carry a wergild debt that your father couldn’t pay. The feud is now yours.

Key stakeholders:

Lady of Tor, Council of Cairns, Feud-Broker, Wergild Counter.

Cultural bonus:

Hill-wise. Once per session when in highland or mountainous terrain, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about weather, passes, hidden valleys, or clan movements.

Ubral Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. The Council of Cairns adds a new stone. It has your name carved into it – but you are not dead.

  2. A guest-token is broken. You are now an outlaw unless you find the piece that was stolen.

  3. The Feud-Broker offers to settle your wergild – but the payment is the hand of a loved one.

  4. The Lady of Tor’s son was killed by a monster. The monster is actually a cursed ancestor of yours.

  5. A Wergild Counter admits he has miscounted. The debt is actually double. He wants you to kill the witness.

  6. A stone-speaker claims the mountain is waking. It demands a tribute of fresh blood.

Valewood — “Empire Under Leaves”

Genre: Fairy tale / dark forest / fey remnant.

Attitude toward magic:

Imperial echoes and fae rites are dangerous. Never eat fruit from a tree with a face.

Typical dress / customs:

Leather, moss-dyed cloaks, way-cords. Speak in riddles or not at all.

Player hook:

A star-road opened in your dream. You walked it – now something walks behind you.

Key stakeholders:

Hazel Queen, Alder King, The Huntsman, Pathweaver.

Cultural bonus (Lethai-al heritage):

Root-memory. You carry the long memory of the wood. Once per session when in forest terrain, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about the oldest trees, hidden paths, or the presence of fey or imperial echoes.

Valewood Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. A star-road appears at midnight. It leads to a throne made of antlers. The Alder King sits on it – and he expects you to kneel.

  2. The Pathweaver offers you a cord. If you tie it, you will never be lost again. If you untie it, you will forget where home is.

  3. The Hazel Queen is dying. Her last request: find the acorn of the first tree, buried under a court that no longer exists.

  4. The Huntsman’s hounds are loose. They will not attack – they will only follow you until you speak your true name.

  5. An imperial echo (a ghost of a long-dead soldier) mistakes you for an enemy. It will not listen to reason – only to a duellist’s blade.

  6. A tree with a face offers you its fruit. Eat it, and you will understand the language of stone. Refuse, and it will uproot itself and follow you.

Vhasia — “The Fractured Sun”

Genre: Courtly romance / chivalric / fractured kingdom.

Attitude toward magic:

The Church of the Flame distrusts arcane magic; secular lords employ court mages anyway. Chevauchée raiders use cantors for morale.

Typical dress / customs:

Velvet, partisan ribbons, duelling scars. Never carry a coin with only one sunburst – treason.

Player hook:

You’re the Last Dauphin – or a convincing fake. Either way, armies march for your claim.

Key stakeholders:

Two Crowns, Queen-Mother, Last Dauphin, Marshal-Exile.

Cultural bonus:

Herald’s eye. Once per session when dealing with nobility, heraldry, or court politics, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about a noble’s allegiance, a marriage alliance, or a hidden feud.

Vhasia Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. The Queen-Mother offers you a marriage alliance. The bride is a ghost bound to a tapestry.

  2. The Marshal-Exile demands a duel. The winner takes the army; the loser takes a beheading.

  3. A chevauchée burns a village that swore neutrality. The only survivor is a child who calls you by a dead knight’s name.

  4. The Two Crowns are secretly the same person – a master of disguise who enjoys war.

  5. The Last Dauphin is a commoner recruited by a desperate lord. He does not know he is a fake.

  6. A duelling scar heals overnight, leaving silver thread in your skin. The wound now whispers treason.

Viterra — “The Hedge-Law Realm”

Genre: Legal drama / low fantasy / frontier justice.

Attitude toward magic:

Hedge-magic common; any thaumaturgy that leaves a trace must be registered. Legal duels may involve wizards.

Typical dress / customs:

Wool, seal rings, boundary stones. Every hedge is a precedent; every dike a court ruling.

Player hook:

Your family lost the border dispute. You have one year to find a lost charter or lose the land forever.

Key stakeholders:

Warrior Queen, Queen’s Justiciar, Fen Reeve, Master Advocate Halric.

Cultural bonus:

Hedge-walker. Once per session when dealing with legal matters, contracts, or border disputes, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about loopholes, precedents, or the true ownership of a piece of land.

Viterra Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. The Warrior Queen comes in person to settle a dispute. She asks each party one question. The liar loses a hand.

  2. The Fen Reeve’s tally-stick is broken. Until it is mended, the river toll is doubled – and the river itself is angry.

  3. Master Advocate Halric offers to defend you for free. His last three clients all died mysteriously.

  4. A boundary stone moves three feet east every night. The neighbors swear they are not touching it.

  5. The Queen’s Justiciar arrests you for “hedge-walking without a license”. The sentence is a legal duel at dawn.

  6. A charter written in disappearing ink is the only proof of your inheritance. The ink will be gone by sunrise.

Ykrul (Orcs) — “Wolf Standards, Winter Camps”

Genre: Steppe nomad / saga / honor culture.

Attitude toward magic:

Geometry of oaths (Kon’reh) and sky-speakers. Written magic is suspicious; sung magic is respected.

Typical dress / customs:

Leather, horse-hair braids, wolf-tooth standards. Never ride between two wolves at dusk.

Player hook:

You broke a hostage string. Now the Khatun demands a blood-price – or your youngest kin.

Key stakeholders:

Khatun of Ring, Khagan’s Kin, Sky-Speaker, Kon’reh Masters.

Cultural bonus:

Steppe-sense. You have an innate feel for open country, weather, and the movement of herds and riders. Once per session when on steppe, grassland, or open plain, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about weather patterns, the presence of riders, or the safest route to water.

Ykrul Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. A white squall separates you from the herd. In the snow, you see the faces of everyone you have wronged.

  2. The Sky-Speaker calls a storm to halt an enemy army. The storm demands a sacrifice: your left ear.

  3. A Kon’reh Master sets you a problem: “How many camps can be pitched before the river freezes?” The wrong answer is death.

  4. The Khagan’s Kin offers you a hostage string. If you accept, you gain a loyal servant – but also a rival who will hunt you.

  5. A wolf-standard is stolen. The clan will not fight until it is returned. The thief is a ghost who died in the last war.

  6. The Khatun of Ring declares a blood-price on your head. You have one day to pay or one day to ride.

Zakov — “Salt & Serpent”

Genre: Pirate / noir / crime syndicate.

Attitude toward magic:

Any magic is welcome – if it pays. Cursed cargoes are a common hazard; fae bargains are made at the Crimson Docks.

Typical dress / customs:

Brine-stained coats, reef-tooth necklaces, smuggler’s tokens. Never take a coin that tastes of brine – the Serpent’s Mark is a debt.

Player hook:

You have a syndicate marker that could buy a fleet – or get you killed. The Salt Prince wants to collect.

Key stakeholders:

Salt Prince, Pirate Queen, Kraken’s Tongue, Syndicate Silent.

Cultural bonus:

Tide-pirate’s eye. Once per session when on or near salt water, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about hidden coves, underwater hazards, or the loyalties of a ship’s crew.

Zakov Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. The Kraken’s Tongue offers a prophecy. To hear it, you must drink a cup of seawater that tastes like your mother’s tears.

  2. A Pirate Queen’s crew is cursed: they cannot step on land until they pay a debt to a man who drowned fifty years ago.

  3. The Salt Prince’s collector comes for your marker. You can pay with a memory, a finger, or a voyage to a reef that does not exist.

  4. A fae bargain is struck on the Crimson Docks. The price: a stolen hour of sleep from every sailor in port.

  5. A smuggler’s token is found in your pocket. It belonged to someone who was hanged last week. The guard assumes you are the accomplice.

  6. A reef-tooth necklace bleeds when you touch it. It belonged to a pirate queen who was betrayed – and she is not dead.

Vilikari — “Laurels & Longhouses”

Genre: Frontier federates / mercantile pragmatism / dual loyalty.

Attitude toward magic:

Hedge-magic and weather-singing are common; written magic is viewed with suspicion. The old families prefer results over rituals.

Typical dress / customs:

Wool tunics over faded imperial tunics, leather breeches, brass torcs. Longhouses with broken marble columns used as door lintels. Never invoke only one law — the oak and the marble must both be honored.

Player hook:

Your grandfather signed the Foedus Stone with a thumbprint. That thumb is now a wax seal on a contract that could destroy your family.

Key stakeholders:

Federate King Aldric, Queen of Marches, Hearth-Mother Vesna, Stone-Sealed Families (Contarini, Morosini, Dandolo).

Cultural bonus:

Two-Law Cunning. Once per session when navigating bureaucracy, customs, or legal disputes, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about loopholes, hidden clauses, or which official can be bribed.

Vilikari Mini-Generator (d6)

The Vilikari — Between Oak and Marble

The Vilikari are the children of two failed empires. When the Utaran legions withdrew from the eastern marches, they left behind not garrisons but grandchildren — legionaries who had married into steppe clans, adopted local customs, and forgotten which flag they had once carried. When the old fey courts of the Valewood sealed their gates, refugees trickled east, carrying memory-cords and grudges. The Vilikari are what grew from that soil: a people who speak two languages badly, honor two sets of laws selectively, and have learned that survival is not about choosing sides but about making both sides need you.

They straddle the border between the Violet Steppe and the lowland kingdoms, a buffer that has been conquered, abandoned, and conquered again. Their settlements are longhouses raised over ruined forums, their markets trade Ykrul horses for Ecktorian grain, and their courts are held under oak trees when the marble benches are too cold.

The Two Laws

Vilikari law is not written in a single codex. It lives in two sources, and every Vilikari learns to walk the line between them.

The Oak (Mallus Law)

The old law, sworn on antler and root. It governs grazing rights, blood-prices, and the bonds of foster-kin. Judgments are made by the Hearth-Mother or a circle of elders. Oaths are sealed with a handshake and witnessed by the fire. Breaking an oak-law oath stains a family’s honor for three generations.

The Marble (Basilica Law)

The imperial remnant, preserved in faded charters and the Archivolt’s sealed records. It governs contracts, tariffs, and inheritance. Judgments are made by notaries and sealed with wax. A marble-law contract can be enforced across borders — but only if the seal is still warm.

The Foedus Stone

At the heart of Vilikari territory stands the Foedus Stone, a weathered monolith at a crossroads, carved with every treaty the Vilikari have ever sworn. The oldest marks are Utaran legionary oaths. The newest are still wet. The stone has grown over the centuries — each new treaty adds a finger of granite. The newest crack has no date. The Vilikari do not speak of what it represents. They only know that when the crack widens, the Hollow listens.

The Stone-Sealed Families

The old Vilikari families do not rule through armies or treasuries. They rule through charters — sealed documents, imperial patents, and foedus clauses that give them the right to collect tolls, operate ferries, and sit on the Federate King’s council. Each family is a Terrestrial Patron (see sidebar), offering influence in exchange for loyalty.

House Contarini (The Archivolt’s Shadow)

“We do not write the law. We file it. The difference is a matter of shelf space.” The Contarini control the Archivolt — the half-ruined scriptorium where Vilikari charters are stored and disputed. Their influence is bureaucratic, slow, and absolute. A Contarini factor can find a precedent to justify almost anything — or lose a document that would have stopped a war. Patron Asset: Archivolt Access — Once per session, spend 1 Boon to produce a sealed document that grants temporary authority (safe passage, toll waiver, legal immunity). The document is genuine. The seal is warm.

House Morosini (The Coin Unspoken)

“Gold talks. Whisper is silver. Silence is a fortune.” The Morosini are merchant-bankers who never appear in any ledger. They finance caravans, underwrite condotta contracts, and lend silver to kings who cannot pay it back. Their symbol is a closed fist — because the hand that counts coin should never be seen counting. Patron Asset: Morosini Voucher — Once per session, spend 1 Boon to produce a line of credit at any market in the Amaranthine. The voucher is honored once. Then the Morosini will ask for a favor.

House Dandolo (The Bridge-Builders)

“A bridge is a promise. A promise is a bridge. Both can be burned.” The Dandolo control the causeways, ferries, and rope-bridges that connect the Vilikari cantons. They are neutral in family feuds — everyone needs to cross. Their influence is geographic; they can open a route or close it, speed a caravan or strand it. Patron Asset: Dandolo Pass — Once per session, treat one water or bridge crossing as Dominant Position. The toll is waived — but the Dandolo will remember the favor.

House Giustinian (The Neutrals)

“We do not take sides. We take minutes. The minutes are confidential. Usually.” The Giustinian are arbiters, not warriors. They settle disputes between families, interpret the Foedus Stone’s cracks, and keep the peace when the Federate King cannot. Their word is not law — but ignoring it starts a feud that no one wins. Patron Asset: Giustinian Witness — Once per session, invoke a Giustinian observer to guarantee a truce for one scene. Breaking the truce ticks the Hollow Attention timer by 2.

The Federate King and the Queen of Marches

The Vilikari are not a kingdom. They are a federacy — a loose alliance of cantons, each with its own Hearth-Mother, its own banner, and its own interpretation of the Two Laws. The Federate King Aldric is elected by the Stone-Sealed Families for a term of nine years. His authority rests not on armies but on the weight of the Foedus Stone — he is the stone’s voice, and when he speaks, the cracks echo.

The Queen of Marches is his consort and his counterweight. She commands no armies and holds no charters. She rules the roads — the inns, the ferry landings, the waystations where travelers sleep. Her network of hearth-aunts and innkeepers knows every rumor, every secret, every unpaid toll. She is the reason the Vilikari have never been conquered: by the time an army arrives, its supply routes have been sold to its enemies.

The Federate King’s Court (d6)

When the party seeks an audience with the Federate King or one of the Stone-Sealed Families, roll or choose the court’s disposition:

  1. Wary: A recent crack in the Foedus Stone has everyone on edge. First social roll is Desperate.

  2. Generous: A caravan arrived with news of a rival’s misfortune. Hospitality is warm. +1 Position on all social rolls.

  3. Feuding: Two families are arguing over a bridge toll. The king is distracted. Social rolls gain +1 DV.

  4. Secretive: The Archivolt has been sealed. No one will explain why. Insight rolls are at +1 die, but lies are harder to detect.

  5. Desperate: The Ykrul have closed the eastern passes. The king needs allies. Social Position starts at Dominant — but the obligation will be steep.

  6. Hollow-Watched: The Foedus Stone’s newest crack has grown. The king speaks in eights. The ninth word is silence. The Hollow is listening.

Adventure Seeds

  1. The Crack That Walked. The Foedus Stone’s newest crack appears to have moved. The Contarini blame the Morosini. The Morosini blame the Dandolo. The party must measure the crack — and discover that it is not a crack at all. It is a door.

  2. The Archivolt Fire. Someone has set fire to the Archivolt’s oldest wing. The seal-keeper is dead. The only surviving document is a charter granting rights to a canal that no longer exists — but the canal’s ghost is now flooding the lower cantons.

  3. The Queen’s Agent. The Queen of Marches hires the party to deliver a sealed letter to a Tulkani caravan judge. The letter is a knot — a promise to pay a debt owed by a Federate King who died forty years ago. The Hollow is collecting.

  4. The Hostage’s Return. A Ykrul hostage string has been returned — cut. The hostage was the Federate King’s nephew. The Ykrul claim he escaped. The Vilikari claim he was murdered. The party must find the truth before the passes close for winter.

The Hollow and the Ninth Crack

In Vilikari lore, the Hollow is not a monster. It is the interest on a promise that was never spoken. The Foedus Stone has eight visible cracks. The ninth crack is the one that appears when a Vilikari breaks an oath sworn under the oak and the marble together. No one has seen the ninth crack in living memory. The elders say that when it appears, the stone will split — and the Vilikari will have to choose which law to keep.

The Vilikari do not speak of the ninth crack. They count to eight and stop.

Peoples of Fate’s Edge — Detailed Mechanics

The following sections give the mechanical traits — Affinity, Limit, cultural talents, Strings, sample Bonds, and sample Complications — for the major ancestries of the Amaranthine. Use them during character creation to bring your character’s heritage to life.

Heritage: Affinity and Limit

Your people do not change how many dice you roll. They change the footing you roll from.

  • Affinity (+1 Position). Each people names one Attribute and one Skill. When a roll uses either of them, improve your Position one step — Desperate \(\rightarrow\) Controlled, Controlled \(\rightarrow\) Dominant.

  • Limit (–1 Position). Each people names one Attribute. When a roll uses it, worsen your Position one step.

Three rules cover every case:

  1. They cancel. A roll that touches both your Affinity and your Limit is made at the Position the GM set. Do not apply either.

  2. They do not stack with themselves. A roll using both halves of your Affinity — your Attribute and your Skill — still improves by one step, not two.

  3. The ladder ends. Position never rises above Dominant or falls below Desperate. At either end the modifier simply does nothing; it never converts into dice.

Why it works this way.

Heritage used to add and subtract dice. That produced characters who rolled nothing — a starting Attribute of 1, a Skill of 0, and a –1 from their people left an empty hand, which is not a dramatic weakness so much as a dead end. Under Affinity and Limit your pool is only ever what you paid for, so the floor is one die and the question is always how badly a thing can go rather than whether you may attempt it at all.

It also puts heritage where it belongs. An Aeler is not stronger than a Ykrul by an arithmetic step. An Aeler is on surer ground underground, with a chisel, in the dark — and on worse ground in a room that wants to be charmed.

Small Folk of the Threshold

The Aelaerem (halflings) and Aelinnel (gnomes) are diminutive peoples attuned to liminal spaces – doorways, hedge-gaps, tide-cut stairs, and the quiet places between hearth and hollow.

Restriction:

Cannot use Heavy Armor. May use any one-handed or two-handed weapon, but Heavy Weapons impose –1 Position on all actions while wielding them (before any other Position modifiers).

Bonus:

When wielding a spear, quarterstaff, or light polearm (any Medium or Light weapon with the Reach tag), treat the weapon as one weight class lighter for the purpose of attack modifiers. Additionally, once per scene when you make a melee attack with such a weapon, you may spend 1 Boon to convert a Partial success into a Clean Success (representing a clever thrust that uses the enemy’s momentum against them).

Bonus — Once per scene, you may improve your Position by one step (e.g., Desperate \(\rightarrow\) Controlled, Controlled \(\rightarrow\) Dominant) as a free action before rolling an action. This represents a flash of luck, a well-timed sidestep, or an unexpected opening.

Affinity and Limit:

Take the Affinity of one parent people and the Limit of the other. You may instead split them — the Attribute half of one parent’s Affinity with the Skill half of the other’s — if both you and the GM think the result describes the character better than the tidy version does.

Talent Access:

You may buy Cultural Talents from both parent lists.

Cultural Talents:

You may select from both parent cultures’ talent lists, but you must meet any prerequisites (e.g., Spirit 3+ for some talents). You may not take mutually exclusive talents.

String Access:

You have access to the Strings of both cultures, but you must choose one as your primary for purposes of social perception (e.g., a half-Aeler, half-Lethai might be Charter-Stamped but not Root-Oath Bound unless you prove yourself).

Sample Bonds

  • “My father’s clan disowned me; my mother’s people took me in – I carry both names.”

  • “I am the living treaty between two feuding houses.”

Sample Complications

  • Neither culture fully trusts you.

  • You are called to honor two conflicting oaths.

  • A purist from one side challenges your right to claim their traditions.
