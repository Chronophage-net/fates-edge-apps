World Interaction

Fate's Edge Player's Guide — Chapter 14

Using This Chapter

This chapter gives you the tools to move through the world, interact with its people, and make your journey meaningful. For detailed descriptions of each region's culture, subgenre, and notable NPCs, see Chapter 12 (World Regions and Cultures). The two chapters work together: this one covers how to travel and interact; the other tells you where and with whom.

In Fate's Edge, the world is not a backdrop — it is a partner in the conversation. Dikes groan under black rain in Viterra, clan horns answer across Acasia's ridgelines, Ecktoria's marble halls echo with careful words, and Kahfagia's pilots read storms by taste. Wherever you go, place, culture, and pressure push back.

The Ways Between

Beyond the physical map lie the Ways Between — the dream-roads and spirit-paths of Obishaal, realm of dreams and death. Travel here is measured not in miles but in choices. A wrong turn might lead to yesterday. A right turn might lead to a version of yourself who made a different decision and is still alive to regret it.

Entering the Ways: Thresholds exist in thin places — crossroads at midnight, bridges that appear only at low tide, caves behind waterfalls, or simply a mist that settles too thick and stays too long. A character may also enter through ritual, dream, or near-death experience.

Navigation in Obishaal: Use the standard travel procedures, but the GM may replace the Spades (place) draw with a Virtue Test. The road judges. Acts of honesty, courage, mercy, or self-sacrifice advance a personal Virtue Timer [4]; acts of greed, cowardice, cruelty, or despair advance a Vice Timer [4]. When one fills, the character ascends (Virtue) to a higher layer or falls (Vice) to a lower one.

The Thirteenth Stone: At the deepest layer, pressed into the silt of the oldest dream, lies the Thirteenth Stone — warm, pulsing, and whispering. Here, Ikasha, She Who Sleeps, dreams of freedom. Her voice offers bargains. Her freedom would remake the world.

Game Structure and Time

Understanding how time works in Fate's Edge helps you navigate both the mechanical and narrative flow of play.

Basic Units

Scene
The basic unit of narrative play, covering a specific situation or conflict (Some Time to Significant Time). Resolves a particular question or challenge.
Player Turn (Beat)
An individual player's action within a scene: Declare action → GM sets position → roll → resolve outcome → manage consequences.
Round
Simultaneous or near-simultaneous actions within a scene (primarily for combat), representing a few seconds of real time.
Session
One complete game session (typically 3–6 hours), containing 2–4 major scenes and resolving significant narrative progress.
Downtime
The narrative time between scenes, used for recovery, advancement, and off-screen activities. Measured in days, weeks, or months depending on fiction.
Campaign
Entire story arc (6–20+ sessions) with major character development and lasting consequences.

Movement and Positioning

Space is tracked with range bands and Position (see the Core Mechanics chapter for full rules).

Range Bands

Close
Touching distance: grapples, knife-work, hand on a relic.
Near
Same room/yard/deck; a rush away.
Far
Same site but distant; requires route or time to reach.
Absent
Off-screen; requires scene change or significant effort to interact.

Movement Actions

  • Move: Shift one range band as a beat.
  • Dash: Shift two bands as your full action (terrain may require a roll).
  • Melee Flag: Mark when two parties are in Near range and directly engaged in combat.

Position States

Dominant
You have cover, leverage, or ritual footing. Failure still leaves options.
Controlled
Standard case: exposed lanes, rivals near, watchful eyes. Failure has teeth, but not ruin.
Desperate
Bad ground, bad odds, bad timing. Failure is severe; success may bring extra XP.

Position Shifting:

  • GM can spend 1 SB to worsen Position by one step.
  • Player can spend 1 Boon to improve Position by one step (once per action).
  • Narrative triggers (flanking, reinforcements, etc.) can shift Position without cost.

Narrative Time

Time is measured by importance rather than duration.

A Moment
A glance, a strike, a whisper over a law-stone.
Some Time
A skirmish, a negotiation, a careful climb.
Significant Time
Hours of march, rites, audits, stakeouts.
Days
Drills, recoveries, research, roadwork.

Social Stakes & Timers

Your GM may use visible timers to track social progress:

  • Alliance Timer (Viterra): Parishes and guilds come to your side.
  • Honor Timer (Acasia): Feasts, oaths, wyrd — trust builds (or frays).
  • Bureau Timer (Ecktoria): Stamps, seals, approvals — delay is pressure.
  • Trust Timer (Kahfagia): Pilots and crews extend favors and routes.
  • Courtesy Timer (Aelinnel): Fae negotiations — speak true or lose standing.
  • Hollow Attention Timer (Aelaerem/Mistlands): The uninvited guest draws near.

Hospitality as a Social Mechanic

In many cultures — especially Aelaerem, Ykrul, Tulkani, Ubral, and Fhara — hospitality is sacred. Accepting a cup, a meal, or a night's shelter creates a bond. Breaking hospitality is worse than breaking a sworn oath.

Hospitality Bond: When you accept hospitality (food, drink, or shelter) from a host in a culture that honors guest-right, you and the host gain a mutual Hospitality Bond [4]. While the bond has segments, neither party may take hostile action against the other. The bond ticks when either party lies to the other, breaks a promise made under the host's roof, or acts to harm the other's interests. When it fills, the bond snaps — and the violator gains a Black Mark in that culture (−1 die on all social rolls with that culture for one season).

Refusing Hospitality: Refusing an offered cup, meal, or shelter is a grave insult in hospitality cultures. The GM may spend 1 SB to worsen Position by one step in all social interactions with that host and their community for the remainder of the visit.

Obligation: The Weight of the World

Every interaction with the world carries weight. A favor accepted is a thread tied. A promise made is a chain forged. A debt incurred is a story waiting to be told. This is Obligation—the accumulated weight of your character's promises, debts, and loyalties.

What Is Obligation?

Obligation is the mechanical representation of your character's debts—the weight of promises made, favors accepted, and powers borrowed. It is not a punishment. It is a promise of future story.

In Fate's Edge, power is never free. When you invoke a Patron's Rite, accept a terrestrial patron's favor, or swear an oath that binds your fate, you mark Obligation. This debt is not a penalty—it is narrative fuel. It ensures that every gain has a cost, every alliance has a price, and every choice has consequences.

The Philosophy of Obligation

  • Power Has Cost: Every Rite, every favor, every Asset gained through patronage is a thread in a web of obligation.
  • Story Fuel: A full Obligation track is not a punishment—it's an adventure hook. Your Patron has a task for you.
  • Mechanical Weight: Obligation creates meaningful choices. Can you afford to invoke another Rite, or are you already over capacity?
  • Narrative Density: Debt creates relationships. Who do you owe? What do they want?

Tracking Obligation

Capacity

Your Obligation Capacity is: Capacity = Spirit + Presence

This represents the total Obligation you can carry across all Patrons, terrestrial patrons, and other debts before the ledger begins to creak.

SpiritPresenceCapacityNotes
112Minimal—one Rite and you're at capacity
213Can invoke three Low Rites before strain
325A dedicated Runekeeper's typical capacity
437A seasoned invoker with social connections
5510A legendary figure with immense will and influence

Marking Obligation

When you take on a debt, you mark Obligation in one of several ways:

  • Invoking a Rite (Runekeeper): Mark the Rite's listed Obligation cost.
  • Pushing a Rite: Mark an additional +1 Obligation for amplified effect.
  • Cracking the Seal (Invoker): Mark double the Rite's base cost.
  • Accepting a Terrestrial Favor: Mark +1 Obligation per favor accepted.
  • Acquiring an Asset through Patronage: Mark Obligation equal to the Asset's value tier (Minor: 1, Standard: 2, Major: 3).
  • Breaking a Promise: The GM may require you to mark Obligation when you break an oath or fail to fulfill a commitment.

Example: Marking Obligation

Kestra (Spirit 3, Presence 1, Capacity 4) invokes a Low Rite of the Traveler (Obligation: 1). She now has 1 Obligation. She later pushes a Rite (additional +1), bringing her to 2. She still has capacity for two more Rites. Later, she invokes a Standard Rite (Obligation: 2), bringing her to 4—exactly at capacity. If she invokes one more Rite, she will exceed capacity and mark Fatigue.

Exceeding Capacity

When your total Obligation exceeds your Capacity:

  1. Mark Fatigue: For each segment over Capacity, mark 1 Fatigue.
  2. No Further Rites: You cannot invoke additional Rites or accept additional favors until Obligation is reduced.
  3. Patron Intrusion (Double Capacity): If you exceed double your Capacity, clear all Fatigue, mark Harm 1, and trigger a Patron Intrusion—a narrative demand from the Patron.

Obligation in Social Context

In the world of Fate's Edge, Obligation is not just a personal resource—it is a social reality. Others can sense your debts. Some can call them in.

Strings and Obligation

A String is a narrative permission—a favor owed, a secret known, a charter held. Strings and Obligation are closely related:

  • A String is a specific favor or leverage. You hold a String over someone when they owe you.
  • Obligation is the weight of Strings you owe. Your Obligation track represents the accumulated weight of all the Strings held against you.

Reading Strings: When you interact with someone who holds a String on you, you may feel the weight of that debt. The GM may offer you a choice: "You remember that you owe this merchant a favor from last year. He knows it too. You can see it in his smile. What do you do?"

Obligation and Factions

Factions—noble houses, guilds, crime syndicates—also track Obligation. When you accept a favor from a faction, you mark Obligation to that faction. When the faction calls in its debt, you must act or face consequences.

  • House Contarini: Archivolt access, forged charters, legal immunity.
  • The Ragman (Silkstrand): Smuggling routes, night-boat passage, stolen goods.
  • The Black Banners: Condotta contracts, war mounts, safe passage.

Faction Reactions: A faction with high Obligation from you will be expectant. They will watch you, waiting for the right moment to call in their debt. A faction with no Obligation from you will treat you as a stranger—useful, but not trusted.

Clearing Obligation

Obligation is cleared through meaningful engagement with the Patron or faction to whom the debt is owed. This is not accounting—it is story.

Acts of Service (Terrestrial Patrons)

Terrestrial patrons—noble houses, guilds, crime syndicates, merchant leagues—have their own expectations. Clearing Obligation to them requires tasks that serve their interests, not cosmic principles.

Clearing Obligation to Terrestrial Patrons

  • Minor Task (Clears 1 segment): A small favor that costs time, not risk. Delivering a message, paying a bribe, serving as a witness, or providing information.
  • Significant Task (Clears 2 segments): A meaningful service with real stakes. Escorting a caravan, breaking a siege, forging a document, or intimidating a rival.
  • Major Task (Clears 3+ segments): A campaign-defining action. Assassinating a rival, brokering a treaty, stealing a relic, or leading a military operation.

Clearing Obligation to a Thieves' Guild

A Thieves' Guild doesn't care about prayers or offerings. It cares about results. Clearing Obligation to a guild might involve:

  • Minor: "The guild needs a message delivered to a fence in the Dye Yards. No questions asked. The less you know, the better."
  • Significant: "A rival crew has been moving in on our territory. I need you to send a message—the kind that doesn't require words. Make sure they understand the cost of competition."
  • Major: "The Velvet Court has been squeezing our informants. I need you to break into their stronghold and retrieve the ledger that lists our people. Burn it, bring it, or bury it—I don't care. Just make sure no one else reads it."

GM Guidance: A guild task should feel like a story hook, not a chore. The message the player delivers might be a lie that starts a war. The person they intimidate might be a future ally. The ledger they retrieve might contain information they wish they hadn't seen.

Clearing Obligation to a Merchant League

A Merchant League doesn't care about honor or secrets. It cares about profit. Clearing Obligation to a league might involve:

  • Minor: "A consignment of spice has been held at the customs house. I need you to grease the right palms and get it cleared. Use my name—but don't mention my name." (Presence + Sway, DV 3)
  • Significant: "The Vilikari route is closed. I need you to find an alternative route for our grain shipments. The Ykrul will want a bribe; the Aeler will want a charter; the Ecktorians will want a tariff. Find the path that costs the least—and if you can't find one, make one."
  • Major: "A rival has been undercutting our prices. They're selling stolen goods. I need you to expose them—publicly, dramatically, in a way that makes everyone else afraid to do business with them. Ruin them. I don't care how."

GM Guidance: Merchant tasks should create tension between profit and principle. The player might find a route that requires them to smuggle untaxed goods. They might have to expose a rival who is actually innocent. They might have to ruin a merchant who once showed them kindness.

Clearing Obligation to a Crime Syndicate

A Crime Syndicate doesn't care about profit or principles. It cares about control. Clearing Obligation to a syndicate might involve:

  • Minor: "A captain owes us money. Remind him of his debt. Don't hurt him—just remind him. He'll know what it means when he sees you." (Presence + Sway, DV 3)
  • Significant: "The Watch has been getting too curious. I need you to make sure a certain file disappears from the precinct. No traces, no witnesses, no bodies. Just a file that was never there."
  • Major: "The Salt Prince has been interfering with our operations. I need you to send a message that will make him reconsider his position. Permanently. The syndicate will provide the resources. You provide the imagination."

GM Guidance: Syndicate tasks should challenge the player's moral limits. The debtor might be a desperate mother. The missing file might have contained evidence against a corrupt official who deserved exposure. The Salt Prince might be the only thing holding back a worse power.

Clearing Obligation to a Noble House

A Noble House cares about reputation and legacy. Clearing Obligation to a house might involve:

  • Minor: "My daughter is attending a ball at House Contarini. I need someone to ensure she is seen with the right people and not the wrong ones. You'll be a guest—dance, smile, and keep her out of trouble."
  • Significant: "A rival house has been spreading rumors about my family. I need you to find the source and make sure they stop. Not with violence—with evidence. Make their lies look foolish in public."
  • Major: "My brother was killed in the last war. I need his standard returned from the battlefield where he fell. The field is occupied by Ykrul riders. The standard is the only proof that his death was honorable. Bring it home, and I will consider our debt settled."

GM Guidance: Noble tasks should entangle the player in politics. The daughter might have secrets. The rival's lies might be true. The standard might be the only proof that the brother was a traitor.

Clearing Obligation to a Pirate Captain

A Pirate Captain cares about wealth and loyalty. Clearing Obligation to a captain might involve:

  • Minor: "We need a new chart of the Smuggler's Run. The old one is three years out of date. Navigate the channels, chart the hazards, and come back with a map. Don't go too deep—the reefs are patient."
  • Significant: "A merchant ship has been sailing through our waters without paying tribute. I need you to intercept it and collect the toll. Bring me its cargo. If it resists, sink it. The sea doesn't judge."
  • Major: "A rival captain has been encroaching on our territory. I need you to capture his ship. Bring me his flag and his first mate. Leave the captain alive—I want to see his face when he learns who took his flag."

GM Guidance: Pirate tasks should test loyalty. The merchant ship might be carrying medicine for a plague-stricken village. The rival captain might be old and desperate. The player might have to choose between profit and principle.

Downtime Clearing

During downtime (see the Downtime chapter), you may clear Obligation through one of the following methods:

  • Off-Screen Service (Clears 1 segment): Describe an Act of Service performed between sessions. No roll required.
  • On-Screen Scene (Clears 1-2 segments): Play out an Act of Service during a session. The GM may call for a roll if the task is challenging.
  • XP Payment (Clears 2 segments): Spend 2 XP to clear 2 segments of Obligation. This represents dedicating time and resources to fulfilling the debt.

Clearing Obligation Through Story

Sometimes, Obligation is cleared not through a task, but through a choice. A terrestrial patron may release you from a debt if:

  • You perform an action that aligns with their deeper values (not just their surface demands).
  • You offer them something more valuable than the original debt (a secret, an alliance, a new opportunity).
  • You help them achieve a goal that matters more than the original obligation.
  • You accept a new, more significant debt in exchange for clearing the old one.

GM Guidance: Clearing Obligation through story should feel like a negotiation, not a transaction. The patron should weigh your offer. They should demand something in return. The scene should be tense, dramatic, and memorable.

Declining to Clear Obligation

If you allow your Obligation to accumulate without clearing it, the patron or faction will eventually act. When Obligation exceeds capacity:

  • Patron Intrusion: The patron demands a task. If you refuse, the patron withdraws its favor—you cannot invoke Rites, use Assets, or benefit from their patronage until you make amends.
  • Faction Action: The faction acts against you—spreading rumors, withholding aid, sending enforcers, or calling in debts you didn't know you owed.
  • Reputation Damage: Your reputation suffers. Others are less willing to offer you favors or trust your promises.

The Hollow's Debt

The Ninth Taboo (see the Core Mechanics chapter) is a special form of Obligation. When you break an Acasian custom—pour the ninth cup, take the ninth step, speak the ninth name—you mark Obligation to the Hollow. This debt is not cleared by Acts of Service in the usual sense. Instead:

  • Clear by Honoring Customs: Respect the old ways. Count to eight and stop. Leave offerings at crossroads. Speak no ninth name.
  • Clear by Making Amends: If you have broken a custom, perform a ritual to acknowledge the debt. The GM determines the form—an offering, a confession, a story.
  • Clear by Fulfilling the Oath: If the Hollow's attention was drawn by a broken promise, fulfill the promise. The Hollow is patient, but it remembers.

The Hollow does not demand. It attracts. You will find your way to settling the debt eventually—whether you want to or not.

Obligation in Play

Player Guidance: Embracing Obligation

  • Embrace It: A full Obligation track means your Patron is about to give you a quest. That quest is a session hook.
  • Clear It Through Story: Acts of Service are not chores—they are scenes. Describe how you honor your Patron.
  • Don't Hoard: Obligation is not a resource to be minimized. It is a source of narrative weight.
  • Use It for Leverage: A terrestrial patron's Obligation can be called in at strategic moments. "I remind the factor that I once carried a message for him."

Obligation and the Ninth Taboo

In Acasia and beyond, the Ninth Taboo is a form of Obligation—a debt to the land itself. The Hollow's Attention timer (see the Core Mechanics chapter) is a form of Obligation to the Curse of Acasia. When the timer fills, the Hollow manifests as an Oath-Keeper and demands payment.

Breaking Customs: When you knowingly break an Acasian custom (pours a ninth cup, takes a ninth step, speaks a ninth name), you mark Obligation to the Hollow. This is not a separate track—it is a narrative debt. The Hollow will collect.

Engaging the World — Player Actions

  • Scout & Signal: A follower can make the next travel action Dominant (mark Exposure or Harm 1 on them — see the Followers and Assets chapter).
  • Local Color: Briefly state what locals notice about you; GM offers a small fictional edge or a tempting timer — choose.
  • Mark the Map: On arrival, declare one change to the fiction (new ford, patron's shrine, toll-skip). GM may attach a minor timer as cost.
  • Asset Activation: Spend 1 Boon to activate an asset dramatically during a scene (see the Followers and Assets chapter).
  • Follower Assistance: Have a follower assist your actions for bonus dice (max +3 from all sources, see the Core Mechanics chapter).
  • Invoke Cultural Knowledge: If your character has a relevant background or has spent significant time in a region, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about local customs, taboos, or safe practices. This typically uses Lore or Insight, but the GM may call for other skills.
  • Call in a Favor: If you have a String on someone in the current scene, you may invoke it to gain +1 die on a social roll or to ask a single favor. The GM may require you to mark Obligation if the favor is significant.

Summary

The world has opinions. Movement is timers and color, position rises and sinks with weather and words, and every suit you draw speaks in a regional accent. Ask the land for a favor — then pay it back on the road.

The Gray Wanderer's Wisdom

"The road does not promise a safe journey—only a true one. Every favor you accept is a thread tied. Every promise you make is a chain forged. Every debt you incur is a story waiting to be told. The world remembers. The Hollow collects. And the Patrons—whatever names they are called—are always watching."

Remember: Every interaction with the world is an opportunity. Use your assets, deploy your followers, and engage with the setting actively. The world responds to your choices, and every journey changes both you and the places you pass through. Your Obligation is not a burden—it is the weight of your story, the threads that tie you to the world, and the promise of adventure yet to come.

The road is not a ledger — it is a conversation. Speak carefully. Listen closely. And always leave something behind for the next traveler.
