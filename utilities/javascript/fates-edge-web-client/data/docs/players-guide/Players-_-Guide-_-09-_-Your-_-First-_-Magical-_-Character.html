Your First Magical Character

Chapter 9 · Your First Magical Character

So you want to play a magic-user. Good choice—but Fate’s Edge offers six different magical paths. Jumping into all of them at once is a recipe for confusion. This guide helps you pick a path and then walks you through building a single, focused magical character that is powerful, fun, and manageable.

Quick Reference: Magic Paths at a Glance

Use this table to choose a path that fits your preferred complexity and play style. New players: start with Runekeeper.

Path Complexity Cost Type Risk Best For
Runekeeper Low Obligation Patron demands First-timers, reliable magic
Hedge Gifts Low None (Talent) None Anyone wanting a magical touch
Familiar Only Low Obligation (Patron’s Gift) Patron demands Companionship + utility
Cantor Medium Corruption Social fallout, madness Bards, performers, risk-takers
Invoker Medium–High Obligation + ritual time Slow casting, Symbol breaks Ritual specialists, planners
Summoner High Leash + Boons Spirit breaks free Pet-class fans, tactical players
Free Caster High Backlash (Fatigue/Harm) Unpredictable effects Improv players, wild magic fans
Psionics (optional) Medium Mental Strain Mental strain, social distrust Mind-power enthusiasts

All paths are viable. The table only shows the relative complexity and risk.

If you are still unsure, follow the recommendation below—you can always learn a second path later (see \(\ref{ch:magic}\)).

Step 0: The Recommendation

If this is your first magical character, play a Runekeeper. Why?

  • One Patron: You swear to a single Patron. No juggling multiple Symbols or conflicting obligations.

  • Clear costs: Every Rite costs Obligation. Track one number.

  • Support from your Thiasos: Your familiar is a built-in roleplay hook and mechanical aid.

You can explore Invokers, Cantors, Witches, Psions, or Summoners later. Start simple.

Not every player enjoys the same kind of magic. Use this table to find the path that matches how you like to play.

Still unsure? Let’s start with Runekeeper. You can always learn a second path later (see \(\ref{ch:magic}\)).

Two Low-Cost Alternatives

Before committing to a full magical path, consider these two low-cost options. Both give you a magical feel without the complexity of Rites, Corruption, or a Leash.

Hedge Gifts: Magic Without a Path

Cost: 4 XP (Craft of the Hedge talent)

Hedge Gifts are simple, no-roll magical abilities. A character with the Craft of the Hedge talent learns two Hedge Gifts of their choice. These are small, reliable effects that never require dice rolls and never incur Obligation.

Why take Hedge Gifts?

  • Cheap: 4 XP gives you two useful abilities.

  • No resource tracking: No Obligation, Corruption, or Backlash.

  • Retrain later: If you want to commit to a full magical path, you can retrain this talent during downtime.

  • Perfect for non-magical characters: A warrior with a few Hedge Gifts is still a warrior—just one who knows a few tricks.

Sample Hedge Gifts:

Gift Effect
Steady Hand Once per scene, touch someone and remove 1 Fatigue.
Knot of Favor Tie a knot; allies gain +1 die to one type of roll for the scene.
Warm Hand Touch someone; their next physical action gains +1 die or suffers -1 die.
Hearth-Sense Ask the GM one yes/no question about a threshold or promise.
Salt Line Pour a salt line; spirits must test to cross.
The Unlit Candle Extinguish a light source you can see.

Build example (32 XP):

  • Attributes: Spirit 3 (15 XP: 1\(\to\)2 = 6, 2\(\to\)3 = 9), Wits 2 (6 XP) \(\rightarrow\) 21 XP

  • Skills: Lore 2 (6 XP: 0\(\to\)1 = 2, 1\(\to\)2 = 4), Insight 1 (2 XP) \(\rightarrow\) 8 XP

  • Talent: Craft of the Hedge (4 XP) \(\rightarrow\) 4 XP

  • Total: 21 + 8 + 4 = 33 XP

Familiar Only: Thiasos Without a Codex

Cost: 2 XP (Familiar talent)

You can take the Familiar talent (Thiasos) without taking a Codex. This gives you:

  • A small creature bound to you (raven, candle flame, fungal creature, etc.)

  • Access to Patron’s Gift: Once per scene, imbue a held item with +1 Melee and +1 Thematic skill for the scene.

  • A narrative companion and roleplay hook.

  • No Rite Obligation (you don’t know any Rites).

Important: Patron’s Gift Costs Obligation

Patron’s Gift is not free. Each time you invoke it, you mark +1 Obligation to your Patron.

Your Obligation Capacity is Spirit + Presence. If you exceed capacity, you mark Fatigue (1 per segment over). You can clear Obligation during downtime by performing an Act of Service for your Patron.

This means:

  • A Spirit 3, Presence 1 character can invoke Patron’s Gift 4 times before hitting Fatigue.

  • You’re still managing a resource—it’s just simpler than tracking Rites, Corruption, or a Leash.

  • You can buy a Codex later to become a full Runekeeper.

Why take Familiar only?

  • Cheapest magical entry: 2 XP for a companion and a useful scene-long buff.

  • Minimal resource tracking: Only Obligation from Patron’s Gift (no Rites, no Corruption).

  • Retrain later: If you want to become a full Runekeeper, buy a Codex (4 XP) during downtime.

  • Follower potential: Your Thiasos can act as a Cap 1 follower, scouting, carrying small items, or delivering messages.

Build example (32 XP):

  • Attributes: Spirit 3 (15 XP: 1\(\to\)2 = 6, 2\(\to\)3 = 9), Wits 2 (6 XP) \(\rightarrow\) 21 XP

  • Skills: Lore 2 (6 XP), Insight 1 (2 XP) \(\rightarrow\) 8 XP

  • Talent: Familiar (2 XP) \(\rightarrow\) 2 XP

  • Total: 21 + 8 + 2 = 31 XP

Patron’s Gift in action: Your familiar is a brass compass that hums when you’re on the right path (Traveler). Once per scene, you touch a map or a walking stick and gain +1 Melee and +1 Lore (navigation) for the scene. You mark +1 Obligation. No Rites. No Corruption. Just a small edge—and a small debt.

Comparison: Hedge Gifts vs. Familiar Only

Feature Hedge Gifts Familiar Only
Cost 4 XP 2 XP
Resource Tracked None Obligation (Patron’s Gift)
Roll Required No No (Patron’s Gift is an action)
Can Retrain Yes Yes (buy Codex later)
Companion No Yes (Thiasos)

Retraining Later

Both options are low-cost investments that can be retrained later.

  • Hedge Gifts: During downtime, you can retrain Craft of the Hedge into another talent of equal or lesser cost (4 XP or less). The Hedge Gifts are lost, but you can buy a Codex or Patron’s Symbol instead.

  • Familiar Only: During downtime, you can buy a Codex (4 XP) and become a full Runekeeper. Your Thiasos remains; you now have access to Rites and still track Obligation.

This means you can start with a magical touch and grow into a full caster as you understand the system better.

The Gray Wanderer’s Advice

“You don’t need to be a wizard to have a little magic. A handful of Hedge Gifts or a familiar who nudges fate is enough to make a character feel touched by the supernatural. But remember: even a small favor from a Patron is a debt. Mark your Obligation. Pay it off in stories and service. The road will teach you the rest.”

Step 1: Choose a Patron (For Runekeepers and Familiar Only)

Your Patron defines your Rites, your Obligation, and your role in the story. Pick one that matches your character concept.

Patron Domain Character Concept
The Traveler Roads, journeys, safe passage Guide, courier, scout
Oath of Flame & Light Dawn, vows, protection Paladin, healer, knight
Grimmir Nature, seasons Druid, ranger, hedge-witch
Ikasha Shadow, secrets, thresholds Spy, infiltrator, night-walker
The Witness Truth, memory, revelation Investigator, judge, archivist
Mykkiel Law, covenant, judgment Templar, inquisitor, notary
The Carrion-King Decay, renewal, compost Necromancer (gentle), healer

New player favorite: The Traveler. Low Obligation costs, useful Rites, and a forgiving Patron.

Step 2: Buy Your Core Talents (Runekeeper)

At character creation (30–36XP), a full Runekeeper needs two talents:

  • Familiar (Thiasos) – 2 XP. Your bond to your Patron. It might be a raven (Traveler), a candle flame (Oath of Flame), or a small fungal creature (Carrion-King).

  • Codex – 4 XP. Your book of Rites. It could be a leather journal, a set of carved slate tablets, or a roll of bark.

Total so far: 6 XP. You have 24–28 XP remaining for Attributes, Skills, and optional talents.

Step 3: Set Your Attributes

Runekeepers rely on Spirit (Obligation capacity, Rite effectiveness) and Wits (Lore, Insight). A simple spread:

Attribute Rating Cost (incremental)
Spirit 3 15 XP (1\(\to\)2 = 6, 2\(\to\)3 = 9)
Wits 2 6 XP (1\(\to\)2 = 6)
Body 1 0 (base)
Presence 1 0 (base)

Total: 21 XP. You have 3–7 XP remaining.

Step 4: Buy Your Skills

You need Lore (to understand your Rites) and at least one social or utility skill.

Skill Rating Cost (incremental)
Lore 2 6 XP (0\(\to\)1 = 2, 1\(\to\)2 = 4)
Insight 1 2 XP (0\(\to\)1 = 2)
Sway 1 2 XP (0\(\to\)1 = 2)

Total: 10 XP. You now have 0–3 XP left—perfect for one minor talent.

Step 5: Choose Your First Rites

You begin with two Low Rites from your Patron. Pick ones that are useful and clear.

Example: The Traveler

  • Road-Sense (Low, 4 XP): Gain +1 die to avoid ambushes or delays on a journey. Use this every travel leg.

  • Traveler’s Boon (Low, 5 XP): Ignore one level of difficult terrain or bureaucracy for the scene. Great for crossing rivers or skipping paperwork.

Example: Oath of Flame & Light

  • Kindle Vow (Low, 4 XP): Declare a short vow (e.g., “I will protect the villagers”). Gain +1 die to actions fulfilling it.

  • Lay on Hands (Low, 5 XP): Cleanse an affliction or downgrade Harm by 1. The classic healing Rite.

Step 6: Understand Your Resources

You track three numbers:

  1. Obligation (to your Patron). Capacity = Spirit + Presence. For Spirit 3, Presence 1, capacity is 4. Each Rite marks +1 Obligation. When you exceed capacity, mark 1 Fatigue per extra segment.

  2. Fatigue. Each point worsens Position. When Fatigue fills your Body track (Body 1 = 1 segment), convert to Harm.

  3. Harm. Harm 1 = –1 die, Harm 2 = –2 dice, Harm 3 = incapacitated.

Player’s job: Mark these yourself. Announce when Obligation fills. The GM does not track them for you.

Step 7: Play Your First Session

Before the session

  • Write your two Rites on an index card.

  • Set your Obligation to 0.

  • Set your Fatigue to 0.

During the session

  1. Invoke a Rite: Say “I invoke Road-Sense.” Mark +1 Obligation. Roll Wits + Lore (or as specified). Apply the effect.

  2. Take a Short Rest: Clear 2 Fatigue.

  3. Accept a Patron’s demand: If the GM says “your Patron requires a task,” treat it as a story hook, not a punishment.

After the session

  • Clear Obligation by performing an Act of Service for your Patron (e.g., The Traveler: guide a lost traveler; Oath of Flame: speak a public vow). Describe the act. The GM will tell you how much Obligation clears.

  • Spend XP to raise Attributes, Skills, or buy new Rites.

Optional: Add a Minor Talent

If you have 2–4 XP left, take one of these:

  • Efficient Invocation (4 XP): Reduce Obligation cost of one specific Low Rite by 1 (minimum 0).

  • Keen Senses (2 XP): +1 die on perception checks (Insight or Insight).

  • Silver Tongue (2 XP): +1 die on persuasion attempts (Sway).

What to Avoid at First

  • Multiple Patrons. Stick to one. Cross-Resonance (see \(\ref{ch:magic}\)) adds complexity you don’t need yet.

  • Crack the Seal (Invoker ability). You are a Runekeeper. Let Invokers handle emergency rituals.

  • Cantor’s Corruption. A great path, but the Corruption timer requires active management. Save it for your second character.

  • Summoning. The Leash timer and spirit whims table are fun, but they add significant complexity. Start with Runekeeper, then explore Summoning later.

Sample Build: Kestra the Traveler’s Runekeeper

Element Choice
Name Kestra
Patron The Traveler
Attributes Spirit 3 (15 XP), Wits 2 (6 XP), Body 1, Presence 1
Skills Lore 2 (6 XP), Insight 1 (2 XP), Sway 1 (2 XP)
Talents Familiar (2 XP), Codex (4 XP)
Rites Road-Sense (4 XP), Traveler’s Boon (5 XP)
Starting Boons 3
Obligation Capacity 4 (Spirit 3 + Presence 1)

Total XP spent: 15+6+6+2+2+2+4+4+5 = 46XP – needs adjustment!

Adjusted (36XP): Spirit 2 (6 XP), Wits 2 (6 XP) = 12 XP; Lore 2 (6), Insight 1 (2) = 8; Familiar (2), Codex (4) = 6; Road-Sense (4), Traveler’s Boon (5) = 9. Total: 12+8+6+9 = 35XP. With one Bond (+2) = 37XP (bank 1 XP or drop Insight).

Kestra is a former caravan guide who swore to the Traveler after being betrayed by her crew. She carries a brass compass (her Thiasos) and a leather journal of route-maps (her Codex). She invokes Road-Sense before every journey and Traveler’s Boon to cross difficult terrain. She tracks her Obligation carefully and pays it off by guiding lost travelers through the mountains.

Talents Craft of the Hedge (4 XP)

Sample Build: The Familiar-Bonded Scout (Low-Cost Alternative)

Element Choice
Name Finn
Patron The Traveler (Familiar Only)
Attributes Wits 3 (15 XP), Body 2 (6 XP), Spirit 1, Presence 1
Skills Lore 2 (6 XP), Stealth 1 (2 XP), Athletics 1 (2 XP)
Talents Familiar (2 XP)
Patron’s Gift +1 Melee and +1 Lore (navigation) (once per scene). Costs +1 Obligation per use.
Starting Boons 3
Obligation Capacity 2 (Spirit 1 + Presence 1)

Total XP spent: 15+6+6+2+2+2 = 33XP (with one Bond +2 = 35XP, bank 1 XP).

Finn is a scout who carries a raven (his Thiasos) that helps him find safe routes. He has no Rites and no Codex—just the raven’s guidance and the Traveler’s subtle favor. Each time he calls on that favor, he marks +1 Obligation. With Spirit 1 and Presence 1, his capacity is 2—he can invoke Patron’s Gift twice before he starts taking Fatigue. He pays off his Obligation by guiding lost travelers through the hills.

Final Advice

The One Rule for New Magical Players

Spend your Boons. Hoarding them for XP conversion starves the table of drama. A re-rolled failure, an improved Position, or an activated Asset creates a memorable moment. The XP will come from your adventures.

You are now ready to play a magical character—whether a full Runekeeper, a hedge-witch with a few tricks, or a scout with a familiar. When you feel comfortable, read about the other paths (see \(\ref{ch:magic}\)). But for now, start simple. The road is long. The magic will grow.

The road is waiting. Pay your toll.
