Running Assets and Followers

Chapter 8 · Running Assets and Followers

In Fate’s Edge, Assets and Followers are not mere mechanical bonuses—they are narrative levers that shape the world between scenes. As GM, your role is to introduce them as earned rewards, adjudicate their benefits only when narratively justified, and use their neglect as a primary source of compelling complications. They thrive when they create tension between player ambition and worldly consequences—not when they become spreadsheets of passive benefits.

Design Principles for GM Use

When managing Assets and Followers in your campaign, remember:

  • They shift fiction between scenes: Assets open doors, quiet rumors, or stock larders off-screen. Followers act offscreen to gather information or manage responsibilities—never autonomously in scenes (that’s player agency).

  • They create hooks: Every Asset or Follower should suggest both opportunities (if tended) and complications (if neglected). Neglect isn’t “broken”—it’s a story waiting to happen.

  • Stay narrative-first: Prefer position, scale, and access over flat modifiers. Instead of “+2 die,” grant “start Dominant in negotiations with the guild” or “Your contact in the watch means the patrol arrives 10 minutes late.”

  • Never let them replace player agency: If an Asset or Follower solves a problem too cleanly, add a cost (obligation, timer advance, or narrative complication). Their value is in how they enable player action, not in removing risk.

  • They scale with tier: In Tier I, a single safehouse feels significant; in Tier IV, players should manage networks of Assets or high-Cap Followers to match their influence.

What They Are Not (GM Guidance)

Ensure Assets and Followers never:

  • Act as scene-solvers (e.g., “My safehouse automatically disarms all traps”).

  • Generate wealth or XP passively (their value is in narrative leverage, not bookkeeping).

  • Stack identical effects—if two Followers would grant the same Assist, the GM chooses the best narrative fit (not the strongest mechanical bonus).

Assets: GM Procedures for Introduction and Adjudication

Assets are properties, charters, networks, or institutions under player control. As GM, follow these steps when introducing or adjudicating Assets:

  1. Introduce as Narrative Reward: Never grant Assets via XP alone. Tie them to fiction:

    • “After clearing the bandit camp, the grateful village grants you use of their old mill as a Safehouse.”

    • “Your successful negotiation earns you a Guild Seat—vote and shop-rights in the artisan’s quarter.”

    Never say “Spend 4 XP for a Safehouse.” Instead: “The mill is yours—but it needs repair before it’s useful.”

  2. Price for Story Weight, Not Just XP:

    • In Tier I (0–40 XP), a Minor Asset (4 XP) should feel like a major investment—adjust cost slightly up/down for narrative significance (e.g., a unique heirloom weapon might cost 6 XP as a Minor Asset).

    • In Tier IV+ (151+ XP), Minor Assets are pocket change—reserve 4 XP costs for trivial holdings; Standard (8 XP) and Major (12 XP) Assets should feel meaningful.

  3. Always Attach a Hook: When introducing an Asset, immediately establish:

    • What happens if tended? (e.g., “The informant ring whispers of a shipment tonight.”)

    • What happens if neglected? (e.g., “If ignored for two sessions, the informants go silent—or worse, start talking to rivals.”)

  4. Adjudicate Benefits Only When Fiction Supports It:

    • Free Use: Once per session, trigger the off-screen benefit only if the Asset is maintained and the fiction supports it (e.g., a Safehouse is only useful if prepared and stocked—track via downtime scenes).

    • Scene Surge: Spend 1 Boon to turn an Asset into a dramatic on-screen intervention (one beat). Limit to one meaningful use per scene—more than that feels like a deus ex machina. Describe it vividly (see §\(\ref{sec:using-assets-followers}\)).

    • Never grant Free Use automatically—ask: “Does the fiction support using this benefit now?” If no, require a scene of preparation or a Boon spend.

  5. Use Neglect as Your Complication Engine:

    • Track Asset condition visibly (Maintained \(\rightarrow\) Neglected \(\rightarrow\) Compromised).

    • When Neglected: Advance a relevant timer (e.g., Informant Network [4] ticks +1 per session neglected) or spend 1–2 SB to create complications tied to decay (e.g., “The safehouse landlord demands rent—or eviction.”).

    • When Compromised: Deny all benefits until fixed via quest or major downtime.

GM Tip: The Asset Neglect Engine

Neglect is your best storytelling tool. When players ignore an Asset:

  • Advance a relevant timer (e.g., Shop Rent [4] ticks +1 per session neglected for a Market Front).

  • Spend 1–2 SB to create a complication tied to the Asset’s decay (e.g., “The Scholar’s Cell custodian leaves—research now requires a DV +1 roll”).

  • Let the fiction degrade the Asset’s benefit (e.g., a neglected Healing House now only clears Fatigue 1 once per downtime).

Never treat neglect as “nothing happens.” A neglected Asset is a ticking time bomb—or a chance for players to re-engage with their world.

Followers: GM Procedures for Introduction and Adjudication

Followers are named NPCs with a personal relationship to the player character. Before any procedure, you must understand what makes a Follower mechanically distinct from an Asset: Cap.

What is Cap?

Cap (Capacity) is a single number that answers one question: how capable is this Follower on their own? Cap governs two things:

  1. Independent actions: When a Follower acts on their own (an off-screen task, a delegated mission, an autonomous duty), they roll up to Capd10 against a DV you set. A Cap 3 Follower rolling 3d10 is performing at the level of a Tier III NPC in their area of expertise.

  2. Assist actions: One Follower may help a PC’s roll. The Follower can provide up to \(\min(\text{Cap},\text{relevant skill})\) assist dice. Spend 1 Boon or 1 Fatigue for each die; total assist dice are capped at +3. With the Exceptional Coordination talent, the cap rises to +4 and the Follower contributes dice equal to their Cap even if their relevant skill is lower. See the Follower procedures in Section \(\ref{sec:followers-assets-gm}\).

Cap is a ceiling, not a skill list. A Cap 3 Follower is not “proficient at three things”—they are “as competent as a Tier III specialist within their defined role.” Their specialty is determined by fiction (the street urchin knows locks; the mercenary captain knows cavalry), not by the number.

Cap is not Obligation Pool. A Follower’s Cap and a Patron’s Obligation Pool are different systems and may share the word “capacity” in colloquial use; this chapter uses Cap exclusively for Followers and Obligation Pool for Patrons. (See the Obligation rules in \(\ref{sec:obligation-corruption-gm}\).)

Follower Cap at a Glance

Cap Independent Action Assist
1 Routine tasks unaided; combat or professional work at +1 DV; rolls 1d10. +1 die
2 Competent hireling; rolls 2d10 at the DV you set. +2 dice
3 Skilled professional; rolls 3d10; may manage up to Cap Assets. +3 dice
4 Veteran leader; rolls 4d10; commands an organization. +3 dice
5 Legendary figure; rolls 5d10; shapes regional events. +3 dice

Remember: Cap is the ceiling. A Cap 3 Follower whose Loyalty is Strained contributes at –1 die; a Cap 5 Follower whose Fitness is Down cannot assist at all.

Introduction and Cost

Followers are earned through fiction, never purchased as a mechanical upgrade. As GM, follow these procedures:

  1. Introduce as Narrative Reward: Never grant Followers via XP alone. Tie them to fiction:

    • “After saving the merchant’s daughter, she swears loyalty—now she’s your Informant.”

    • “The defeated captain yields—now he follows you as your Mercenary Lieutenant.”

    Never say “Spend XP for a Follower.” Instead: “They’ll follow you—but only if you prove yourself worthy.”

  2. Price for Story Weight, Not Just XP:

    • Base cost is \(\text{Cap}^2\) XP (Cap 1 = 1 XP, Cap 2 = 4 XP, Cap 3 = 9 XP, Cap 4 = 16 XP, Cap 5 = 25 XP).

    • Tier I Floor: Until the character reaches Tier II (41 XP), Cap 1 Followers cost a minimum of 3 XP. This prevents Tier I characters from accumulating a small army of urchins before they have anything else.

    • Adjust for narrative weight: a unique spirit ally might cost 3 XP as a Cap 1 Follower; a politically pivotal Cap 3 might cost 12 XP. High-Cap Followers (4–5) should be rare and hard-won—reserve them for Tier III+ play.

  3. Always Attach a Hook: When introducing a Follower, immediately establish:

    • What happens if tended? (e.g., “Your Informant whispers of a shipment tonight.”)

    • What happens if neglected? (e.g., “If ignored for two sessions, your Informant goes silent—or worse, starts talking to rivals.”)

Adjudicating Follower Benefits

Followers do not act autonomously in scenes. Their benefits come only through three channels:

  1. Bonds (1 Boon): Draw on trust and loyalty for insight or support—a whispered warning, a remembered favor, a moment of hesitation in an enemy.

  2. Assists: Spend 1 Boon or 1 Fatigue for each die the Follower adds to a PC’s roll. The Follower can provide up to \(\min(\text{Cap},\text{relevant skill})\) dice, to a maximum of +3 assist dice from all sources. With the Exceptional Coordination talent, the cap rises to +4 and the Follower contributes dice equal to their Cap even if their relevant skill is lower.

  3. Offscreen Action (1 Boon or 1 downtime scene): The Follower handles tasks that would otherwise demand PC time (“Your Informant spends the week gathering rumors—report next session”).

Never say “My Follower scouts ahead.” The player must spend a Boon for an Assist or declare a Bond-driven action; otherwise the Follower waits off-screen.

Cap 1 Followers and the Boon Economy

Because Tier I characters have few Boons, Cap 1 Followers do not require Boon expenditure for baseline loyalty actions: asking Pip to watch a door, carry a note, or notice a familiar face is a narrative request, not a Boon spend. Boons are required only when the Cap 1 Follower’s contribution replaces a die roll (Assist) or provides unprompted insight (Bond). This preserves the player’s Boon economy while keeping the Follower useful.

What Cap Determines

The Follower’s Cap dictates what they can credibly attempt without your intervention:

  • Cap 1: Routine personal service. They can carry messages, watch doors, identify familiar faces in their home neighborhood, and notice details in their area of expertise. They cannot fight trained opponents, forge documents, scout wilderness, or lie convincingly to authority figures. If asked to perform a Cap 2+ task, they roll their 1d10 at the normal DV and likely fail—if they fail a task the PC pressed them into, Loyalty drops one step automatically (the Follower tried and was exposed to danger for nothing).

  • Cap 2: A competent hireling. They can hold a post, run a small operation, or fight alongside the party in light skirmishes.

  • Cap 3: A skilled professional. They can manage a Cap number of Assets, lead a small team, or serve as a regional specialist.

  • Cap 4–5: Veterans and legends. They command organizations, shape regional events, and have their own agendas that may conflict with the PC’s.

Loyalty and Fitness as States

Instead of numeric timers, track each Follower’s Loyalty and Fitness as one of three states each:

Follower States

State Levels Mechanical Effect
Loyalty Faithful / Strained / Broken Strained = Assist at –1 die, may refuse risky orders. Broken = leaves or becomes hostile.
Fitness Ready / Hurt / Down Hurt = Assist at –1 die, no independent actions. Down = requires rescue or medical care; contributes nothing.

Change states only when the fiction demands it. No rolls are required; you change states in response to events. A Follower whose sister is sold into indentured service does not need a Persuasion check to feel Strained—they simply are.

Loyalty Degradation by Cap

A Cap 1 street urchin’s loyalty fails for different reasons than a Cap 5 spymaster’s. As GM, use these triggers as defaults, then adjust for fiction:

Loyalty Degradation Triggers by Cap

Cap Strained Trigger Broken Trigger
1 1 session without direct contact 2 sessions without direct contact, or asked to do Cap 2+ work without success
2 2 sessions without meaningful interaction Betrayal witnessed or pay withheld
3 Betrayal of stated principles Failure to clear a major obligation
4–5 Failure to honor a factional oath Public humiliation or sacrifice of the Follower

Rationale: Cap 1 Followers are tied to the PC personally, not institutionally. Their loyalty is maintained by attention, not abstract duty. High-Cap Followers have obligations and agendas of their own; their loyalty is more durable but their breaking points are more dramatic.

Neglect as Your Complication Engine

  • If a Follower’s Loyalty becomes Strained or Broken, all Assets they manage immediately become Neglected.

  • If a Follower’s Fitness becomes Down, they cannot perform off-screen tasks—they need tending first.

  • Neglecting a Follower’s upkeep degrades their state—use this as your complication engine, just as you do with Assets.

Follower Upkeep by Cap

Upkeep cost scales with Cap, but the form changes:

Follower Upkeep by Cap

Cap Upkeep Cost If Neglected
1 1 downtime scene per chapter (no XP option) Loyalty degrades per Cap 1 triggers; Failure to perform task the PC demanded drops Loyalty one step.
2–5 XP = Cap \(\times\) 2 per session OR 1 downtime scene Loyalty/Fitness degrade per Cap-appropriate triggers; Asset neglect cascades.

Why the asymmetry: A Tier I character cannot afford 2 XP/session/Cap 1 Follower without going bankrupt by session 5. Cap 1 Followers cost attention, tracked by the Loyalty table; Cap 2–5 Followers cost XP or time, scaling with their capability.

GM Tip: The Follower Neglect Engine

Neglect is your best storytelling tool. When players ignore a Follower:

  • Degrade their Loyalty/Fitness state based on fiction (e.g., “After missing pay for two sessions, your Mercenary Lieutenant’s Loyalty turns Strained—Assist at –1 die”).

  • If neglected for 2+ sessions, trigger a consequence (e.g., “Your Informant sells your secrets to a rival—spend 1 SB to learn what they revealed”).

  • Let the fiction reflect the strain (e.g., a Hurt Follower can’t undertake offscreen tasks—they need tending first).

Never treat neglect as “nothing happens.” A neglected Follower is a relationship in crisis—or a chance for players to rebuild trust.

The Interaction Loop: Assets, Followers, and the Core Mechanics

Assets and Followers integrate seamlessly with Fate’s Edge’s core resolution loop. As GM, guide players through this cycle:

  1. Player declares intent and approach (Attribute + Skill).

  2. You state Position and set relevant Timers.

  3. Player rolls d10s; counts Successes (6+) and Story Beats (1s).

  4. Compare to DV \(\rightarrow\) use Outcome Matrix.

  5. Apply consequences and update the fiction—this is where Assets and Followers shine:

    • On Partial or Miss: Players may spend Boons to activate Assets/Followers (Scene Surge or Bond-driven action).

    • On Success: Players may use Assets/Followers offscreen to prepare for next scene (Free Use or downtime action).

    • Always: Check Asset/Follower condition—neglect triggers complications before applying the roll’s outcome.

  6. Pass the spotlight—but first, remind players of pending upkeep:

    • “Before we move on—your Safehouse needs tending this downtime. Pay 4 XP or devote a scene to it, or it becomes Neglected.”

    • “Your Informant’s Loyalty is Strained—they’ll refuse risky orders until you make amends.”

GM Tip: The Upkeep Reminder

Make upkeep a narrative moment—not bookkeeping. At scene end:

  • Ask: “Who tends their Assets/Followers this downtime? What does that look like in fiction?”

  • Let players describe the action (e.g., “I spend the morning greasing the Safehouse chains,” “I buy my Follower a drink and apologize for the last mission”).

  • Only then apply mechanical effects: pay XP, change state, or advance neglect timers.

This turns upkeep from a chore into roleplay—exactly where Assets and Followers belong.

Using Assets and Followers in Play: GM Procedures

As GM, your role is to:

  • Frame Asset/Follower benefits only when fiction supports it:

    • A Safehouse is only useful if prepared and stocked (track via downtime scenes).

    • A Follower only assists if their Loyalty/Fitness state allows it (track via fiction-driven state changes).

    • Never grant benefits automatically—ask: “Does the fiction support using this benefit now?” If no, require a scene of preparation, a Boon spend (Scene Surge/Bond-driven action), or a narrative cost.

  • Allow flashbacks only with a modest cost:

    • To show prior work (e.g., “You spent yesterday preparing the Safehouse”), require 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene.

    • Never allow free exposition—flashbacks must cost something meaningful.

  • Tie complications directly to Asset/Follower condition:

    • If Neglected: Spend SB against it first (e.g., “The Safehouse is Neglected—spend 1 SB to advance the Landlord Dispute [4] timer”).

    • If Compromised: Deny benefits until fixed via quest or major downtime.

    • If Follower Loyalty Broken: They leave or become hostile—spend 1 SB to show their departure or betrayal.

  • Spotlight spread: Rotate which Assets/Followers take the stage so each investment breathes on-screen now and then—never let one Asset or Follower dominate all scenes.

  • Use them to create obligation and timers:

    • Leveraging an Asset against faction interests may add Obligation (see \(\ref{sec:obligation-corruption-gm}\)).

    • Neglecting a Follower may advance a Trust Eroded [4] timer.

    • Using a Follower for offscreen work may fill segments on a Reputation [6] timer.

Example of Play (GM Perspective)

A player has a Safehouse (Minor Asset) and an Informant (Cap 1 Follower). As GM:

Scene Start

Ask: “Is the Safehouse maintained and stocked? Is the Informant tended?”

  • Both tended: Grant Free Use to start an entry/exit scene at Dominant position. Describe: “The landlord nods at your signal—’All clear. Proceed to the cellar.’ ”

  • Safehouse Neglected: Describe: “The floorboard creaks differently. Your landlord eyes you over tea: ’Business has been slow. I wonder why.’ ” Then spend 1 SB to advance Landlord Dispute [4] timer +1.

  • Informant Loyalty Strained: Describe: “Your Informant avoids your gaze. ’I’ve got nothing for you this week—business’s been rough.’ ” They refuse to assist until tended.

During the Scene

When the player wants to use Asset/Follower:

  • Safehouse Scene Surge (1 Boon): Describe: “You grip the hidden lever—’Hold!’ The wine rack slides aside, revealing the escape tunnel. For one beat, pursuit is negated—just enough to slip into the alley.”

  • Informant Bond-driven action (1 Boon): Describe: “You slide a coin across the table—’Remember the river bend?’ Their eyes light up: ’The shipment leaves at midnight. Take the north ridge.’ ” Grant +1 die on the next Stealth roll (Cap 1, baseline loyalty).

Scene End

Remind players of upkeep:

  • “Your Safehouse needs tending—pay 4 XP or devote a scene to avoid Neglect.”

  • “Your Informant’s Loyalty is Strained—spend downtime mending trust or they’ll go silent next session.”

Pacing and Scaling Guidance

As GM, use Assets and Followers to scale challenge naturally with tier:

  • Tier I (0–40 XP):

    • Assets are rare and hyper-local (e.g., a single Safehouse, a trusted Informant).

    • Followers are low-Cap (Cap 1–2) and deeply personal (e.g., a sibling, a mentor).

    • GM Guidance: One Asset or Follower should feel significant—neglect creates immediate, personal complications (e.g., “Your Informant won’t help until you repay their favor”). Cap 1 Followers are introduced through fiction (oath, blood-debt) and their cost is attention, not XP—this preserves the Tier I economy.

  • Tier II (41–90 XP):

    • Assets begin to show regional reach (e.g., a Guild Seat, a Harbor Office).

    • Followers include mid-Cap (Cap 2–3) figures (e.g., a guild factor, a veteran sergeant).

    • GM Guidance: Assets/Followers should tie into regional politics or trade—neglect invites rivals or invites scrutiny (e.g., “Neglect your Guild Seat for two sessions, and rivals challenge your voting rights”).

  • Tier III (91–150 XP):

    • Assets scale to national influence (e.g., a Banking Charter, a Fortress).

    • Followers include high-Cap (Cap 3–4) figures (e.g., a bank director, a garrison commander).

    • GM Guidance: Assets/Followers should tie into national economics or military power—neglect risks invasion, embargo, or revolt (e.g., “Neglect your Fortress for two sessions, and the garrison mutinies”).

  • Tier IV+ (151+ XP):

    • Assets are empire-shaping (see Tier III Institutions, \(\ref{sec:tier-iii-assets}\)).

    • Followers include Cap 4–5 figures who can manage Assets on your behalf.

    • GM Guidance: Assets/Followers should tie into imperial succession or continental wars—neglect risks civil war or foreign invasion (e.g., “Neglect your Palace Complex for two sessions, and court factions secede”).

GM Tip: The Scaling Mantra

At high tiers, Assets and Followers aren’t “more powerful”—they’re “more complex.”

  • A Tier V character doesn’t need “a better Safehouse”—they need “a Safehouse network with competing landlords and shifting loyalties.”

  • A Tier V character doesn’t need “a stronger Follower”—they need “a Follower with their own agenda, Obligations, and potential to betray.”

The challenge isn’t in the mechanical bonus—it’s in navigating the web of relationships and responsibilities they create.

Common Pitfalls and Fixes

As GM, watch for these issues and apply these fixes:

Pitfall Fix
Asset hoarding: Players collect Assets like XP, neglecting most. Enforce Capacity (optional rule: Assets > Spirit cost +50% upkeep or extra downtime scene). Make neglect hurt—advance timers, spend SB, or degrade benefits.
Follower automation: Players treat Followers as scene-solvers. Remind them: Followers only act through Bonds/Assists (Boon spend) or offscreen work. Never grant autonomous scene actions.
Cap mismatch: Players assign Cap 1 urchins to tasks only Cap 3+ Followers can credibly attempt. Enforce Cap as a ceiling. A Cap 1 Follower asked to do Cap 2+ work rolls 1d10 at the normal DV and likely fails; if they fail a task the PC pressed them into, Loyalty drops automatically.
Power creep: Assets/Followers make rolls too easy. Never grant flat modifiers (+2 die). Always use narrative benefits (Position shift, DV \(-1\), timer fill). If it feels too strong, add a cost (e.g., “Using the Safehouse tonight costs 1 Obligation to the guild”).
Neglect ignored: Players overlook Asset/Follower decay. Make neglect visible and consequential: describe the decay (rust, empty coffers, strained voices) and tie it to immediate complications (SB spends, timer advances).
One Asset/Follower dominance: One investment overshadows all others. Enforce spotlight spread: require players to rotate focus. If one Asset dominates, advance a neglect timer on it to force diversification.
Free Use abuse: Players treat Free Use as automatic. Only grant Free Use if fiction supports it. Require a Boon spend (Scene Surge) or downtime scene for offscreen benefits when neglected or unprepared.
Boon economy collapse: Tier I players spend every Boon on Follower upkeep, starving the core loop. Cap 1 Followers do not require Boons for baseline loyalty actions (watching a door, carrying a note). Boons are required only when the Follower’s contribution replaces a roll or provides unprompted insight.

Optional Advanced Rules

Use these sparingly to add depth—but only when the fiction demands it.

Asset Networks (GM Guidance)

For players with multiple Assets, as GM, allow them to declare a “Network” once per session (cost: 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene) to:

  • Treat two Minor Assets’ upkeep as waived this downtime (like the Market Front Standard Asset benefit).

  • Grant +1 die to a roll involving both Assets (e.g., using a Safehouse and Informant to gather intelligence).

  • Mitigate 1 SB from a complication tied to either Asset (e.g., “Your Informant warns of the landlord’s visit—spend the Boon to avoid the rent dispute”).

GM Guidance: Limit to one Network declaration per session. Networks should feel earned—require players to describe how the Assets connect (e.g., “I pay the Informant to tip off the Safehouse landlord about incoming inspectors”).

Follower Management (GM Guidance)

For players with high-Cap Followers (Cap 3+), as GM, allow them to delegate Asset management once per session (cost: 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene) to:

  • Have the Follower tend up to Cap Assets (e.g., a Cap 3 Follower tends 3 Assets).

  • The Follower’s own upkeep (XP or downtime scene) automatically covers the upkeep of delegated Assets—no separate cost.

  • If the Follower’s Loyalty becomes Strained or Broken, all delegated Assets immediately become Neglected.

  • Cap 1 and Cap 2 Followers cannot manage Assets. Asset management requires organizational capacity the Follower does not possess. Cap 1–2 Followers can perform single discrete tasks on an Asset’s behalf (“Watch the safehouse door for one night,” “Carry this message to the landlord”), but not ongoing management.

GM Guidance: This creates meaningful tension—players must choose between tending Followers directly or delegating Asset management. Neglecting the Follower risks losing multiple Assets at once.

Obligation Integration (GM Guidance)

When Assets or Followers are tied to a faction (e.g., Temple Complex, Informant Ring), as GM, use them to drive Obligation:

  • Leveraging an Asset against faction interests adds 1–2 Obligation segments (e.g., “Using the Banking Charter to freeze a rival’s account adds 2 Obligation to the bank”).

  • Neglecting a Follower tied to a faction adds 1 Obligation segment per session neglected (e.g., “Ignoring your Informant’s pleas adds 1 Obligation to the spy network”).

  • Tending a Follower’s welfare reduces Obligation by 1 segment per scene (e.g., “Spending downtime with your Informant clears 1 Obligation”).

GM Guidance: Track this Obligation visibly—use it to create complications when full (e.g., “Your Obligation to the bank is full—spend 1 SB to advance the Bank Audit [6] timer”).

Quick Reference (GM)

Asset/Follower Quick Reference (GM)

  • Asset Costs: Minor 4 XP / Standard 8 XP / Major 12 XP / Tier III 16–20 XP. GM Guidance: Adjust slightly for narrative weight (e.g., a unique artifact might cost 6 XP as a Minor Asset).

  • Follower Costs: \(\text{Cap}^2\) XP, with a Tier I floor of 3 XP for Cap 1 until the character reaches Tier II. GM Guidance: Adjust for story weight (e.g., a unique spirit ally might cost 3 XP as Cap 1). High-Cap Followers (4–5) reserved for Tier III+.

  • Cap (Capacity): Maximum die a Follower may roll independently; maximum assist contribution to a PC’s roll (capped at +3 dice per action). Remember: Cap is a ceiling, not a skill list. It is not the Patron’s Obligation Pool.

  • Asset Free Use: Once per session, off-screen leverage only if fiction supports it. Frame at scene start or as flashback with GM assent (cost: 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene).

  • Asset Scene Surge: Spend 1 Boon for a one-beat, on-screen intervention. Limit to one meaningful use per scene.

  • Follower Benefits: Bonds (1 Boon for insight/support), Assists (1 Boon or 1 Fatigue per die, up to \(\min(\text{Cap},\text{relevant skill})\) and capped at +3; Exceptional Coordination raises the cap to +4 and lets the Follower contribute dice equal to Cap), or Offscreen Action (1 Boon or 1 downtime scene). Cap 1 baseline loyalty actions are free.

  • Asset Upkeep: Pay XP = base cost OR devote a downtime scene. Neglect degrades condition (Maintained \(\rightarrow\) Neglected \(\rightarrow\) Compromised).

  • Follower Upkeep: Cap 1 = 1 downtime scene/chapter (no XP option). Cap 2–5 = XP = Cap \(\times\) 2 per session OR 1 downtime scene.

  • Asset Capacity (Optional): Assets \(\leq\) Spirit are easy; beyond that, upkeep taxes mount (+50% per extra Asset) or requires extra downtime scene.

  • Follower Management (Optional, Cap 3+): A Follower tends up to Cap Assets once per session (cost: 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene). Cap 1–2 Followers cannot manage Assets.

  • Neglect is Your Engine: Always use Asset/Follower neglect to create complications—advance timers, spend SB, or degrade benefits. Never let neglect be “nothing happens.”

Worked Example: A Cap 1 Follower

“Pip,” the Locksmith’s Apprentice

Cap

1 (independent rolls: 1d10; assists: +1 die)

Loyalty

Faithful (introduced this session; player saved him from a press gang)

Fitness

Ready

Hook

Pip’s sister is indentured to the Velvet Court; he’ll do anything to buy her contract back.

What he does for free

Watch a door, carry a note, identify a familiar face in the locksmith’s district, notice a new lock on a familiar shop.

What he does for 1 Boon

+1 die on a Craft or Deception roll in his home neighborhood (he knows the locksmiths and the beggars); unprompted Bond insight (“The lock on that warehouse—I can tell you who made it.”).

What he can’t do

Fight a soldier, travel farther than one day’s ride, keep a secret from his sister for more than a day, forge a document, scout wilderness.

If asked to do Cap 2+ work

He rolls 1d10 at the normal DV and likely fails. If the PC pressed him into a task beyond his capacity and he failed, his Loyalty drops to Strained automatically (he tried and was exposed to danger for nothing).

Neglect consequence

If 2 sessions pass without the player asking after his sister, Loyalty drops to Strained and Pip starts leaking small rumors to the Velvet Court.

Why this works: Pip is useful in his niche, free to maintain (1 downtime scene per chapter), and his limitations are encoded in the fiction, not in arbitrary rules. The player can choose to invest in him (asking after his sister, spending Boons to use him well) and see those investments pay off, or neglect him and watch him become a liability. Either path produces story.

Summary

As GM, remember:

  • Assets and Followers are narrative levers, not mechanical bonuses—they shape the world between scenes, not during them.

  • Their true value lies in the hooks they create—what happens if tended? what if neglected?

  • Neglect is your best storytelling tool—use it to advance timers, spend SB, or degrade benefits.

  • Always tie benefits to fiction—never grant Free Use or Follower assistance automatically. Require a Boon spend, downtime scene, or narrative cost when unprepared or neglected.

  • Cap is the Follower’s ceiling, not their skill list. A Cap 1 Follower is not a weaker Cap 5—they are a different kind of tool, useful in their niche and limited outside it.

  • Spotlight spread is essential—rotate which Assets/Followers take the stage so each investment feels earned and consequential.

  • At high tiers, complexity > power—the challenge isn’t in stronger bonuses, but in navigating webs of loyalty, obligation, and competing interests.

Choose what to own and who to follow—then let the world answer. The best Assets and Followers create as many problems as they solve, and the best complications come from neglect, not power.

From Faction to Asset/Follower: Taking Over a Terrestrial Patron (GM Guidance)

Sometimes you stop owing the guild – and start owning it. Rising to lead a faction (Thieves’ Guild, Mercenary Company, Banking Charter, etc.) is a major story arc, not a simple XP purchase. As GM, run this arc as a 2–3 session collaboration with the player.

Narrative Prerequisites (GM Verification)

Before approving a takeover attempt, verify:

  • The player has at least 6 Obligation with the faction (deep entanglement).

  • They completed a major service for the faction (your discretion–e.g., saving it from destruction, winning a war, exposing a traitor at great cost).

  • They secured at least one internal ally with significant influence (another captain, a guildmaster, a wealthy backer).

If prerequisites aren’t met, the takeover attempt fails narratively–no XP cost, just a complication.

The Takeover Arc (GM Collaboration)

Collaborate on a 2–3 session arc that typically involves:

  1. Crisis or Power Vacuum – The current leader dies, disappears, or is disgraced (you define the crisis).

  2. Claim of Right – The player presents a valid claim (charter, election, duel victory, popular support–you judge validity).

  3. Consolidation – Defeat or co-opt rivals, bribe or intimidate dissenters, and secure the faction’s resources (you play the opposition).

This arc should test the player’s ingenuity, not just their XP pool.

Mechanical Cost (GM Enforcement)

Once the arc is complete, the player must spend 12 XP (the cost of a Major Asset) to convert the faction into a personal Asset. As GM:

  • Enforce this cost strictly–no conversions without payment.

  • Describe the conversion as institutional restructuring: “The ledgers are rewritten, the oaths renewed–it’s yours now, but the work has only begun.”

Converting the Asset/Follower (GM Guidance)

The new Asset uses the statistics of the most appropriate existing Asset from this compendium, modified by the faction’s nature:

  • Thieves’ Guild \(\rightarrow\) Informant Ring (Standard) or Smuggler’s Network (Standard)

  • Mercenary Company \(\rightarrow\) Mercenary Contract (Minor/Standard) or Military Outpost (Major)

  • Banking Charter \(\rightarrow\) Banking Charter (Major) or Trading Consortium (Major)

  • Royalist Network \(\rightarrow\) Intelligence Bureau (Specialized) or Diplomatic Enclave (Major)

  • Ecktorian Mystery Cult \(\rightarrow\) Temple Complex (Standard) or Magical Laboratory (Specialized)

As GM, feel free to create a custom Asset using \(\ref{sec:gm-assets-procedures}\) if none fit the faction’s nature.

What You Gain (GM Description)

Upon conversion, grant:

  • The Asset’s normal Free Use and Scene Surge benefits.

  • The Access Tag associated with the faction (e.g., Guild-Ledgered, Harbor Handsigns).

  • A permanent obligation track toward the faction’s members (now your underlings). This track is smaller (4 segments) and fills when you neglect their welfare or make reckless decisions that endanger them.

As GM, track this obligation and use it to create complications when neglected (e.g., ’The crew grumbles–pay is late again. After 2 sessions neglected, they threaten to walk.’)

What You Lose (GM Enforcement)

Enforce these losses strictly:

  • The player can no longer use the faction as a terrestrial patron to gain Obligation-free perks–they are now responsible for it.

  • The faction becomes a target for your enemies–advance a Rival Faction timer whenever they use the Asset aggressively.

  • They must pay normal upkeep (XP or downtime scenes) to keep the Asset Maintained. Neglect causes internal dissent, corruption, or defections–use this as your complication engine.

Optional: Gradual Takeover (GM Permission)

For a slower, more story-driven approach, the player may forgo the 12 XP cost but must instead spend a downtime scene per session managing the faction (equivalent to Intensive upkeep) and accept that the Asset’s benefits are unreliable until they have successfully completed a full arc of consolidation. As GM, you may allow the XP cost to be paid in installments (e.g., 4 XP per downtime period) to represent gradual investment.

Example: Rellan Takes the Black Ledger (GM Narration)

Rellan has served the Black Ledger smuggling syndicate for two arcs, accruing 6 Obligation. After exposing a rival who betrayed the syndicate (your narration), the aging leader steps down. Rellan spends 12 XP, completes a short arc consolidating loyal captains (you play the opposition), and converts the syndicate into a Smuggler’s Network (Standard Asset). He now commands hidden routes and safehouses–but must keep the crew paid and protected, or an internal revolt timer will start ticking. As GM, describe the takeover’s cost: ’The syndicates’ marks are scratched from the walls. New oaths are sworn–and new enemies are made.’

Compendium of Assets

In Fate’s Edge, Assets are your off-screen levers—properties, charters, networks, and institutions that change the fiction between scenes. They do not fight beside you (see \(\ref{ch:running-assets-followers}\) for Followers) but they shape the ground you walk on. As GM, your role is to:

  • Introduce assets as narrative rewards (not automatic power-ups).

  • Price them appropriately to campaign tier and fiction.

  • Adjudicate their benefits only when narratively justified.

  • Use their neglect as a source of meaningful complications.

Assets thrive when they create tension between player ambition and worldly consequences—not when they become spreadsheets.

Design Principles for GM Use

When introducing or homebrewing assets for your campaign, remember:

  • Assets shift fiction between scenes: Use them to open doors, quiet rumors, stock larders, or calm crowds off-screen. Their power lies in what happens when players aren’t looking.

  • Assets create hooks: Every asset should suggest both opportunities (if tended) and complications (if neglected). A neglected asset isn’t “broken”—it’s a story waiting to happen.

  • Stay narrative-first: Prefer position, scale, and access over flat modifiers. Instead of “+2 die,” grant “start Dominant in negotiations with the guild.”

  • Never let assets replace player agency: If an asset solves a problem too cleanly, add a cost (obligation, timer advance, or narrative cost).

What Assets Are Not (GM Guidance)

As GM, ensure assets never:

  • Act autonomously inside a scene (that’s Followers’ role).

  • Generate wealth or XP passively (their value is in narrative leverage, not bookkeeping).

  • Stack identical effects—if two assets would grant the same edge, the GM chooses the best narrative fit (not the strongest mechanical bonus).

Narrative First, Mechanics Second (GM Application)

When an asset grants “start Dominant”, it applies to social/informational rolls where positioning matters. For practical challenges (lock-picking, repairs), “reduce DV by 1” is preferred. As GM, choose the form that best serves the fiction: - If the asset represents influence (e.g., a noble’s favor), use Position shifts. - If it represents tools or preparation (e.g., a well-stocked workshop), use DV reduction. - When in doubt, ask: “Does this change how the character approaches the problem, or just make the roll easier?” Favor the former.

Introducing Assets: GM Best Practices

Assets should feel earned, not bought. Use these guidelines when placing assets in your campaign:

  • Tier Appropriateness:

    • Tier I (0–40 XP): Assets are rare and local (e.g., a single safehouse, a trusted informant). Cost 4 XP feels significant.

    • Tier II (41–90 XP): Assets begin to show regional reach (e.g., a guild seat, a harbor office). Standard (8 XP) assets emerge as meaningful investments.

    • Tier III (91–150 XP): Assets scale to national influence (e.g., a banking charter, a fortress). Major (12 XP) assets become campaign-defining.

    • Tier IV+ (151+ XP): Assets are empire-shaping (see Tier III Institutions, \(\ref{sec:tier-iii-assets}\)). Reserve 16–20 XP assets for hard-won victories.

  • Fiction First, Cost Second: Never assign an asset cost based solely on XP. Ask:

    • What narrative justification exists for this asset? (e.g., “The character saved the merchant’s caravan—now they’re granted a trade charter.”)

    • Does the asset’s scope match the fiction? (A village herbalist shouldn’t grant a “National Healing House” asset.)

    • What complication arises if neglected? (This is your hook for future scenes.)

  • Price for Narrative Weight, Not Power: A 4 XP asset in Tier I should feel as impactful as a 12 XP asset in Tier III—because it represents a larger proportion of the character’s influence. Adjust XP costs slightly up/down to reflect narrative significance (e.g., a unique artifact might cost 6 XP as a Minor Asset due to story weight).

  • Always Attach a Hook: When introducing an asset, immediately establish:

    • What happens if tended? (e.g., “The informant ring whispers of a shipment tonight.”)

    • What happens if neglected? (e.g., “If ignored for two sessions, the informants go silent—or worse, start talking to rivals.”)

GM Tip: The Asset Neglect Engine

Neglect is your best storytelling tool. When players ignore an asset:

  • Advance a relevant timer (e.g., Informant Network [4] ticks +1 per session neglected).

  • Spend 1–2 SB to create a complication tied to the asset’s decay (e.g., “The safehouse landlord demands rent—or eviction.”).

  • Let the fiction degrade the asset’s benefit (e.g., a neglected Scholar’s Cell now requires a DV +1 roll for research).

Never treat neglect as “nothing happens.” A neglected asset is a ticking time bomb—or a chance for players to re-engage with their world.

Asset Mechanics (GM Procedures)

All assets share these core mechanics. As GM, apply them consistently:

Cost

Minor 4 XP, Standard 8 XP, Major 12 XP. GM Guidance: Treat cost as a narrative threshold. In Tier I, 4 XP is a major investment; in Tier IV, it’s pocket change. Adjust slightly for story weight (see above).

Establishment

1 day / 1 week / 1 month respectively to become operational. GM Guidance: Use this for downtime storytelling. “The safehouse takes a week to prepare—what do you do while waiting?”

Upkeep

Each downtime, either pay XP equal to the Asset’s base cost or devote a full scene tending it. Neglect degrades condition (Maintained \(\rightarrow\) Neglected \(\rightarrow\) Compromised). GM Guidance: Track condition visibly. When an asset becomes Neglected, it’s your cue to introduce complications. Compromised means it’s broken—fixing it requires a quest or major downtime.

Free Use

Once per session, trigger the listed off-screen benefit (framed at scene start or as flashback with GM assent). GM Guidance: This is not automatic. Ask: “Does the fiction support using this benefit now?” If yes, grant it; if no, require a scene of preparation or a Boon spend (Scene Surge).

Scene Surge

Spend 1 Boon to turn an Asset into a dramatic on-screen intervention as described (one beat, fiction permitting). GM Guidance: This is the asset’s “big moment.” Limit to one meaningful use per scene—more than that feels like a deus ex machina. Describe it vividly (see \(\ref{sec:using-assets}\)).

Extra Use

Spend 2 XP to trigger the off-screen benefit an additional time this session. GM Guidance: Rarely allow this—it risks making assets feel like vending machines. Reserve for narrative crescendos (e.g., “You’ve poured everything into this safehouse—use it twice this session to flee the duke’s guards.”).

Risk Interface

When an asset covers risk, it typically improves Position, lowers DV by 1, fills 1 segment on a relevant timer, or mitigates 1 SB (Story Beat) from a thematically linked complication. GM Guidance: This is where assets shine. Instead of “+2 die,” say “Your contacts in the watch mean the patrol arrives 10 minutes late—start Dominant.” Make the benefit feel earned by the fiction.

Capacity (Optional GM Rule)

A character comfortably maintains a number of assets up to their Spirit rating. For each asset beyond this:

  • Increase its upkeep by +50% (round up) OR

  • Require an additional downtime scene to keep it Maintained.

GM Guidance: Use this to prevent asset hoarding. At Spirit 3, a 4th asset should feel like a strain—perhaps requiring a scene of frantic paperwork or causing minor complications until tended. This creates natural tension between ambition and upkeep.

Cross-Reference to Faction Ties

Assets tied to a specific faction (e.g., Temple Complex, Banking Charter, Guild Seat) may generate Obligation if used recklessly or ignored. As GM, consult \(\ref{sec:obligation-corruption-gm}\) to:

  • Award Obligation segments when players leverage assets against faction interests.

  • Let Obligation decay naturally if players tend the faction’s welfare.

  • Use neglected assets as Obligation sources (e.g., “Ignoring the temple’s pleas adds 1 Obligation to the gods each session neglected”).

Minor Assets (4 XP): GM Tools for Local Leverage

Minor assets are small holdings, charters, or kits that grant reliable, localized leverage. As GM, use them to:

  • Reward early-game ingenuity without unbalancing the campaign.

  • Create intimate, character-driven story hooks (e.g., a family heirloom, a neighborhood favor).

  • Introduce complications that feel personal and immediate when neglected.

Each entry below includes GM-specific guidance on introduction, adjudication, and neglect consequences.

Signature Weapon

A named heirloom or masterpiece. GM Free Use Guidance: If gear damage would compromise it (under the equipment-condition rules in the SRD), downgrade to a minor flaw and require a quiet maintenance scene to clear—do not auto-repair. GM Scene Surge Guidance: Produce it at the perfect moment; improve Position one step for the first attack this scene. Describe the draw: “As the bandit lunges, your father’s blade sings free—your grip is sure.” GM Neglect Hook: If unused for 2+ sessions, the weapon requires repair (Cost: 1 XP or a downtime scene). Neglect further risks it being stolen or broken beyond repair. Example: “The hilt is loose—swinging it feels like shaking a tooth. Repair it, or it flies from your grip next clash.”

Scholar’s Cell

A quiet room, permits, and a custodian. GM Free Use Guidance: Begin one research scene at Dominant (or lower DV by 1). Only grant this if the fiction supports it (e.g., the cell is prepared, the custodian is present). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Reveal a cited source now; fill 1 segment on a relevant Information timer. Describe the find: “Between the mossy pages, you find the exact passage—ink still wet.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, the custodian leaves or permits lapse. Research now requires a DV +1 roll (bribes, forgery, or breaking in). After 3 sessions neglected, the cell is seized by authorities. Example: “The landlord knocks: ’Your rent’s due—and I’ve heard whispers about those strange books.’ Pay up, or they’re calling the watch.”

Safehouse Network (Quarter)

Trapdoors, false walls, and a landlord who never saw you. GM Free Use Guidance: Start an entry/exit scene Dominant. Only grant if the safehouse is prepared and stocked (track via downtime scenes). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Produce a hidden egress; convert one pursuit consequence into a temporary complication you can interact with. Describe the escape: “Behind the wine rack—a stone slides aside. Cool night air hits your face as you slip into the alley.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, the landlord grows suspicious or sells the property. After 1 session neglected, safehouse use costs 1 Boon to start Dominant; after 2 sessions, it’s compromised (requires a scene to re-secure). Example: “The floorboard creaks differently now. Your landlord eyes you over tea: ’Business has been slow. I wonder why.”’

Artisan’s Bench

Tools for field repairs and fabrication. GM Free Use Guidance: Remove Compromised from one mundane item between scenes. Only grant if the bench is maintained and stocked (tracking via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Improvise a single-use tool; gain a Boon on the immediate Craft check. Describe the improvisation: “With wire and prayer, you bend the lockpick just so—it slips in smooth.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, tools rust or go missing. Repair requires a downtime scene + 1 XP. After 2 sessions neglected, the bench is Compromised (no conversions work until fixed). Example: “The file’s teeth are choked with rust. One good stroke—and it snaps. Time to visit the smith.”

Courier Chain

Runners, dead-drops, and codes in one city. GM Free Use Guidance: Move a message or small parcel safely; start the related parley Dominant. Only grant if the chain is active (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: A courier appears now; mitigate 1 SB tied to a missed appointment or lost message. Describe the arrival: “A breathless kid skids to a halt, packet in hand: ’He said to give this to you—now.”’ GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, couriers go missing or codes change. After 1 session neglected, message delivery requires a Boon spend; after 2 sessions, the chain is Compromised (messages arrive late, altered, or not at all). Example: “The drop-box is empty. The usual sign—a blue ribbon—is gone. Someone’s been here.”

Small Holding (Farm/Orchard)

Steady larder and a trusted foreman. GM Free Use Guidance: Waive a minor Supply complication or clear Fatigue 1 for one PC during downtime. Only grant if the holding is tended (track via downtime scenes). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Produce a timely wagon, mule, or crates to justify a plausible cover story. Describe the arrival: “The creak of wheels on gravel—just in time. Three sacks of grain tumble from the cart as you dive behind it.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, crops fail or foreman quits. After 1 session neglected, clear Fatigue 1 only once per downtime; after 2 sessions, the holding is Strained (yields half, requires 1 XP to maintain). Example: “The orchard’s quiet too quiet. No birds, no wind—and the trees are dropping fruit before it’s ripe.”

Neighborhood Inn/Tavern

A familiar house of bread and rumor. GM Free Use Guidance: First social opener in that neighborhood starts Dominant. Only grant if the inn is maintained and the proprietor is friendly (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Call in the proprietor; shift a hostile crowd one step toward neutral. Describe the intervention: “The innkeeper steps between you and the mob, arms wide: ’Not in my house. Take it outside—or buy a round.”’ GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, rumors turn sour or the inn changes hands. After 1 session neglected, social openers start at Controlled; after 2 sessions, the inn is Strained (proprietor drunk or surly—–1 die on social rolls there). Example: “The usual smile is tight. ’Rough crowd tonight,’ the proprietor mutters, wiping a glass that’s already clean.”

Ritual Niche/Shrine

A consecrated corner with a small flame. GM Free Use Guidance: Clear Fatigue 1 or mitigate 1 SB from spiritual/psychological complications (fear, despair, doubt). Only grant if the niche is maintained and consecrated (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Sanctify a doorway for one scene; hostile supernatural presence must reveal itself or back away one band (\(\ref{ch:world-interaction}\)). Describe the effect: “You press your palm to the lintel—warmth flows like water. The shadow at the door flinches and retreats.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, the flame sputters or the niche is desecrated. After 1 session neglected, mitigate only 0.5 SB (round down); after 2 sessions, it’s Compromised (no spiritual benefits until reconsecrated). Example: “The wick is low, the oil gone. The flame guttered out hours ago—leaving only cold stone and regret.”

River Skiff/Barge Share

Limited access to a hull and a taciturn pilot. GM Free Use Guidance: Water crossing scenes begin Dominant. Only grant if the skiff is maintained and pilot is available (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Evade a patrol with a practiced channel; reduce DV by 1 on the escape roll. Describe the maneuver: “You lean into the turn—current catches the keel, sliding you into the blind eddy where the patrol’s lanterns can’t reach.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, the hull leaks or pilot is unavailable. After 1 session neglected, water crossings require a Boon spend to start Dominant; after 2 sessions, the share is Compromised (no access until repaired/replaced). Example: “The pilot eyes the cracks in the hull. ’One more storm, and we’re swimming. Pay for tar, or we walk.”’

Herbal Plot

Drying racks, clay pots, and a steady hand at dawn. GM Free Use Guidance: Remove Fatigue 1 from one character or gain a Boon to a single mundane healing attempt this downtime. Only grant if the plot is tended (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Produce a rare poultice; convert a Severe healing timer into two Moderate ticks. Describe the find: “Under the damp cloth—a bundle of silverleaf, still glistening with dew.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, herbs fail or plot is overgrown. After 1 session neglected, remove Fatigue 1 only once per downtime; after 2 sessions, it’s Compromised (no herbs until replanted). Example: “The mint’s gone to seed. The soil’s hard as baked clay—and the rabbits have turned it into a warren.”

Personal Library

Shelves of annotated tomes and a reading chair. GM Free Use Guidance: Gain a Boon on one Lore roll per session. Only grant if the library is maintained and accessible (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Quote a relevant passage; reduce DV of a related Lore or Insight roll by 1. Describe the insight: “You trace a finger along the margin—’Ah. So that’s why the ritual failed.”’ GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, books are damaged or stolen. After 1 session neglected, gain a Boon only every other session; after 2 sessions, the library is Strained (–1 die on Lore rolls there until repaired). Example: “The spine’s cracked, the pages water-stained. One more rain—and that treaty copy is pulp.”

Stable/Coach House

Reliable mounts and drivers who ask few questions. GM Free Use Guidance: Land travel scenes begin Dominant. Only grant if the stable is maintained and mounts/vehicles are ready (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Produce a fresh mount or spare vehicle; ignore the first exhaustion complication. Describe the arrival: “The stable boy leads out a chestnut—nostrils flared, coat gleaming. ’She’s rested and ready,’ he says.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, mounts sicken or vehicles break down. After 1 session neglected, ignore exhaustion only once per downtime; after 2 sessions, the stable is Strained (–1 die on travel rolls there until repaired). Example: “The mare’s favoring her left foreleg. The vet’s bill is steep—but better than a lame horse on the road.”

Alchemist’s Workshop

Reagents, retorts, and a suspicious chimney. GM Free Use Guidance: Produce one dose of a common alchemical item between scenes. Only grant if the workshop is maintained and stocked (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Brew a specialized compound; gain a Boon on the next Craft (Alchemy) roll. Describe the brew: “The liquid shifts from sickly green to deep blue—as if the sky decided to join the potion.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, reagents degrade or equipment fails. After 1 session neglected, yield halved; after 2 sessions, Compromised (no production until restocked/repaired). Example: “The vitriol’s eaten through the glass. Time for new bottles—and a stern word to the supplier.”

Messenger Birds

Trained pigeons or ravens with coded leg bands. GM Free Use Guidance: Send a simple message across moderate distance safely. Only grant if the birds are maintained and trained (track via downtime). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Receive urgent news now; mitigate 1 SB from miscommunication. Describe the arrival: “A raven lands on your shoulder, legs twitching—’Danger. East gate. Now.”’ GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, birds go astray or codes break. After 1 session neglected, message sending requires a Boon spend; after 2 sessions, the network is Compromised (messages arrive late, wrong, or not at all). Example: “The coop’s silent. No wings beat the dusk—and the leg bands are dull with dirt. Someone forgot to feed them.”

Standard Assets (8 XP): GM Tools for Regional Influence

Standard assets are established holdings with regional reach. As GM, use them to:

  • Reward mid-tier accomplishments with meaningful influence (e.g., a guild seat, a harbor office).

  • Create hooks that tie into regional politics, trade, or faction play.

  • Introduce complications that scale with neglect (e.g., losing favor, inviting rivals).

Each entry below includes GM-specific guidance on introduction, adjudication, and neglect consequences.

Charter or Minor Title

Recognized papers and privileges. GM Free Use Guidance: Secure an audience or official hearing; start Dominant. Only grant if the charter is valid and recognized (track via downtime/faction standing). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Invoke prerogative to delay immediate sanctions; mitigate 1 SB of civic backlash. Describe the delay: “You slap the seal on the table—’By order of the Marshal, this inquiry pauses till dawn.’ The clerk pales and nods.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, the charter lapses or is challenged. After 1 session neglected, secure audience starts at Controlled; after 2 sessions, it’s Strained (requires 1 XP or a scene to renew/defend). Example: “The ink’s faded, the seal cracked. Only grant if the command is maintained and signals are honored (track via downtime/Obligation). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Redirect a squadron; improve Position for a maritime raid or escape. Describe the redirect: “You signal the flagship—’New course bearing 270.’ The fleet turns as one, prows cutting the waves.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, the command decays or signals lapse. After 1 session neglected, declare blockade only every other session; after 2 sessions neglected, it’s Strained (requires 1 XP or a scene to renew infrastructure/defend signals). Example: “The signalman eyes the broken mast. ’We can’t send flags until it’s fixed. Pay for new spars—or we drift.”’

Hydraulic Works (16 XP)

Water is wealth. GM Free Use Guidance: Cancel the first shortage/drought fallout this arc. Only grant if the works are maintained and flowing (track via downtime/Obligation). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Flood or feed a field at the critical moment; fill 1 segment on a Famine Relief timer. Describe the flood: “You open the gate—water rushes forth, sweet and cold. The field drinks deep.” GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, the works clog or flow dwindles. After 1 session neglected, cancel shortage/drought only every other arc; after 2 sessions neglected, it’s Strained (requires 1 XP or a scene to clear clogs/restore flow). Example: “The foreman points to the crack. ’One more freeze—and we’re leaking. Pay for new stone—or we watch the crops die.”’

Palace Complex (20 XP)

Keys to office and omen. GM Free Use Guidance: Political openers start Dominant. Only grant if the palace is maintained and the court is active (track via downtime/Obligation). GM Scene Surge Guidance: Convene a chamber; convert one scandal consequence into a public inquiry you can contest. Describe the convening: “You strike the gavel—’The Chamber is in session. All rise.”’ GM Neglect Hook: If neglected, the palace decays or court factions secede. After 1 session neglected, political openers start at Controlled; after 2 sessions neglected, it’s Strained (requires 1 XP or a scene to renew infrastructure/mediate factions). Example: “The gardener sighs at the wilted roses. ’One more neglect—and we’re digging graves instead of growing joy.”’

Using Assets in Play: GM Procedures

As GM, your role is to:

  • Frame asset benefits only when the fiction supports it (e.g., a safehouse is only useful if prepared and stocked).

  • Allow flashbacks only with a modest cost (1 Boon or 1 downtime scene) to show the asset’s prior work—never as free exposition.

  • Tie complications directly to asset condition: If neglected, spend SB against it first; if compromised, deny benefits until fixed.

  • Spotlight spread: Rotate which assets take the stage so each investment breathes on-screen now and then—never let one asset dominate all scenes.

Example of Play (GM Perspective)

Rellan uses his Harbor Office (Standard Asset). As GM:

  • Frame the benefit: At scene start, ask: “Is the harbor office maintained and honored?” If yes, grant free use to start customs inspection at Dominant position. Describe: “The harbormaster nods at your seal—’All clear. Proceed to Berth 7.”’

  • Allow flashback: If Rellan wants to flash back to preparing the office, require 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene (e.g., “You spent yesterday greasing the chains and bribing the night watch.”).

  • Scene Surge: When a rival tries to trap him on the wharf, Rellan spends a Boon for a Scene Surge. As GM: Describe the intervention vividly: “You grip the chain lever—’Hold!’ The great iron links groan into place, blocking the channel. For one beat, the pursuit by water is negated—just enough to slip into a waiting skiff.”

  • Neglect consequence: If Rellan neglects the harbor office for 2+ sessions, advance a timer (e.g., Harbor Silt [4] ticks +1 per session neglected) or spend 1–2 SB to create complications (e.g., “The harbormaster frowns: ’Berth 3’s silted up. Pay for dredging—or we wait for spring.”’).

Asset Quick Reference (GM)

  • Costs: Minor 4 XP / Standard 8 XP / Major 12 XP / Tier III 16–20 XP. GM Guidance: Adjust slightly for narrative weight (e.g., a unique artifact might cost 6 XP as a Minor Asset).

  • Free Use: Once per session, off-screen leverage only if fiction supports it. Frame at scene start or as flashback with GM assent (cost: 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene).

  • Scene Surge: Spend 1 Boon for a one-beat, on-screen intervention. Limit to one meaningful use per scene.

  • Upkeep: Pay XP = base cost OR devote a downtime scene. Neglect degrades condition—use this as your complication engine.

  • Capacity (Optional): Assets \(\leq\) Spirit are easy; beyond that, upkeep taxes mount or requires extra downtime scenes.

Summary

Minor assets grease doors; Standard ones bend routes; Major holdings set tables; Tier III institutions tilt the map. As GM:

  • Introduce assets as narrative rewards, not automatic power-ups.

  • Price them to reflect story weight, not just XP.

  • Always attach a hook (what happens if tended? what if neglected?).

  • Use asset neglect to drive complications—never let it be “nothing happens.”

  • Spotlight spread: Rotate asset focus so each investment feels earned and consequential.

Choose what to own, then let the world answer—and remember: the best assets create as many problems as they solve.
