Chapter 7: Running Assets and Followers

Fate's Edge GM Guide — Chapter 7

In Fate's Edge, Assets and Followers are not mere mechanical bonuses — they are narrative levers that shape the world between scenes. As GM, your role is to introduce them as earned rewards, adjudicate their benefits only when narratively justified, and use their neglect as a primary source of compelling complications. They thrive when they create tension between player ambition and worldly consequences — not when they become spreadsheets of passive benefits.

Design Principles for GM Use

When managing Assets and Followers in your campaign, remember:

  • They shift fiction between scenes: Assets open doors, quiet rumors, or stock larders off-screen. Followers act offscreen to gather information or manage responsibilities — never autonomously in scenes (that's player agency).
  • They create hooks: Every Asset or Follower should suggest both opportunities (if tended) and complications (if neglected). Neglect isn't "broken" — it's a story waiting to happen.
  • Stay narrative-first: Prefer position, scale, and access over flat modifiers. Instead of "+2 die," grant "start Dominant in negotiations with the guild" or "Your contact in the watch means the patrol arrives 10 minutes late."
  • Never let them replace player agency: If an Asset or Follower solves a problem too cleanly, add a cost (obligation, timer advance, or narrative complication). Their value is in how they enable player action, not in removing risk.
  • They scale with tier: In Tier I, a single safehouse feels significant; in Tier IV, players should manage networks of Assets or high-Cap Followers to match their influence.

What They Are Not (GM Guidance)

Ensure Assets and Followers never:

  • Act as scene-solvers (e.g., "My safehouse automatically disarms all traps").
  • Generate wealth or XP passively (their value is in narrative leverage, not bookkeeping).
  • Stack identical effects — if two Followers would grant the same Assist, the GM chooses the best narrative fit (not the strongest mechanical bonus).

Assets: GM Procedures for Introduction and Adjudication

Assets are properties, charters, networks, or institutions under player control. As GM, follow these steps when introducing or adjudicating Assets:

  1. Introduce as Narrative Reward: Never grant Assets via XP alone. Tie them to fiction:
    • "After clearing the bandit camp, the grateful village grants you use of their old mill as a Safehouse."
    • "Your successful negotiation earns you a Guild Seat — vote and shop-rights in the artisan's quarter."
    Never say "Spend 4 XP for a Safehouse." Instead: "The mill is yours — but it needs repair before it's useful."
  2. Price for Story Weight, Not Just XP:
    • In Tier I (0–40 XP), a Minor Asset (4 XP) should feel like a major investment — adjust cost slightly up/down for narrative significance (e.g., a unique heirloom weapon might cost 6 XP as a Minor Asset).
    • In Tier IV+ (151+ XP), Minor Assets are pocket change — reserve 4 XP costs for trivial holdings; Standard (8 XP) and Major (12 XP) Assets should feel meaningful.
  3. Always Attach a Hook: When introducing an Asset, immediately establish:
    • What happens if tended? (e.g., "The informant ring whispers of a shipment tonight.")
    • What happens if neglected? (e.g., "If ignored for two sessions, the informants go silent — or worse, start talking to rivals.")
  4. Adjudicate Benefits Only When Fiction Supports It:
    • Free Use: Once per session, trigger the off-screen benefit only if the Asset is maintained and the fiction supports it (e.g., a Safehouse is only useful if prepared and stocked — track via downtime scenes).
    • Scene Surge: Spend 1 Boon to turn an Asset into a dramatic on-screen intervention (one beat). Limit to one meaningful use per scene — more than that feels like a deus ex machina. Describe it vividly.
    • Never grant Free Use automatically — ask: "Does the fiction support using this benefit now?" If no, require a scene of preparation or a Boon spend.
  5. Use Neglect as Your Complication Engine:
    • Track Asset condition visibly (Maintained → Neglected → Compromised).
    • When Neglected: Advance a relevant timer (e.g., Informant Network [4] ticks +1 per session neglected) or spend 1–2 SB to create complications tied to decay (e.g., "The safehouse landlord demands rent — or eviction.").
    • When Compromised: Deny all benefits until fixed via quest or major downtime.
GM Tip: The Asset Neglect Engine

Neglect is your best storytelling tool. When players ignore an Asset:

  • Advance a relevant timer (e.g., Shop Rent [4] ticks +1 per session neglected for a Market Front).
  • Spend 1–2 SB to create a complication tied to the Asset's decay (e.g., "The Scholar's Cell custodian leaves — research now requires a DV +1 roll").
  • Let the fiction degrade the Asset's benefit (e.g., a neglected Healing House now only clears Fatigue 1 once per downtime).

Never treat neglect as "nothing happens." A neglected Asset is a ticking time bomb — or a chance for players to re-engage with their world.

Followers: GM Procedures for Introduction and Adjudication

Followers are named NPCs with a personal relationship to the player character. Before any procedure, you must understand what makes a Follower mechanically distinct from an Asset: Cap.

What is Cap?

Cap (Capacity) is a single number that answers one question: how capable is this Follower on their own? Cap governs two things:

  1. Independent actions: When a Follower acts on their own (an off-screen task, a delegated mission, an autonomous duty), they roll up to Capd10 against a DV you set. A Cap 3 Follower rolling 3d10 is performing at the level of a Tier III NPC in their area of expertise.
  2. Assist actions: When a Follower helps a PC's roll, the Follower contributes dice equal to min(Cap, their relevant Skill), capped at +3 total assist dice per action. Cap is what they could do; the Skill is whether they know anything about this. A Cap 5 quartermaster adds one die to a lockpicking roll, and it is the right one die.

Cap is a ceiling, not a skill list. A Cap 3 Follower is not "proficient at three things" — they are "as competent as a Tier III specialist within their defined role." Their specialty is determined by fiction (the street urchin knows locks; the mercenary captain knows cavalry), not by the number.

Cap is not Obligation Pool. A Follower's Cap and a Patron's Obligation Pool are different systems and may share the word "capacity" in colloquial use; this chapter uses Cap exclusively for Followers and Obligation Pool for Patrons.

Introduction and Cost

Followers are earned through fiction, never purchased as a mechanical upgrade. As GM, follow these procedures:

  1. Introduce as Narrative Reward: Never grant Followers via XP alone. Tie them to fiction:
    • "After saving the merchant's daughter, she swears loyalty — now she's your Informant."
    • "The defeated captain yields — now he follows you as your Mercenary Lieutenant."
    Never say "Spend XP for a Follower." Instead: "They'll follow you — but only if you prove yourself worthy."
  2. Price for Story Weight, Not Just XP:
    • Base cost is Cap² XP (Cap 1 = 1 XP, Cap 2 = 4 XP, Cap 3 = 9 XP, Cap 4 = 16 XP, Cap 5 = 25 XP).
    • Tier I Floor: Until the character reaches Tier II (41 XP), Cap 1 Followers cost a minimum of 3 XP. This prevents Tier I characters from accumulating a small army of urchins before they have anything else.
    • Adjust for narrative weight: a unique spirit ally might cost 3 XP as a Cap 1 Follower; a politically pivotal Cap 3 might cost 12 XP. High-Cap Followers (4–5) should be rare and hard-won — reserve them for Tier III+ play.
  3. Always Attach a Hook: When introducing a Follower, immediately establish:
    • What happens if tended? (e.g., "Your Informant whispers of a shipment tonight.")
    • What happens if neglected? (e.g., "If ignored for two sessions, your Informant goes silent — or worse, starts talking to rivals.")

Adjudicating Follower Benefits

Followers do not act autonomously in scenes. Their benefits come only through three channels:

  1. Bonds (1 Boon): Draw on trust and loyalty for insight or support — a whispered warning, a remembered favor, a moment of hesitation in an enemy.
  2. Assists (1 Boon): The Follower contributes dice equal to min(Cap, their relevant Skill), capped at +3 total assist dice per action. The Boon represents the PC's attention to coordinating the Follower; a Cap 1 street urchin and a Cap 5 spymaster cost the same Boon because the cost prices the player's effort, not the Follower's capability.
  3. Offscreen Action (1 Boon or 1 downtime scene): The Follower handles tasks that would otherwise demand PC time ("Your Informant spends the week gathering rumors — report next session").

Never say "My Follower scouts ahead." The player must spend a Boon for an Assist or declare a Bond-driven action; otherwise the Follower waits off-screen.

Cap 1 Followers and the Boon Economy

Because Tier I characters have few Boons, Cap 1 Followers do not require Boon expenditure for baseline loyalty actions: asking Pip to watch a door, carry a note, or notice a familiar face is a narrative request, not a Boon spend. Boons are required only when the Cap 1 Follower's contribution replaces a die roll (Assist) or provides unprompted insight (Bond). This preserves the player's Boon economy while keeping the Follower useful.

What Cap Determines

The Follower's Cap dictates what they can credibly attempt without your intervention:

  • Cap 1: Routine personal service. They can carry messages, watch doors, identify familiar faces in their home neighborhood, and notice details in their area of expertise. They cannot fight trained opponents, forge documents, scout wilderness, or lie convincingly to authority figures. If asked to perform a Cap 2+ task, they roll their 1d10 at the normal DV and likely fail — if they fail a task the PC pressed them into, Loyalty drops one step automatically (the Follower tried and was exposed to danger for nothing).
  • Cap 2: A competent hireling. They can hold a post, run a small operation, or fight alongside the party in light skirmishes.
  • Cap 3: A skilled professional. They can manage a Cap number of Assets, lead a small team, or serve as a regional specialist.
  • Cap 4–5: Veterans and legends. They command organizations, shape regional events, and have their own agendas that may conflict with the PC's.

Loyalty and Fitness as States

Instead of numeric timers, track each Follower's Loyalty and Fitness as one of three states each:

Change states only when the fiction demands it. No rolls are required; you change states in response to events. A Follower whose sister is sold into indentured service does not need a Persuasion check to feel Strained — they simply are.

Neglect as Your Complication Engine

  • If a Follower's Loyalty becomes Strained or Broken, all Assets they manage immediately become Neglected.
  • If a Follower's Fitness becomes Down, they cannot perform off-screen tasks — they need tending first.
  • Neglecting a Follower's upkeep degrades their state — use this as your complication engine, just as you do with Assets.

GM Tip: The Follower Neglect Engine

Neglect is your best storytelling tool. When players ignore a Follower:

  • Degrade their Loyalty/Fitness state based on fiction (e.g., "After missing pay for two sessions, your Mercenary Lieutenant's Loyalty turns Strained — Assist at −1 die").
  • If neglected for 2+ sessions, trigger a consequence (e.g., "Your Informant sells your secrets to a rival — spend 1 SB to learn what they revealed").
  • Let the fiction reflect the strain (e.g., a Hurt Follower can't undertake offscreen tasks — they need tending first).

Never treat neglect as "nothing happens." A neglected Follower is a relationship in crisis — or a chance for players to rebuild trust.

The Interaction Loop: Assets, Followers, and the Core Mechanics

Assets and Followers integrate seamlessly with Fate's Edge's core resolution loop. As GM, guide players through this cycle:

  1. Player declares intent and approach (Attribute + Skill).
  2. You state Position and set relevant Timers.
  3. Player rolls d10s; counts Successes (6+) and Story Beats (1s).
  4. Compare to DV → use Outcome Matrix.
  5. Apply consequences and update the fiction — this is where Assets and Followers shine:
    • On Partial or Miss: Players may spend Boons to activate Assets/Followers (Scene Surge or Bond-driven action).
    • On Success: Players may use Assets/Followers offscreen to prepare for next scene (Free Use or downtime action).
    • Always: Check Asset/Follower condition — neglect triggers complications before applying the roll's outcome.
  6. Pass the spotlight — but first, remind players of pending upkeep:
    • "Before we move on — your Safehouse needs tending this downtime. Pay 4 XP or devote a scene to it, or it becomes Neglected."
    • "Your Informant's Loyalty is Strained — they'll refuse risky orders until you make amends."
GM Tip: The Upkeep Reminder

Make upkeep a narrative moment — not bookkeeping. At scene end:

  • Ask: "Who tends their Assets/Followers this downtime? What does that look like in fiction?"
  • Let players describe the action (e.g., "I spend the morning greasing the Safehouse chains," "I buy my Follower a drink and apologize for the last mission").
  • Only then apply mechanical effects: pay XP, change state, or advance neglect timers.

This turns upkeep from a chore into roleplay — exactly where Assets and Followers belong.

Using Assets and Followers in Play: GM Procedures

As GM, your role is to:

  • Frame Asset/Follower benefits only when fiction supports it:
    • A Safehouse is only useful if prepared and stocked (track via downtime scenes).
    • A Follower only assists if their Loyalty/Fitness state allows it (track via fiction-driven state changes).
    • Never grant benefits automatically — ask: "Does the fiction support using this benefit now?" If no, require a scene of preparation, a Boon spend (Scene Surge/Bond-driven action), or a narrative cost.
  • Allow flashbacks only with a modest cost:
    • To show prior work (e.g., "You spent yesterday preparing the Safehouse"), require 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene.
    • Never allow free exposition — flashbacks must cost something meaningful.
  • Tie complications directly to Asset/Follower condition:
    • If Neglected: Spend SB against it first (e.g., "The Safehouse is Neglected — spend 1 SB to advance the Landlord Dispute [4] timer").
    • If Compromised: Deny benefits until fixed via quest or major downtime.
    • If Follower Loyalty Broken: They leave or become hostile — spend 1 SB to show their departure or betrayal.
  • Spotlight spread: Rotate which Assets/Followers take the stage so each investment breathes on-screen now and then — never let one Asset or Follower dominate all scenes.
  • Use them to create obligation and timers:
    • Leveraging an Asset against faction interests may add Obligation.
    • Neglecting a Follower may advance a Trust Eroded [4] timer.
    • Using a Follower for offscreen work may fill segments on a Reputation [6] timer.

Example of Play (GM Perspective)

A player has a Safehouse (Minor Asset) and an Informant (Cap 1 Follower). As GM:

Scene Start
Ask: "Is the Safehouse maintained and stocked? Is the Informant tended?"

  • Both tended: Grant Free Use to start an entry/exit scene at Dominant position. Describe: "The landlord nods at your signal — 'All clear. Proceed to the cellar.'"
  • Safehouse Neglected: Describe: "The floorboard creaks differently. Your landlord eyes you over tea: 'Business has been slow. I wonder why.'" Then spend 1 SB to advance Landlord Dispute [4] timer +1.
  • Informant Loyalty Strained: Describe: "Your Informant avoids your gaze. 'I've got nothing for you this week — business's been rough.'" They refuse to assist until tended.

During the Scene
When the player wants to use Asset/Follower:

  • Safehouse Scene Surge (1 Boon): Describe: "You grip the hidden lever — 'Hold!' The wine rack slides aside, revealing the escape tunnel. For one beat, pursuit is negated — just enough to slip into the alley."
  • Informant Bond-driven action (1 Boon): Describe: "You slide a coin across the table — 'Remember the river bend?' Their eyes light up: 'The shipment leaves at midnight. Take the north ridge.'" Grant +1 die on the next Stealth roll (Cap 1, baseline loyalty).

Scene End
Remind players of upkeep:

  • "Your Safehouse needs tending — pay 4 XP or devote a scene to avoid Neglect."
  • "Your Informant's Loyalty is Strained — spend downtime mending trust or they'll go silent next session."

Pacing and Scaling Guidance

As GM, use Assets and Followers to scale challenge naturally with tier:

  • Tier I (0–40 XP):
    • Assets are rare and hyper-local (e.g., a single Safehouse, a trusted Informant).
    • Followers are low-Cap (Cap 1–2) and deeply personal (e.g., a sibling, a mentor).
    • GM Guidance: One Asset or Follower should feel significant — neglect creates immediate, personal complications (e.g., "Your Informant won't help until you repay their favor"). Cap 1 Followers are introduced through fiction (oath, blood-debt) and their cost is attention, not XP — this preserves the Tier I economy.
  • Tier II (41–90 XP):
    • Assets begin to show regional reach (e.g., a Guild Seat, a Harbor Office).
    • Followers include mid-Cap (Cap 2–3) figures (e.g., a guild factor, a veteran sergeant).
    • GM Guidance: Assets/Followers should tie into regional politics or trade — neglect invites rivals or invites scrutiny (e.g., "Neglect your Guild Seat for two sessions, and rivals challenge your voting rights").
  • Tier III (91–150 XP):
    • Assets scale to national influence (e.g., a Banking Charter, a Fortress).
    • Followers include high-Cap (Cap 3–4) figures (e.g., a bank director, a garrison commander).
    • GM Guidance: Assets/Followers should tie into national economics or military power — neglect risks invasion, embargo, or revolt (e.g., "Neglect your Fortress for two sessions, and the garrison mutinies").
  • Tier IV+ (151+ XP):
    • Assets are empire-shaping (see Tier III Institutions, in the Compendium of Assets below).
    • Followers include Cap 4–5 figures who can manage Assets on your behalf.
    • GM Guidance: Assets/Followers should tie into imperial succession or continental wars — neglect risks civil war or foreign invasion (e.g., "Neglect your Palace Complex for two sessions, and court factions secede").
GM Tip: The Scaling Mantra

At high tiers, Assets and Followers aren't "more powerful" — they're "more complex."

  • A Tier V character doesn't need "a better Safehouse" — they need "a Safehouse network with competing landlords and shifting loyalties."
  • A Tier V character doesn't need "a stronger Follower" — they need "a Follower with their own agenda, Obligations, and potential to betray."

The challenge isn't in the mechanical bonus — it's in navigating the web of relationships and responsibilities they create.

Optional Advanced Rules

Use these sparingly to add depth — but only when the fiction demands it.

Asset Networks (GM Guidance)

For players with multiple Assets, as GM, allow them to declare a "Network" once per session (cost: 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene) to:

  • Treat two Minor Assets' upkeep as waived this downtime (like the Market Front Standard Asset benefit).
  • Grant +1 die to a roll involving both Assets (e.g., using a Safehouse and Informant to gather intelligence).
  • Mitigate 1 SB from a complication tied to either Asset (e.g., "Your Informant warns of the landlord's visit — spend the Boon to avoid the rent dispute").

GM Guidance: Limit to one Network declaration per session. Networks should feel earned — require players to describe how the Assets connect (e.g., "I pay the Informant to tip off the Safehouse landlord about incoming inspectors").

Follower Management (GM Guidance)

For players with high-Cap Followers (Cap 3+), as GM, allow them to delegate Asset management once per session (cost: 1 Boon or 1 downtime scene) to:

  • Have the Follower tend up to Cap Assets (e.g., a Cap 3 Follower tends 3 Assets).
  • The Follower's own upkeep (XP or downtime scene) automatically covers the upkeep of delegated Assets — no separate cost.
  • If the Follower's Loyalty becomes Strained or Broken, all delegated Assets immediately become Neglected.
  • Cap 1 and Cap 2 Followers cannot manage Assets. Asset management requires organizational capacity the Follower does not possess. Cap 1–2 Followers can perform single discrete tasks on an Asset's behalf ("Watch the safehouse door for one night," "Carry this message to the landlord"), but not ongoing management.

GM Guidance: This creates meaningful tension — players must choose between tending Followers directly or delegating Asset management. Neglecting the Follower risks losing multiple Assets at once.

Obligation Integration (GM Guidance)

When Assets or Followers are tied to a faction (e.g., Temple Complex, Informant Ring), as GM, use them to drive Obligation:

  • Leveraging an Asset against faction interests adds 1–2 Obligation segments (e.g., "Using the Banking Charter to freeze a rival's account adds 2 Obligation to the bank").
  • Neglecting a Follower tied to a faction adds 1 Obligation segment per session neglected (e.g., "Ignoring your Informant's pleas adds 1 Obligation to the spy network").
  • Tending a Follower's welfare reduces Obligation by 1 segment per scene (e.g., "Spending downtime with your Informant clears 1 Obligation").

GM Guidance: Track this Obligation visibly — use it to create complications when full (e.g., "Your Obligation to the bank is full — spend 1 SB to advance the Bank Audit [6] timer").

Summary

As GM, remember:

  • Assets and Followers are narrative levers, not mechanical bonuses — they shape the world between scenes, not during them.
  • Their true value lies in the hooks they create — what happens if tended? what if neglected?
  • Neglect is your best storytelling tool — use it to advance timers, spend SB, or degrade benefits.
  • Always tie benefits to fiction — never grant Free Use or Follower assistance automatically. Require a Boon spend, downtime scene, or narrative cost when unprepared or neglected.
  • Cap is the Follower's ceiling, not their skill list. A Cap 1 Follower is not a weaker Cap 5 — they are a different kind of tool, useful in their niche and limited outside it.
  • Spotlight spread is essential — rotate which Assets/Followers take the stage so each investment feels earned and consequential.
  • At high tiers, complexity > power — the challenge isn't in stronger bonuses, but in navigating webs of loyalty, obligation, and competing interests.

Choose what to own and who to follow — then let the world answer. The best Assets and Followers create as many problems as they solve, and the best complications come from neglect, not power.

From Faction to Asset/Follower: Taking Over a Terrestrial Patron (GM Guidance)

Sometimes you stop owing the guild — and start owning it. Rising to lead a faction (Thieves' Guild, Mercenary Company, Banking Charter, etc.) is a major story arc, not a simple XP purchase. As GM, run this arc as a 2–3 session collaboration with the player.

Narrative Prerequisites (GM Verification)

Before approving a takeover attempt, verify:

  • The player has at least 6 Obligation with the faction (deep entanglement).
  • They completed a major service for the faction (your discretion — e.g., saving it from destruction, winning a war, exposing a traitor at great cost).
  • They secured at least one internal ally with significant influence (another captain, a guildmaster, a wealthy backer).

If prerequisites aren't met, the takeover attempt fails narratively — no XP cost, just a complication.

The Takeover Arc (GM Collaboration)

Collaborate on a 2–3 session arc that typically involves:

  1. Crisis or Power Vacuum — The current leader dies, disappears, or is disgraced (you define the crisis).
  2. Claim of Right — The player presents a valid claim (charter, election, duel victory, popular support — you judge validity).
  3. Consolidation — Defeat or co-opt rivals, bribe or intimidate dissenters, and secure the faction's resources (you play the opposition).

This arc should test the player's ingenuity, not just their XP pool.

Mechanical Cost (GM Enforcement)

Once the arc is complete, the player must spend 12 XP (the cost of a Major Asset) to convert the faction into a personal Asset. As GM:

  • Enforce this cost strictly — no conversions without payment.
  • Describe the conversion as institutional restructuring: "The ledgers are rewritten, the oaths renewed — it's yours now, but the work has only begun."

Converting the Asset/Follower (GM Guidance)

The new Asset uses the statistics of the most appropriate existing Asset from this compendium, modified by the faction's nature:

  • Thieves' Guild → Informant Ring (Standard) or Smuggler's Network (Standard)
  • Mercenary Company → Mercenary Contract (Minor/Standard) or Military Outpost (Major)
  • Banking Charter → Banking Charter (Major) or Trading Consortium (Major)
  • Royalist Network → Intelligence Bureau (Specialized) or Diplomatic Enclave (Major)
  • Ecktorian Mystery Cult → Temple Complex (Standard) or Magical Laboratory (Specialized)

As GM, feel free to create a custom Asset using the Asset procedures above if none fit the faction's nature.

What You Gain (GM Description)

Upon conversion, grant:

  • The Asset's normal Free Use and Scene Surge benefits.
  • The Access Tag associated with the faction (e.g., Guild-Ledgered, Harbor Handsigns).
  • A permanent obligation track toward the faction's members (now your underlings). This track is smaller (4 segments) and fills when you neglect their welfare or make reckless decisions that endanger them.

As GM, track this obligation and use it to create complications when neglected (e.g., "The crew grumbles — pay is late again. After 2 sessions neglected, they threaten to walk.")

What You Lose (GM Enforcement)

Enforce these losses strictly:

  • The player can no longer use the faction as a terrestrial patron to gain Obligation-free perks — they are now responsible for it.
  • The faction becomes a target for your enemies — advance a Rival Faction timer whenever they use the Asset aggressively.
  • They must pay normal upkeep (XP or downtime scenes) to keep the Asset Maintained. Neglect causes internal dissent, corruption, or defections — use this as your complication engine.

Optional: Gradual Takeover (GM Permission)

For a slower, more story-driven approach, the player may forgo the 12 XP cost but must instead spend a downtime scene per session managing the faction (equivalent to Intensive upkeep) and accept that the Asset's benefits are unreliable until they have successfully completed a full arc of consolidation. As GM, you may allow the XP cost to be paid in installments (e.g., 4 XP per downtime period) to represent gradual investment.

Example: Rellan Takes the Black Ledger (GM Narration)

Rellan has served the Black Ledger smuggling syndicate for two arcs, accruing 6 Obligation. After exposing a rival who betrayed the syndicate (your narration), the aging leader steps down. Rellan spends 12 XP, completes a short arc consolidating loyal captains (you play the opposition), and converts the syndicate into a Smuggler's Network (Standard Asset). He now commands hidden routes and safehouses — but must keep the crew paid and protected, or an internal revolt timer will start ticking.

As GM, describe the takeover's cost: "The syndicates' marks are scratched from the walls. New oaths are sworn — and new enemies are made."

Compendium of Assets

In Fate's Edge, Assets are your off-screen levers — properties, charters, networks, and institutions that change the fiction between scenes. They do not fight beside you (see Followers, above) but they shape the ground you walk on. As GM, your role is to:

  • Introduce assets as narrative rewards (not automatic power-ups).
  • Price them appropriately to campaign tier and fiction.
  • Adjudicate their benefits only when narratively justified.
  • Use their neglect as a source of meaningful complications.

Assets thrive when they create tension between player ambition and worldly consequences — not when they become spreadsheets.

Before the catalogue The design principles and the neglect engine are stated at the top of this chapter and apply unchanged to everything below. What follows is the catalogue — what a 4, 8, 12 or 20 XP asset actually looks like at the table — plus two pieces of adjudication that only come up once you are holding a specific one.

What Assets Are Not (GM Guidance)

As GM, ensure assets never:

  • Act autonomously inside a scene (that's Followers' role).
  • Generate wealth or XP passively (their value is in narrative leverage, not bookkeeping).
  • Stack identical effects — if two assets would grant the same edge, the GM chooses the best narrative fit (not the strongest mechanical bonus).

Narrative First, Mechanics Second (GM Application)

When an asset grants "start Dominant," it applies to social/informational rolls where positioning matters. For practical challenges (lock-picking, repairs), "reduce DV by 1" is preferred. As GM, choose the form that best serves the fiction:

  • If the asset represents influence (e.g., a noble's favor), use Position shifts.
  • If it represents tools or preparation (e.g., a well-stocked workshop), use DV reduction.
  • When in doubt, ask: "Does this change how the character approaches the problem, or just make the roll easier?" Favor the former.

Introducing Assets: GM Best Practices

Assets should feel earned, not bought. Use these guidelines when placing assets in your campaign:

  • Tier Appropriateness:
    • Tier I (0–40 XP): Assets are rare and local (e.g., a single safehouse, a trusted informant). Cost 4 XP feels significant.
    • Tier II (41–90 XP): Assets begin to show regional reach (e.g., a guild seat, a harbor office). Standard (8 XP) assets emerge as meaningful investments.
    • Tier III (91–150 XP): Assets scale to national influence (e.g., a banking charter, a fortress). Major (12 XP) assets become campaign-defining.
    • Tier IV+ (151+ XP): Assets are empire-shaping (see Tier III Institutions, below). Reserve 16–20 XP assets for hard-won victories.
  • Fiction First, Cost Second: Never assign an asset cost based solely on XP. Ask:
    • What narrative justification exists for this asset? (e.g., "The character saved the merchant's caravan — now they're granted a trade charter.")
    • Does the asset's scope match the fiction? (A village herbalist shouldn't grant a "National Healing House" asset.)
    • What complication arises if neglected? (This is your hook for future scenes.)
  • Price for Narrative Weight, Not Power: A 4 XP asset in Tier I should feel as impactful as a 12 XP asset in Tier III — because it represents a larger proportion of the character's influence. Adjust XP costs slightly up/down to reflect narrative significance (e.g., a unique artifact might cost 6 XP as a Minor Asset due to story weight).
  • Always Attach a Hook: When introducing an asset, immediately establish:
    • What happens if tended? (e.g., "The informant ring whispers of a shipment tonight.")
    • What happens if neglected? (e.g., "If ignored for two sessions, the informants go silent — or worse, start talking to rivals.")

Asset Mechanics (GM Procedures)

All assets share these core mechanics. As GM, apply them consistently:

Cost
Minor 4 XP, Standard 8 XP, Major 12 XP. GM Guidance: Treat cost as a narrative threshold. In Tier I, 4 XP is a major investment; in Tier IV, it's pocket change. Adjust slightly for story weight (see above).
Establishment
1 day / 1 week / 1 month respectively to become operational. GM Guidance: Use this for downtime storytelling. "The safehouse takes a week to prepare — what do you do while waiting?"
Upkeep
Each downtime, either pay Efficient upkeep — max(1, base cost ÷ 3, rounded up) — or pay 1 XP and devote a full scene tending it. Neglect degrades condition (Maintained → Neglected → Compromised). GM Guidance: Track condition visibly. When an asset becomes Neglected, it's your cue to introduce complications. Compromised means it's broken — fixing it requires a quest or major downtime.
Free Use
Once per session, trigger the listed off-screen benefit (framed at scene start or as flashback with GM assent). GM Guidance: This is not automatic. Ask: "Does the fiction support using this benefit now?" If yes, grant it; if no, require a scene of preparation or a Boon spend (Scene Surge).
Scene Surge
Spend 1 Boon to turn an Asset into a dramatic on-screen intervention as described (one beat, fiction permitting). GM Guidance: This is the asset's "big moment." Limit to one meaningful use per scene — more than that feels like a deus ex machina.
Extra Use
Spend 2 XP to trigger the off-screen benefit an additional time this session. GM Guidance: Rarely allow this — it risks making assets feel like vending machines. Reserve for narrative crescendos (e.g., "You've poured everything into this safehouse — use it twice this session to flee the duke's guards.").
Risk Interface
When an asset covers risk, it typically improves Position, lowers DV by 1, fills 1 segment on a relevant timer, or mitigates 1 SB (Story Beat) from a thematically linked complication. GM Guidance: This is where assets shine. Instead of "+2 die," say "Your contacts in the watch mean the patrol arrives 10 minutes late — start Dominant."

Capacity (Optional GM Rule)

A character comfortably maintains a number of assets up to their Spirit rating. For each asset beyond this:

  • Increase its upkeep by +50% (round up) OR
  • Require an additional downtime scene to keep it Maintained.

GM Guidance: Use this to prevent asset hoarding. At Spirit 3, a 4th asset should feel like a strain — perhaps requiring a scene of frantic paperwork or causing minor complications until tended. This creates natural tension between ambition and upkeep.

Cross-Reference to Faction Ties

Assets tied to a specific faction (e.g., Temple Complex, Banking Charter, Guild Seat) may generate Obligation if used recklessly or ignored. As GM:

  • Award Obligation segments when players leverage assets against faction interests.
  • Let Obligation decay naturally if players tend the faction's welfare.
  • Use neglected assets as Obligation sources (e.g., "Ignoring the temple's pleas adds 1 Obligation to the gods each session neglected").

Minor Assets (4 XP): GM Tools for Local Leverage

Minor assets are small holdings, charters, or kits that grant reliable, localized leverage. As GM, use them to:

  • Reward early-game ingenuity without unbalancing the campaign.
  • Create intimate, character-driven story hooks (e.g., a family heirloom, a neighborhood favor).
  • Introduce complications that feel personal and immediate when neglected.

Each entry below includes GM-specific guidance on introduction, adjudication, and neglect consequences.

Signature Weapon
A named heirloom or masterpiece. Free Use: If gear damage would compromise it, downgrade to a minor flaw and require a quiet maintenance scene to clear — do not auto-repair. Scene Surge: Produce it at the perfect moment; improve Position one step for the first attack this scene. Neglect Hook: If unused for 2+ sessions, the weapon requires repair (Cost: 1 XP or a downtime scene). Neglect further risks it being stolen or broken beyond repair. Example: "The hilt is loose — swinging it feels like shaking a tooth. Repair it, or it flies from your grip next clash."

A quiet room, permits, and a custodian. Free Use: Begin one research scene at Dominant (or lower DV by 1), only if the cell is prepared and the custodian is present. Scene Surge: Reveal a cited source now; fill 1 segment on a relevant Information timer. Neglect Hook: If neglected, the custodian leaves or permits lapse — research requires a DV +1 roll; after 3 sessions neglected, the cell is seized by authorities. Example: "The landlord knocks: 'Your rent's due — and I've heard whispers about those strange books.' Pay up, or they're calling the watch."
Safehouse Network (Quarter)
Trapdoors, false walls, and a landlord who never saw you. Free Use: Start an entry/exit scene Dominant, only if the safehouse is prepared and stocked. Scene Surge: Produce a hidden egress; convert one pursuit consequence into a temporary complication you can interact with. Neglect Hook: If neglected, the landlord grows suspicious or sells the property. After 1 session, safehouse use costs 1 Boon to start Dominant; after 2 sessions, it's compromised (requires a scene to re-secure). Example: "The floorboard creaks differently now. Your landlord eyes you over tea: 'Business has been slow. I wonder why.'"
Artisan's Bench
Tools for field repairs and fabrication. Free Use: Remove Compromised from one mundane item between scenes, only if the bench is maintained and stocked. Scene Surge: Improvise a single-use tool; gain a Boon on the immediate Craft check. Neglect Hook: If neglected, tools rust or go missing — repair requires a downtime scene + 1 XP; after 2 sessions, the bench is Compromised. Example: "The file's teeth are choked with rust. One good stroke — and it snaps. Time to visit the smith."

After 1 session, clear Fatigue 1 only once per downtime; after 2 sessions, the holding is Strained (yields half, requires 1 XP to maintain). Example: "The orchard's quiet — too quiet. No birds, no wind — and the trees are dropping fruit before it's ripe."

After 1 session, social openers start at Controlled; after 2 sessions, the inn is Strained (proprietor drunk or surly, −1 die on social rolls there). Example: "The usual smile is tight. 'Rough crowd tonight,' the proprietor mutters, wiping a glass that's already clean."

The flame guttered out hours ago — leaving only cold stone and regret."

Specialized Assets: GM Tools for Niche Power

Specialized assets are narrow tools with sharp edges. As GM, use them to:

  • Reward specific, high-skill accomplishments with targeted leverage (e.g., a magical lab, an intelligence bureau).
  • Create hooks that tie into arcane, espionage, or technical complications.
  • Introduce complications that reflect the asset's fragility (e.g., lab accidents, blown covers).
Magical Laboratory (10 XP)
Wards, benches, focus crystals. Free Use: Once per session, reduce Ritual Backlash by 1 SB, only if the lab is maintained and wards are intact. Scene Surge: Contain an emergent effect for one beat; begin the control roll Dominant. Neglect Hook: If neglected, wards fade or equipment degrades. After 1 session, reduce backlash only every other session; after 2 sessions, Strained (requires 1 XP or a scene to renew wards/replace gear). Example: "The sigils are smudged. The crystal's cracked — and the air smells of ozone. Time for new wards — and a stern look at the apprentice."

After 1 session, reduce skill cost only every other downtime; after 2 sessions, Strained (requires 1 XP or a scene to renew masters/replace mats). Example: "The mat's split in two. One more fall — and it's useless. Time for new gear — and a lecture on safety."

The signal's weak — and the cut-outs are missing three names. Time for new wire — and a headache."
