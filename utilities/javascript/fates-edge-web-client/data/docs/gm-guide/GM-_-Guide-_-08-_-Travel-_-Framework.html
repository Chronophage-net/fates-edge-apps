World Interaction

Chapter 9 · World Interaction

Using This Chapter

This chapter gives you the tools to move through the world, interact with its people, and make your journey meaningful. For detailed descriptions of each region’s culture, subgenre, and notable NPCs, see Chapter \(\ref{ch:lore-compendium}\) (World Regions and Cultures). The two chapters work together: this one covers how to travel and interact; the other tells you where and with whom.

Key Reference: For the complete travel procedure, see Appendix A.1 (Global Travel Procedures) in the Worldbook. This chapter provides the principles and cultural context; the appendix gives the step-by-step mechanic.

In Fate’s Edge, the world is not a backdrop – it is a partner in the conversation. Dikes groan under black rain in Viterra, clan horns answer across Acasia’s ridgelines, Ecktoria’s marble halls echo with careful words, and Kahfagia’s pilots read storms by taste. Wherever you go, place, culture, and pressure push back.

GM Tip: Travel in 60 Seconds

If you need to run a journey right now, here is the stripped-down procedure:

  1. Name the destination. Which region? Which route?

  2. Set a Travel Timer. Short leg = 4 segments; standard = 6; long = 8; epic = 10.

  3. Assign roles. Guide (Wits+Lore), Scout (Wits+Stealth), Quartermaster (Wits+Craft), Watch (Presence+Insight).

  4. Make the Guide roll. DV 3. Clean Success = tick timer +1; Partial = tick +1 but gain a Boon; Miss = no progress and GM gains SB.

  5. Introduce one encounter. Draw a Heart from the regional deck or invent an NPC.

  6. Repeat until the timer fills. When it does, the party arrives – changed by the road.

For the full procedure, see the Travel Quick Reference at the end of this chapter (Section \(\ref{sec:travel-quickref}\)).

The Known World: A Geographic Overview

The following summary orients the major regions relative to one another. (See Chapter \(\ref{ch:lore-compendium}\) for cultural details.)

Taharka Highland kingdom of the Monsoon Crown. Coffee terraces, ridge-watchers, and passes that remember every army.
Fhara Desert peninsula of oasis courts and water-clerks. Well-judges, coffee rituals, and the Sunken City of Qalat al-Madina.
Sidhi Diaspora people of the Brass Coast. Lighthouse courts, freedman’s seals, and the green flame of memory.
Pereshi Highland fire-temple country extending toward northern Dhahara and the Himadri. Ash Prince’s seal, fire-readers, and the ninth coal that never cools.

Tulkani Itinerant storytellers and dream-carriers. Wagon circles, memory-cords, and the Dreamer who sleeps beneath the road.
Ngomebe Walking cities of the southern plateau. Keystone chargers, roller-pits, and the Still Voice of abandoned stone.
Dhahara (Nine Kingdoms) Eight present kingdoms and a mythically missing ninth, between northern Himdal Aeler country and the Kahfagian-controlled southern ports. Peacock Throne and monsoon river wars.

The Ways Between

Beyond the physical map lie the Ways Between – the dream-roads and spirit-paths of Obishaal, realm of dreams and death. Travel here is measured not in miles but in choices. A wrong turn might lead to yesterday. A right turn might lead to a version of yourself who made a different decision and is still alive to regret it.

Entering the Ways:

Thresholds exist in thin places – crossroads at midnight, bridges that appear only at low tide, caves behind waterfalls, or simply a mist that settles too thick and stays too long. A character may also enter through ritual, dream, or near-death experience.

Navigation in Obishaal:

Use the standard travel procedures, but the GM may replace the Spades (place) draw with a Virtue Test. The road judges. Acts of honesty, courage, mercy, or self-sacrifice advance a personal Virtue Timer [3]; acts of greed, cowardice, cruelty, or despair advance a Vice Timer [3]. When one fills, the character ascends (Virtue) to a higher layer or falls (Vice) to a lower one.

The Thirteenth Stone:

At the deepest layer, pressed into the silt of the oldest dream, lies the Thirteenth Stone – warm, pulsing, and whispering. Here, Ikasha, She Who Sleeps, dreams of freedom. Her voice offers bargains. Her freedom would remake the world.

How to Travel: The Core Journey

A journey in Fate’s Edge is a series of legs, each a self-contained scene with its own pressure. The full procedure is detailed in Appendix A.1 of the Worldbook; this section gives you the principles and a practical summary.

The Anatomy of a Leg

Each leg of a journey follows this rhythm:

  1. Name the destination and route. Which region? Which road or waterway?

  2. Set a Travel Timer. Based on distance and difficulty:

    • 4 segments: Short leg (a day’s ride, a river crossing)

    • 6 segments: Standard leg (several days, moderate opposition)

    • 8 segments: Extended leg (a week, dangerous terrain)

    • 10 segments: Epic leg (a month, extreme conditions)

  3. Assign roles. Each character takes a role; each role has a mechanical benefit.

  4. Make the Guide’s roll. This determines progress.

  5. Introduce one meaningful encounter. Draw a Heart from the regional deck or invent an NPC with a need.

  6. Apply a hazard. Draw a Club from the regional deck or use a universal hazard.

  7. Repeat until the timer fills. When it does, the party arrives.

Travel Roles

Assign one role per character each leg. Roles may rotate each leg.

Role Key Test Benefit on Success
Guide Wits + Lore Tick the Travel Timer +1.
Scout Wits + Stealth Party cannot be surprised on the first encounter of the leg.
Quartermaster Wits + Craft Prevent one Supply drop or recover 1 spent Supply.
Watch Presence + Insight Party gains +1 Position on the first defense roll of any encounter.

Each role action requires a roll (Attribute + appropriate skill, DV set by the GM). Failure may tick the Travel Timer backwards or introduce a complication.

Travel Timer Integration

The Travel Timer is not isolated – it interacts with your campaign’s other pressures. Use this guidance:

  • On a Clean Success: Tick the Travel Timer +1. No other effect.

  • On a Partial: Tick the Travel Timer +1. The player gains 1 Boon. Additionally, the GM may advance a relevant campaign timer (e.g., Blight Spreads, Cult Influence) by 1 – the delay or detour has given the opposition time.

  • On a Miss: No progress on the Travel Timer. The player gains 2 Boons. The GM gains Story Beats (SB) and must advance a relevant campaign timer by 1 – the party’s failure has cost them time, and the world has moved on.

  • When the Travel Timer Fills: The party arrives at their destination – but the journey has taken its toll. Mark 1 Fatigue per character for each segment beyond 4 (e.g., a 6-segment journey means 2 Fatigue, a 10-segment journey means 6 Fatigue). The GM may also spend accumulated SB to introduce a final complication: a welcoming party they did not expect, a change in the local situation, or a debt incurred on the road.

GM Tip: Timers Talk to Each Other

Your campaign timers (Mandate, Crisis, faction timers) should not sit idle while the party travels. A journey is not a pause – it is a pressure cooker. If the party is traveling to stop a ritual and the Travel Timer ticks slowly, the Ritual Completion timer should tick too. The road is not a safe space; it is a narrowing window.

Encounters and Events

Every 1–2 segments filled, the GM introduces an encounter or hazard. At minimum, each leg should include one meaningful encounter.

Type Examples Typical Resolution
Discovery Ruins, strange omens, hidden sites Insight or exploration
Hazard Terrain challenge, illness, extreme weather Endurance test; may use Supply
Encounter Bandits, emissaries, beasts, rival travelers Combat or social resolution
Complication Bond tested, supplies strained, pursuit gains ground Roleplay or skill challenge

On a failed travel roll, the GM may spend SB to introduce an encounter immediately.

Weather and Terrain Modifiers

The GM may apply modifiers based on conditions:

Condition Effect
Difficult Terrain Movement costs double; Charge/Skirmish requires a roll.
Extreme Weather (rain, snow, heat) First travel roll each leg is Desperate.
Hazardous Weather (storm, blizzard, sandstorm) Travel Timer ticks +1 at leg start; navigation rolls at –1 die.
Favorable Weather First travel roll each leg gains +1 Position.

Foraging and Subsistence

A character may forage during a leg instead of taking a role action. Roll Wits + Lore (DV by terrain, typical 3–4):

  • Clean Success: Gain 1 Supply.

  • Partial: Gain 1 Supply but mark 1 Fatigue (time or exposure).

  • Miss: No Supply; GM gains 1 SB (scarce resources, dangerous plant, predator sign).

Region Forage DV Typical Finds
Forest (Valewood, Aelinnel) 2–3 Berries, mushrooms, roots, small game
Steppe (Ykrul, Kuvani) 3–4 Edible roots, tubers, small game
Desert (Ashaan, Fhara) 4–5 Cactus water, insects, rare desert herbs
Marsh (Mistlands, Theona) 3–4 Cattails, frogs, medicinal mud
Mountain (Ubral, Pereshi) 3–4 Mountain herbs, small game, clean springs
Coast (Linn, Sidhi, Zakov) 2–3 Shellfish, seaweed, fish
Tundra (Northern Violet Steppe) 4–5 Lichens, frozen berries, small game

Game Structure and Time

Understanding how time works in Fate’s Edge helps you navigate both the mechanical and narrative flow of play.

Basic Units

Scene

The basic unit of narrative play, covering a specific situation or conflict (Some Time to Significant Time). Resolves a particular question or challenge.

Player Turn (Beat)

An individual player’s action within a scene: Declare action \(\rightarrow\) GM sets position \(\rightarrow\) roll \(\rightarrow\) resolve outcome \(\rightarrow\) manage consequences.

Round

Simultaneous or near-simultaneous actions within a scene (primarily for combat), representing a few seconds of real time.

Session

One complete game session (typically 3–6 hours), containing 2–4 major scenes and resolving significant narrative progress.

Downtime

The narrative time between scenes, used for recovery, advancement, and off-screen activities. Measured in days, weeks, or months depending on fiction.

Campaign

Entire story arc (6–20+ sessions) with major character development and lasting consequences.

Movement and Positioning

Space is tracked with range bands and Position (see Chapter \(\ref{ch:gm-core}\) for full rules).

Range Bands

Close

Touching distance: grapples, knife-work, hand on a relic.

Near

Same room/yard/deck; a rush away.

Far

Same site but distant; requires route or time to reach.

Absent

Off-screen; requires scene change or significant effort to interact.

Movement Actions

  • Move: Shift one range band as a beat.

  • Dash: Shift two bands as your full action (terrain may require a roll).

  • Melee Flag: Mark when two parties are in Near range and directly engaged in combat.

Position States

Dominant

You have cover, leverage, or ritual footing. Failure still leaves options.

Controlled

Standard case: exposed lanes, rivals near, watchful eyes. Failure has teeth, but not ruin.

Desperate

Bad ground, bad odds, bad timing. Failure is severe; success may bring extra XP.

Position Shifting:

  • GM can spend 1 SB to worsen Position by one step.

  • Player can spend 1 Boon to improve Position by one step (once per action).

  • Narrative triggers (flanking, reinforcements, etc.) can shift Position without cost.

Narrative Time

Time is measured by importance rather than duration.

A Moment

A glance, a strike, a whisper over a law-stone.

Some Time

A skirmish, a negotiation, a careful climb.

Significant Time

Hours of march, rites, audits, stakeouts.

Days

Drills, recoveries, research, roadwork.

Social Interactions

Social scenes use the same engine with cultural color (see Chapter \(\ref{ch:gm-core}\)).

Cultural Skill Emphases

Different regions value different approaches. Use the table in Chapter \(\ref{ch:lore-compendium}\) (World Regions and Cultures) to see what skills are most effective where. In general:

Region Valued Approach
Viterra Insight with parishes; Sway for markets; Presence + Sway under writ.
Acasia Insight for kin-bridges; Presence + Sway with banner-rights; Deception risks honor timers.
Ecktoria Sway in salons; Deception at court; Performance in temple fora.
Kahfagia Insight aboard ship; Sway at piers; Presence + Sway on a storming deck.
Vhasia Presence for courtly etiquette; Sway for diplomacy; Insight for hidden loyalties.
Thepyrgos Lore for academic debate; Sway for rhetorical argument; Insight for uncovering truth.
Mistlands Lore for reading fog signs; Resolve for resisting spirit influence; Ritual for bell-law.
Ykrul Command for leadership; Performance for boast and saga; Lore for steppe navigation.
Sidhi Sway for negotiation; Deception for smuggling; Insight for lighthouse justice.
Tulkani Performance for storytelling; Insight for reading truth; Lore for road etiquette.

Social Stakes & Timers

Your GM may use visible timers to track social progress:

  • Alliance Timer (Viterra): Parishes and guilds come to your side.

  • Honor Timer (Acasia): Feasts, oaths, wyrd – trust builds (or frays).

  • Bureau Timer (Ecktoria): Stamps, seals, approvals – delay is pressure.

  • Trust Timer (Kahfagia): Pilots and crews extend favors and routes.

  • Courtesy Timer (Aelinnel): Fae negotiations – speak true or lose standing.

  • Hollow Attention Timer (Aelaerem/Mistlands): The uninvited guest draws near.

Hospitality as a Social Mechanic

In many cultures – especially Aelaerem, Ykrul, Tulkani, Ubral, and Fhara – hospitality is sacred. Accepting a cup, a meal, or a night’s shelter creates a bond. Breaking hospitality is worse than breaking a sworn oath.

Hospitality Bond:

When you accept hospitality (food, drink, or shelter) from a host in a culture that honors guest-right, you and the host gain a mutual Hospitality Bond [3]. While the bond has segments, neither party may take hostile action against the other. The bond ticks when either party lies to the other, breaks a promise made under the host’s roof, or acts to harm the other’s interests. When it fills, the bond snaps – and the violator gains a Black Mark in that culture (–1 die on all social rolls with that culture for one season).

Refusing Hospitality:

Refusing an offered cup, meal, or shelter is a grave insult in hospitality cultures. The GM may spend 1 SB to worsen Position by one step in all social interactions with that host and their community for the remainder of the visit.

Supply and Resources

Track scarcity with a Supply Timer shared by the party’s expedition.

Segments State & Effects
0 (Full) Well-provisioned; no penalty.
2 (Low) Minor frictions; –1 to resource checks.
3 (Dangerous) Each PC gains Fatigue 1.
4 (Empty) Severe penalties; desperate measures.

Engaging the World – Player Actions

  • Scout & Signal: A follower can make the next travel action Dominant (mark Exposure or Harm 1 on them – see Chapter \(\ref{ch:running-assets-followers}\)).

  • Local Color: Briefly state what locals notice about you; GM offers a small fictional edge or a tempting timer – choose.

  • Mark the Map: On arrival, declare one change to the fiction (new ford, patron’s shrine, toll-skip). GM may attach a minor timer as cost.

  • Asset Activation: Spend 1 Boon to activate an asset dramatically during a scene (see Chapter \(\ref{ch:running-assets-followers}\)).

  • Follower Assistance: Have a follower assist your actions for bonus dice (max +3 from all sources, see Chapter \(\ref{ch:gm-core}\)).

  • Invoke Cultural Knowledge: If your character has a relevant background or has spent significant time in a region, you may ask the GM one straightforward yes/no question about local customs, taboos, or safe practices.

Travel Quick Reference

This one-page summary consolidates the travel procedures. Keep it handy during sessions.

The Core Travel Loop

  1. Name destination and route.

  2. Set Travel Timer (4/6/8/10).

  3. Assign roles: Guide, Scout, Quartermaster, Watch.

  4. Guide rolls (Wits+Lore, DV 3):

    • Clean Success: Tick timer +1.

    • Partial: Tick timer +1; player gains 1 Boon; GM may advance campaign timer +1.

    • Miss: No progress; player gains 2 Boons; GM advances campaign timer +1.

  5. Encounter: Draw a Heart (NPC) from regional deck.

  6. Hazard: Draw a Club (complication) from regional deck.

  7. Repeat until timer fills.

  8. Arrival: Mark Fatigue = segments beyond 4; GM spends accrued SB for final complication.

Summary

The world has opinions. Movement is timers and color, position rises and sinks with weather and words, and every suit you draw speaks in a regional accent. Ask the land for a favor – then pay it back on the road.

Remember:

Every interaction with the world is an opportunity. Use your assets, deploy your followers, and engage with the setting actively. The world responds to your choices, and every journey changes both you and the places you pass through. The road is not a ledger – it is a conversation. Speak carefully. Listen closely. And always leave something behind for the next traveler.

GM Tip: Simplify Travel for Your First Session

If this is your first time running travel in Fate’s Edge, use the stripped-down version from the opening of this chapter:

  1. Set a Travel Timer (6 is a good default).

  2. Make the Guide roll once per leg.

  3. Introduce one NPC encounter.

  4. Introduce one hazard.

  5. When the timer fills, they arrive.

Add Roles and full timer integration once you are comfortable. The system rewards depth, but it does not demand it.
